Another one bites the dust

~ enough singing… This sort of thing puts me more in the mood to cry or kick over sand castles… While I happen to like operatic rock in the Queen mold this is about the un-natural demise of sessions and other musical and dance related events. I suspect all of us with sufficient experience (age) under our belts will have felt the pain of loss when some jerk or jerks by whatever means have caused a session to come to a close. I’ve had it happen via the tele and the stereo, where we suddenly found the sound raising and eventually took the hint we were no longer welcome, that the publican didn’t get enough profit from our presence and preferred the tele and the stereo and this was his way of telling us to move on.

In another case we had a sudden and unexpected change of landlords and they told us we were no longer welcome.

So, what has happened to you? How did you deal with it? Did you manage to pull out something more up tempo in the way of a Queen song sort of rise? In both the cases previously mentioned that was it, those sessions are no more and nothing came to replace them. Folks just gave up, or so it seems.

So, I don’t want just moping about this and misery, which does love company ~ I want a brass band and Sousa marches sort of pep talk. I want to hear success over adversity and ways around those dark and desperate lows such an occurance can throw up. I want to vanquish the demons…

Oh, there was one satisfaction with one of these places, it is no longer a pub. It dried up and now is a veterinary clinic. The a*shole that took over didn’t last long after giving us the boot… Maybe it was the little bit of voodoo I put into practice?

Re: Another one bites the dust

Ceol…. about 1 1/2 years ago my main music partner and I killed a session on purpose, the owners were getting intolerable. The wife was pretty nice, but he bullied her and put her in a bad mood which she took out on us, just a few times too many. I worked really hard at bringing diners and drinkers out, put it in the local papers, did mass emailings, did what I could, and from what I hear they went dead on Friday nights when we walked.

If a lot of people came in to drink, they got mad no one was eating, and if they ate, they didn’t drink enough. I know we brought in more than we were paid. It was essentially a hard gig for us, we had to bring sound, we could not take a break in four hours, even if others were playing, we were told we’d better keep playing since we were paid, and not all that much, we couldn’t even talk for a few minutes without a nasty stare.

I brought in a table of friends, about eight of them, one time who ran up a $1000 tab. It was a pricey place, but good food, and it’s out in the sticks too. The owner said they didn’t drink enough. It was a constant battle…. the owners advertised their other rock music, but they couldn’t add one little line about us to the ad. I was expected to do it myself…promote it, which I did.

The final blow was when the other regular person couldn’t make it, so I got someone else in last minute. They were nice to him, which was different! They let him play all night, four hours, and he had to catch a train so left right at the end with someone. Then the owners said they would pay me but not him, since he is not who they hired. I had gotten subs before without clearing it, and we discussed it being OK in the beginning….this guy was a great musician, so what was different…. they even had a great night at the bar. I had paid him before he left, which meant I wouldn’t be paid, and they really worked us, the leaders anyway. Others could take breaks but not us, and no food, and one free beer or wine was the deal for all. She of the couple ultimately gave me the full pay after an argument, but it left such bad feelings I said I would never go back. No one else wanted to take it over either, they are so obnoxious. They called a few times wanting it back but we just said no.

So I shifted gears and we started one at an Irish center (IAANJ) which is a lot more fun. It has its complications too, but we get loads of people out, kids, old folks, everything between. People get up and dance, it’s a lot more fun. I do way more work towards this session than for any other in the past, I record them to produce an in-house CD to support the thing, pay the guest hosts etc. So I work on that about 30 hours during the month between sessions, and all the proceeds go to the club to support the session, I make nothing on them. We’ve branched out into concerts there too, which are major work and I don’t make a cent on them, so this is not about the money for sure. We have Maeve Donnelly & Tony McManus this Friday (hint hint, contact me for reservations, we are honoring ticket holders and reserved people first and are nearly full so important to send your name in). With free refreshments, parking and a session afterwards.

On the other weeks between those IAANJ sessions and concerts, I just go to other friends’ sessions and relax, talk, take breaks, etc.

In the pubs it’s usually all about money, but that’s what they’re in business for. I just wish some of them wouldn’t be so hard on the musicians.

Re: Another one bites the dust

Ceolachan…some people have no taste!!

Our session at IAANJ, it has a different set of problems, which I won’t go into deeply, but suffice it to say, when all decisions have to be run through a “board” and have a meeting it can be a bit of a slowdowner. They have always ultimately OK’d everything, but I think they like their meetings and routines and notes and accounting and minutes taken etc. Everything is by the book, the bylaws, etc. it’s fine really though…. it’s lightened up and they have seen we have turned the place around and they are now known for being a great concert venue too, where both the house and the musicians are very happy with the profits, in addition to the sessions.

One board member, is a musician and he decided to get the ball rolling with this initially. He and his wife do all the set up, the food, CD sales etc. I do all the other arranging, and produce the CDs, do publicity, emailings etc. We get a massive crew out usually both listeners and musicians. The session is generally pretty good too, in spite of the large numbers. The concerts, which are overgrown house style concerts are a lot of fun, always a session afterwards too, great food etc.

This is the first non-pub session I have been involved with. it is much more pleasant overall, a lot more happy people (some crabapples though! But not many) and we have a great time. It’s a lot of work for me, and a few others who set up the room and deal with the food and drink (no alcohol, tea, coffee, but you can BYOB) and keeping a large crowd happy is sometimes challenging, but overall, this is a breath of fresh air compared to the pubs.

Still…. small kitchen sessions are calling….. and the joy of washing the dishes after! As in the other thread… I am not kidding, I love to wash dishes. So be sure to invite me to yours! This way money is never a thought, just a relaxing time.
