What should be expected of a good Session member?

This thread is not intended to level criticism at any individual member or their contributions. So, don’t worry, Jeremy.
It’s just that I’ve been around here for about two years now. The first year, I spent my time lurking and downloading the odd tune or two. In recent months, I’ve joined in the discussions and done a lot of “waffling” but not much else. Also, I’ve downloaded even more tunes. However, just this last week or so I’ve got around to actually submitting tunes -it’s quite addictive-and I can see myself adding a new one almost every day. For the first time, I feel that I’m making a useful contribution to this site and I get a feeling of satisfaction when I see that other members are actually downloading some of the tunes. In many ways, this represents what a real session should be about i.e finding and swapping tunes.

Of course, there’s a social side to sessions as well and it’s good to exchange views and jokes from time to time. Also, some of the discussions can be very informative. So, I’m just wondering how important it is to fully participate in all aspects of this site. Should any of us feel guilty if we just make the odd comment or joke in the “Discussions” section or even ignore them alltogether and just use the site for downloading a few tunes? I’m sure that we all have the desire to contribute something but is it necessary? After all, Jeremy states that the only rule is to “Be Civil”.

Re: What should be expected of a good Session member?

There’s no obligation on anybody to contribute anything.

In most online communities, mailing lists or even real-world gatherings, there tends to a 4:1 ratio of “lurkers” to active contributors. I think that’s also the case with The Session: about 20% of members have contributed something, be it tunes, comments, recordings, sessions or links.

Nobody should feel guilty about not contributing tunes. In fact, rather than submitting a tune for the sake of it and leaving it uncommented, it would be better to find a tune you feel strongly about and leave a heartfelt comment.

Having said that, the more tunes and stuff you contribute, the more weight your voice will have. For instance, if someone complains about the way tunes are listed or notated, I’ll check out their member profile: if they’re a heavy contributor, I’ll take their comments to heart. But if the complaint is the first thing they’ve ever posted to the site then it won’t carry much weight.

For anyone who’s worried that they’re not contributing enough:

1. Add a few words about yourself in your member profile: where you live, what you play, anything you want. If you do this before you start posting comments, it helps to “put a face to the name”.

2. Don’t fall into the trap of believing that the discussions section is the only place where chat goes on. The truth is that comments here are more fleeting whereas a comment on a tune, a recording or a session is likely to be read by more people more often.

3. Read the Frequently Asked Questions.

4. Have fun.

Re: What should be expected of a good Session member?

Personally, Jack, I mainly come here because I am unemployed. I sit on the site all day sometimes, go through the tunes in my tunebook, play my fiddle, skim through the new postings, peruse the discussions, occasionally put up a tune that isn’t already here (although it isn’t easy to think of one!)

I would never fault anyone who does not post tunes. The nature of this site (in that anyone can put up any string of notes and call it whatever they like as long as it makes some kind of melodic sense) is such that if you are not completely sure of your ability to transcribe tunes accurately (and hopefully post a comment about where you got your setting) you might be detracting from the overall quality of the Session as a resource for sheet music.

When I first became a member of the site a few years ago I think there were about 200 tunes in the archives. Now I can ask “what do you call that tune?” at any session anywhere in the world, come here and (usually) find some version of it. It amazes me. I deeply appreciate the major contributors here (you know who you are), but if everybody posted something every day we’d end up with JC’s ABC tune finder, which just confuses me.

Re: What should be expected of a good Session member?

My groatsworth: this site is an important addition to the general lore, the environment, the landscape of the music. This is down to the calibre of the regular contributors. Many folks here have good valid views on the music, how sessions should be run, what defines a good tune, and all the other navel gazing stuff we do.

Yes, there are a few stingy nettles and other weeds and misanthropes around - and even occasionally they can make valid contributions, so weedkiller should be applied with caution.

Yes, also, comments made here in the dicussion section are more ephemeral than those in relation to a tune or a session, or a link. But the discussion section here is the metabolism, the living heart of The Session -- as it should be in a real session. So the comments here will of course be more fleeting.

Also, I’m fussy these days who I play tunes with…and this is not a lot to do with anyone’s musical ability (although some people are improving at a frightening rate) - more whether I like them as friends or not. Right now we’ve got a nice wee session nearby where no one ego features too strongly and there’s a real healthy lift to the tunes.

When a session loses that spark, it becomes moribund, or a litany of obsequious pandering to a few inflated egos. Likewise, here at the sesh, there is a rich organic ecological biodiversity of opinion, which does not need too much maintenance.

So, as Jeremy indicated, John, I don’t think you need to feel too dutybound, or gung-ho about having to be a “good” The Session member. As I said, my groatsworth.
