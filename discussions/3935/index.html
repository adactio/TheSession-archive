As we’re only allowed to submit one discussion per day I thought I’d cheat and put three things on the one thread. These are:

1. Comments about sessions.

2. TheSession T-shirts.

3. Southwark Irish Festival.

1. Comments about sessions.
This is not an appeal, just an observation and an invitation, if you like. I notice that our vociferous little group of sessioneers down here in SE London, myself, Cath, Aidan and Sarah the Flute are in the habit of posting comments, not just here, but also in the sessions section. Almost like a log, in some cases. Personally I think it’s a nice idea as it shows people (even potential visitors) that the sessions are very much alive and kicking, and it’s just nice to be able to chat specifically about your particular session. I also notice, after a quick surf around, that not a great deal of other sessions do this. Just to say, I would recommend to do so. It’s fun.

2. TheSession T-shirts.
Just an idea - maybe in the same colours as this site. Any thoughts?
Before we actually *DO* anything we’d best wait till Jeremy is back - his site after all. But I thought it would be fun.

3. Southwark Irish Festival.
This isn’t really a notification that this event will be taking place this coming weekend, Sat 3rd Sun 4th July, in Peckham Rye Park London SE15. More like some thoughts on the nature of this type of event from one who is “biased” in favour of the Pure Drop. This event, supported by Southwark Council, will include lots and lots of country music, the old bit of ceili band, some Irish bands of various musical tastes, Irish dancing, etc…but NO SESSIONS. Or any actual trad music. Of any description.

I for one will have better things to do. Like cleaning the skidmarks out of Dow’s underpants, for example. In other words anything is better than going to that fiasco. I’ve been a couple of times before so I speak from bitter experience. Do any other folk have similar experience of these “Oirish” Festivals?

Don’t be too harsh on Southwark Irish Festival. The programme may not be to everyone’s taste, but the main organisers are Southwark Irish Arts and Cultural Development (SICAD) who are a voluntary organisation, and part of the proceeds raised over the two days goes toward maintaining SICAD’s first-rate classes in Irish language and Irish traditional music which take place on Thursday nights oin term-time in St Thomas More’s Hall, Lordship Lane, East Dulwich. The teacher of the music classes (beginners through to advanced) is Gerry Diver, a fantastic fiddler and multi-instrumentalist, frequently seen in sessions as well as many gigs. These types of class are the seedbeds for the ITM of the future, as well as providing much enjoyment for older beginners or more experienced players. Anyone who feels brave enough to attend, despite Lord of the Flies’s comments, would be most likely to find something they like on the Sunday, when the programme starts at 12 noon after open-air Mass. And even if they hate it, it’s all in a good cause…

Jane R
