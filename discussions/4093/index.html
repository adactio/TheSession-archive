Re: Name dropping

Does it count if the ‘big name’ in question packs up and leaves soon after you start playing?
:o)

Seriously, name dropping is only name dropping if you or your story’s audience worship the names you’re dropping. I’ve played with some ‘big’ names, but found them to be comfortably sized people, just like the rest of us, certainly not worthy of deification, no matter how musically talented they might be. Just folks, enjoying a tune. And they would see the humor in it as much as I do if someone else thought more of *me* because I’ve played with some known names.

In short, I wish we could collectively get over our tendencies toward worshipping good musicians as celebrities and recognize that they’re just somebody’s neighbors--*our* neighbors in the overall scheme of things. Yes, it’s exciting and fun to play with really good musicians, but there are lots of really good musicians who don’t have recording contracts or a publicity agent. As participants in Irish trad music in particular, shouldn’t we be more mindful of that?

Dow, about the extras - I *did* keep my mouth shut, who am I to rain on their parade? But it’s funny that you should mention tantrums and incessant whinging - the latter is such a characteristic of the professional extra, that it put me off doing much more of it.

I learned a life lesson from that; if you want to be an actor, be an actor, or do something else for a living and amateur dramatics in your spare time - but don’t be an extra, which falls between all the stools and guarantees that you will be treated as a faceless slave. Yes, I suppose you’d conclude that any post you read, you should infer only the nicest possible meaning if there is any ambiguity. It’s just that sometimes that’s very *very* hard to do.

I feel like being serious for a brief moment cuz I want to explain myself.

From the posts I’ve been reading in the last few weeks, it appears that people think I’m outright opposed to mentioning the names of famous trad players.

That’s *not* what I’ve been on about.

My only motive in making a sort of light-hearted fuss about the title of this thread was not the mentioning of “names” per se (for example Jeremy’s benign listing of famous players he saw at Willie Week which people assumed I might accuse of being namedropping) but rather the negative implications of badly-worded self-seeking posts, which I don’t think belong here @ thesession.org. I’ve not been playing tunes seriously for very long (just over 2 years) so I’ve got a long way to go. I therefore consider myself to be a newcomer to the music. Being a newcomer can make you feel very small and crappy at times, and it’s not nice to come online for some tunes and craic and be made to feel even more small and crappy. What’s wrong with being “juvenile and laddish”? I call it having a laugh, which was what I was trying to do with Zina, Jack and Will this afternoon before our interaction was hijacked. So, if we can all make a concerted effort not to make newcomers feel small and crappy, I reckon we can all get along just fine.

Basically, I’m saying that as a newcomer to the music, I perceive some of the interaction on this website to be this way (i.e. all about “who you know”, “who you play with”, “how good a player you are”, “who your local session enemies are who you want to flame online”) and worsening to the extent that it’s becoming the norm and people are becoming numbed to it. For some people the issue to moan about is the way tunes are being posted, or that the database is being cluttered in some way by comments that have nothing to do with the music or whatever. Well, it’s the undercurrent of better-than-you hierarchy, and sneering humourlessness that’s my issue. Others might not see it the same way. Well, I do, and I feel as though I’m within my rights to say so and make my point without being flamed.
