Beware!

So, we’re all merrily playing away a rake of Jigs Reels H/pipes, etc. Then one of our regulars alerts us to the fact that “a fiddler” has turned up. We invite him to join in. A young guy in his 20’s I reckon. That’s young for us. Join us he does. I make the effort to do introductions. Tunes start up. He, like the rest of us, joins in. Finally after being asked a couple of times he does lead some tunes, in a rather individual fashion. He’s fairly good.

Later on, at the end of the session, myself and Max trot out some nice wee tunes, old standards, like wot we do. French Scottisches and reels like the Seasguin.

But we can’t finish a set cos the new guy is arguing with one of our regulars! A blazing row, at some points close to fisticuffs, is underway. The few of us who were playing when it erupted missed the beginning of the ruction, but what we did catch was our regular guy being informed that he wasn’t even Irish! (because he’s from the north of the country.)
Things were beginning to look rather ugly (or beautiful -- depending how much you wanted the blow in to suffer a broken jaw.)

Many more words were said. Blow-in “whizz kid” assured us that the Blythe will henceforth have a bad reputation among the elite players in London. We kindly inform him that that’s fine so long as his type of attitude personna leaves to wallow in the mire of his perceived mediocrity. That’s our choice.

I’m sure yer man is a lovely chap underneath it all, albeit misunderstood, but surely his fortunes would be better enriched elsewhere, don’t you think?

↳ Posted by Guernsey Pete on November 4th, 2005.

Re: Beware!

Just to give this thread some first-hand perspective …

A new player joined the session last night. During the usual chit-chat about how are you, who are you, etc. it emerged that he used to live locally and had been back over in Ireland for five or six years. He’d just returned about a week ago and was delighted to find a local session to play in.

I was pleased for him … finding a local sesh is often a great way of putting down roots and building up a network of pals. We all know that casual acquaintanceships developed at sessions can often grow into lifelong firm friendships. That our session mates can often be helpful to us in so many other ways. That the mere fact of one evening a week with real musical purpose can, at our low ebbs, provide us with a degree of comfort and sustenance way beyond its ostensible face-value.

So, yes … I was pleased for him, in a vague kind of way.

And he was useful on the fiddle … and although our session doesn’t NEED more useful players, it never turns its back on them.

Now … the commotion started with me overhearing him bad-mouthing the local Irish Centre. Now, local musicians might all have views on how the Centre was run in the past, even on how it’s currently being run but the new manager, the Management Committee and volunteers such as myself are putting big efforts into making a forthcoming gig by a fairly “hot” Irish Traditional act an outstanding success in the hope that we can use this a platform to bring other big-name acts across to our neck of the woods.

We’ve had to struggle a bit with a slight nervousness from within the Centre’s ranks and overcoming the apathy which many in the local Irish community have towards the Centre, which has kept a fairly low profile in the past.

I know at first hand the amount of effort which many, myself included, are putting into this venture - publicising the gig … drawing up press releases, asking Irish pubs, music venues, tourist information offices, etc. all over London to display posters, hassling movers and shakers in the Irish music community to do some free publicity for us and so on.

So … when some blow-in to the session started mouthing about the Centre, I saw red.

See, if I turn up to a strange session, I try to keep my gob shut for fear of saying anything that might cause offence. I always work on the basis that I don’t know who I’m talking to, I don’t know their sensitivities, I don’t know their connections. And a harmless comment could be read as a major piece of dissing when it comes from a virtual stranger … even if that stranger happens to be a player of the music. (Being a man or woman of the music opens a lot of doors, and excuses a multitude of sins … but it’s not a carte blanche!)

Yer man’s mistake - in my eyes - was to start loudly running down something which many of us locally have invested a bit of effort in. It only takes one or two ear-wiggers at the bar to overhear that sort of talk and a bad reputation emerges. To my way of thinking, whatever our personal views on the matter, where other local Irish music ventures are concerned - particularly those which are in some way the “babies” of sessioneers from within our own ranks - then publicly we ought to give them our support. (We are, of course, free within our own niches and in our own circles to say what we feel and if there are one or two criticisms, then I for one am quite happy to accept these and respond to them.)

So I called yer man on it … perhaps more forcibly than I needed to. But it flushed out his true colours. Some of the other stuff he came out with was off-the-wall … “You’re not even Irish” for one (I am … although it’s pretty irrelevant given that our session harbours people from all over the place and their place of birth is not an issue) and (I was playing mandolin last night) “That’s not even a traditional instrument … there are sessions I go to where you wouldn’t be allowed to play if you brought one of them with you.” (Incidentally that last remark gave rise to my only truly witty riposte of the evening … “Well, bucko, whether you’re playing a ”traditional“ instrument or not, you’ll not be allowed to play at this particular session in future!”) If it hadn’t been for my mate ushering me home, the incident could well have got even further out of hand.

Apparently yer man spent a long time afterwards pouring his heart out to another of our number who gallantly tried to smooth things over and calm things down, saying how sorry he was, etc., etc. I was almost prepared to forgive him on the basis of his youth and impetuosity. I’ve been young and foolish … and middle-aged and foolish to boot … and I’ve often welcomed the acceptance of my shamefacedly-offered olive branches. But apparently it now emerges he was scathing not just about me, but about the quality of the music at the session generally. So basically, there’ll be no accepting apologies from me. He should get himself off to a session or two where people are playing at “his level” … if such a rarified session exists.

Anyway … all that stuff about how good he is or thinks he is … I’m not that bothered … if Paddy Keenan or Tommy Peoples came to our local session and gave that sort of attitude, then I’d not want to play tunes with them either … they’d go or I’d go. It’s a good enough session as it is and it’s got a hell of a lot better as the years have rolled on and while one or two good new players are always welcome, they’re welcome only insofar as they don’t wind people up. Give me a handful of people who are good enough and who get along with each other and give each other a bit of space and a bit of respect and I’m happy. We’ve not got to accommodate primadonnas who have an highly-developed sense of their musical ability and a complete lack of common-sense when it comes to settling into a new session.

My only regret about the whole incident is that I lost my cool … but I did what I did, said what I said and I’m content not just to live with that, but to set it out in print, warts and all. And I’m prepared to take anyone’s opinion as to how appropriate or otherwise my behaviour was now that I’ve laid the facts out, as I witnessed, nay experienced, them!

So there you go … there’s my stint in the confessional over.

I think there’s a lesson in this for all of us …

Established sessions are generally formed of a bunch of people who’ve turned out for several years come rain, hail or snow to share tunes and to swap yarns and to enjoy each other’s company. When a newcomer enters this sort of environment, then they’d be advised to take a while to get to know people before they embark on criticisms which might go against the session’s grain … Otherwise they may find … as happened last night … that the session closes ranks at alarming speed.
