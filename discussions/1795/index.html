*Sets* of tunes

Dear All.
….and I mean All, not just self appointed *good* players. A topic awaits to which all may contribute, and from which all may learn.

The next stage up in the evolution of a player of the music (a stage to which I would be striving to attain in maybe two or three decades - I can only hope - everyone else I know seems to be dying in front of my eyes) must surely be to construct balanced sets of tunes….balanced in the same way an artist balances a painting composition.

I’ve heard people play, very competently, sets of tunes which do nothing, because they’re either all in the same key, or they’re in keys which don’t fit in with one another. Superglue can’t stop these ad hoc arrangements from drifting apart naturally.

Yet on other occasions, some sets hold together by all the tunes *being* similar, because they’re unusual tunes in the first place.Then there’s others which just need to change key.

Please allow me to exemplify:

Normal tune, with a ‘lift’ tune on the end:
Jig of Slurs/Atholl Highlanders.
Blackberry Blossom/Dick Gossip’s/then back *Down* to the third one in the John Bow Set…it’s on Music at Matt Molloy’s. That set is known here as the John Bow set.They sellotape great with each other.

‘Up’ tune, then dropping down:
The Curlew, then down to McDermott’s, as in thon Altan album.They hardly need more than a few inches of string to hold them together.

‘Unusual tunes’, but which sound great blutacked together:
Dinny O’Briens
Farewell to Connacht

I can’t remember any more. But that should get those, who know what I’m droning on about, thinking about it.

Perhaps people could let us know what their choice changes are, or do they bother. I for one think it’s a great way to make the journey more interesting, even if people are unaware why the music appears more lively maybe tonight…

Danny.

Re: *Sets* of tunes

I don’t like actually speeding up or down in a set, unless it’s an air followed by a reel, which I think is more of a Scottish thing. So we might play Floating From Skerry followed by The Islay Rant and B. Stubbert’s. Changing tempo actually within a set of reels, say, is awkward in a session because not everyone will do it exactly the same, then it just sounds like everyone is chasing everyone else. I reckon it’s good, though to appear to accelerate by picking tunes where the emphasis on the beat changes (does that make sense?). So, for example, a set I like playing is Devlin’s Jig, followed by Aaron’s Key, and then into Collins‘ Jig. The first two are like regular jigs, but Collins’ is somehow more like a ‘Gallop’ and it really lifts the set.
Other current ‘favourite’ sets are:
Maurice Lennon’s, Charlie Harris’s, The Trip to Durrow - They all have a different rhythmic feel, yet they hang together nicely as a set.
Lads of Laois, The Bag Of Spuds, The Coast of Austria - going from Em to Am to Amaj.
Or sets that go from Em/G to BMinor like Killavill Fancy / Sweeneys Buttermilk, or Cottage in The Grove / The Otter’s Holt.
or The White Petticoat / The Lilting Banshee (Em - Am).
A current concoction that seems to rattle along nicely is The Road to Rio / The Green Mountain / The Shaskeen - it sounds like a sort of ’instant classic!"

Criteria for the Comhaltas sets? I see one set was on that BothyBand 1975 album - Salamanca set. If that’s anything to go by, looks like they’re standard sets. Personally I prefer messing around, making up my own sets, ones that I think hang together well.

Another example being:

Pigtown
Mayor Harris’s Fedora
Sheehan’s.

I know to lead sets properly you have to do it ethically, ie, not know what’s coming next, until the last bar of the current tune, then, as if by magic, breenge effortlessly into the next unexpected tune.
That’s our method of improvising. We do it with whole tunes rather than phrases, like wot jazz people do. And we play faster (…hell, I’m not knocking Jazz….please forgive me, St. John Coltrane). But the reality is Modern Jazz musicians more or less planned their improvisations in advance! Another reality is that a well-devised set is very worth doing as you know it works, cos you’ve thought about key changes, and thus how the ‘mood’ of the set changes as a result. I think that’s what I’m getting at rather than Highland (Strathspey) into Reel sets, which has been previously dicussed on the Session. But feel free to go in that direction, but it has been done.

Danny.

Sets of tunes - Comhaltas Book

Re dfost’s query on criteria for Session Sets Book, this evolved over a 2 year period at the regular Wednesday night Culturlann sessions. Initially very few people had many tunes in common and it was agreed to put together a few consensus sets. This worked so well that enough sets were put together to justify doing the Book (it saves an awful lot of photo-copying when new musicians join the group!). Selection of tune sets was mainly agreed beforehand based on various criteria (tunes associated with favourite musicians such as the Joe Cooley reels or Humours of Tulla set, tunes popular in certain areas like the Sonny Murray hornpipes from Clare, not to mention the odd Bothy Band selection, etc). While there is a case for Danny’s argument of varying sets, it all depends on the ethos of the session and what people feel comfortable with - I think that that’s one of the attractions of trad sessions in that they each have their own particular character “Vive la difference” or whatever they say in France!
The agreed sets work very well at the Culturlann on Wednesday nights and we’ve now almost enough sets for Book 2. While the URL for Book 1 has been given above, hard copies can also be obtained from Comhaltas at http://www.comhaltas.com/seisiun/foinn.htm
