Single/primary instrument or multi-instrumentalist (in sessions)?

I’m posting a thread strictly for curiosity sake. My question is motivated by something as simple as how we get to know each other on this forum. There are several ways, of course, though for this thread’s intents & purposes perhaps we can find out about our choice of instruments for session playing?

Many of the regular posting members I am familiar with which instruments you play. Some are multi-instrumentalist for sessions while others’ tend to play their primary instrument, yet also play another instrument (if not more).

I’d like to hear responses from anyone willing and pleased to reply! Thanks in advance.

Here is my situation. My primary instrument is flute. I play tunes on flute every chance I get. Though I’m returning to session playing after a spell of not attending local sessions. When I last played sessions it was mostly flute. I also play whistle in sessions. Mostly high D or low D. Sometimes it’s other key whistles & flute. In sessions I’ve played them in keys of G, Bb, C, A & F. The F probably least though it’s one of my favourite. Got it from Colin Goldie!

Re: Single/primary instrument or multi-instrumentalist (in sessions)?

I have always tended to arm myself with two instruments in a session. Very early on, it was guitar and mandolin, then, as my focus shifted towards tune-playing, mandolin and whistle. Since taking up the fiddle, I have neglected the whistle (to the point where I would be hesitant about playing it in public). Even playing the mandolin is harder work now than it was when it was my main focus, but it still comes with me to every session – I tend to save it for the slower tunes and for song accompaniment (by which I mean harmonies, countermelodies and embellishments, not ‘backing’).

I am often self-conscious about bringing multiple instruments to a session, partly because of the extra space they take up and partly because it could be perceived as ostentatious, or as ‘quantity over quality’. I was heartened, at a session yesterday, that there was a musician next to me with a fiddle and a set of pipes (although his multi-instrumentalism was far more justified than mine by his proficiency on both instruments).

I should add that, at my local session, I am often seen with a piano accordion as well. This is somewhat under duress – I would much rather not have to lug it there, and it certainly takes up space. I play it in a klezmer band, and if some of my band mates happen to be there (it is a ‘no styles barred’ kind of session), it is requested that I bring it.

Re: Single/primary instrument or multi-instrumentalist (in sessions)?

I take a guitar and mandolin to my regular sessions. Even though I consider myself primarily a guitar player, because of other accompanists being present, I probably play the mandolin 95% of the time in one session and 70% of the time in the other.

To be honest, it’s slightly easier playing the mandolin, not from a technical point of view, but from a stress one. Melodic mistakes on the mandolin don’t stand out quite as much as harmonic or rhythmic mistakes on the guitar. And with melody you’re playing a set script, whereas with accompaniment you’re often having to write your own in real time.

↳ Posted by sfmans on April 6th, 2026.

Re: Single/primary instrument or multi-instrumentalist (in sessions)?

I started playing Irish trad later in life on mandolin, and played that for a number of years before getting interested in flute as a way to explore a wider range of ornamentation and expression. So I’ve been bringing both mandolin and keyed D flute to sessions.

I still have more learned repertoire on the mandolin than on flute, so that’s one reason I bring both. I like having the mandolin for tunes that have important notes on the fiddle/mandolin G string instead of “folding” or harmonizing on the flute. The mandolin also gives me a rest if I feel my flute embouchure getting weak or tired, but that’s less of a problem lately.

There are times when I think I should just settle on flute and leave the mandolin at home. Keep it more “pure” as a session experience, if that makes sense. I may eventually get there. I’ve always been a multi-instrumentalist at heart though, so the habit is hard to drop.

As a side note, the mechanics of managing more than one instrument at a session can be difficult, but I cobbled together a flute holder tube lashed to a folding mandolin stand that doesn’t take up much floor space. Switching is easy, and it provides a relatively safe space to hold both instruments for a trip to the loo or the bar. I try not to think about the combined financial value sitting on that little stand when I’m away from it, but so far there have been no disasters.
