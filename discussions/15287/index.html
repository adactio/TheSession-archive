Understating the Rhythm in Hornpipes.

I notice that a lot of the famous players play hornpipes understating the dotted and cut aspect of the consecutive eighth notes so that they almost sound like reels. To me this
sounds more pleasing to the ear and more stimulating to the
musical intellect, (without sounding like a complete tosser! )
On the other hand I notice a lot of people swing reels like they’re playing a hornpipe, which is not so effective to me.
Any thoughts on this subject.

(“Field Guide to the Irish Music Session” by Barry Foyle, page 79)

“…The funny thing is, whereas divergent reel rhythms usually find a common groove in which to coexist, the two contrasting hornpipe styles are almost impossible to meld. Playing next to someone who’s in the opposing hornpipe camp is a lot like being poked with a butter knife at a metronome speed of, oh, 110 for three minutes straight…”

He’s talking about the way boxes and fiddles play hornpipes: DAH da-DAH da-DAH da-DAH

…and the way flautists, pipers and whistlers do: DA-DA-DA-DA-DA-DA
