Instant Gratification …

Seven years‘ larnin’, seven years‘ practisin’, seven years‘ playin’ …

I mind my great-uncle Tom Lennon intonin’ the above like it was the one and only rule of life and if you didn’t get it then you wouldn’t cut it!

(He may also, bless him!, have believed that he made the rule up himself. As a boy of five or six, sitting in his dusty house, fighting to breathe through the smoky chimney and the thick fog of pipe tobacco which surrounded him like a halo, I certainly got the impression that he did! We were always told that “Uncle Tam” was a very clever man and a fount of all knowledge and we were to take very seriously any of his pronouncements.

Well he was and he wasn’t a clever man - he wasn’t exactly rigorous in testing out the hypotheses that he developed, but the boy knew more about most things than those like myself who had the “benefit” of years of edjimication could hope to know.

I digress. Close the brackets, Crossey and get on with your yap!)

The old 7-7-7 mantra is, of course, a cliche. But like all cliches, it owes its existence/persistence to the fact that there’s a germ of truth in it. Which is that if you’re to make a go of the music you’ve got to set time aside; you won’t get there quickly. (Despite the claims that some make that the music is easy to learn/play, whatever.) And it’s not just a case of setting a wee bit of time aside here and there; you’ll be with the music (and it’ll be with you) for life.

Now … this brings me to the real point of this ramble.

In the old days when my Uncle Tam was starting out on his adventures, people may have been able to see the relevance of the 7-7-7 rule. After all, if you didn’t travel very far and you were maybe not all that literate, etc., simply picking up the tunes took a while; developing a repertoire depended on encounters with other musicians who you met now and again; exposure to radically different repertoires, styles was a very hit-and-miss affair.

But we live in a very different world. And I feel that the pace of this new world forces many of us to want to become a technical expert overnight, to play our tunes at the speed of light, to increase our repertoire by a dozen tunes a week … Which has (at least) two side-effects. On the one hand it makes people competitive; on the other it depresses those whose ability to develop lags behind their absurdly high expectations.

I’m all for the 7-7-7 rule myself; maybe not its letter, but certainly its spirit …

Over to you!

Lose the plot if you want - that’s a good question! Maybe playing music was a bit more middle class? Cos, as you say, all the rest of them were down the pits for 12 hours a day….maybe that was more in post-industrial revolution England and central Scotland, though.

But I read somewhere (could have been in Breathnach’s book) that one reason for the popularity of the crossroad dances was that the Irish labouring classes had a lot of free time on their hands, when it wasn’t the harvest or sowing season, (poor as church mice and close to starvation, needless to say) so they danced at the crossroads (as in the cross at Spancil Hill) along with dancing masters and fiddle masters, wandering pipers and harpists, and so on.

So, I would have thought, those who did choose to play The Music would have done so with considerable vocation. But sure, there must have been a sliding scale of abilities even back then in the “Golden Age”

Leisure time

… there would have been/still is plenty in unindustrialised communities; no need to work (or sleep) in shifts, but just as required, which did mean certain times of year all hell broke loose. And fewer possibilities as to how to spend it: probably not even the option of reading, since they often couldn’t. So loads of time for the music and dancin’.

I read an article once in New Scientist (probably) about pre-industrialisation sleep/work patterns; a couple of hours sleep at nightfall, then up and gad about/eat/make love/music/whatever for a few hours in the wee small hours, then a couple hours more sleep, then maybe a bit of work, breakfast, a bit more work, a nap, eat, a bit of work or not, then leisure time, and back to bed. Sounds like my ideal day (apart from those work bits)! Shiftwork (of any sort) really is a heinous crime against a body’s inclinations.

Sorry, what was the question? 7-7-7? In my case, 7 years blissful ignorance, 7 years flogged all the way to the piano, 7 years flirting with it, and another 21 free of it.
