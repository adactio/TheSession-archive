Traditional Irish Music and the Role of the Rythm Section…

Hi,
I’ve been intrested in the possiblities of mixing traditional Irish music with more contemporary styles for a number of years.
There are many examples of bands trying this over the years. Some successful, some not so.
My approach is rooted in adding structure to the rhythm section, while keeping the melody intact (as much as possible).
Personally, I find it interesting to try a fuller, less modal harmony. Also, in the case of ballads, to try alternative time signatures.
There’s a lot of potential in this area of Irish music, especially with the onset of groups that play with contemporary instruments i.e. drum-kit, bass, guitar, keys, etc.
I wonder what do people involved in Irish music feel about the role of a rythm section in trad? To what extent do they feel there’s room for development?

Nick

Re: Traditional Irish Music and the Role of the Rythm Section…

Starting with a wee bit of controversy, eh? I hope someone is making the popcorn.

1. Play the music how you want to play it. If you want to add funky bass/didjeridoo//djembe/tabla/Tibetan mouth music/xylophone/alpenhorn accompaniment to it, then it’s your prerogative. It’s music, man. Experiment with what you want.

2. Just don’t expect anyone else to like it. Especially those of us who prefer more straightforward, traditional approaches to the tunes and who believe the music is sublime as it is and doesn’t need any “new” rhythmic structure. I generally dislike modern renditions of trad where people have gone to great lengths to develop the rhythm section in the overdeveloped way it exists in other kinds of music. The rhythm is inherent in the melodies. A “rhythm section” is unnecessary, though it can sound nice in the right -- and not overbearing -- hands. The tunes are perfectly capable, and indeed at times at their best, when standing alone.

Re: Traditional Irish Music and the Role of the Rythm Section…

One of the reasons I like Irish traditional music is that it doesn’t need a rhythm section.

For example, this (http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jHqFlS3gNMs) cannot be “improved” by adding a drum or strummer or bass thumper.

This music fuses melody and pulse in such a way that the good players are always being playful and inventive with the timing and phrasing. Adding a drum beat almost always constrains the musicians, and the music, rather than “improving” it.

Most of the people I’ve met who think this music could benefit from drum kits, guitars, and basses are accustomed to today’s popular music. Not all of them learn to hear and appreciate the nuances of traditional music.
