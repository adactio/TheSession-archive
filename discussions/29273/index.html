Re: Hello, and tune sharing.

There’s a few possibilities. Submitting an original composition isn’t the only option. Perhaps not even a wise one. Doesn’t mean you cannot share the jig in other ways.

But first a bit of the backstory on submitting your own composition. It’s not strictly forbidden. However, the webmaster included this in the FAQs ~

Can I submit my own compositions?

Well… The Session isn’t really intended for that. If you do post one of your own compositions, then you must do your “penance”, so to speak, by balancing each original composition with about five trad tunes.

So, there’s that. You can always post the abcs in your profile. Or if you’re very patient you can wait until someone hears your tune, thinks it is worth submitting, & they post it to the database.

IMHO, if you really do want to share the tune online, make a video or audio recording & put up a link in the *Discussions*.

Re: Hello, and tune sharing.

Two things you might want to consider.

First thing - if you’re new to the music, it’s likely that your tunes will change as you get more familiar with it. You might want to hang on to them for a little while, they could change over the next year or so.

Second thing - the tunes that are posted here should really be ones that are in play in the world somewhere. The idea is that you should have the tune in your ears before you try to play it.

I’d suggest you teach the tunes to some people in person and let them play them for a while, then if they get passed along someone will come and enter them here.

No reason not to put them up in your profile, though, if you really want criticism. That has the advantage of allowing you to change your mind about how they go, later on. :)

Re: Hello, and tune sharing.

“I’m pretty new to ITM”

In that case, it’s probably time enough to think about composing tunes when you understand the music a little better.

Of course, if a good tune “comes to you” it can’t be helped but it might be better to sit on it for a wee while as some have suggested just in case you might be able to improve on it at a later time once you are more familiar with the genre.
Also, there’s always risk that you might come up with something which is already out there. Even the most experienced musicians do this either unwittingly or (perhaps?) deliberately.

However, many of the great players compose very few original tunes if any while many mediocre musicians are extremely prolific. So, composing isn’t the be all and end all.
