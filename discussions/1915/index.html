Is “the session” any good? I think it’s as good as the people are who log on that day. Jerry Holland wrote a tune called “You’re only as good as your last gig” and I think that applies here as well. Talking about playing

This site has helped my playing immensely, by helping me to find tunes, listen to tunes, read tunes, talk about tunes and, accordingly, think about tunes. I recommend this site about once a session. (That’s right: I’m most likely to blame for octogreg’s presence. I have to plead guilty to the question “Who invited the banjo player?”) TheSession has been enormously valuable to me.

I’m pretty surprised at some of the other replies on this thread. With reference to Michael Gills’s comment--that talking about music doesn’t improve your playing--I suppose that could be true, as long as you’re not thinking while you’re talking. Or as long as you’re not listening. Otherwise, for me, conversation about something regularly gives me fresh perspective and insight into the topic of that conversation, even if only to marshall my own thoughts on the subject in a new utterance.

With reference to JMH/Eddie’s comment about this site not having helped his playing because other resources are more helpful… well, I’m seeing far too much of this kind of thinking in the world. If A is of great benefit and B is of lesser benefit, that doesn’t mean that B is of NO benefit. Two things can be true at the same time! Propositions aren’t necessarily binary, and two of them together aren’t necessarily mutually exclusive. I’ve probably learned more from playing with other people too, but that doesn’t negate the fact that this site has been enormously helpful. Has it really not helped your playing at all in any dimension, Eddie?

All thanks to Jeremy for this remarkable resource.

---Michael B.

Re: Is

*blush*, garsh, thanks Paul.

Clearly some of us are more verbally oriented than others. Doesn’t make us better or worse, as people or musicians. When I learn some bowing idea or something like Kevin Burke’s little slide/hammer-on nuance, I enjoy hearing the player talk about it--what s/he thinks about it, what metaphors they use to describe the feel. It helps me remember it later, and probably even helps me get closer to the same feel. Of course, all the while I’m trying to absorb everything with my ears and eyes and hands too! Nothing in the programmer’s guide to the human brain says we can’t multi-task. In fact, the integrated approach seems to work better for me than compartmentalizing all the bits.

The one caveat I try to keep in mind is that, as a teacher, I have to back off with the words sometimes so I don’t overwhelm or confuse a student who’s more focused on the doing or watching. This site must be frustrating for seriously non-verbal types, and to them I apologize for contributing more than my share of the sheer bulk of words. But other people--myself included--obviously can learn a lot from reading and talking about this stuff, and for us The Session is a mother lode of lore, humor, and information.
