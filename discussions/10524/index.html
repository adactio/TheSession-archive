“Traditional Irish Music” ~ ?

I recieved in the post this evening what I assume is a syndicated article written by a man in Miami, Florida name of Dave Barry, about his visit to Eire, the green isle, and what mostly reads as disappointment. The following is just a small extract from that article, with regards to ‘Traditional Irish Music’…

~ some names have been removed and items edited to protect the ‘innocent’?

" ~ in my opinion the cultural highlight of the trip occurred in the town of _____, where a pub called _____ had a sign outside that said ‘Traditional Irish Music.’

This turned out to be a traditional Irish Elvis impersonator.

I realize that there are literally thousands of quality Elvis impersonators, and I’m sure you’ve seen some excellent ones, but I am here to tell you that this one, in this unremarkable town in _____ Ireland, was beyond question the worst Elvis impersonator in world history.

He sang along to a tape of instrumental Elvis tunes, which he played on a sound system that he never, not once in two solid hours, got adjusted right.

Every time he’d start singing a song, the sound system would screech and honk with feedback; Elvis would then whirl around and spend minutes at a time unsuccessfully adjusting various knobs while he mumbled the lyrics, so that for most of the evening all you saw was Elvis’ butt, accompanied by screeching and honking and vague off-key singing.

Often, by the time he’d finished twiddling the knobs, Elvis had lost track of what song he was singing; he’d frown into the distance, trying various tunes until he thought he was on the right track, at which point the screeching and honking would start up, forcing Elvis to whirl back around, like a man being attacked by bees, and treat the audience to another lengthy view of his butt.

The crowd, which I will frankly admit was consuming alcoholic beverages, enjoyed this performance immensely, cheering wildly at the end of each song."

~ Dave Barry - Miami, Florida

So, anyone else with a similar tale to tell, disappointments along the lines of ‘not as advertised or expected’?

Re: “Traditional Irish Music” ~ ?

One August, on the way to the Feakle Festival, I stayed a night in Tulla. This being home to the eponymous ceili band - and Mary and Andy McNamara - I thought I would try my luck at finding some good music there. It was a Saturday night, so I felt I was in with a good chance. I asked in the chip shop if they knew where I could find a session, and they directed me to one of the pubs.

I made my way expectantly to the said pub, the melifluous strains of East Clare fiddles and concertinas already sounding in my head. On entering, I was dismayed to see the ‘alternative’ Tulla Ceili Band in the corner. This was a one man ceili band - a piano-accordionist backed by a drum machine and programmed chords. Now, I’m not one to hold predjudices against particular instruments or their players, but clearly, the reason this man was playing on his own was that no other musician would want to play with him, he was that bad. He stumbled through a handful of session standards, then stumbled through them again. This ‘music’ was clearly intended to accompany dancing, but no-one was dancing - except two elderly ladies, who got up and danced a waltz at one point (the highlght of the evening). I sat down with a Club Orange and dozed off.

Wullahbabylemmebeyoahluvin . . . . Oh, jeslemmegetouttacharcter.

Okay, yes I am a bona fide EI Imp and I was deeply saddened to read the comment on this site re my perf in ________, Republic of Ireland. True, I did have a few sound system probs, but this was due to the unexpected departure of my Sound Man. Over black pud and rashers in the morning, he did casually mention something called Willie Week (I think WW is a new folkie). That took on a new significance when my sound man (not) didn’t show up at the pub for my El gig. Anyway, you know the old saw, show must go on, so I gave it my best. The comments about my butt were a little hurtful; usually I get compliments about that region of my anatomy.

So, apologies, friend, but I think you must have had an early night. During the break, a local kid, looked about 16 to me, got the sound tweaked. Second half was a blast, especially my trad fiddle numbers. Not widely known, but El in his later years took to ITM bigtime, sneaking off to places like Doolin on weekends, in disguise of course. . . . You’re the devil in disguise . . Pardon, just slipped back to EI there for a mo. Anyway, the minions of Graceland were well acquainted with his fads: every Wed. only ITM in the mansion. Wrote, in fact, a very complicated 6-part reel called The Gates of Graceland. Some ITM affish saw similarities between it and The Bucks of Oranmore, but no question of plagiarism. El was above all that. Do a fair version of it myself. Did in fact. Thunderous applause. Very appreciative audience. Had everyone on the floor for a great set of polkas from the Sliabh Luachra area. My finale, The Dear Irish Boy (An Buachaillin Ban), beautiful rendition if I may say so. Not a few red eyes in the house. And not just because it was closing time. So, you see, friend, when you come over here to Ireland, you have to be a bit more patient. Okay, enough. Long live ITM.
Wellahblessahmahsowellwhassarongwithme

PS There’s an American guy over here, 60s, never misses a sesh in Tigh Neachtain. Doesn’t look anything like El, but, you know, disguises . . .

mmelec ~ yes, the ‘tongue-in-cheek’ of this piece was obvious, but there was no heart in it, not like say Bill Bryson and others who show an honest passion for the subjects of their humour. The complete article, not given, and I wasn’t able to find a link but now will see if I can find one just for him in general, lacked any heart at all. To compare it with some musicians, it was about him and what he thought was funny, and the background was just grist for his cuteness. I got some laughs out of it, like the bit above offered here, but he was like some “See What I can Do” fiddler or bodhran player or whatever, beating the hell out of a melody and screwing it every which way but affectionately. It felt for me to be one of those things that when you’re finished reading it you look for the final line ~ “Ain’t I a genius!” ~ and a big f-k-n grin of self-satisfaction… But, I did get a chuckle or two, but on the whole didn’t think much of it. I’ve not read anything else by this man, but this piece did leave me a little cold…

As I’ve said, there’s the likes of Bill Bryson and Joseph O’Connor who have this yank (and here not used as a geographic tag) beat hands down, humour with heart…
