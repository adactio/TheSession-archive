Added by showaddydadito on April 22nd, 2004

“Traditional” - is it age or is it style?

I guess this risks the “academic pedantics” branding, but I am interested to know what people think.

We talk about playing Irish Traditional Music. No one seems to dispute what is meant by the word Music. And most people seem to accept that the name Irish denotes a style, rather than specifically a tune written either in Ireland or by an Irish emigrant.

But what about the Tradional bit? On another thread yesterday there was a discussion about posting a tune after consulting with its author - the implication is that the tune was composed comparatively recently. Can a tune be traditional if its this new?

I realise that it would be difficult, and downright foolish to try to legislate that a tune must be of a certain age, or have been played a certain number of times in order to class as traditional. And I would hate to have anyone in our session told not to play a tune becaused its not old enough.

So: what does “Traditional” mean?

Well, two of my own tunes I posted here appear to be in the process of escaping into the wild tradition. One so far has kept its name (Bang Your Frog on the Sofa) as it shows up in Chicago sessions and a London Fleadh. I think of BYF as a modern tune with some traditional elements. In short, one answer to Dave’s question is, “A tune is traditional when it’s found in the traditional repertoire.”

↳ Posted by Cath on April 23rd, 2004.

Re: “Traditional” - is it age or is it style?

The dilemma here is that “traditional” is an adjective, describing a very complicated noun, “music”. In the initial post, Showaddydadito said “No one seems to dispute what is meant by the word Music.” That’s the key. In reading such discussions over the years, I notice that what people mean by “music” keeps shifting silently throughout the discussion. They don’t seem to dispute it, but dispute it they do. Music has many aspects. “Traditional” could modify each of these aspects independently.

Has the melody been passed down over generations or been adopted by a community into its subculture? Is it played on traditional instruments (always? sometimes? this particular time?) Is it played in traditional contexts? Is it played in a traditional style? What about when a performance merges music and performance styles that are traditional, but from several different traditions? Does “music” refer to some abstract platonic universal unchanging thing (and does “traditional” describe it), or is each individual performance unique (in which case “traditional” would describe aspects of one concrete rendering, not the piece itself)? Or is it somewhere in between?

We may have a pretty good understanding of the meaning of “traditional”; it’s “music” that’s elusive.
