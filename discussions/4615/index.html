Re: Mexed missages

I think it’s all about selfish instananeous gratification behaviour. I’ve not atteneded a session ever, but the same thing seems to apply to many other types of gatherings. One example I think of immediately is at business meetings where people seem to find it appropriate to excuse themselve to get that “really important call”. People are not naturally inconsiderate, I think they just like to multitask and ‘get things off their plate’ so-to-speak. However, the message they are sending is that their call is more important than the time you are currently giving of yourself to be there. Which may be true in some cases, but let’s face it…that’s rare. Personall, if I’m expecting an emergency call, whether it be for family or buisness, I let everyone know it might happen and appologise ahead of time. But otherwise, the phone is OFF OFF OFF and you have my undivided attention, and likewise I have yours.

Where your sessions are concerned, perhaps if it’s becoming an epidemic, there could be a no phones on at the sessions rule with some exceptions for emergencies. You are all giving each other time and energey to learn, grow and play some great music and when that starts getting treated like a walk in documentary at the museum, it all breaks down.

Our rules in the office are no phones in meetings, none, nothing we have is imporatnt enough that it can’t wait an hour or so. And for all day meetings, we check at breaks and lunch and do callbacks at those times to satisfy that urge to get sh_t done. This way, everyone who is in attendance gives their undevided attention.

So, I think that many people young and old sometimes need to be reminded, but not preached to, about giving their undivided attention and becoming part of the group culture for the time they are there…giving and taking…I think they may respond positively to that.

Re: Mexed missages

I’m ambivalent at best as regards the presence of cellular phones at musical events, whether sessions or concerts, but I have to say that in one instance the device was put to very good use.
Earlier this year, I went to see the Boston area band Halali, one of whose members, Lissa Schneckenburger, was out of town. Early on in the concert, after the obligatory “meet-the-band” introduction, one of the Halalis, Laura Cortese, explained that they were normally a quartet, and Lissa would have really liked to play at the gig but had a previous commitment, etc.
“But,” she said, whipping out her cellphone, “maybe we can help her be part of the fun.”
So she dialed Lissa’s number, waited for her voicemail to kick in, and said, “Hey, it’s me, I’m calling from the stage just to say ‘Hi.’” Then she held up the phone so everyone in the audience could yell “Hi Lissa!”

PS: Being (a) a smartass and (b) an inveterate Firesign Theater fan, I couldn’t help but add, “No anchovies!”
