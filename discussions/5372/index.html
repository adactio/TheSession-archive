Re: am i just a pariah?

Planxty was the culmination of several years of innovation started by groups such as ‘Sweeny’s Men’, Wee 3(or 4), ‘Scara Brae’, ‘Shades of McMurragh’ and the album which led to Planxty being formed ‘Prosperous’? Like all old records, some survive the passing of time, others don’t. Perhaps ‘The Well Below the Valley’ was not quite up to scratch but I don’t think you can fault either the arrangements or the singing. Certainly the first (Black Album) and ‘After the Break’ were classics of their sort and played an enormous part in the evolution of Irish music for listening, along with the ‘Cheiftains’ and the ‘Bothy Band’. The 60’s and 70’s were a magic time in Ireland and if it had not been, I don’t think it would have spread as far around the world as it has.

Guys, guys, guys,

I log on for the first time in ages (lots of playing in the sessions at the Woodford Festival here in sunny Brissie) and what do I find - some bloke putting forward an honest and polite opinion and getting flamed. Funny, that’s just what happened to me when I first posted…

Bard, I hear what you are saying (and I thought you put it very succinctly) and I agree that you’ve perhaps picked up one of the weaker Planxty albums. As you seem to have guessed, there are better ones out there - hence the big fan base.

I’ll second what Zina says about your tastes being likely to change in the future (although saying it happens as you mature seemed a bit patronising - perish the thought that Zina should ever be so, since I know from her many posts that she’s one of the most sympathetic people on this board).

When I first got into ITM it was through Steeleye Span. I was mainly into heavy metal, so I tended to prefer their louder stuff. Even so, I liked them so much I collected every album I could - even the obscure American release compilation albums. About 10 years later I realised my favourite Steeleye albums were actually the ones I originally didn’t like (Ten Man Mop was almost entirely acoustic and very rough around the edges). Perhaps I’d just played the other albums to death, perhaps since I’d got heavily into playing the music myself I was just hearing completely new elements in the recordings which I hadn’t picked up on before.

These days I am as happy listening to unaccompanied singers as a full band - hence my love of sessions and the amateur scene.

I like both Christy Moore and Andy Irvine as singers. They’re both distinctive and individualistic, and I find Andy’s voice really suits some of the songs he chooses. Also, be aware these guys have been performing for a LONG time, in a lot of guises. I recently found some tracks by Andy and Dick Gaughan (an all time hero and definately worth checking out) and they’re fantastic. If you don’t like what Planxty did you might well find some of their solo stuff more to your liking - have you heard Christy’s ‘Ride On’ or ‘Ordinary Man’ albums?

One last point in a very long post - your age has nothing to do with the validity of your opinions. I know several middle aged ITM fans who wouldn’t know a good tune if it bit them on the a**e, and have listened to a couple of teenagers who have an innate understanding of the tunes they have picked up, even though they weren’t born to the tradition and had no ‘mentors’ to guide them. You definately will find that your opinions change over time - eveyone’s do - mainly as your experience increases. But having said that, that was what I figured you were asking in your initial post anyway!

Enjoy yerself, and don’t take the old grouches on this site too seriously.

Eno ;-)

The Well Below The Valley is an odd choice for a ‘first Planxty Album’. I remember being underwhelmed by it when it first came out. It seemed rambling, and not as tight as other albums, and I wasn’t knocked out by the songs on it. I really like it now, it sounds refreshing as it is maybe not so tightly arranged as some of the the others. I think you should listen to some of the other albums, Bard.
I’d not compare them competitively with The Bothy Band myself, they are rather different animals, but I would argue that they had a much greater influence than the BB, who might display more of ‘a good exchange of instruments’, as you put it, but who’s sphere of influence was narrower. The Bothy Band were very much a ‘Musician’s Band’ (IMHO), and I know many people who were actually persuaded to take up the music after being blown away by Planxty. The Bothy Band were a bit to awesome to make beginners want to have a go(!) Both Christy Moore and Andy Irvine had very distinctive and individual voices. Some people love them, some people hate them, but that’s inevitable, but their songs and Planxty’s music have entered the collective consciousness far more than any of the other groups. You can go to any ‘low level’ session in England even today and Denis Murphy’s /Shaun Ryans Polkas will be gamefully be being struggled through, and someone will be grappling with the intecracies of Merrily Kiss The Quaker(!), and someone will be slaughtering the Cliffs Of Dooneen. Those people who only breathe the air at the heady hights where the Bothy’s fly maybe don’t see the way that Planxty reached out and pulled people into the tradition.
The above is not meant to suggest that Planxty was technically inferior to the Bothy Band, but that their music was pitched in such away that it touched a nerve that no other trad band managed…

I think that we younger folk are spoilt and we are more demanding of our recordings. Regardless of whether it’s live or studio, and regardless of whether there’s a bit of “wilderness” and wrong notes (that’s a different thing), I demand absolute perfection in both technique and quality of recording/mixing/stereo setup etc. Anything less is just rubbaaash :-)

I’ll have to agree to disagree with cunnla about Planxty’s backing. If you dissect what they’re doing, they’re simply pre-arranged countermelodies which often sound great but sometimes sound too busy for my ear. I want to hear the space in the tune, but I’m not even saying I want to hear it unbacked. Also there’s this rhythm thing that annoys me and which makes me think that so many other bands do it much much better. At the moment I’m thinking specifically of the bonus track on After The Break where LO‘F does this amazing solo rendition of Rolling Down The Hill and then everyone else comes in for Chattering Magpies, and the backers force the rhythm to flatten out gradually more and more until by the final tune in the set (Lord MacDonald’s) that wonderful groove that LO’F set up is completely lost and you just want to break down in tears. Stuff like that doesn’t pass my perfection test ::snaps CD in half and throws it out the window::

I’ve often thought that Planxty’s harmonies could have been a bit more sophisticated and the bass could move a lot more whilst still keeping it simple, but then I have to remind myself that these guys were pioneers in the whole bouzouki backing thing and were feeling their way round the possibilities, as opposed to modern bands who’ve been able to take what the likes of Lunny have done and build on it. And some of the ostinato bass riffs Lunny sets up are incredibly imaginative. Every zouk player I have ever spoken to has been influenced by him to some degree, even if they don’t like his more recent work.
