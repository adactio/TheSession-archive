Pointilism - what’s slipping between the curves and jagged edges

Are we tending to miss or overlook something(s)? If so, what? We reach out to embrace traditions with our arms and fingers spread wide. While the essences and contexts easily slip past we grab at a few pearls while also catching a few sticky turds. Some of us put all our focus and efforts on one or more of these extractions, distractions. It’s so easy to ignore everything else while attention is taken by some sweet or sparkly little morsel, like a raven obsessed with shiny blue things… Is this true? If we are missing something(s) what do you think it is, they are? - aside from others not realizing how amazing you are? :-/ What are the distractions? What are those things we’re tending to miss, to overlook, to ignore, to neglect?

Breathing spaces ~

Why ask it? Well, as if more is needed in a sea and potential tsunami of points, this begins with me being recently caught up in one of those many modern and frequent ‘festivals’. In the aftermath of this, enjoying one of my favourite brews, Redbreast, I started thinking about the house gatherings in Ireland I’d attended, been party to. Related, I also caught myself in pleasant memories of the many stories of such events I’d heard from all kinds of folks, including some of the often listed leading sources in Irish music. And there was the evident conflict, the differences, naturally, between the two events. At this ‘festival’, lovely as the folk were, it was all very ‘revival’, another species of re-enactment society. There wasn’t anyone that wasn’t obsessed with what they were doing and the event and fantasy of it all. Yes, there were some children dragged along for the ride in baby buggies or holding on tightly to a hand, but it wasn’t what one could call ‘regular’ folk. This lot were without question obsessed. I never met such folks at those events I’ve been to I was reflecting on, but even some village festivals I’ve been to across Europe, where ‘everyone’ comes out for the craic.

In the history of Irish traditions, back to those house gatherings and small village events, and especially before the pubs, halls and media laid their claim on it, when it was still ‘communal’, it was just that, wide open, for everyone. All sorts of folks came, all kinds, all interests and inclinations. You didn’t have to be a singer, story teller, musician or dancer. It was in the truest sense ‘the community’, not a clique or a club or a fraternity of specialist interest. You might have just come along for the tea, a chat, or merely out of curiosity. The welcome was warm, open and evident. And it was NEVER non-stop music and dance. There was space to breath, to connect, to share time and thoughts… I doubt I have to confes my bias for that. I much prefer ‘regular’ folk to the obsessive…

It’d be no surprise for me that there would be people who have not feeling for this or who see nothing missing… How can you miss something if you’ve never experienced or imagined it?

I think I get the gist of what Ceol’s saying. The thing about the ‘sparkly morsels’, though - the tunes in particular, in this context - is that they go right on to give pleasure and interest to those who have harvested them and taken them maybe a long, long way from their ancestral environment. The pleasure and interest are usually unassociated with truly grotesque misdemeanours and often enough give rise to proficient and sometimes beautiful music and happy dancing / drunken conversations / people-watching / other. What’s not to like?

I realise that this remit includes the world of folk fragments tweaked into Victorian parlour ballads, the more precious echelons of Scottish dancing, the modern uptake of Celtic interlace art and various other things not all Sessioners might be keen on. But the positives I mentioned can apply to these also. Anyway, more than the above (as far as I can see) Irish traditional music has maintained a very strong unbroken tradition which has been also recorded and annotated to the nth degree; its hardly on its deathbed - and even if it was, and its players all vanished tomorrow, we’d be left with a pretty good idea of what it was like and how and why it was played.

For all that, I am personally one of those who have seen Irish music as an Art Form and an acquisition, rather than as my natural heritage and environment. As I come from a background outside it, that isn’t surprising. But I am finding that my personal, family, social and session life is intersecting more as the years go by, with the whole thing becoming just a wee bit more like the holistic environment Ceolachan is so fond of.

Looking between, but also valuing the curves and jagged edges

Seeking greater understanding, or to explore whether I’m missing something or something is missing, doesn’t lessen the craic for me. Looking with more depth into a piece of literature, poem, fiction or professed non-fiction, has never lessened the experience, it widdens it, raises it, gives me new perspectives and an increased understanding. But, I can still go back to the literature and appreciate it just for itself, and more… For me, that’s also true of tradition. To seek and know more, about what works and doesn’t, about the good and the bad, does not lessen the experience any more for me. I choose not to opt out of also having a critical perspective of it all. I choose not to ignore what might be missing, missed or at fault with a thing. And, that critical curiosity doesn’t spoil it for me.

Knowing those rough edges and problems helps me to better my contribution and experience of it all. Besides, more often than not there’s a chuckle in it all too, often self-directed, the most constructive aim of any criticism, the thing I can always improve on. Are there things missing, well, I’ll be wandering and looking till I return to the clay… All of it interests me, good and bad and all the in-between. I find it damned hard to improve on anything without understanding first.

What’s missing? With much deemed ‘trad’ or ‘folk’ - youth, a varied community, a healthy and prospering future… Does that concern me beyond my own imperfect contributions? Yes!

The seeds of my discontent ~ hairpin & horseshoe turns

Over recent times I’ve found myself in a few situations, music and dance, where I experienced a feeling that ‘somthing’s missing’… I admit that one of these is in essence a ‘performance’. Another was a kind of street festival, but also more about performance. But, the feeling has also been experienced in a momeht of sharing tunes, and not just the one time that dip in mood resulted, something more unsettling that just butterflies, a sinking feeling, a kind of emptiness. Also, it has happened on the dance floor, in the middle of a dance when certain folk were taking things farr too seriously while also losing patience with someone ‘new’, and robbing them in that impatience of any hope of finding confidence. This latter type of incident, music or dance, is something that always affects me with that sinking feeling, a nudge of mood in the wrong direction…

With these recent events to ponder it’s probably more diplomatic to start with the immediate, the source of that sinking feeling and emptiness, that sense that ‘something’s missing’ - me! I’ve had the ‘missing’ like a badly out of tune single cyclinder old Triumph motorcycle, things ‘missing’ within me, in the music I was making, in the communication between me and others - musicians or dancers… But the biggest nudge into introspection and questions on this comes from ‘costumed’ events, activities dominated by those around my age and older - and their fervour, seriousness and obsessing…

The most enjoyable part of one such event was talking woodworking and learning about the life history of one of the local piano accordion players there, who I caught enjoying old show band tunes with a Latin influence on his wet tuned Chinese made accordion. Curiously, as a contrast to the original subject, things missing, for that short moment, which included some banter and a bit of laughter, nothing was found missing…

I’m well aware that things are ‘for the moment’, another of those universal truths often carted out - “BE HERE NOW!” For me, in my nature, when I’ve been blessed with a ‘good thing’ I have the drive and urge to want to further that, a want for others to enjoy it too, for it to survive beyond me. Whether or not I’m the right one to do that, who knows. I can but hope some of that goodness in those that shared their traditions with me is in me, has rubbed off and will rise when needed. If I don’t see it happening I often find myself doing whatever I can to get it to happen, and fully knowing the self interest involved, that it is also for myself…

Now to admit also that there are things colouring all this, and that have been the bigger nudges into darker moods of introspection. Some very close to us are in tight spaces, experienceing difficult times - including our dearest musical collaborator, family to us - with lots and lots of shared experiences, and many of those in the vein of Ethical’s pluses / bonuses, always worth more in our heats than anything contrary - this generous character is having to deal with terminal cancer. They’re managing better than I am… I’m a sop. This kind of thing, despite the possible uselessness of letting it effect me, has me in a deep and pensive mood, making me look inward with questions, as if I really needed anything to flip that hair trigger switch… :-/

Oops! :-P

Howdy Al! I enjoy a good festival too, and we have, but recent experiences and influences had me somewhere else.

As to ‘history’ and ‘tradition’, that’s just what it is, even if your only source is a book or a friend returning from a festival with some new tunes. That’s ‘tradition’. As to whether or not we can or should promote elements of that history. While I am always bubbling over with questions and query, yes, with my heart and mind I believe we do have a duty to pass on and preserve the best of what the past has, not out of fantasy, not without a full awareness of the times we’re in, or that with every new participant it is ‘new’, unique, revived. We can keep and preserve the best, and we can even try to better it. But the best as I’ve known it is a welcome and a patience and a sense of humour - not just merited out to a select few members of an in group, but wide open, come along for the craic, with open space to breath and be social as well as to play some music and do a bit of dancing, for those so inclined. I also enjoy the song of it, and recitation, but I’m particularly fond of the heart in it, and I do feel a duty to those that were kind to me to try my damnedest to make sure that isn’t missing in anything I do, though sometimes I fail miserably.

I count it as a gift to have that feeling and awareness that ‘something is missing’. That doesn’t mean I can’t make the best of any situation I find myself in, but it’s good to be reminded of what’s missing so I can do my damnedest, if it’s in my power, to help add that extra. Why do anything without trying to do a little bit more? If you’re going to do something do it with the intent to improve on it next time, to swing your hammer with more care, knowing the value and history of the slates you’re working with, and not wanting to blacken another fingernail, or waste a slate by breaking it.

Do it, do it well, as best you can, and strive to do better. That includes welcome, patience, humour… ‘Craic’ to me is more than just a flush of tunes and fancy bits or one tune or dance after the other. Yes, there’s a selfishness in what drives me, to make some attempt to create the kind of events that give me the greatest satisfaction, because they have something of that past, that tradition, that I’ve been lucky to have been part of. I owe it to all those lovely generous people to try to in some way emulate them and the things they valued and nurtured for their communities…

If something is missing doesn’t mean that it can’t be found and brought back into the mix… That’s ‘tradition’, a hell of a lot more than just melodies, twiddles, steps and figures… A HELL OF A LOT MORE!!! ;-) It’s worth the effort…
