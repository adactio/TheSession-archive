Re: Just Go For It!

I think that confidence has a lot to do with progressing on an instrument. If you consistently think, “Oh this instrument is so hard; I’ll never be able to play it like *insert-name-of-musician*” then, indeed, you will struggle, my friend.

I’ve got a long,long,long,long ways to go, but I’ve also come a long ways, so I’ve been told. For me, I know that is because of 1) obsession 2) being fortunate enough to be exposed to really great players 3) an extended period of unemployment (which is thankfully over) 4) I used to be hot sh1t on the classical piano. I once had confidence in that area musically, so I knew what that felt like. And when I approached trad music I said, “I can do this.”

I don’t approach learning trad music the same way I did classical music but I know that if I could be good at that, I can be good at this. Now, don’t get me wrong. There are often times when I think, “I suck.” But I try to remember that I can do this.

A few weeks ago I was at my parent’s house because my sister was getting married. I was looking at some old sheet music of mine and I though, “Alright, if I could play this when I was 12 then I can certainly learn that new set of tunes” (thoughts paraphrased using the Jerball Artistic License).

Honestly, it’s never entered my mind that I wouldn’t be able to play an instrument. Maybe that’s the American in me - hard work = success. I know it’s not always true (luck, privilege, Deus Ex Machina all play a part), but hopefully it’ll prove true in this case.

Re: Just Go For It!

I bought an expensive flute with the intention of learning it. Spending a lot of loot on an instrument is a motivation to practice the thing more often too.

IMO acc’s, flutes & pipes come in two grades; playable or not playable. If you get a entry level instrument you’ll have a harder time learning. Stringed instruments on the other hand have a much broader grey area, a $100 yamaha guitar or a decent factory fiddle won’t hold you back as much as a pakistani flute would. Sure the tone won’t be as nice as it could be but you’ll be able to get in your basics & you don’t have to drop $5k on a really good instrument to get started. Sure there are the pretzel-necked tossers, but these can be spotted a mile away.

Pitnekit - I think it’s just a personality thing, I can’t keep my hands off of a new instrument. Even if I don’t like it, I want to try to play it.

Re: Just Go For It!

One thing I have started to notice after three years of exposure to Irish traditional music, is that we of the American-born persuasion seem to vizualize ourselves as becoming ‘a fiddle player’ or ‘a banjo player’, etc.

What I am starting to slowly understand is that the music is all about the tunes, the tunes, the tunes - and the dancing that would happen if dancers were here while the tunes were.

If I were to go back three years and start over, what I would do is buy a $6 Clarke tinwhistle, and start learning the tunes, concentrate on what the tunes had to tell me, listen to recordings or live sessions, and go back and work some more on the tunes. Meet people who know the tunes, hang out on websites where tunes are being discussed and debated (:-)

Encountering a rich and varied musical tradition, and at the same time trying to master a complex musical instrument as an adult learner is a way, way optimistic challenge for even the most self-confident among us. (I think I’m in about the 99th percentile:-)

Pick up whatever’s handy, sing it, hum it, whistle it… that’s my 2 cents for today.
