Performance or session perspective

I am assuming that most people here will make a distinction between a session and a performance, though I’ve read comments(with which I disagree) on this site and elsewhere to the effect that a session *is* a performance. I’ve also seen performances on stage which are no more than glorified sessions. These may or may not still be quite entertaining.

Anyway, although this site is known as “The Session”, the contributors here probably fall into three broad camps.
1. Those who almost exclusively play at sessions.
2. Those who play mostly in public performance whether as amateurs or on a professional basis.
3. Those who do a mixture of both.
There are also those who have maybe retired from “gigging” and now just enjoy sessions or those who play gigs and have either “grown out of”(their words not mine)or do not have time for sessions. Sometimes, of course, the latter group either won’t or can’t “fit into” a session but that’s another argument.

As you know, there are a lot of conflicting opinions on this site about various topics. I’m wondering how much these are informed by the current “status” of the individual members. For example, the likes of Messers Gill , Gilder, Rab C Nebitt etc seem to be firmly within the “session” camp whereas others like Will and Zina appear to spend as much time in “performing situations” as sessions. There are others who seem to spend all their time “gigging”. I have gained the impression that those members here who predominantly play within “sessions” are much more concerned about etiquette, good behavior (no noodling etc), the “correct way” to play tunes, appropriate settings, acceptable instruments, and all the other things that seem to matter. Those who also play gigs seem to be a bit more relaxed but are still respectful whereas as infrequent sessioners but public “performers” are more likely to disregard or be oblivious to the “unwritten rules and codes” of a session. These are the ones who are likely to talk about “jamming”, amplification, introducing different instruments, “improvising” etc. They may also wish(if they can get away with it) to “perform” rehearsed sets when they enter a session.

I realise that the above is probably an over simplification. A lot of people here will have had experience in both sessions and performance situations. Most of these, I imagine will broadly understand and respect what is “expected” at a session.

So, would you agree that the context or circumstances in which you play traditional music(Irish or whatever) influences your views here to any great extent? Or is it “horses for courses” in that you adapt to the circumstances you find yourself in at the time?

Re: Performance or session perspective

Gigs and sessions are definitely different. Even though you’re performing ITM in both scenarios, the purpose and experience for the musician is completely different. Gigs, or shows, are where you’re paid to play for the entertainment of an audience. You’re most likely playing pre-arranged sets and carefully arranged material. Sessions, whether paid or not, are where you get together with people and share tunes and conversation etc. There is an infinite array of definitions for what a session can be, and it’s usually for the mutual enjoyment of the musicians themselves, and whoever is nearby that appreciates what’s going on.

A professional flute player living in Dublin told me once that he stopped going to local sessions because no one would come to his gigs otherwise. I have also been told by professional touring musicians that touring is bad for the music because if forces you to concentrate on just a segment of your repertoire and you start to lose bits here and there. This is why they are usually very happy to participate in sessions along the way.

As for me, I’m gigging frequently and it forces me to practice and be prepared. When I get to the session I might be a little more relaxed simply because I have logged many hours of practice and have a lot of experience playing in front of people. But I try to play tunes that my friends also know, and only occasionally introduce tunes that aren’t common in our circle.

My concern for session etiquette comes from many years of session experience where I’ve witnessed bad manners spoiling what would have otherwise been an enjoyable evening of tunes. The offenders in most cases are well meaning and usually unaware of how their behavior effects the session. I do “gig” for the money, but I go to sessions for the “craic”… even if I’m being paid. We have a shared hosting arrangement at my local, so I attend as a participant in about 3 sessions for every one I’m paid to host.

Re: Performance or session perspective

Sessions and performances give a person different outlets and feed the musical soul in different ways.

Sessions are communal. There are no arrangements. You are playing with other people, not for other people. There is both form and freedom in a session. You also have the opportunity to play with a wide variety of individuals.

Performance is about entertaining others, but it can be fulfilling in other ways. With solos, you can be as loose and expressive as you wish. You can arrange the heck out of a set of tunes, or you can play them straight up in unison with others. You can mix and match instruments. You can play new tunes or composed tunes with no fear of ostracizing others.

One’s approach to performance also affects one’s viewpoint about sessions and the people in them.

Rab, I do like a tight session, but I just want to be clear about the fact that at any session I’m hosting, all levels of musicians are welcome. I always give everyone a few opportunities to start tunes, or suggest medley ideas if they like, and I’ll play with them at whatever tempo they set.
