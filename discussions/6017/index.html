Re: Why do you go to sessions?

I go (on the rare occasion I can) primarily to listen and learn, rather than with intent to play. It’ll be that way for a long time yet! I don’t have a *need* to play. (Maybe it’ll be different in five years time when I have half a chance of keeping up without wrecking it for everyone else, who knows!)

Number two reason for going is to recharge the enthusiasm battery. I go through patches where I have wall-to-wall other music to do after the day job’s done, and don’t even get to pick up the fiddle for long periods. A session props up the determination to keep at it and reminds me why I want to play, much more than recordings do.

A side benefit, rather than something I particularly seek, is that I can socialise a bit without it being the sole reason for the gathering. Socialising for its own sake is stressful for me, and I much prefer the “incidental socialising” in doing something active with friends or fellow enthusiasts. At a session it’s not the end of the world if I don’t have much to say, but I can chat a bit if I want, and it’s not a struggle to find topics of conversation even with people I’ve not met before. (If “conversation” is quite the right word for shouting over the pub noise!)

Re: Why do you go to sessions?

For fun, just for fun. And it comes in many different ways - that’s part of the fun. You never know what’ll happen.

For me it’s certainly not out of lofty reverence for the art form, or a pursuit of thruth and beauty in the form of jigs and reels. Not even a link to my heritage (well, maybe a bit of that.)

It’s closer to playing a regular game of soccer with some friends who are about your level. Sometimes you work together and it all clicks, and sometimes you’re just horrible and it crashes, but it’s nearly always great fun.

The conversation metaphor works too. Sometimes you have the same conversation multiple times, and the same people say the same things, and it all seems pointless, but it’s still worthwhile for some reason. Maybe it’s just keeping social contact alive. Often you make “small talk” about something you’re not particularly interested in, but you enjoy it anyway because you’ve hung out with people you like. (Works with playing the Kesh together for the 9,000th time too.)

And then there’s the refreshing surprises - the visiting hot-shot, the bongo/shakey egg player who comes one day and you can laugh about it later, the punter who buys a round for “the band” and tells you you’re better than the Chieftans, the new tune someone brought with them and you have the pleasure of learining it. And the times when it really does come together just right for some reason, and you sit back and sigh in happiness… “That was better than… what?. oh sorry… My mind wandered there…”

But I’d give it a miss any day in favour of watching my kids compete in sports or dance and cheering them on.
