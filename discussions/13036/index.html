Re: Aurstralians and their music

That’s a really good question that’s been nibbling at the edges of my consciousness for a couple of weeks now. I should stress that I’ve only been here (Sydney) for a bit over a year, so I’m no expert on this by any means, but I have just started looking at it.
First let’s admit that just as there has long been a market for “stage Irish” , there is a quite parallel “stage Australian”. Val Doonican, Darby O’Gill, Rolf Harris… Just as you can get CDs at any Irish airport or tourist shop in Killarney with favourite Irish drinking songs (often with a rough voice - after all, Ronnie Drew had a rough voice), or with ballads like the Rose of Tralee, so at an Ozzie airport you can get CDs of rousing songs about droving and kangaroos. If it needs to sound a bit more Irish or Australian, you can add a bit of bodhran (or even pipes) in the one case, or a bit of didgeridoo in the other. In both cases these entertainment forms exist, in both cases they are not what is likely to interest most of us.
I should also make it clear that there is a very ancient tradition of indigenous music, but that civilization is so far from ours it is really not relevant here either.
Anyway, the thing is, that a tradition of “bush music” *does* exist. It is, of course, mainly rooted in Irish and English “folk” music, but with a lot of other influences. I have been told (but I’m too new to this question to be able to confirm) that the range of tune types and paces is much wider than you find at a typical Irish session.
I find it ironic that the two or three Irish sessions I’ve seen in Sydney play a much narrower range of tune types than I was used to hearing in Ireland (perhaps I was just lucky - I do know that there are relentless reelers in Ireland too), and, if my information is correct, the bush music tradition might also be wider and more open to tunes played at a measured pace.
But like I say, I don’t know much about it at all, just that it really does exist. I look forward to finding out more.
One link you might want to follow is http://www.celt.com.au/

Re: Aurstralians and their music

Who wouldn’t understand, McK? I think aboriginal music is pretty comprehensible to just about anyone. That’s the point of it - it is partly a teaching aid for the young.

In my experience the big difference with Australia is that to the average person in the street, music is just music. There doesn’t seem to be the raft of subtle differentiation there is in the UK. We get two distinct types of reaction from listeners in pubs here. One is an ex-pat Celt of some description (Irish / Scots / Manx) who feels a touch of homesickness. The other is just an Aussie who likes to have live music in the pub played by members of the public like themselves in an informal setting. 90% of our audience couldn’t tell Scottish from Irish from English from French - they don’t care, either.

Funnily enough, most of the positive comments I have recieved have been from the sort of people I would least expect (having grown up in Manchester and the Midlands of England); tradesmen having a quick beer. We even had one guy recording our practice session to use as a ring tone on his works mobile - ‘the forty pound float’ was his favourite and he absolutely raved about it.

Once again, it might be the relative lack of class distinction here. I’ve been playing Wongawilli in the car for the last couple of weeks. Half a dozen of the tracks are tunes that we play in our ‘Celtic’ session on a Friday. No one bats an eyelid - not even the musicians ;-).

Eno

↳ Posted by ricthewhistle on March 19th, 2007.

New ‘trad’ Australian music

There are so many strands to this thread. So I’ll take it in yet another direction.

My name ( bypassing the nom-de-plume on this occasion) is Simon Wells, Brisbane muso.

I hereby invite any Aussie players ( whether Aussie-born or new chums) to contribute original tunes to my forthcoming CD ( later this year) of CTM-informed tunes. By CTM I mean Celtic Traditional Music, as although my main stock-in-trade is Irish music, I do also love the Scottish, Breton and Spanish stuff.

The Cd will be based around my flute and concertina, aided by friends on fiddle, mandolin, banjo, bouzouki and guitar. The tunes will be my own reels, jigs, hornpipes, polkas and 6/4s, plus more from people such as Melb fiddlers Felix Meagher and Catherine Moser, NSW banjoist John Spencer, and Brisbane’s Randall Mathews, to name a few.

But I’d like to make this a celebration of modern Aussie composition, and there are still many vacancies left. So if you have something you’d like us to have a crack at, pls send it to me.

My touchstone, frankly, is not material from the colonial tradition. I leave that up to the dave de hugards, jacko keavans’ (RIP), Wongawillis etc.

Whilst that heritage has its moments, and was my musical beginnings in the halcyon bush-band era of the 70s and 80s, my musical well-spring is much more the rich vein from the other side of the world. That is what has always driven me, and what earns me money, good times and friendships.

The CD will not try to define or represent an Australian musical tradition, or pay hommage, but produce something quite new. Also it will be my first solo CD, after several other appearances in bands, collaborations and choirs.

OK, hope to hear from tunemongers, or see you in a couple of weeks in the session bar at the National.
