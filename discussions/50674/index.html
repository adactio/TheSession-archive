Join me in starting a practice habit

Join me in practicing your instrument (any instrument for your Irish music) or practice your vocals 50 days in December and January. Any 50 days, does not have to be consecutive. When I say practice, it could literally mean just one minute. The idea is that one minute could lead to ten or more minutes . There are 62 days in December and January combined. So that will be fifty times out of the sixty- two available days that you pick up your instrument and play for at least one minute. The purpose here is to establish a practice habit, not go deep in your learning. Each week I will ask if anyone would like to report out there weekly stats!

If you want to add a little habit science, you could also tie your practice habit to another triggering action: Example: as soon as I get home I will play, as soon as dinner is over I will play, or when the kids have gone to sleep I will play.

Please reply if you are interested. I will post this in all of the instrument categories. Thanks! Brian

It’s an actimeter. They’re quite sophisticated, I use one for swimming that knows what stroke I’m doing and how efficient it is (distance travelled per stroke).

They should work on a bowing arm and possibly on a bellows arm for button accordion. Probably not for flutes or whistles – not sure about a piper’s bellows arm – but what might work for those instruments is the heart rate monitor. Re: Join me in starting a practice habit

I probably play something on at least one instrument several times a day under normal circumstances.

Sometimes it may be no more than for a few minutes. Having an instrument set up ready means that odd bits of time, like waiting for a kettle to boil, a bath to run etc, can be devoted to a quick tune

I am not sure where the boundary between playing and practicing lies for me. I do not consciously practice very often. But I do often get ‘wrapped up’ in a new tune and keep repeating it. I don’t do stuff like scales, arpeggios and studies. I know these things are theoretically good for me - and would make me a better and more rounded player. But I’ve reached a point where I think life is too short to put a lot of time into playing things that I don’t really enjoy.

I’m not sure that it would help me to record how much time I devote to practising. I’m more interested in whether I achieve a goal - such as getting through a tricky section of a tune

It might seem counter-intuitive but, for me, I do not learn in direct correlation to how much time I put in. Sometimes things click quickly, other times I can demoralise myself by trying and trying to play a tricky tune, give up after a couple of hours, only to find that the next day (when my brain has seemingly continued to process it all even when I am away from the instrument) I can play it almost straight away.

So I think for me counting minutes doesn’t really mean anything - it is too much like focusing on the process rather than the outcome. I just want to play the tunes, play more tunes, be able to improvise more fluently, be able to hear and repeat things more easily and so on

I think is probably a good thing to set up self motivating techniques like this idea posted here. But in the end we either want to play or we don’t. Nobody is making us

When I was eight, my brain could learn very fast - I just wasn’t that interested in classical music lessons. Now I am very interested in learning all manner of music., my brain struggles to learn anything. Oh to go back in time and have a conversation with my eight year old self :)

For whistle players living in car culture (like me), take your whistle on your commute.

I play at red lights and in traffic jams, keeping an eye ahead and behind me (I don’t want to miss my light or get rear-ended). In heavy traffic I can usually play a whole tune 3-4 times in a row at one light, or practice trouble spots over and over until they smooth out and I can move on.

I try to get a new tune well enough into memory while I’m at home so that I can really hammer on it in the car. At really long lights I can even turn on my metronome app if I’ve preset it to the tempo I want to practice.

To deal with affective barrier during open window season (I feel shy about ppl hearing me squeaking along), I put my AirPods on noise cancelling like, I can’t hear them, so they can’t hear me. What the heck, it’s only for one light ¯_(ツ)_/¯

Here’s another idea with the goal of encouraging folks to practice regularly. There’s a writing competition (I can’t remember the name) in which everyone who begins, finishes, and submits a novel of (say) 50000 words or more during the month of October wins at least a certificate of honorable mention, no matter how good or bad it is.
Something similar could be done with PRACTICE (not noodling) time. 1) Charge a minimal entry fee--that encourages commitment. 2) Require a minimum number of practice hours to earn a certificate and 3) award the two or three with the most accumulated hours some sort of prize or a portion of the pot.
