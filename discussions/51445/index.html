TradMate – an Irish session practice companion app

Hello everyone,

I would like to introduce TradMate, a free app for iOS and Android combining a tune library, set building, notation and automatically generated accompaniment.

★Tunes, sets and setlists

TradMate includes over 20,000 tunes from thesession.org, with multiple settings for many tunes. Save your preferred versions, arrange them into sets, and combine sets into longer setlists, with your own tempos, repeats and tune order.

★Humanised accompaniment

Accompaniment is generated entirely on your device from ABC notation, following rules specific to each dance type and instrument. Piano, guitar, fiddle, button accordion and bodhrán, plus an optional whistle melody, have their own rhythmic patterns, ornaments and playing techniques.

Choose your instruments, balance their levels and stereo positions, and adjust dynamics, ornamentation, harmonic richness and ensemble density. Fiddle and accordion can follow the melody or take supporting roles. Each newly generated performance can introduce different ornaments and expressive details, giving some variety while maintaining a dependable pulse for practice.

★Notation, sharing and export

Practice with staff notation, ABC or numbered notation, with highlighting and automatic scrolling. Double-tap a bar to start there, adjust the tempo, or use the count-in and metronome. You can also transpose a tune within a set without changing its original library setting.

Import your own ABC, share sets and setlists, generate an ensemble score PDF for a set, or export a tune or set as MP3 with your chosen accompaniment.

★Player Mode

Use TradMate as a music player, with a queue of tunes, sets and setlists, plus repeat and shuffle options. The library, notation and generated playback work offline. Further settings include instrument EQ, reverb and dance-specific default tempos.

★Free downloads

Version 1.0.0 is officially available on the App Store. Android is available as a direct APK download. Both are free.

- [iPhone and iPad – App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6813165863)
- [Android – APK download](https://www.galeonwhistles.com/?galeon_download=5)

Help with Google Play testing

I would also appreciate help completing Google Play’s testing requirements:

1. Join the [Galeon App Testers group](https://groups.google.com/g/galeon-app-testers).
2. With the same Google account, join the [TradMate test](https://play.google.com/apps/testing/com.andyxu.tradmate).
3. Install through Google Play, remain opted in, and try the app during the testing period.

Feedback is welcome, especially on chord choices, rhythmic feel, ornaments and tune transitions. A tune name, setting and bar number would be particularly useful.

Many thanks to Jeremy and The Session’s contributors, and to the creators of the libraries and instrument samples used. Source and license information is included in Settings.

Thank you for trying it!

Thank to you all for understanding and for your kind words. I also really enjoy playing live sessions with friends.

I first heard Irish music by chance when I was a teenager and fell in love with it. Later I started making whistles, and then flutes. It has been a 17-year journey now.

Here in China, Irish music is still quite a small interest. There are many people who love it, but we are spread out. We watch sessions online and really wish we could join something like that. In Beijing, where I live, we are trying to keep one session a month going. Shanghai has them more often, partly because there are more international musicians there.

This is why I made the app. I hope it can help people practise at home, so when they finally get a chance to join a session, they feel ready to play. I have seen beginners sitting in the corner of a pub, holding their whistles but too shy to join in. I would like to help with that.

I have talked with many local beginners and listened to what they need. A lot of the ideas for this app came from those conversations.

Of course, it won’t be useful for everyone, and that is completely fine. I just wanted to explain where the idea came from. Maybe these difficulties are less easy to notice when you have regular sessions and experienced players nearby.

Re: TradMate – an Irish session practice companion app

@Darth Piper:
Switch to “Player mode” in Settings and add the tune to your playlist. You can then repeat it without stopping by setting the first round button to “repeat tune”.

@simonds:
Thanks for the thoughtful suggestion! I’ll make a note of it for future updates.

@Jim Dorans, JustAFiddler:
I see what you mean. Let me explain how the accompaniment in the app is designed.

To create a layered ensemble sound rather than have every instrument jump in at once, I’ve set up a priority system so the instruments leave room for one another.

The tin whistle is the primary lead instrument. I may also add flute and pipes in the future.
The fiddle and accordion can take on either of two roles, selectable from their respective dropdown menus:

*Melody Guide: They play a version of the main melody that closely follows the lead, but isn’t exactly the same.
*Session Support: They leave space for the main melody and provide varied harmonic accompaniment instead.

The guitar only plays chords.

Since the piano isn’t particularly common in sessions, I’ve given it a mainly harmonic role too. Most of its accompaniment sits in the lower, left-hand register, while the right hand adds occasional melodic accents.

But you’ve made a good point: when the fiddle and accordion are turned off, the piano’s right hand could take on more of the main melody. I’ll consider adding that option in a future update.

Re: TradMate – an Irish session practice companion app

“I ‘practice’ by sitting down with an instrument and playing tunes. I don’t see how any app is going to help with that….”

Practicing alone is great for honing technique and learning tunes. But to play in a session, it helps to have some experience playing while surrounded by the sounds of other instruments. You learn to listen and latch onto what the other musicians are doing—their pace, pulse, degree of swing, even tone and intonation. All while sustaining your own playing of the tunes.

That involves skills and an ability to focus on more than one thing at once that can’t be learned playing solo without the racket of other instruments. Ideally, we develop these skills by playing in casual sessions or with a couple of friends in someone’s kitchen. But playing along with recordings also works and is a decent second-best option.

This app would be lower on my list of options, but I can see how some people would make good use of it, specifically for developing the skills of ensemble playing.
