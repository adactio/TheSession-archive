Maybe the further from the epicentre of Irish music you get (geographically), the more likely tunes will be judged purely on aesthetic, rather than by cultural criteria. If you view Irish music simply as a collection of tunes, adrift in the critical universe, then to wonder whether the Bucks is too ‘daggy’ to be worth conserving might be valid. Maybe this is one very negative aspect of worldwide session creep, that the cultural aspect of Irish music becomes completely subsumed by by the aesthetic aspect.

I see your point about the cultural thing, but I don’t think that’s actually how it works in practice. Places that are far removed from the geographical source can often be more conservative of cultural capital, not less. Anyway, think of the title of the discussion: “…tunes that are hammered out across the world nightly”, i.e. dag gets played everywhere. In fact I’d go so far as to say that dag is more popular elsewhere than in Ireland. Think of all those daggy sessions with scruffy looking tramps playing the spoons and women in floaty dresses and too much makeup singing folk songs and playing “Rakes Of Mallow” on their English concertinas. England, America and Australia have their fair share of dag thankyou very much!

Right then. First of all, it’s my thread title, not Michael’s, so you can blame me.

I didn’t really expect a new thread to arise from the “Penguin Cafe Orchestra thing”, but there you go.

My original point was, why pick on “Music for a Found Harmonium” as a bad tune, when there are tons of other naffs in I.T. OK, in my opinion, “Music for a Found Harmonium” is not a bad tune at all, but it’s definately not the type to thing to play simply as a melody as you would an Irish reel, which is what everyone seems to have done. It’s a band tune, full of harmonies and dynamics which simply get lost when played my multiple melody instruments, made even worse by naff accompaniment. That’s why (IMO) it sounds so anodyne when it’s rattled out in a session in the midst of other (perfectly good and melodic) reels. It’s gone from a band tune to a bland tune simply because it’s been given the I.T treatment.

So, back to the thread - as I said, “naff”, “twee, trite & sh*te” tunes have been discussed before in other threads. To me, a tune is in this category at the most basic level - simply because of the combination of notes that make it sound less than good.
Forget about the big boys, a crap tune will not sound any better by them, there will just be less mistakes and the timing and punch will be better.

No crime in playing them either - I sometimes have to do them at gigs, but I wouldn’t always play them at a session. Why should I? If they’re the I.T. equivalent of “Una Paloma Blanca”, then I think a trip to the bar is in order.
