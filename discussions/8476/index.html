Why it is important to hate Robbie Williams

A year or two ago commercial radio did a listeners poll of the best musician in history. Number one was Mozart. Number 3, JS Bach. So far so good. But number two was Robbie Williams. I read later, that Robbie was aggrieved that he wasn’t number one.

For his current record deal he will earn £38 million. He is, he says, “rich beyond his wildest dreams of avarice”.

A number of points present themselves. Mozart was a surpassing genius. JS Bach posessed the finest mind our species ever produced. Robbie Williams is an odd looking englishman with an unremarkable voice and a negligible talent for writing songs.

In a world where children starve to death it is obscene that anyone earns such ludicrous sums.

But from a musician’s point of view it is laughable. Consider the thousands of classical, jazz and irish musicians in the world who work incredibly hard, with real talent. They play purely for the love of it. The gifts they have are shared freely.

If Irish music were as popular as classical or Jazz music many of us would make a perfectly respectable living. Instead we are condemned to wage slavery. The music we play is great fun. But it is also important. Really important. One of the most crucial things people do is express themselves. The capacity to reflect and share our experiences through music is one of our crowning achievements.

But it is demeaned and cheapened by multi national corporations brand name pop music. That is a cause for real sadness.

I have never heard of Robbie Williams before, nor have I read the subsequent responses to Pól’s original post (yet)…

Pól wrote: “But it is demeaned and cheapened by multi national corporations brand name pop music.”

After many years in and on the edges of the pop music “industry” I have come to the conclusion that pop music has only the slightest passing resemblance to actual music, and that what the “pop industry” does should have as much relevance to people who actually play music for reasons other than financial success as the industries that make cars or soap or any other sort of goods.

Maybe I’m just doing that to protect myself from the sort of sadness that Pól mentions. In any case, Pop Music is now just a ‘language’ that I speak, but I planet that I hope not to visit again.

There is so much going on, of great value, among musicians with values that are more … human… than that of ‘the industry’ that there is no real need to go there. This, for me, includes musicians who work at making music in the various ‘pop song’ forms, but who understand that the ‘industry’ and it’s weird notions of success and rapacious values, can only destroy their lives and music.

That dinosaur is dying and it will go thru many ugly contortions, and probably do quite a bit of damage in it’s dying throes.

I think we should just turn away and devote our best to the music we value, and to the people who share those values, for all our best reasons.

Re: Why it is important to hate Robbie Williams

I’m amazed at the latent and actual jealousy displayed in some of these posts. I don’t hate Robbie Williams, and I find the use of the term “hate” distasteful in this context.

I don’t “love” Robbie Williams either, but bear in mind that he is not directly responsible for the appalling lack of appreciation for music in the modern world, nor is he responsible for the relative lack of success of other musicians - rather, he confirms how far you can go on very little in the music business.

Also, Robbie CAN sing (unlike many of his contemporaries) and he does put on a good live show, for which people seem to want to pay megabucks. AND according to my lady friends he is devilishly attractive in a “bad boy” sort of way. And believe me, that’s what sells music in the 21st century.

In some ways, Riverdance and especially Flatley have exploited ITM and made a lot of money out of it - but they have raised the profile of ITM and provided direct and indirect employment for thousands of people in the process.

I say the best of luck to Robbie Williams, and hang the begrudgers (now Robbie, I hear you’re looking for a lead guitarist?…….)
