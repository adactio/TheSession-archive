On the home page, Jeremy states

However, the point I’m making is that many people seem to consider the “Irish Session” to be the norm and everything else is derived from this in some way or some form of deviation or a watering down of the real thing when, in fact, there are many “different beasts”.

As I say, we do have many true Irish sessions here too…ranging from so called “pure drop” to those which will feature tunes from other areas. On the other hand, there also loads of sessions which are obviously NOT Irish but might also feature lots of Irish tunes.

Confused? Sorry.

Furthermore, many tunes have now been “absorbed into the Irish tradition” eg Flowers of Edinburgh, Mrs McLeod and so on. When played in an “Irish session” they are accepted as such and played in the appropriate(Irish) style.

However, in a Scotish session, they are still regarded to be Scottish tunes and usually played in that fashion.

Re: The composing of traditional tunes.

Charlie Lennon, Paddy Fahey, Liz Carroll, Sean Ryan, Ed Reavy, Mike McGoldrick, Sean O Riada etc. etc.

All people who are living or lived until relatively recently, all composers of ‘traditional’ tunes. The chances are if you are in a session you will hear at least one tune composed by each of these composers played. In fact I’ve noticed an awful lot of ‘traditional’ sessions are even dominated by tunes by these composers and others who are still alive or recently deceased.

When are people going to get it in their heads that ‘traditional’ music does not mean music written 300 or more years ago, it means music written in the style of a tradition that has been cultivated for many centuries. This tradition has continually evolved within relatively strict boundaries but it is still a living tradition and so if Peter Maxwell Davies happens to write a jig or a reel then there’s nothing wrong with calling them ‘traditional’ jigs or reels because he is writing in a style within that tradition rather than the ‘classical’ tradition for which he is best known.

I’m not a big fan of PMD but I think it’s wrong to suggest he can’t compose a ‘traditonal jig and reel’, I know that he is very involved in the traditional music of the Orkney Islands and that combined with his well established compositional talent qualifies him to compose in a ‘traditional’ style.

Let composers compose without ground rules of what is ‘traditional’ or not otherwise you might miss out on such gems as ‘The Swedish Jig’ aka ‘Arthur Darleys’ which was composed by Arthur Darley who was a classical violinist and member of the Radio Eireann Orchestra in the early 20th Century. It’s extremely close minded and ignorant to deny the possibility that PMD hasn’t composed tunes which will be played in the Orkney Islands in 100 years. I’d bet my life that he has!
