Re: 5-row reels

Lovely playing Matti, appreciated. However, as someone said earlier “it sounds like a piano accordion”, it also suffers from the same ills - legato, slurring, lack of rhythm and phrase based dynamics, the punch and lift that makes this ‘dance music’. You’ve talent, and it’s beautiful, but it lacks some of the heart, blood and guts of this music. Listen to some single row boxes, and two row D/D# and C#/D chromatic boxes, to get some of that breathing and lift into your playing. That’s something that those obsessed with ornamentation often skip over. I’d much rather hear that lift and life than a tune packed with ornaments that just kind of rolls along numbingly. While I enjoyed your playing, was impressed, my feet weren’t. It didn’t make them itch or tap…

Keep up the good works, and consider exploring those bits you’ve missed…

Re: 5-row reels

I thought it was nice. There may be some truth in what ceolachan says - that it’s difficult to make it sound like a box, but maybe he hasn’t considered that you just might not WANT it to sound that way. There are a lot of people out there who won’t accept instruments like this into “their” tradition and will give you a million reasons why. The “bellows” thing is the biggie, but they don’t realise that not everyone wants to sound like Joe Cooley or a rumpty-tumpty ceili band. Then they’ll say “but it’s DANCE music!” Well yes it is, but those people forget that on the pipes - that most Irish of all instruments - it’s impossible to do bellows changes, and much of the lift in pipes music (with its continuous air supply) comes from ornamentation, staccato notes and good timing with the fingers. Will the pipes then get frowns of disapproval too?

I’d say do it if YOU like it and it makes YOU happy. This music isn’t some kind of exclusive club, and there’s room for people to express themselves in different ways to cater for diverse preferences. For many of us, this music is simply something to play with friends at people’s houses and down the pub. If they like it and think it sounds authentic and they enjoy playing with you, then that’s important. But even more importantly, have you achieved the sound you want from yourself? If so, just enjoy it for what it is. If YOU like it and it makes YOU happy, then keep doing what you’re doing.

Boom Chucking to death a tune, as some well known piano bangers and bodhranistas do - and for fear of my life I won’t mention names - I like even less than the legato playing of certain others. With the latter, I can find that the first track has me take notice, I can be impressed, amazed, and then the second track, O.K., and by the third I can find myself becoming increasingly more aware of the diddling rather than anything I value most in music, dance or otherwise, and one of the first involuntary proofs is that it doesn’t move me, or, to be more precise, it doesn’t move my feet, or my heart. With me that counts at least as much if not more than the brain, or that bit of it that is easily impressed by acrobatics and the exhitition of it all, the diddling. It doesn’t surprise me that some folks only see that in this music, that, as some have said, “It’s all diddly music isn’t it?” “No!” I would answer. But I also think the diddly stuff is or can be fun, and not just about showing off and trying to lay as much as possible of it over the music, like a life smothering layer of asphalt given the final press with one of those weighty great rollers, flattening it all out.

The examples of ‘different’ box players I’d mentioned previously, above, is ‘varied’, each having their own way with the music. It barely scratches the surface and is just the usual repetition I make of needing to listen and listen carefully, not just to the obvious diddling. It was just a small sample, but most of them were not unfamiliar with the dance floor, and not just as musicians playing for dancers, in the rooms and kitchens of houses and in the great halls during the times of big city dances. I’d expect that the same is true of Matti, but he can tell you if he comes back this way. My wife and I have known, appreciated and enjoyed the music and dance of Finland, from couple dances to the quadrilles. Maybe listening to too much compressed music and too much show stuff, some of the commercial and session stuff, some of it great, some of it shight, at least in my experience and opinion of it - that the steam rolled danceless lull of diddling it to death, that asphalt, one can mistake that for ‘all there is’. Well, all I’m saying here, repeatedly, is that “that ain’t all there is to it!” I’d prefer less rock and tar and more heart, even if it gives away its grounded origins with the occassional potholes or exposed roots…

I appreciate the different views and passions expressed here, it makes for an interesting read.

I have to agree with ceolachan on the “boom-chuck” left hand playing, I too can only take it in very small portions at a time. When I play the 2-row, I normally don’t use the basses at all. With the 5-row I may play bass if I’m not having a guitarist or other accompanist playing with me. But when I do, I prefer to drone more - and play open chords on the left hand.

This accordion which is featured in my clip, has a customised bass-chord row, which has had the thirds removed. I’ve started experimenting with it, and also using a very light-sounding register, so the bass wouldn’t be too overpowering. One can actually achieve a bouzouki-ish tonality with it, to an extent. Of course there is also the option of just having all stops out, full-on thick 4-reed sound, which has quite an old-school feel to it, in this case the “boom chuck” comes quite naturally for the left hand.

Maybe I’ll have to post something more, to showcase the different possibilities…
