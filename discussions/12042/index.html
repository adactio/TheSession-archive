Dave Mallinson

I really like and recommend the Dave Mallinson (Mally) books:

100 Vital Session Tunes
100 Enduring Session Tunes
100 Irish Polkas
100 Essential Session Tunes (temporarily out of print)
100 Evergreen Session Tunes (temporarily out of print)

The selections are session friendly, and the typesetting is crystal clear with sensible ornamentation for any instrument. The books are thin and light, and easy to carry along. Much better than any edition of O’Neill’s for learning. They really helped me when I first started sessioning.

Look about halfway down the page:
http://www.mally.com/results.asp?CategoryID=45

Re: Re: O’Neill

MTGuru, you’re right, I’ve been a bit harsh, and I didn’t back up what I said with examples of what I’m talking about, which really are very minor in the whole scheme of things. They’re pretty good really, and they’ve served me well, so I shouldn’t be so negative about them. I’ll give you some examples of the sort of thing I had in mind, from the 100 Essential book.

1) Salamanca, p.3, full bars 1 & 3: |ADF{E}D ADF{E}D|. I think this is meant to show that you can either play |ADFD ADFD| or |AD (3FED AD (3FED| but as it is, it looks confusing and very unirish.

2) Farewell To Ireland p.5, bar 7 of B & C part |cABA EAea|. This is too “box-specific”, and a more standard phrase would have been more appropriate for a tune book I think, like |cAA2 (3Bcd ef|, instead of the acrobatics.

3) McMahon’s, p.8, bar 1: |G3D EDEG|. Just like the version here on the session. It breaks my heart to hear people play this. It’s |G3D EDB,D|. It’s the nicest part of the tune, so why steamroller over it?

4) Longford Collector, p.6, bar 6: |eBdB ~d2ef|. That sounds unirish to me. Roll those Bs! |eB~B2 d2ef|.

5) pp. 2, 8, 21 etc: Those shuffle patterns |G2BG dGBG| and |c2ec gcec| are consistently represented as |(3GFG BG dGBG| and |(3cBc ec gcec| which does not sufficiently take into account what you’d be doing on other instruments. I think they’re best left unornamented.

6) Blarney Pilgrim p.39, bar 2: |{Ac}A2G ABc|. This is also a bit too D/G box-orientated and should have been omitted.

Well okay there’s a case for saying that he’s put his personal stamp on the tunes and putting them across as is, and that that’s a good thing, but I think what I’m getting at is that D/G melodeon ornaments are specific to that instrument and associated with a particular style that can often sound more English and “morrisy” than Irish. That’s fine in itself, but the book’s supposed to help you through Irish sessions.

As for the chords, they’re not only very uninteresting, but also seem to be based around the particular melody of each bar in isolation, rather than being a soundly constructed chord structure as a whole. So if you play them on guitar or something, you’re changing chords far too many times and it sounds too busy.

BUT, the choice of tunes is good (they’re all standards), and I have to say, most of the settings are alright too, so I’m just being unnecessarily nitpicky (sorry Mally!)

I agree with Nox that the Mally books, like all books, should be approached with caution. That being said, I often find that the settings in the Mally book are closer to what is heard in current sessions than the settings in many other books, especially O’Neill’s.
Like Nox, I found that the Mally setting of McMahon’s is very much like the setting we play in the local pub, which is the only setting I know--I will have to try his alternate starting bar to see how it sounds--but Nox is more criticising the way we play the tune in sessions, more than he is criticising the collector of the tunes.
As far as the Mally chords go, I also think that they are too often overly busy, and sometimes to my ear not really as “Irish” sounding. Sometimes I have tried the chords from Mally and really liked them, but in other cases I go “ick” and when I do, I generally go in a more simple direction. For example, I liked his chords for the Dmodal Toss the Feathers, but not the Eminor Toss the Feathers. With chords, there is a lot of personal taste involved, different tunings lead to different approaches, so I think it is harder for a collector to include chords than capture the melody. I find it is an interesting evolution to try to play chords straight from a book, I may not accept them all as written, but they often show me something nice that I would not have thought of myself.
In the end, all tune books have their limits.

Re: Re: O’Neill

Say, Nox … that’s a very good analysis. And I see what you mean. In each of the examples you cite, I agree with you 100%.

One of the things I like about the books is the very sparing use of marked ornamentation. But the markings that are there should pretty much be ignored, in favor of whatever is appropriate for your own instrument and style. That’s probably true for most written out ornaments, except in tutorials for specific instruments.

Yes, those “Mally-isms” you describe are a bit off. They make more sense on box, concertina, banjo etc. than on fiddle, flute, pipes etc. with more flexible ornamentation.

1) Salamanca. Actually, I almost never play or hear anything but |AD~D2 AD~D2| or |A~D3 A~D3|. That repeated figure seems like such an essential characteristic of the tune.

3) McMahon’s. Yes, it’s |G3D EDB,D| … except on whistle (which I play) or flute or pipes! But I either fold the B, |G3D EDBD| or cran the D |G3D E~D3|. In any case, the Mally version more or less clashes with all of these.

But in general, a beginner walking into an Irish session playing the Mally selections and settings, Mallyisms and all, would probably do just fine. And that kind of confidence boost can be important at first. I’ve seen beginners try out uncommon tunes or settings they got from O’Neill’s or some other source, and get shot down pretty badly. In my case, the Mally tunes gave me a decent working repertoire until I could pick up the local session “dialect”. I’m sure ceolachan’s suggestions would do very well, too.

Of course, in response to enirehtac, I guess we assume she’s looking for a good compact tune collection, and not necessarily O’Neill’s specifically.
