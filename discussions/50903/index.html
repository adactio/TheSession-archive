Re: Looking for a fife in C, in Canada

Wren, do you want a fife in Concert C? Fifemakers, and piccolo makers, as in simple, unkeyed instruments are rather thin on the ground.
I am uncertain what your intended use will be. While simple, straight tubular fifes in C are made, both in modern pitch key of C, and traditional fifing pitch of C, rather closer to modern Bb in Concert pitch, I don’t know of any active makers in Canada, tho’ you might enquire about that of Forbes Christie, of Windward Flutes, in Nova Scotia. If your purpose is to be fifing in a Fife and Drum band your best bet would be to enquire of a local (if any) Fife corps who they’re supplier is.
To be certain we are on the same page; if you wish to play traditional Irish/Scottish traditional music, you really wouldn’t want a fife (or piccolo) in ‘C’, since it’s native scale would be in ‘C’, rather than ‘D’, ‘C’ being rather awkward to play along in Session.
You might be able to expand your search to the UK, since you are in the Dominion. Northern Ireland is a hotbed for fifing, so McNeela Music would be a place to start. Perhaps Terry McGee will be able to weigh in.

Re: Looking for a fife in C, in Canada

I can’t offer any specific advice on sourcing an instrument in Canada I’m afraid, but would add to the requests to define exactly what you mean by ‘in C’.

(Forgive me, please,if you know all this already!)

Most fifes are named according to the note produced when all 6 fingers are down - so the most useful key instrument for playing Irish and other traditional musics is D.

Then you get into the further complication of ‘old pitch’ and the like, where a note written in the sheet music as D, and referred to as such in discussions, would actually register on a tuning meter as closer to a ‘concert pitch’ Bb. And taking a Bb fife to a standard-tuning session or playing opportunity will not go well!

Re: Looking for a fife in C, in Canada

I think you can see, Wren you have opened up a bit of a can of worms with your request for ‘a Fife in C’. First out of the gate the terminology is a bit squiggly. A fife is generally thought of as a straight bore flutophone that has a tendency to be quite loud, and must be ‘humoured’ a great deal to get them to play in tune with themselves, let alone other instruments. Fife Marching Bands get around this somewhat by specifying all members source their instruments as sourced from one maker; i.e. Cooperman, Morneaux, or Healy or some such. But the ambiguity here is that not all simple, because some people include what can be called simple system (one key or keyless)
piccolos in this group. The famous Fasnacht Festival in Basle, Switzerland features Fife and Drum Corps playing what I would call simple system piccolos-instruments with tapered bores that play well in tune with themselves and others, and can be managed to a high degree of musicianship. I own one of these I got off of the French E-bay some years ago, which was described as ‘Une ancienne fivre’. A lovely one-key piccolo with a tuning slide that plays in a native scale of modern pitch ‘D’ and producing a bang-on A=440hz. And it is capable of playing pianissimo. Thomas Aebi of Basel, Switzerland, has been known to make and supply such piccolos. Ulster also has a tradition of Marching Flute Bands, and I am sure there are current makers there. Again McNeela would be the man to know these. . .I don’t think there are honorous tariffs between Commonwealth Nations.

“cork”, I remember having trouble with it, mine was leaky I think. Just peeked inside, looks like wine-bottle-cork material. as for position, you move it until the high and the low octave are equally out of tune.

“yamaha fife”, yes I rememeber seeing them, never played one. but the plastic is very different, guoflute is hard high-density plastic, ebonite-like, while yamaha is regular plastic.

“aulos flute”, I have their 1-key baroque flute in A440, and it’s a dog, the blow hole is not well defined and cannot be recut (non-machinable cheap injection-molding plastic).

Speaking of guo flutes, I have their low-C typewriter flute and it’s excellent, the valves seal well, the adjustment screws work, and being plastic it works in the rain and in cold weather. it is also super light. downside, it cannot be taken apart, so you carry it in it’s bulky long container. it’s a good player, if you see one, grab it, or at least try it, I recommend.

Canada import tariff on flutes, 9205.90.10 (and 9209.99.10 for flute parts) is “free”,
https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/trade-commerce/tariff-tarif/2026/html/tblmod-eng.html
https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/trade-commerce/tariff-tarif/2026/01-99/01-99-2026-eng.pdf
search for “flute”.
do contact the seller before they ship, confirm they know this stuff and use the correct tariff code.
the CAD-USD exchange rate and the arbitrary “S&H” charges by the shipping companies is the painful part.
my latest flute purchase from the US was about 1 year ago, a delrin F from Dave Copley. in addition to his charges and his shipping fee, I paid the BC sales tax, the Canada post “brokerage fee” and that’s it.
