What is an air button?

↳ Posted by Respect on February 26th, 2026.

Re: Tunes you can’t play…

On both diatonic button accordions and Anglo concertinas, both which play different notes on a push vs. a draw, it is a button (or lever/bar on an accordion) you can press that doesn’t have a reed.

On the Anglo concertina it’s operated by the right thumb.

On diatonic button accordions it’s operated by the left thumb.

It is used to allow you to dump or draw in additional air as required either to play a passage or set up the bellows in an optimal position for the notes that follow and avoid either the bellows getting too closed or too open as a result of a long string of continuous push or draw notes.

Experienced players of both instruments “ride” the air button as required while playing to optimize the bellows position continuously.

Piano and chromatic button accordions (and English concertinas) don’t necessarily need air buttons since they play the same note in both directions of the bellows, so you can reverse the bellows at essentially any time as required. They generally do have an air button, but it’s only necessary to initially set the bellows position before playing or to dump all the air before putting the instrument away.

Re: Tunes you can’t play…

There’s several types of “can’t play” tunes:

1) some notes not available on your instrument
2) can’t memorize properly
3) has difficult jumps or fingerings
4) can play at pace, but not at speed
5) can’t bother to practice

Once you’ve identified one of types 1-4, you know what you can do about it.

I think there’s no reason to feel guilty about type 5 tunes. There are so many tunes that I like and would love to play better. Time is too precious to practice a tune I don’t really like, unless it’s for a gig or some special occasion.
