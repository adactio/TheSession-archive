Noise

I have something of a new problem although I rather suspect it is a common experience among some of you.

In October I moved into my new home - a semi-detached house in a quiet village. All seemed well and we settled down to our new life. In a previous posting I pointed out that in addition to my guitar and mandolin I was also trying to learn the whistle. Now on face value these may not seem connected but as my story unfolds you will discover this is not the case.

About a month ago while practicing my whistle (a Dixon high D) my neighbour gave several loud thumps on the wall. Now it didn’t need a degree in rocket science to work out that he was complaining about the noise - so I stopped. Several incidents have occurred since then and on each occasion with the same result. (Today he thumped following 5 minutes of the whistle at 1.10 pm). I really don’t want to have to giveup on the whistle, nor do I feel inclimed to ram paper clips down the fipple etc.

So far he has made no comment about my mandolin or guitars .. although G-d help me if I were to plug in my Les Paul and belt out some rock anthem!!!

It seems at the moment my sole solution is to go and practice in the local open countryside unless anyone has a better idea. Of course I could hire a room locally and practice there - but I am on a limited income since my stroke and this is not my favourite option. My neighbour is unlikely to be moved on his position - others have had similar experiences with him in the past with no beneficial effect.

Options I have so far considered include:-
1. Impale my neighbour on the sharp end of a Generation D
2. Practice in the open countryside - often cold and wet but at least it is an option.
3. Give up the whistle and go all out on the mando
4. Move - not my first option

If anyone can offer a realistic alternative I would truly appreciate their contribution.

D

Actually dear Welshman there are often dry places (if not warm) out of doors that you might possibly try (if the stabbing and other alternatives don’t have the desired effect). This is from someone who has been down that track and taken it to ridiculous extremes.

Here are some places you might have access to: Bandstands in parks are great, the stands at show grounds (often prettywell deserted for most of the year), porches of unoccuppied office buildings (at the weekends), deserted shopping centres when they are closed, the verhanders around the changing rooms at football ovals, deserted bus interchanges, even bus shelters will do the trick. Move around a bit so it never becomes an issue anywhere.

Alternatively you could see if you could get a key to a practice room at the local school or university (I’ve done both of these as well).

I have a trail of old haunts across the country, but I admit that after five years of living in op’s houses, ressies, group digs etc and not being able to play when and where I wanted, I bought a house, sole (or soul) reason, so there wouldn’t be an issue … and now there isn’t :-)

Fortunately she is not a neighbor. Though I cannot help but wonder who may be within earshot. I enjoy playing at home, but never in a box. I often go for hikes with the tinwhistle. Great aerobics. I manage to find an overhang when it is raining & other shelter when the wind blows. Respect your neighbors but do not let anyone prohibit your playing!
