Re: Analogy -- what it’s like to play in a session.

Nice picture Jack.

The conversation analogy is better in my mind, but it only works with people who understand how a good conversation works - and there are a lot of people who don’t understand that. A good conversation is a different thing from a debate or even a discussion. A good conversation has asides, and changes of tack - it doesn’t have a fixed subject or theme. And so a good session has moments out where someone plays (or even sings) something so different from what comes before or after it, that it stands out as a highlight, not as a digression from subject.

Sometimes El Grumpo and I sing the Bonzo’s song (can’t remember what its called, goes “the stork has brought a son and daughter for Mr and Mrs Mickey Mouse” etc). Now that has nothing at all to do with Irish Traditional Music - but it has everything to do with having a good session. In the conversation analogy, it’s like when someone spills their coffee, and you all rush round finding cloths and tissues before getting back to where you were. Perhaps in Jacks picture it is the equivalent of the moment when someone remarks that the object on the table looks like a pancreas with stubble. You all laugh for a while before you carry on your studies.

Re: Analogy -- what it’s like to play in a session.

I agree with Zina - Jack’s analogy is more about the tune than the players’ experience.

For me, playing in a session is almost beyond words. It’s such a different experience. If I had to reach, I might say it’s like playing ice hockey--each player with a distinct role to play, wheeling independently in Brownian motion, but still moving cohesively as a team, responding to each other’s trajectory and a common purpose.

But for me, any analogy that doesn’t capture the power of the _sound_ in a session just doesn’t cut it. Hmmm…playing in a session is like being an instrument yourself, vibrating in sympathy with all the other players’ sounds and resonating it back to them.

It also feels like telepathy, like communion, like dancing without ever leaving your chair, like eating from a well-stocked smorgasbord, like body-surfing in the ocean, like flying five stunt kites all at once with your eyes closed, and--when it goes wrong--like riding a shopping cart full of snare drums down the up escalator….
