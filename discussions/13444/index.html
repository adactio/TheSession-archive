Where do you get your tunes?

I’m sure this will change as I learn more and get more session experience, but for now, this is how it works for me:

From my teacher: 40%
From cds: 40%
From session recordings: 2%
From the actual session: 2%
From YouTube (or other internet source): 2%
From books: 2%
From television: 2%

Are there other sources to use? How does everyone else do it?

Re: Where do you get your tunes?

Osmosis is probably my main source too. I’ve been listening to this music for over 30 years, so I’ve heard nearly all of the old tunes many, many times, from many people, in many different settings. They eventually seep out, under my own fingers.

I also pick up tunes at our session. And members here frequently (several times a week on average) send me sound clips or abcs to suss out or identify for them. If the tune is new to me and I like it, I usually learn it right off.

But I rarely listen to cds or other recorded sources. I used to, but I don’t like how they fossilize the music.

Speaking of fossils, I’ve also written down thousands of tunes over the years just as a memory jog. Later, I’ll dig up the abcs or dots to remind me of the person playing the tune and what sort of lilt or lift they gave it, and I’ll get the bones of it going and then leave the dots and just play it every day for a week or two until it becomes mine as well.

Honestly though, I don’t think the source matters much at all compared to how you treat the tune as you’re learning it. The point is to breathe new life into a tune each time you play it, rather than simply re-creating what you’ve heard someone else play, or seen the dots for, or played yourself last week.

Let me qualify that--Kennedy, in the early years, listening to recordings is a great way to learn the nuances because you can listen over and over, and tickle out the nuances. This is a crucial step to building your own vocabularly and competencies.

Re: Where do you get your tunes?

In descending order of frequency: teacher (at lessons and from the recordings he makes for me), session, commercial recordings, sheet music. And I’ve learned a good many tunes from all four sources, so it’s hard for me to assign percentages. Most commonly (60% or so of my tunes): I hear the tune at the session; I absorb it into my subconscious; my teacher teaches it to me, records it, and gives me the sheet music for it (I rarely use the sheet music, though); I practice the tune; I dig up the half dozen recordings I have of it to get ideas of variations; I test-drive the tune at the session and come away with more ideas; I practice the tune some more.

Less commonly, but still how I’ve gotten 25% or so of my tunes: I hear a tune at a session; I figure out the key, the time signature, and a few notes; I go home, look the tune up here and in some other database, and see if I can find the its name; if so, I dig up the sheet music (and a recording, if there’s one handy) and learn the rest of (one version of) the tune; if not, I go to the session next week and repeat the process on that until I either have enough information to get the tune name, or until I’ve figured out the whole tune without a name. I never ask for tune names at the session - for some reason that feels like cheating. Of the *counts* ten tunes I’ve learned this way, I still don’t know the names of two of them; other names I’ve only found out after a fellow musician mentions them, or until my teacher and I have a conversation like this one:

“Have we done Ger the Rigger yet?”
“No…”
“OK, it starts like this *plays a few bars*”
“Oh! That one! Yeah, I can play most of that one.”

I get a lot of my tunes by osmosis these days. I pick them up in sessions (dare I say “noodling” them?). We run a tune learning session. When I’m not teaching a tune there, I always try to make sure the person teaching is doing one that I don’t know :-)

I very rarely pick up tunes from commercial recordings. But I do have a number of session recordings from Ireland (and elsewhere) that I listen to quite often. I learn tunes from those on occasion.

Re: Where do you get your tunes?

Kennedy - I don’t ask for tune names because I prefer figuring them out for myself. It’s hard to explain, but I feel like I should get at least somewhat familiar with a tune before I know what it’s called. It’s something of a game for me, too - I can easily figure out key and time signatures, and if the tune is slow enough (or familiar enough) I can also figure out a bar or twelve, and then I go searching online, and it’s very satisfying when I’m able to find the name that way. Once, my teacher and one of the other fiddle teachers brought a new tune the session that he runs; I was able to figure out about around half of it, along with key and time signature, in the three times it was played. Later that night I found the tune on this site, and I learned the rest of it, and I practiced and practiced and practiced it, over the course of the week. The next week at the pub, toward the end of the evening, my teacher asked if I had any requests.

“How about that new one, that waltz in A minor,” I suggested innocently. No one else in the pub was familiar with the tune, and I hadn’t asked either of those two fiddlers its name, so they assumed that I was just requesting to *hear* it. The look on my teacher’s face was priceless when he saw that I alone, with my eight months of playing history, was able to join the two experienced fidders, who collectively have a good 60 or so years of playing experience between them. Good times, good times.

That said, I actually do know many tune names, just because I have a vast CD collection (which has been growing at an alarming rate ever since I took up the fiddle - damn, this is an expensive addiction…) and because I have a good memory for these things. People have started asking *me* for tune names lately!

Re: Where do you get your tunes?

Osmosis is not a good word because only water moves by osmosis. The tunes would remain stubbornly on their own side of the membrane. Water molecules contain only three atoms each, so are small enough to pass through the pores, whereas tune molecules are huge, each consisting of hundreds of dots which, furthermore, have the annoying habit of rearranging themselves and attracting additional bits known as “variations” and “ornamentation.” So choose another word.

I can only say, when you are sturated with music, it oozes out of every poor in one form or another, like the soundtrack for a documentary or movie, but not taken for granted, sometimes it takes ahold of you and it writes itself, using me as the medium. Most of it I just enjoy for the moment, just let it pass by and don’t reach out to hold on to it, or it to hold on to me, and that is at least 9 times out of 10, being very conservative, but sometimes it tickles me to the point that I know I have to answer that with putting it down and sharing it.
