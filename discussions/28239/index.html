Re: C#D Button Accordion & Comhaltas

plus, the opposition between the two systems is faux. ersatz. made-up. ridiculous.

on a b/c box, when you play on your c row in the key of c (say, a “D tune,” but playing it in C), you ARE playing what the turistas and poseurs call, “c#/d style.” that is all so-called “c#/d style” IS, is playing “on the row” like a one-row melodeon. on a c#/d box, you do this with D tunes on the D row, and also A, G, and all of their relative minors. but once you leave those keys, you are no longer playing so-called “c#/d style.”

so, even “c#/d -style players,” including the great ones, keep b/c’s around for playing in flat keys. see, the wonderful cd a couple years ago by peter carberry of music from longford. he is playing “on the row” on a b/c. that is the SAME as so-called “c#/d style.”

so when you see marvelous clips of b/c players, if they are playing in C or its relative minor, a minor, or F or G and THEIR relative minors, that IS what turistas call “c#/d style,” i.e., “on the row,” like a one-row melodeon.

so the whole thing is ridiculous.

Re: C#D Button Accordion & Comhaltas

I don’t read music well either, Patrick, and am also (a bit half-heartedly at the moment) learning C#/D. I’ve run out suitable tab from Michael Eskin’s tool, which I find easy to read.

I haven’t invested in learning to read music because I pick up tunes best by ear, but it’s well worth doing if you’re not an ear learner. In the meantime, the staff would give you info about key, structure, note length, etc, and the tab would tell you which button to press and which direction to move the bellows. It’s helping me to start building a mental map of the instrument, and once I can get through a few tunes from memory, I’ll find a tutor.

I wouldn’t try to adapt beginner lessons designed for a B/C box, it’s a different system (when you think about it, it’s a bit perverse for a music centred on neither of those keys). The initial mind mapping is probably more difficult, but once you’ve got that nailed, it’s a more flowing playing style. I went for C#/D because I love the drive of it, but it’s true there are fewer learning resources around.

Re: C#D Button Accordion & Comhaltas

Just realised I posted that without including a link to Michael’s tool. Here it is: https://michaeleskin.com/abctools/abctools.html

Paste some ABC notation into the box, and the staff will appear below. Click Settings, then More Tools, then Inject C#/D Box Tab. You can then save that as a PDF.

You could collect up the ABC for all the tunes you want to learn (I keep the versions I like on a word processor document), and paste them as a block into the tool. It’ll allow you to export them as a PDF tune book.
