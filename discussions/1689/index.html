Re: Irish music and the internet: a virtual community

Sorry, Lenny, just realized that I never finished the Comhaltas thought… The Comhaltas thing is that they are not really a virtual community -- they’re a meat-space community that happens to have a website to disseminate information to the members of the community. Each branch is a community within the larger global Comhaltas community and most have their own subset websites.

Virtual communities, on the other hand, are by and large completely on the Internet (and actually usually completely and only on the Web), and most if not all of the larger communications take place by computer-mediated communication (CMC) forms such as newgroups, bulletin boards (such as this one), web portals, or by e-mail, or possible combinations of the latter three (such as the Yahoo Groups).

True virtual communities depend upon a tacit (and sometimes not so tacit) agreement to behave as true commuinities. A willingness to ‘see’ a human being on the other end of the keyboards is key. (As opposed to role playing, where the personae taken on by members of the group are different from their own selves in significant ways.) When all goes well, the virtual community can become a meatspace community -- I’ve had several The Session members in my home and session, and many of us have gotten together in our meatspace communities.

In comparison, one can usually hear many stories of online communities failing upon meatspace meeting, usually because of false (whether consciously so or not) communication of self. The most obvious is of course the usual story of online romances failing upon meatspace meeting… :)

We’ve had many discussions about whether this can be considered a true community or not. Do a search for “community” and you’ll probably find a few of them, mainly in the earlier threads. Try https://thesession.org/discussions/1258 -- I think that was our most recent burp in the smooth running of the community. Ironically, these things rarely to never get spelled out in meatspace, and a meatspace session might actually be poorer for it…painful as it was.

Hmmm. So much for not getting started on this, yeah? *snort*

Re: Irish music and the internet: a virtual community

“But isn’t music supposed to be something that you just instintively do, not talk about?” Suky, I think the answer to that is no and yes -- yes, music is something that people do and that almost every people has some form of music, including music with rocks, howling at the moon and all such other “instinctive” forms of music -- but also no, in that the music we all make isn’t exactly instinctive. If that was true, everyone would be able to do it bang off.

I don’t think it’s a yes or no question, in other words…

When I teach beginners dancing, one of the first things I tell them is that Irish stepdancing is not a natural form of dancing. If it feels natural, they’re probably doing it wrong. I’ve found it necessary to remind them that they should not feel stupid or clumsy or idiotic if they can’t do it right off. No one is born knowing how to stepdance.

Same thing is a bit true of the music, I think. And for those of us far from the roots of the tradition, communities like these are godsends -- we can communicate with folks with the same problems and interests, with those who are better connected with the tradition, we can learn and teach, all of that good stuff.

Part of what one has to do in order to address your points, Suky, is to define “community” -- what is it? Is it any group of people who live together in one place only? There are many communities that don’t live together and who only came/come together infrequently and with great fuss rarely, and the Net simply makes it possible for them to communicate more quickly and in some cases, more efficiently.

What we’ve got here is a community that got too big for “normal” methods of communication -- we’re all over the globe. (The global Irish music community is still quite small, however.) It’s both brilliant and amazing that bb and Dow and Glauber and Susan and Sarah and Emily and I and hopefully more will be gathering in Montana this August. But what’s truly astonishing about it is that we’ll already have shared background and private jokes and a knowledge of each other that usually you only get in a community of people who have met in meatspace before.

Sure, CMC is not the SAME as Face to Face (FTF) communication, and there are many things that keep it from being a complete communication form, but it’s also true that FTF also has it’s own problems and is also not a complete communication form (otherwise there would be no such thing as divorces!), and there are things that CMC is better for than FTF. (One thing that convinces me that this is a viable community is that we have our differences and arguments and disagreements, just like a meatspace community.)

Also, CMC is not completely un-natural for many people any longer -- including me -- and for many growing up in an electronic age, it’s a perfectly natural form of communication and community. For many younger people, it’s only an un-natural form of communication in that you have to have a computer and connection to do it with, it’s not part of the standard equipment. The rest of it is as natural to them as being able to spell words and read words.

As a living tradition, Irish music keeps changing and assimilating (and as a traditional music form, does so slowly), but as a community, we tend to pick up whatever works when we want to, with no argument. The Web and its various communities are no different -- I still can’t believe that I regularly talk with people in such far-flung places, but kids I teach think little to nothing of it.

What a world! What fun to be alive right now!

Oh God, session etiquette. Here we go again! :)

Most Irish people think session etiquette is ridiculous. However, there’s still a session etiquette whether they realize it or not; largely, it’s the etiquette of Irish society and in fact that subset of that society that makes up the session. For us non-Irish, the session is a very strange little world and you can put your foot wrong so easily and in so many ways.

I’ve said it before: there’s an etiquette to inviting friends over for pizza (ie: you generally don’t show up in white tie, most people offer to bring beer or something else, you don’t expect to have a gift waiting for you on the table, etc.), it’s just not a very formal etiquette. There’s definitely a session etiquette and for those of us not born to the society, knowing what that etiquette is is less “natural” than for those who have grown up with it.

You can’t compare this to a meatspace session. There are different aspects of Irish music and sessions that interest different people in this community, with a common uniting factor of tune playing. Like someone on this site might be really interested in the way session etiquette works, but it’s not the sort of thing they could talk about at length at a session is it? So I think you can learn about different aspects of the music here than in meatspace. I treat this as an additional community to my actual instrument playing in sessions, not as a substitute for it.

Suky, just to clarify the quasi-etiquette of participating online, there’s nothing wrong with contributing your own two cents to a discussion here in hopes someone else will find it useful, whether or not you’re the “best fiddler in Chicago.” I’m sure some people here have an exaggerated sense of my musical abilities because I’ve posted lots of comments about fiddle technique, but I’ve always tried to couch my input as coming from a student of the music and the fiddle, not an expert. In fact, non-experts have a lot to offer to others who are still climbing the learning curve because the all-too-familiar hurdles are so familiar and fresh in their experience. Besides, I can’t recall much of anyone here claiming to be the next Michael Coleman…most of us enjoy self-deprecation too much for that. :o)

At our cabin session, Alice was saying that the web is a great equalizer, allowing everyone from rank beginners to A-tier players access to the conversation, not just as lurkers, but full-on participants. Again, it seems to me that most people participate in good faith, not over reaching their actual level of knowledge or ability. When we do over reach, there’s no shortage of (usually) friendly come-uppance from the peanut gallery, and that’s as it shoud be. If anything, such checks and balances are more freely applied here because it’s less threatening and embarrassing to call someone on the carpet online than face to face.

One last thought--I guard my playing time because I have so little of it, but I’ve learned stuff here that’s improved my playing beyond anything an extra 100 hours would’ve done--a fair degree of our musical ability comes from how we think and feel about the music, not just honing the nerve synapses through dogged repetition. This site helps me understand and enjoy the tunes I play and how other people relate to the music, and that has made me a better (but still middling :o) musician.
