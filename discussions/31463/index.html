Re: Your Dream ‘Folk Band’?

I like where you’re going with this, Fiddle Aunt. I agree with the rest that the key is good musicians, not instruments-- but really, there are some instruments that sound better together than others, especially in a session when everybody’s playing melody together. Accordion and banjo, great. Accordion and fiddle, not so great. Banjo and harp, not so great. To my ears, instruments that produce expressive, sustained notes sound best paired with those that produce short, percussive notes. That way the individual voice and personality of the instruments stand out. The same effect comes through when some players provide chords or a drone while others play melody. And one (one!) good bodhran player fits in just about anywhere.

So how about fiddle, banjo, guitar (or piano or bouzouki), and bodhran?

Re: Your Dream ‘Folk Band’?

When you stop to think about it, two of the “big bands” which came out at the “revival” period, The Chieftans and The Bothy Band, were based around a melodic core of pipes, fiddle, and flute. To me the traditional music is most fully realised with that core, representing the older tradition (pre- free-reed, pre-fretted strings).

Both groups added string accompaniment.

Not that any accompaniment is necessary: I have albums with that core trio (pipes/fiddle/flute) which have no other instruments and they’re easily as good as albums that include accompaniment.

There is a driving sound that The Bothy Band got with their pounding guitar and keyboard that no other group has ever quite matched. So perhaps something that like would be my “ideal traditional band”. Of course it’s the quality of the melody players that’s paramount, I agree with that!

The newer instruments in the tradition, the box and the banjo, brought with them greater range than the flute/pipes and also greater chromatic facility. I must say that much of the box/banjo repertoire, tunes with massive range and with accidentals all over the place, doesn’t do much for me. Syncopated twanging of B flat down on the G string sure sounds cool the first time you hear it but in the main I prefer the older tunes.

Of course the fiddle has always had the same potential but in the traditional core trio (pipes/fiddle/flute) the fiddle was outvoted so to speak and the more limited gamut of the pipes and flute tended to hold sway.

So anyhow my “ideal traditional band” would have a very strong core of pipes, fiddle, and flute and add to that a great guitar player. Perhaps a keyboard or electric bass could be added. The group Vent du Nord shows how an electric bass can add a lot to an otherwise acoustic band, and not sound intrusive.
