Session Demise Theory #2 - Hierarchy vs. Democracy

Have I waited long enough to introduce controversy? My first topic had to do with large rooms and overly large sessions. This is somewhat related.

How do you find that hierarchy and democracy play out in your sessions? In most situations, there may be a designated leader. People will give way physically and musically to that leader. Is it fair game then for anyone to sit anywhere? First come first serve on seats? Or is there a relative pecking order?

An example: a few years ago, we had a number of new musicians join our regular session, two of which were youngsters. (We were delighted of course to get fresh blood.) The new crew would get there early so as to get “prime” seating, and then the rest of the musicians would fill in around them. So, what we ended up with was a big hole in the session and this did not help the continuity, rhythm or overall quality of the session (not to mention learning experience for the new-comers).

I guess I am not talking about the music here, but more the physical arrangement of the session. Will people get up and move of their own accord? Will they move their chairs to make room? Will everyone demand a seat at “the table”, or will people back off to let others sit closer together and lead the session?

Most sessions in London have paid anchor musicians. From the publican’s point of view, this provides a guarantee of live music on a particular night, so that he/she can advertise in an draw in punters. There are several theories about when and where ‘sessions as we know them’ started. Hammy Hamilton, who did his Ph.D. dissertation on this, narrows it down to either London/Liverpool during or shortly after WWII, or in the States during the 1920s and 1930s, as immigrants met in pubs over some tunes and pints for a taste of the old country. Can’t we then presume that sessions are something of an international phenomena, not strictly Irish (certainly not nowadays), and that different people bring different ideas about how sessions work, and that this continues to evolve?

If you buy into this premise, then it’s easier to understand why Jack’s longed-for common sense isn’t so common--people come to an unfamiliar session with different preconceptions about what’s acceptable behavior. There may be several time-worn traditions of sessioning, but no universal code of session behavior, and no one, not even the Irish in Ireland, have a monopoly on what a session “should be.”

In a small room at the back of a hotel, away from the two bars where most people look to find the music, the music is thumping. The only incongruity is a young, rather tipsy American lady who had bought a bodhran earlier in her holiday, and decided that she had found the ideal place to hone her skills.
The way she was dealt with a joy to watch. One of the gentlemen at the table got her to sit next to him, rather than have her behind him banging in his ear. He then said she should try it like this - and took her drum from her and showed her a simple beat and told her to keep it nice and quiet and listen so she could follow the music. She was visibly thrilled to bits at being asked to sit down and at being given this little ‘lesson’, and was quite well behaved for about quarter of an hour, during which the music was great, but of course then she became a bit overconfident and took to thrashing the thing again. The man took her drum off her again to ‘show her how it should be done’, whilst another started asking her leading questions about where she was staying, and wasn’t she having to get up in the morning? When it became obvious that she was oblivious to all subtlety, he handed the drum to a friend behind him, saying, “Thank you for sharing your music with us, Sean will put it on a shelf where no more harm can can come of it this evening.” at which everyone in the room applauded. The young lady took this as the greatest compliment, and sat there for the next couple of hours glowing with pride and happy as Larry at having ‘played’ in a real session, and at being in the thick of it, and the music carried on and a great night was had by all.
When I think back to that weekend. That incident sticks in my mind more than any other - the night would not have been as much fun without her intervention …
I know that you might say that it’s all very well dealing with a young woman like that but what if …..
