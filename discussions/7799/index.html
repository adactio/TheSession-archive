Re: Djembe Patterns for Celtic Music

*Any* suggestions? Careful what you wish for.

you aren’t going to get much joy here, the djembe isn’t usually very welcome at traditional irish music sessions, and the way of things round here is to heap scorn on djembe or shakey egg or didge players (as with all individuals and communities, we define ourselves in part by our relationship to what we aren’t, a sort of inverse identity delimited by the Other. And the djembe is definitely Other). But if you’re really interested in the djembe AND irish music, and want to do a serious study of rhythmic patterns, listen to lots and lots and lots of Irish music, and what bodhran players get up to (prepare to be disappointed, however, there’s not much in the way of rhythmic acrobatics to be had), then check out
the collection of African rhythms assembled by Dr Tracy, who went walkabout round the African continent early in the 20th, recording tribal music. That collection is administered by his son, Dr Andrew Tracy, and grandsons, at Rhodes University in Grahamstown in the Eastern Cape, South Africa.

I think you may have to register for a degree in ethomusicology at the University to gain acces to the collection, however. But I’m sure you’ll enjoy it immensely, the weather’s great down there! Good Luck!

Re: Djembe Patterns for Celtic Music

Before taking up ITM I studied Middle Eastern percussion on a wide variety of drums. Now I’ve played ITM for around 20 years and if I chose to I could provide competent percussion on any of the drums I’m familiar with that would fit rhythmically with the tunes. Because of the understanding of ITM I’ve gained over the years -- I don’t. Sometimes I’ll play the bodhran like a tar for the odd Breton tune, or if it seems appropriate on a sexy slow reel, but otherwise I don’t think it fits in a session environment.

Having said that, I have entertained the idea of arranging some music using Middle Eastern patterns and slightly altering the form of some tunes to fit -- but that would definitely be a side project. If I’m going to a session I leave the drums at home.
