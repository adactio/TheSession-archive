How Old Are We All

am interested in seeing what age groups we have interested in traditional music. I’m 16 and my farther was Irish. When I was young he would play old records of Irish music to me which i didn’t really find that interesting. However when i reached about 13 I fount one record that really blew me away and the sounds of the banjo and the fiddle just amazed me. When i was 14 i bought my dad a Dubliners album. I didn’t know who the hell the Dubliners were back then just that my uncle Jim really liked them. However even the album was a birthday present for my dad i listened to it a few times and eventually it became mine. Since then I have collected zillions of folk and Irish music cd’s.

I then got a tenor banjo and have been playing for some time now. I feel i have made a huge progress since i got my banjo but have never had the opportunity to meet other young Irish musicians like myself. It wasn’t until recently after all this time of playing I learnt there was an Irish music centre in Nottingham that did sessions.

Any ways what my question is how old are you? I am interested to see what average age the people are who visit sites like this. i think this would be interesting because where I live in long Eaton near Nottingham there seems to no young Irish musicians who could possibly start a band with me. And if there are young musicians who visit this site wherever in the world you mite be it would boost my confidence to know there are other young musicians out there.

I don’t intend to be rude by asking what age you are but if you can post your age (whatever it is) and maybe what Irish music interests you have that would be great!

Im 16 and play the tenor banjo, guitar and tin whistle and recently I bought a bodrhan. How old are you?

Hi S. McMullen,

I’m 52, and got my love of traditional music from my Scots/Irish grandfather who played the fiddle. I am also an orchestra teacher in the public schools, and after becoming more and more appalled at the music the my students were listening to I decided to see if I could sway them a bit , and introduced some Irish reels and jigs in class. They loved them! We took some funds from our account and bought some mandolins, beginning a whole new curriculum. We are required to participate in a state contest at least once a year; instead of tired old bad arrangments of classical music we did a set of Irish tunes. The judge had such a quizzical look on his face when we ended that I thought we were doomed--turned out he loved it and called it a “fresh new approach…” odd, considering how long the tunes have been around.
Anyway. This was too much of an answer to your question! My students range in age from 11-18. Keep playing--the music must live on.

S.McMullen, I enjoyed reading about your introduction to Irish traditional music. Both my parents had lots of folk albums: Joan Baez, Judy Collins, Pete Seeger and the like. But there are two that stand out in particular for me. One was a history of the 1916 Easter Uprising and its aftermath, told largely through songs and poetry, featuring the Clancy Brothers and Tommy Makem. The other was an album of Irish traditional music and song that was part of a series on “folk and primitive” music distributed by Columbia Records in the 1950s. The Irish entry was a Seamus Ennis production: Besides playing on several tracks himself, he went around recording musicians and singers, including Joe Heaney and Margaret Barry.

There were numerous other influences and inspirations for me, but I just remember those two as being foundational for my interest in Irish traditional music. And in fact, I was about your age when I really started exploring it in earnest.

I don’t want to make you envious, S.McMullen, but there are a ton of teens and young people in my area (Boston) who play traditional music. In fact, I’m lucky enough to be playing with a few of them in a band! Unfortunately, one’s off to college in Chicago, and another to New York!

Re: How Old Are We All

A few of us, ahem, older people have remarked on feeling younger than they are, chronologically. I suppose that holds true for me, since I often play with people who are anywhere from 15 to 30 years younger than me, and it does rub off.
One of the things that I’ve found interesting, and occasionally amusing, is the generational differences that surface every now and then, in conversation or elsewhere, during such get-togethers. For instance, in _my_ day (sorry, had to say it), most of the 20-somethings who played Irish trad music were scruffy, bearded and long-haired -- all right, the _women_ didn’t have beards, but you know what I mean. Nowadays, all the 20-something guys I see are clean-shaven and wear their hair short back and sides. OK, to be honest, I wear _my_ hair significantly shorter than I used to, too.
I also find that many -- though not all -- of the players younger than me have a lifeline connected to their cellphones. The tune set is over, and half the musicians whip out their cells in almost perfect unison to see who called. I know this sounds like the set-up for a complaint about social graces, but I really am not intending it to be. It’s really just an observation: In this wireless age, a lot of people simply seem compelled to stay “on call” -- there’s a whole sociological study in there somewhere, I’m sure.
