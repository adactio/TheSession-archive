the Mighty Craic -- why or why not?

I’m heading to Wyoming in the morning to see Zina, Will, Richard, Aimee, Eliot, Doug & a bunch of other musos for an Irish Rendezvous Weekend! (All of whom are better players than me, I might add.) So I probably will not be checking in til next Tuesday at the earliest, as if anyone cares. But in the meantime, I wanted to jumpstart a chance for peeps to talk about this project, The Mighty Craic, & why they feel they can’t or won’t submit a track, or why they will.

For anyone who is just tuning in, The Mighty Craic is a proposed CD project where members of this site submit recordings of themselves playing or singing pretty much whatever they feel comfortable with. It can be a recording of pretty much any type -- audio cassette, MP3, mini-disc, whatever, studio produced, live session tape, you playing in your bathroom, whatever. The deadline has been extended to April 30, just over 2 weeks from today, for anyone who is interested to send their sample to Max aka fiddlecrazy in California (along with some other stuff, see this link
https://thesession.org/discussions/3320 for more details).

So what does it mean to record ourselves & submit our playing to the scathing opinions of our peers, providing a permanent record of our inadequacies &/or triumphs? Do we record ourselves out of show-offy pride, or more for the sake of setting an example of transmitting the music, no matter how amateur we are, for the sake of the music itself? Do we decline to be recorded b/c of fear -- fear of how we might sound to ourselves, or to others? Or to dodge a challenge, or simply out of laziness? Would submitting a track pose just simply too great a challenge in terms of an accumulation of factors, including ego, even though it might persuade others to take the same risk some day? What if the recording artists we love never took that first step into the studio?

For myself, I realized going solo for the Mighty Craic was simply out of the question at this point in my playing. I was shocked at how long 4-5 minutes became without flubbing a very worn-in tune. I hope that Will & Zina might accompany me on a track over the weekend, or some others, or some other combination I haven’t thought of, but at least I realized what my personal limits are with this sort of project, & it isn’t to abstain completely, b/c this site means a lot to me. Or more specifically, the people on this site mean a lot to me, & even if I think my playing sucks, I think participating builds camaraderie, & more importantly, thick skin!

What are your personal goals, limits, objections, observations, etc, with a project like this, & why?

Re: the Mighty Craic -- why or why not?

Yup. Recording yourself is like looking at a very cold sea and thinking about diving in - it’s a hell of a lot easier if other people do it at the same time.
I took the plunge this week with a cheapo ($4) microphone in the room under my house. Listening to the recording is a very sobering experience - especially since I was being drowned out by the Beach Boys… turns out my cheap microphone picks up a local radio station better than it picks up my playing.
Managed to shield out the radio signals with a bit of deftly applied aluminium foil - which only shows up the faults in my technique. and the fact I appear to be playing inside a tin can.
Ho hum, lots more practice required. But it has been a very positive experience. You really need to listen to a recording to hear what your playing sounds like to others (a bit like the way you never really hear your own voice). I’ve already picked up several notes I’m dropping - they were right there on the ground in front of me, and found which particular ornaments sound better in which reels…
Thank goodness the dibs list is shot to hell - I can play what I like now!

Anyone concerned about the quality of their playing or the quality of the recording is missing the point. It’s not about *you* (or me). It’s about us and the music.

Following is all the technical advice you’ll need for this project..The technical aspects of minidisc or PC recording shouldn’t be stopping anyone from participating.

Instructions:

Push an old re-recorded (many times over) cassette in the slot of any 20 year old, beat up boombox or cassette recorder.

Push the record button.

Play a tune.

Take the cassette out of the slot.

Put it in a mailer. Address the mailer.

Put a stamp on it.

Put it in the mailbox (or take it to the post office).
