Midnight on the water but Smoke in the session

I know that this has been discussed before and the subject will again cause controversy----

Last night, I returned home from a session with sore eyes and smelly clothes due to the excessive smoke permeating in the bar where we were having our tunes. It wasn’t the musicians --They were too busy playing and, in any case, they tended to move away for a puff. I’ll not name the bar because it’s quite nice really and no better or worse than any other as far as this problem is concerned. This happens all the time and wherever I go. If there’s one thing that irritates me and likely to stop me attending sessions (It won’t though!! ) is the smokey atmosphere. It’s worse than 20 bodhrans, 19 spoon players, 18 guitarists playing the wrong chords, 17 sets of warhorse tunes, sixteen singers and so on.

I’m not necessarily advocating a smoking ban in public houses/places, although you can argue for it if you wish. It’s more why we volunteer to submit ourselves to congregating in such conditions. Perhaps, we should actively seek out smoke free environments to play our music? Then, again, maybe it’s only these smokey places that will take us? I know there’s lots of musos who smoke too and, perhaps, it would spoil the “craic” if they weren’t able to smoke at the table. What do you think?

As I said, this isn’t going to stop me attending sessions although it probably deters others. I’ll just put up with it as I’ve always done. I’ve always been a non smoker but smoke in pubs has only bothered me in recent years. This is probably because it has been successfully restricted elsewhere and I just become more aware of it when I visit my *many* locals. :>))

John
