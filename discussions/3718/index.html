What connotates “pure drop” in ITM, and where do you draw the line?

Our set dance CD was reviewed recently and the reviewer became pedantic and lectured us that the music on our CD wasn’t “pure drop” because we had guitar accompaniment. I was just looking in the comments section for tunes, and in the one about “The Stoney Steps” hornpipe, Jonothan says, “Eamonn Cotter’s wonderful pure drop recording ‘Traditional Music from County Clare’” I have that recording (it’s great!) and there’s both guitar and piano back-up on it, so obviously the fellow that reviewed our CD would disagree.

I have followed some discussions on other sites about this, and it seems like the folks that fall into the sans back-up camp think ITM should be frozen in place somewhere shortly after the turn of the 20th Century. But why draw the line there? Why not draw it at an earlier part of ITM history when ITM was primarily something you would only hear on one instrument? Furthermore, why exclude the guitar and not the bodhran? And if we want to go further down this road, what about the piano or even relatively recent ITM arrivals like the banjo, concertina, accordion, etc.?

Thoughts?

Re: What connotates “pure drop” in ITM, and where do you draw the line?

Because it doesn’t always. It’s kind of like, well, when you think of someone older, don’t you almost always think of someone older than yourself, no matter what age you are?

“Pure drop” is one of those terms that’s rather like “traditional” -- it means what you want it to mean, basically, within a particular range of definitions.

To one person, “pure drop” is going to mean an unaccompanied set of pipes or a fiddle, and that’s about it. For another, “pure drop” means that the music is infused with the spirit of the thing, whether there’s accompaniment, a studio orchestra, and a didgeridoo ont he disc or not. And for yet another, “pure drop” means that it’s just purer than they play the stuff. And of course everything in between.

As a reviewer myself, I’m always interested in how the things are written. But I don’t take them very seriously, they’re just one person’s opinion, after all. Sort of like, well, everything else, really.
