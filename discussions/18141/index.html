Ben, the lack of dirt in my clips is purely because I was “trying” too hard (as wolfbird so astutely pointed out in another thread). And it’s the one thing I really don’t like about my clips. But then I had sat myself down for the sole purpose of “proving it,” so it’s no wonder I wasn’t as free and easy as usual.

I too like a bit of dirt in Irish fiddle. My session playing certainly reflects that--I think it adds a lot to the overall sound. But as a teacher, I’ve also found it helpful to learners if I can play as cleanly as possible, so they can hear more clearly all the tiny twiddly bits (a student of mine recently started calling them the “diddley squats” LOL).

Two things spring to mind. First, with just a few clips, I think the contrast between my playing and Ben’s is wonderful, well showing the range of personal and stylistic approaches to this music. Second, I’m guessing that if Ben and I sat down over pints and started playing tunes, we’d quickly and easily find ways to blend and meld, and it would be great craic. Which is what this is all about, eh?

Bliss, I was surprised at how easy it was, even for an old technophobe like me. Go to the sound lantern home site and click on “sign up.” They lead you through a simple registration process. Then you can upload any soundclip you have on your computer.

So the trick for me was figuring out how to record onto my computer. I used the internal mic and recorded straight into a program called (ironically enough) Audacity (which is free, available online). I didn’t mess with any of the editing tools, just took the straight recording and saved it as a WAV file. Then uploaded it on Sound Lantern.

I’d love to have some drum tracks to play along with! Give us reels, jigs, and slip jigs, eh?!
