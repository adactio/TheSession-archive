Re: Tin Whistles for Inhalers vs. Exhalers

I couldn’t read all of it but it may be something to do with the variations in the mysterious ‘breath support’.

If you take a full breath, block off your throat and relax all the breathing muscles you feel pressure. That’s the elastic recoil of your lungs and other tissue that are stretched on a full breath. Hold the breath without blocking of your throat and you are using the muscles you just used to inhalee and as you breath out you are slacking them off to control the airflow. The exhalation muscles can also work against them for fine control but you don’t actually need them to exhale until you get towards the end of the exhalation and have run out of elasticity - we then have less fine control. Blow up a party balloon from a full breath and relaxing the muscles won’t get you far. You need the exhalation muscles to push air out even at the top of the breath.

There are several academic papers involving flute players kitted up with electrodes and things like motion capture suits on their torsos, the above is my understanding of the gist of it. Others may read them differently.

So there are different sorts of breathing and maybe the size of the windway shades between them.

Re: Tin Whistles for Inhalers vs. Exhalers

About all this diaphragm talk, it’s interesting what Nancy Toff has to say:

"When teachers speak of the diaphragm they are generally referring to the abdominal muscles.

The diaphragm cannot push air out of the lungs. Its role is inhalation, to contract downward to create a partial vacuum in the lungs. In exhalation it does the opposite: it relaxes upward towards its starting position.

Thus the diaphragm cannot push the air out of the lungs; the abdominal muscles do.

Nor can the diaphragm support the air column, that being the job of the abdominal and intercostal muscles."

I’m guessing somebody has done MRI imaging or something to see all this stuff, not that it matters in practice.

Re: Tin Whistles for Inhalers vs. Exhalers

It’s worth bearing in mind that individual muscles can only contract or relax - they can’t ‘push’ by lengthening. So everybody’s breathing action is generated by the same set of contractions and relaxations of the diaphragm, ribcage, abdominals, etc. There aren’t two ways to do it, because muscles don’t work like that - you can’t choose whether to put effort in on the ‘push’ or the ‘pull’.

Re: Tin Whistles for Inhalers vs. Exhalers

I’ve never understood why people seem to overcomplicate something as simple as breathing. They do this for voice, woodwinds, brass, etc. Breathing really is not that complicated. Take deep breaths. If you find yourself running out of breath, take deeper breaths, or breathe more often - but in general, you’re not going to run out of breath while playing whistle (unlike flute), as long as you take breaths at the most obvious spots in the tune.

My whole life, I’ve listened to voice teachers and music teachers talk about breath as if it’s some kind of magic thing, where if you learn to breathe properly you’ll suddenly sing better or play better. That just isn’t true, in my experience. As long as you’re taking deep breaths, you can forget about breathing and focus on other things that are actually important, like making sure that your throat is relaxed (for voice) or that your fingers are relaxed/agile (for whistle). Focusing on breath too much is a red herring that will distract from things that actually improve your musicianship.

Leaving aside the whole inhaling/exhaling/moon thing, I think that what this author is TRYING to say is that some people prefer soft-blowing recorders, while others prefer hard-blowing recorders. That’s true enough, but that’s a matter of preference, and it has nothing to do with some fundamental way you breathe. Hard blowers have smaller windways and take more force to push the air out. Soft blowers have larger windways and take less force (but sometimes more air) to make a sound. If you’re fairly new to whistle, it’s a good idea to try out some hard-blowers and soft-blowers and find out what you like best.
