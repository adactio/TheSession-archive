16in Viola as Octave Fiddle

It seems like such a no brainer that this would be the way to go. An octave fiddle being tuned a fourth lower than a Viola, would need an instrument at least as large to do it justice. But nobody seems (either now or in the past) to have made strings to suit. They are either octave fiddle strings that are too short, or octave viola strings that turn it into a cello. I don’t want a chin cello, I want a baritone fiddle that sounds like a proper instrument.

I’m now thinking that the best option would be to get the heaviest viola strings I can find, and just tune them down a fourth. Does anyone here have any idea what those would be? I know there are several heavy options, (especially by D’Addario), but which of those would give the most tension?
Or maybe someone has a better solution?

Re: 16in Viola as Octave Fiddle

similar discussion often happens on mandolin cafe, we can use basically guitar strings of different guages and guitar string tension calculators to figure this out.

consider this sequence:
fiddle/violin - GDAE
viola, add low C, remove high E - CGDA
repeat, add low F, remove high A - FCGD
octave violin, tune up to higher pitch (F to G) - GDAE

observe:
if you started from violin, you reused the violin low G and D string.
if you started from viola, you reused the low CGD strings, but had to tune them up.

i know nothing about this, but if a standard viola is strong enough to support “light viola CGD(A)” strings tuned up to “(?)DAE”, you are 3/4 of the way to octave violin and only the low G string needs to be figured out.

Re: 16in Viola as Octave Fiddle

You could try a set of 1/4 size cello strings. A 1/4 cello has a string length of about 21“, your 16” viola is probably about 14.5 - reducing the length by about 1/3 will raise the pitch by about a fifth, which is what you are looking for. But the end result will still be a compromise, the body size is still too small to produce fundamentals down to G2 (an octave below violin). What you really need is a Cello de Spalla. (If you just ignore the C string the other four give you the octave violin you are looking for.)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sTQ9Nl2Jb7k

Re: 16in Viola as Octave Fiddle

btw, op is looking for low GDAE strings for a VIOLA body. octave violin and octave viola is bog standard stuff, here is the full line up from high voice to low voice:

high BfFCG or high CGDA -> piccolo violin
GDAE strings + violin body -> violin
CGDA strings + viola body -> viola
low GDAE strings + violin body -> octave violin
low GDAE strings + viola body -> what OP wants
low CGDA strings + viola body -> octave viola, chin cello
CGDA cello

P.S. piccolo mandolins are as rare as piccolo violins.

Re: 16in Viola as Octave Fiddle

Update: I have tried tuning the current strings (Dominant Mediums) a fourth down to GDAE but it was far too loose and buzzy and sounded pretty horrible. Then I tried a minor third to AEBF# and it sounds pretty good. It reminds me of the sound of a fiddle tuned down to match the tuning of a C flute. Sort of soft and mellow, but still with enough tension to maintain its clarity. Internet searching has revealed that one of the string sets with the highest tension is Thomastik 79 Precision. Their tension is a:8.1, d:7.4, g:7.o, c:6.8kg compared to the Dominant’s a:6.0, d:5.0, g:4.8, c:4.9. Hopefully this extra tension will allow me to tune down the extra 2 semitones. I’ll see how they go.

Re: 16in Viola as Octave Fiddle

Wish I’d seen this discussion earlier!
@Philfy, how did your experiment go?
I understand you want an Octave ‘VIOLIN’, not an Octave VIOLA - so you need to be able to at least reach down to a G2.
Just want to say: specialized bowed strings are pricey.
The good news, I know Thomastik makes an ‘F2’ for VIOLIN, but the bad news - not one for VIOLA. An option might be a smaller scale Viola, with good bass bar & deep body, then use the F2 VIOLIN string. 🤔 …or if you have luthier skills, maybe shorten the neck of a VIOLA. Don’t laugh, I believe Hardangar fiddles have shorter necks than standard violins.

Aquila brand Violoncello da Spalla strings (https://aquilacorde.com/en/early-music-strings/cello-da-spalla/) - note: they do say, “ if [their] strings are too long, it’s possible to cut them at the proper length”. At least they make some synthetic core strings - still pricey!
I’m not sure if there are any possible strings used for something in maybe the Viol family that could work - Baroque instruments usually use gut.
The only other thing I thought of was to use fractional size cello strings on VIOLA. Larsen makes fractional cello strings down to 1/8 size (https://larsenstrings.us/larsen-fractional/) - still pricey! I found this string length chart (https://woodsoundstudio.com/images/setup-charts/cello&bass-sizes.pdf), but it’s not a chart from Larsen.

Mikael Marin (Väsen) had a huge influence on me. I saw he would tune his strings down lower, he also used octave strings, including octave VIOLA strings (when they were still available) - and, he plays a violoncello da spalla under his chin!
Unfortunately, by the time I bought my 5-string VIOLA (16") the octave strings were no longer made!
Not putting octave strings on my acoustic/electric 5-string VIOLIN, because: I don’t feel the body is meant to resonate those lower frequencies, plus I’d definitely have to modify my bridge & nut notches (possibly the pegs, too). I could’ve chosen to only play plugged into an amp, with the sound modified, but I like playing acoustically too much - in any case I figured I’d be better off just using an octave pedal.
THEN… I saw Jesus Florido playing the 1st Glasser 6-string VIOLIN (he taught a workshop at Fiddle Hell)! I’ve had my 6-string VIOLIN over a year & I LOVE IT! It’s actually easier to play than my 5-string VIOLIN & made for that low F2 string to sound good! The low F2 string means I do have an ‘Octave Fiddle’ now. So, it’s still above the Cello C2, but I can comfortably tune it down a couple steps if I need to for something special.
The bad news… the Thomastik Vision VIOLIN F string is pricey (so is the C string) - but I think they’re worth it & last a long time!

I’d still like to try to find an F2 string (or close) that will work on my 5-string VIOLA (I don’t mind loosing the E string)… just been sidetracked, enjoying my 6-string VIOLIN too much right now.

Made an account just to post in this thread! I had the same idea you did and I ended up finding a French company called Atelier Boussoir who sell string sets for this “tenor viola” instrument. I’ve got them installed on a 16.5" viola and I love the sound, although I really struggle to tune (I had to remove my fine tuners as the strings don’t have ball ends) and I suspect the tension is a little more than ideal, although not by much. I’m very new to violin family instruments - this is my first - so my complaints might just be skill issues, but at the least it seems like there’s one company who sell a set of strings for this purpose.

I’m constantly on the lookout for a ball-end, mass produced cello string I could use for the low G which would have lower tension that the current string (which is about 6kg). Just using maths it seems like the closest would be the Larsen Aurora 1/16 D string, but that would produce a tension of about 4kg on a 410mm scale length Viola so it might not be tense enough. Maybe one day I’ll buy one and try it.
