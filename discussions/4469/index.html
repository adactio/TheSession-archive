I’ll just say that I for one am learning a lot from this website about people’s reactions to irony in general. I used to think, for example, that Americans tend not to use it and therefore misunderstand it. However, I now think that it’s not so much a cultural thing as an individual thing. Doesn’t matter where you are in the world, some people make their points and understand other people in a round-about way using irony, hyperboles, metaphors, satire etc, and some people communicate on a more straight-forward level and take other people’s comments at face value. If we assume that Ottery is the first type and that you, the reader, are the second type, then you can try to translate it into your straight-forward language so that it becomes less offensive to you, rather like taking the swearing and violence out of the lyrics to a gangsta rap.

So in this case you can read it as “What is your opinion about the learning of ornamentation by numbers, as with Grey Larsen’s notation method?” As it happens, if we were right in assuming that Ottery is a type 1 (notice that I’m not taking sides here), I can tell you that there is also another level of meaning behind his first post, which is just him making fun of people (on this website) who actually *do* or *might consider* criticize people who overanalyse theory. This is what we call “satire”. Am I being overanylitical or am I just being sarcastic, or am I being ironic or just condescending?

Oh never mind :-)

Here’s my opinion. I’ve read about GL’s notation method and I think it’s very logical and clever. Some people like to analyse stuff. Ornamentation in Irish music is complex and for someone who likes to analyse it’s ideal. Everything in the world gets analysed in depth by someone. Think of any topic and most likely someone somewhere will have written a PhD on it. Irish music needn’t be an exception to this. Musicologists analyse classical music, and if you’re interested in music theory, their writings make fascinating reading. So why not do the same for Irish music? Just because of the traditional way it’s passed on doesn’t mean it has to be enveloped in mystery. If someone wants to write a book on ornamentation, great, fab. If you don’t like that, then don’t read it.
