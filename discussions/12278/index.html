Plonkerometer

A question for the techies. Could someone go and invent a plonker detector, like those stud detectors that chippies use to locate studs in plasterboard:

http://www.screwfix.com/app/sfd/cat/pro.jsp?id=57327

Some here might agree there’s nothing worse than opening the door of the session pub to find it jam-packed with eggs and spoons and rainsticks etc. So with this device, you could stand outside a session pub and if it gave a positive reading you wouldn’t bother going in. Problem solved.

This could be based on sonic profiling, detecting bum notes, out of tune instruments, tunes played too fast or too slow, or with bad tempo, or it could have a database to recognise donkey tunes.

This controversial device maybe or may not be popular with members here. But the sooner it gets invented the better, I say. Let’s face it you can’t halt the march of progress.

Ron, what I mean is that having a guitar playing during tunes imposes a regularisation to the music which, in my humble opinion, castrates it. Ludicrous “clever” chord progressions, inversions and voicings, and funky syncopations, all designed to demonstrate the guitarists‘ cool virtuosity, have nothing to do with the feel of these tunes. But deeper than that is the fact that the edge, ie., the subtle variations in tempo from tune to tune, and within tunes sometimes, and the infinitely variable slightly off-key grace notes, which to gether with the rhythm of the tunes, are the lifeblood of them, is completely ironed out by the simple fact of acompaniment. I suppose the nearest example I know is the difference between the Chieftains’ first 3 albums and the fourth, when they signed to Island and turned into a bland band.
Hope that makes some sort of sense.

Max
