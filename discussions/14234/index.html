Re: Name that Tune!

If two people in a pub session in England or the USA, where the language commonly used is English, suddenly started talking to each other in Welsh (for example) then what would you think? The clear inference would be that they were having a private conversation and what they were saying wasn’t intended for the ears of anyone else.

Here, discussion threads are often analogues (albeit imperfect ones) of conversations in real-life sessions. It is a fact that the common language of this site is English (as it is indeed throughout most of the Web), so it is reasonable to suppose that if an individual deliberately posts here in a language other than English without indicating (in English) what they are talking about, when they could indeed have used English, then the use of that other language can be seen as excluding all those members who do not have that language. That in itself could be understood by some as a form of arrogance. Are they having a private conversation - in which case email or a website devoted exclusively to that language would seem to be more appropriate – or are they winding people up?

One obvious and important consequence of posting here in an untranslated non-English language is that some useful replies and information will not be posted because the language that has been used is not understood by the general readership.

So does the use of a non-English language in a post without some sort of translation into the common language of the discussion achieve any useful purpose? I do not think so.

Irish is an important language in the study of Irish traditional music and culture, and I like to see it used here - but with some indication of its meaning. The membership of this website is well in excess of 30,000; many have English as their native language; others have some English, it is not be their native language, but they still post here. It is not reasonable to expect everyone to acquire Irish. Many will have neither the time or opportunity to learn a language that, for most, is difficult and has a structure far removed from their own language.

Some years ago, an Italian member posted a question here in Italian. That member evidently did not feel able to post in English. Another member replied in Italian but also outlined in English for the benefit of the readship the content of the initial post and the reply. That was an excellent example of good manners.
