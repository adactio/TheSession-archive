Added by Kilcash on April 26th, 2005

Why do you waste your time playing music??!!!

I’m in a philosophical mood today! Why do you play music? I only took up playing an instrument about 3 years ago but now I would feel ill at ease if I don’t have my daily fix of playing a few tunes. Sometimes I wonder though, what do I have to show for it … after the last bar has been played and the final notes have evaporated into the silence that must follow…..
I like a bit of woodwork as a hobby as well, making things with my hands and there is always something solid or tangible to hold or touch or look at or give away after the few hours of shaping. But what about making music, there is nothing to show for it except the memory………. I suppose the music is a language by which to share a few thoughts with others of like mind but what happens to all those wild tunes and haunting melodies when a musician dies, where do they go? What is there left except maybe a few memories and a CD or two these days. Above all, why do you waste your time playing a few tunes!!!!

↳ Posted by fer on April 26th, 2005.

Re: Why do you waste your time playing music??!!!

It’s *not* just the memories. It’s the sense of accomplishment that you carry with you long after you’ve put the instrument down.

The same question could be asked about many art forms, and even sports. Once you’ve run the marathon, and healed, what do you have left but the memories? Once a stage actor’s performance is done, what’s left but the memories? Once you’ve read a good book, all you have is the memories.

And yet, for some unexplicable reason, millions of people continue these pursuits year after year. Just because you don’t have a physical remnant of your labors does not imply that there is no meaning in the effort. What it does for your mental state, your attitudes, your outlook on life are much more important than some piece of sanded wood you have lying about.

Think about it. If it were just the end result that was important, you could just go out and buy that bookself instead of making it yourself. But its much more than that, of course. Its the sense of *accomplishment* that you get for having created it yourself.

Same for music. :-)

I’ve wanted to play, and have played, since I was ten or twelve years old, and the feeling that I wanted then, the things I wanted to experience from the music (mostly emotional, it seems) I now have whenever I play.

There are those who still think that I am “wasting my time” by playing, but I think that what they mean is that I am wasting -their- time! <GGG>

I’ve always wanted to play as a vocation, and I have, through a lot, but not all, of my life. When I wasn’t playing for a living, the music got a lot more important for me personally, but I haven’t had the experience of tiring of music when I was playing professionally. Most of the time when I wasn’t playing professionally I was in the recording business, so I wasn’t very far from ‘the life’.

If I had to work at a job that had nothing to do with music, I think I’d really need to play, as often as possible.

I enjoy the experience of community in playing ITM. Playing by myself has one set of rewards, but playing with others is a big thrill for me. Traveling and playing with folks I don’t know (or don’t know well) or with those whom I don’t often get to play is even bigger. Playing ITM is the most fun by far of music I’ve played, and the nature of the tradition and the communities is wonderful. There are some nice similarities with the American blues communities.

I don’t think much about what I can take away from it, I just enjoy being -in- it.

Re: Why do you waste your time playing music??!!!

“What is there left except maybe a few memories . . .”

Let us test what value memories might have by entirely absolving you of yours, shall we?

“. . .why do you waste your time playing a few tunes!”

On the one hand, who says it’s wasting my time? The very question presumes a value system which I do not share. My own instinctive reaction to your query is nearly identical to Ottery’s response.

And indeed, what an absurd way of looking at life.

Because, on the other hand. . . life is little more than a process of wasting time until you die. The acquisition of another birdhouse is really no more important than the ‘acquisition’ of another tune.

So you might as well waste your time well.

The very word you yourself choose to use, “hobby”, denegrates your own productive activities. It is a vile word. It implies that your productive activities are merely a *waste of time* which you engage in ‘merely’ for your own amusement while you are not being ‘productive.’ “Productive” has come to mean “paid to do.” Thus people who are paid to destroy are being ‘productive’ and those that are produce for their own amusment are ‘wasting time.’

The birds may view it differently.

If I play fiddle for the Blackbear Jamboree I shall be deemed ‘productive.’ If I play ‘merely’ for the enjoyment, well being and love for myself and my family I am ‘wasting time.’

I too work with wood. I’ve already posted that I spin, weave, knit, make flutes and other instruments. I like making things. I like being surrounded by the things I’ve made. I do not consider this a “hobby.” I consider it “taking care of myself.” Producing by my own hands that which I need and/or desire.

This includes my music.

But it isn’t simply the end product that I value. I value the making, in and of itself. The doing can be as, or more, valuable than the having done.

However, having done making music, you are right, all I am left with is the memory and the following silence.

How fortunate for me then that the only thing more upliftingly glorious than music is. . .silence.
