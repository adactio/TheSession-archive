This can be true but this *shouldn’t* be a problem with those who are in the habit of playing together on a regular basis.

I’m not suggesting that there’s anything wrong with a close knit arrangement between “musical friends” or, indeed, with a very open session either. However, there’s bound to be different levels of cohesion depending on the circumstances.

Since when does having your head down preclude you from listening to what’s going on around you? If you need to look to see that you’re in time with the other end of the circle, then the circle is too big, and why bother playing?

Eye contact is an important communication tool in a session, and can convey a lot of different things, with raised eyebrows, shrugged shoulder, nods, etc.

But if there is constant eye contact, you lose the impact of it when it actually means something. If someone noticeably glances my direction in the middle of a tune, I spend too much time trying to figure out what they heck they were trying to convey to me… Is my A string out of tune? Was there a really cute girl who just wandered by and I completely missed it? Is the waitress standing behind me with a pint of Guinness?

The norm around most of the sessions in which I play is to NOT make eye contact until you’re trying to convey something. People start carefully looking around in the B part of the second or third time through a tune to see if any of the eye contact communication is happening.
