Re: Does anyone know any Irish tunes?

My two pence-
I don’t have a problem with self-composed tunes per se, but the difference between a tune composed by Michael McGoldrick (or the tunesmith of your choice) and a tune composed by your average session.org member is that people are actually likely to play or hear said McGoldrick tune in sessions. In other words, I think the yardstick used to determine whether you should add your creation to the database is whether or not it is actually played regularly in a session by people other than yourself. That then means there is a certain level of quality assurance ‘built-in’ as someone has to have found the tune good enough to bother learning it.

I don’t, however, think it’s a great idea to use this website as a means of sending your tunes out into the world in the hope that they’ll be picked up and played from Inverness to somewhere suitably distant also beginning with I. I’ve written quite a few tunes myself, most of which, though I might have been happy with them right after I wrote them, I have later realised are utter testes, and I’m glad I didn’t go ahead and post them straight up. I haven’t posted the ones I still like either, because nobody else plays them, nor are they likely to.

But having said all that, as I generally search the database rather than browse it, the amount of “chaff” in there doesn’t really bother me. But maybe there could be a tag for member-composed tunes?

Finally, I think the session has outgrown being able to have just Irish tunes in the database, given the number of tunes. I haven’t submitted a single Irish tune, though I have put up a few Scottish ones. It’s already a great resource for Scottish tunes (I personally play very few Irish tunes), and in any case, I have yet to meet the Scottish or Irish musician who only played Scottish or Irish tunes exclusively, most people being more concerned about playing good tunes.

Basically, for me, I think the principle should be the tunes on the website should reflect the tunes being played in sessions, be they Irish, Scottish, new, or otherwise. Probably that means there should be slightly less member-composed tunes, with the vast majority being Irish/Scottish.

Sorry for the length!

Re: Does anyone know any Irish tunes?

skreetch and Jack Campion, perhaps you’re reflecting on the Scottish scene because it’s very different over here.

I can easily think of dozens and dozens of tunes that get played regularly round these parts that I’m pretty sure haven’t been “captured”. And if one was to include distinct setting associated with individual musicians or townlands there must be hundreds. It’s not unknown for local musicians to make a point of clearing it with respected members of their community before putting local tunes on commercial recordings. No doubt they’ll all get out eventually but it’ll be a very sad day.

I know a good few, if not most, of the musicians that make commercial trad recordings over here and easily half of their output consists of tunes learnt in sessions. I was only speaking last night to a box-player with half a dozen CDs out who was looking for the turn of a tune he heard in a kitchen session at our house over Christmas a couple of years ago with a view to recording it. I get similar requests a good few times a year and so do plenty of other people.

It’s a small scene and easy enough to track down authorship, if it’s known, within a couple of calls.

One of the consequences of there being perhaps two degrees of separation in the scene over here is that it doesn’t take a commercial recording for a tune to get passed around. Many “composed” tunes were played in sessions for donkey’s years before first appearing on a recording.
