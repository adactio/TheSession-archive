Re: Sean nos recordings

Kevin’s message and much of the resulting thread is based upon a fundamental misunderstanding.

The reason why the melodies of sean-nos songs (note the hyphen - and I’d also add the fada if I could find a way of doing so) do not match subsequent instrumental performances is simply because they are always sung ‘as is’. The singers take the lyrics, as they know them, and interpret them in their own way, generally using the same tune, but often employing all manner of melismatic variations - which might mean, at times, wandering off into another melody, if they feel like doing so.

So there is absolutely no question of the singers considering the melodies ‘hackneyed’!

Next , sean-nos refers only to songs in the Irish language and singers who’ve learnt the songs from previous generations,, so there’s no way that Paddy Tunney (who only knew English) could ever be considered as a sean-nos singer. Neither is Dolores Keane such, though she does occasionally sing in Irish.

There are many fine recordings of sean-nos singers available, not least from RTE and Clo Iar-Chonnactha, however, one of the best is ‘The Road from Connemara’ , an album of Joe Heaney’s songs issued by Topic. Thankfully, CIC does still have the sublime Nioclas Toibin’s album in its current catalogue - an utterly wonderful singer.

As for Pádraigín Ní Uallacháin - great if she ever managed to sing in tune, or , for that matter, hold a tune.

Geoff

Re: Sean nos recordings

David, I was getting more at the notions of quarter and micro tones and different senses of consonance. Some music traditions include sounds that strike European ears as dissonant, but once you get used to them and understand their context, they aren’t dissonant at all, except by some inflexible textbook definition. I’ve heard some sean-nos (and instrumental) Irish styles that don’t fit Continental senses of consonance, but they work well and are intentionally used within the tradition.

Then again, some people can’t hit the notes they mean to hit. I haven’t heard Ms. Uallachain that I know of, so I can’t comment either way on her singing. I’m just talking in broad, meaningless generalities here.

Also, I’m not at all suggesting that technical prowess and precision aren’t appreciated in the sean-nos tradition. But my understanding is that their weighed about the same as they are in the instrumental music--that is, lilt and passion and depth of understanding count for more than tone and pitch. This is at odds with much of popular music, though you do find exceptions (Bob Dylan, Blink 182).
