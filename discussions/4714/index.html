Thesession.org as a real life social network

We’ve had a few people (especially from Western Australia) passing through Sydney who have found out about the sessions from this website. It’s been really nice to get to know some new people. I found it very strange that these people already “knew” all about me even if I knew very little about them - stuff like my job, views on the music and even aspects of my personality! We’ve had a right good time slagging you all off behind your backs too. If anyone else would like to sit down over a beer and slag off the regulars at thesession.org, please come to Sydney and talk to me!

PS Donough I was thrilled to get a parcel today with some CDs that you sent me. Have been playing them all today whilst studying since it has been raining all day.

PPS Thanks to Graeme who also gave me a CD which I’ve been listening to today. I wish I’d had more time to sit with your kids and let them play on Tina for longer. I was disturbed at how interested they were in R’s anglo tho’! Also if you turned up on Sunday sorry, I wasn’t there because I had stayed out until it got light on Sat. so was hungover - decided I needed to give my poor body a break :-)

Re: Thesession.org as a real life social network

A local lady who comes out to the sessions from time to time came up a couple weeks ago to tell me how much she was enjoying reading our discussions. It made me feel all warm and fuzzy. Then another person I know mentioned (apparently) that I talk too much or something. That was disturbing. A flute player from san Fran came up and I caught myself saying “Ooh, San Fransisco - do you know Jack Gilder?” She said “yes,” and I said “I don’t.”

It’s well into the territory of real life social network for me, because everybody I know is aware of the site, and everybody always knew who I was even with my witty tag. It’s a little disconcerting when people I’ve never met say, “Oh YOU’RE fiddler on vermouth!” as if they’ve suddenly been flooded with a lifetime worth of information about me and I still know nothing about them.

It certainly is weird meeting “virtual” friends in the real world… but it is sure is cool :-)

I can’t express how great it was to meet people at Willie Week this year like Conan, Halldor, David and Peter…

After a while, I guess all this will stop feeling weird and start just feeling like what it’s like to live at the start of the 21st century.

In a way, it feels weird that something so hi-tech as the internet should bring together people with a common interest in something so low-tech as Irish traditional music. But in another way, it seems like the logical extension of meeting up in a pub.

If it brings people together, it can’t be too bad, right?
