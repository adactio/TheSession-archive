Re: Shall I give up now, do you think?

To be fair, though, Alex, you did invite criticism, even if it was negative, both in your profile and on youtube, so you can’t complain now that you’ve got it. You have to ask yourself “why did I invite negative criticism? Was it because I didn’t expect it to appear and so would be able to feel good about my playing and not have to challenge myself to get better?” Everyone in this music has had negative criticism at some point. The trick is to turn it around and use that criticism to your advantage.

First of all, assess for yourself whether the criticism is worth taking on board or not. Who is the person who criticised you? Can they play? You will know yourself whether or not they are a better player than you. If they are a lesser player than you, then there is a chance that the criticism is sour grapes on the part of the critic, in which case you can ignore it, and e-mail them back telling them they are a tw@t. If, however, they are better than you, then the chances are the criticism is accurate, and you need to work on your playing in some way. If something specific about technique was mentioned, then you should work on that until it’s the best aspect of your playing. If not, or if you don’t know who the critic is, then simply ignore it.

Getting comments like this is disheartening, and it’ll make you feel like giving up. In the end, you have 2 choices, you can either give up now, or carry on and get better. It’s up to you in the end.

You asked for comments, so here goes with two specific things, one that I am probably qualified to say and the other that I’m not …

1 I’d agree with the post above that your main problem is the rhythm. I would suggest that, for a while, you go back to the very basic tunes - NO ornaments. Re: Shall I give up now, do you think?

Well done there.

IMO it’s too fast in any case, but for the particular purposes I’m going to suggest it’s *definitely* too fast.

You need to really *fix* the peculiar rhythm of Irish jigs in your head. To do that, at this stage of your fluting development, you need to s-l-o-w i-t r-i-g-h-t d-o-w-n. Listen to as many flutists playing jigs as you can but meanwhile try and re-learn the Kesh, but much slower - about half the speed you did on that clip. And take out the ornaments altogether. They are definitely getting in the way right now - there’ll be plenty of time to put them back in later.

I think you need to make yourself practise this one tune - the Kesh - at dotted crotchet = 69mm. Practise it *with* the metronome and make yourself put up with playing it like that, with no ornaments, over and over, for at least a week. Half an hour a day.

After that first week, do what you like with the tune - unless you feel your rhythm is slipping again, in which case return to Step 1, as it were. But when you do play it after that first week, please try and put ornaments in only gradually, over a period of time, and very sparingly. You’re not handling them right at the moment - they’re tripping you up - and if you put them in one at a time and only end up using them sparingly, you’ll find you cope with them much much better and they will be what they should be - an embellishment of the music rather than a hindrance.

The above exercise will only take a week - is a pain, but will improve your other jigs as well, and should last you in good stead for a long time to come.

btw, many of the finest ITM musicians use ornaments very sparingly indeed.

btw btw your breathing sounded much better to me on this clip than on the one with the polkas …
