How many tunes do you know..? A hopelessly unscientific but fun competition…

I found the popular tunes list today through another thread.
https://thesession.org/tunes/popular

I do not think of myself as having very many tunes so I was amazed to discover it was No 73 before there was one I didn’t know. By 500 I still knew about 50% per page. By 1000 I only knew about one per page.

A couple of things occur to me from this. One is, my tune list matches the popular list. This is not surprising as I have always targeted popular tunes so I can join in more often.

The other is, how far down the list can very experienced players get before there is a tune they don’t know..?

Re: How many tunes do you know..? A hopelessly unscientific but fun competition…

For one thing the relative “popularity” of tunes varies from session to session. But it is interesting the way this site allows one to rank tunes by their popularity as regards how many tunebooks they’re in.

Of the first 100 such tunes I think I know them all. Of the next 100 I know all but a few. However I can sit at our local session for long stretches as they play tune after tune I don’t know.

Then there’s the issue of how one defines “knowing a tune”. There are quite a few tunes that I can play along with at a session that I don’t “know” outwith the session. Then there is a very large number of tunes that I know well but that I can’t play at a session because my version too different. In a way it could be said that the tune I know is a different tune than the one the session plays… depends on how one defines “a tune”.

The most impressive display of a huge repertoire is something I heard at a session many years ago, when a fiddler played, for nearly an hour, nothing but polkas in D Major. None of the other people present knew all of them, but many people knew some of them. Later this same fiddler mentioned the relative rarity of double jigs in B minor.

Re: How many tunes do you know..? A hopelessly unscientific but fun competition…

There’s a basic problem in the competition in not necessarily being able to recognize the name of a tune as one you know, thus biassing the results. I often - perhaps too often - have a problem in assigning a name to a tune I know perfectly well, either in the sense that I can play it straight off without prompting or that I can join in immediately I hear the first few notes (i.e. I’ve been prompted). The problem may be compounded by knowing a tune by an entirely different name. Anyway, it’s a fun competition and not to be taken seriously.

Playing polkas for an hour (would that be one set?) in a session is by no means out of the question. They are among the easiest tunes to learn, which is why they get taught to beginners and so often end up being played as dirges even when those who have learnt them are no longer “beginners” (but that’s another story). Depending on how fast that fiddler played them and the number of repeats, there could have been between 40 and 60 in that hour, and I’ll bet he slipped in a few in G, and how do we know he didn’t improvise one or two, which isn’t too difficult for an experienced fiddler on a roll. In one of my local sessions we have a couple of fiddlers (who are also a band) who will quite happily blast off into a full-throttle 12-polka set without warning, to everyone’s delight because polkas aren’t played that often.

“Getting rid of pesky tune names and calling out the numbers” - what an interesting idea, seeing that we have a ready made list of tune numbers here on this website, all 12000+ of them.
