Slow reels

I like them a lot.
1) I’d like to know whether people here play them much in sessions.
2) Do you always play them straight without swing or do you make them a bit bouncy? Do you think they work swung, or does it depend on the individual tune?
3) It fascinates me how they often don’t work at all when you speed them up to normal reel speed. Often there’s no reason why they shouldn’t work on paper but they just don’t. Likewise, some reels written to be played at normal reel tempo often don’t work slowed down.
4) When did it become fashionable to record slow reels on albums, or have they always been popular?
5) What’s your favourite slow reel?

Re: Slow reels

Maudabawn chapel: The wize maid: The dublin reel: The earl’s chair: The mouse skin shoe.

This is a great set we like to play in our session. The speed is just a little bit quicker than Eileen Ivers plays her Maudabawn chapel, but still very slow for a reel. And they have to be played very straight, no swing at all.

The second part to the wise maid is often fudged when played at “the proper speed”

Great change into the Dublin reel, and the chord changes into the second part are also great. (Strummers: try playing an E major here instead of the A major).

The Earls chair is fantastic slow (but you have to chord the first part in G or it’s too sanguine)

Then Nollag Casey’s mouse skin shoe is really terrific slow (you can find it posted here, but don’t listen to the midi thing, it’s no where near). There are some beautiful rests in it that emulate a flute players breathing. And the back to front turns are really open out when you can hear them turning.

Re: Slow reels

Learners and improvers of necessity learn reels (and other tunes) at a slow speed. Full session speed (whatever that may be!) doesn’t come to most people until they’ve been playing for at least 2-3 years. General musicianship and knowledge and understanding of The Music will also progress at more or less the same rate, so when someone is able to play at speed they may take another look at a reel they learnt (at a slow speed) 2 years before and find that, in the light of their experience, it doesn’t now sound so good when speeded up, whereas another tune they learnt “slow” now begs to be played fast.
Trevor

Re: Slow reels

Eldarion, I was just about to write what you wrote, when I skipped back and noticed that you’d already written it. But it might be worth mentioning that in Cathal McConnell’s whistle rendition of The Sunset, he does away with the weird chromatic bit heard on Frankie Kennedy’s, playing the repeat of th B-part the same as the first time - I don’t know if it Cathal originally composed it like that.

As for tunes composed as slow reels, once tunes pass into the tradition, they tend to assume a life of ther own. When smebody composes a slow reel, somebody, somewhere is bound to try speeding it up to dance speed - and if it works, it may well catch on, and the notion of it ever having been, by design, a ‘slow reel’ is lost. Furthermore, the speed at which reels are habitually played varies enormously, anyway. The normal speed at which some Clare musicians, for example, play reels would probably be regarded as a ‘slow reel’ pace by other musicians.

I’m reminded of the notoriously fast-fingered London banjo player, Brian Kelly, who, giving a concert at the Return to Camden Town festival last autumn, announced that he would play a ‘slow reel’, and proceeded to play at a pace that would make my fingers drop off.
