Re: Welsh Session Tunes

There’s really no such thing as “Welsh music” or “Irish music” in any absolute sense. Those kinds of labels go back to the rise of romantic nationalism in late 19th-century Germany. Still, Welsh music does have its idiosyncrasies. In the 18th century, for instance, the harp might have been heard in a Merthyr ballroom playing a waltz, or in Meirionnydd accompanying a penillion singer.

As I mentioned earlier, one feature common in Irish music is the frequent use of heptatonic and hexatonic modes, which also seem to appear widely in your repertoire. Take the tune Rhocesi Aberteifi (“Girls? of Cardigan”), for example. Its A section is almost identical to the Young Wolfe Tones’ Decent Cup of Tea, and to my ear it bears no distinctly Welsh characteristics. It may not have been your intention to present it as “Welsh,” but as Jeremy has shown a tune with a Welsh name, played by a Welsh musician, is enough for it to be received as such. My real concern in all this is a wish for Welsh music to develop a recognisable sound of its own, in the way Irish music clearly has.

Re: Welsh Session Tunes

There is a real shortage of good Welsh dance music, especially compared to surrounding musical cultures. This may owe something to the rise of Methodism, and to the lack of enthusiasm or at times outright contempt shown by later collectors such as J. Lloyd Williams. Very little Welsh dance music has survived in manuscript or in recordings, and even less has been published. I doubt there are more than three volumes devoted solely to Welsh dance music, and even those may no longer be in print.
