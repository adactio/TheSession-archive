Sesh Hogs & Sesh Rage

I have recently suffered from a mad black Mitsubishi sportscar, again, as something like that almost killed us awhile back after tailgating us at 85 miles and hour in the fast lane of the M6 North bound ~ with us being trapped there by a long line of HGVs… Anyway, the recent hell that left me with the shakes. This black car pulls up along side us, and this is two lanes, one either way, he straddling the centre line. He is first just a shadow a little behind the drivers seat, then he pulls up parallel and starts nosing toward us, catching the wing mirror twice. I was scared, really, he was seeming to want to drive us into the curb or into the bicycle lane. I just kept facing forward trying not to think about it. I then slowed down and he cut right across in front of us and then slammed on the brakes. I just touched his back. His door flew open and I locked ours… I did get all his details and I phoned them in, but I have felt like shight since then, since it happened yesterday, and the last thing I want to do is to drive anywhere. I really want to find a desert island really bad.

Now, what has this to do with Irish Music and sessions. Well, it reminded me of a couple of other times I’ve felt similarly, with regards to our passion, this music… I’m glad to report it was rare, but it is the sort of thing you never forget, and it does put you off of this thing we share a love for. Like not wanting to drive or wanting a desert island to oneself. Well, a few times interactions in the music have done that too us. One, only one I’ll be repeating, were the IRA thugs, or thugs pretending to be IRA, that quashed a little annual singing session in the West trading songs between South and North… Anyway, it would be interesting to hear if others have had this hell descend on them. For me it has been rare, unexpected, and crippling… I do mean ‘shakes’ ~ and an awful quesy feeling…

Don’t stop looking, you deserve to have your art and music… I never was quite sure of it, but when someone gave me acupuncture for a serious bout of back pain, it worked, it was amazing… I have since learned that strengthening the muscles and doing micro-manipulations help to stay off the pain, and to make it shorter lived when it does happen…

But, now back to the music side of it. I’m also hoping some coping strategies will arise here, and ways of staving off similar incidents, figuratively, in the music and dance.

Someone touched on the more usual sense of my meaning, ‘tailgating’… I find that really scary, but also annoying as hell. I always leave space ahead of us, even more so after a couple of folks slammed their brakes on, one incident leading to my first ever accident ever, in a long long time, having started driving legally at 13. Anyway, the music, I have more often than in driving experienced being pushed, tailgated by speed monkeys, or monkeys on crystal…

I’ve had it done to me more often by the louder or more percussive of our rich selection of instruments ~ piano, accordion, bodhran, banjo, as a few examples, but it can happen with anything, even more so if it is a gang doing the pushing. It is always out of control, meaing that it isn’t a steady thing, it fulctuates and is usually without limits. It isn’t just tempo that people can tailgate you over, it is also ‘volume’. Sometimes that means eating up the sounds of other less present instruments, tone wise, such as the mandolin or flute. It also affects the character and substance of the music over all, and I know this has been gone over many times ~ it flattens everything and you lose the geographic interest of the melodies, and the ease to vary and play with them.

Don’t take this wrong piano accordion players, as some of you are damned good and sweet tempered and hospitable, and considerate ~ I’d best make that aside just because of the scary and threatening size of your box… ;-) ~ but, there are some who play everything flat and legato and with all the buttons going, bass, chords, and whatever, and as loud as they can squeeze it, poof!, no mandolin, poof!, there goes the flute, poof!, there’s a little fiddle that can be distinquished now and again ~ the pipes are still there… I used to unfairly refer to those full blown boxes as the Mack Trucks of Irish musit. Basically, your run over the top of, shouldered aside. It isn’t just piano accordions and the folk behind them doing the squeezing, this is just the presented example. You are forced on the sidelines and have to go along with that way of playing, whatever they choose to do with the melody or tempo or whatever ~ if you can actually hear yourself…

So that is a bit of what I’m talking about, though the sort of shaking that brings out in me is considerably different…

But to be honest about this, I have on occassion had to be something that equally bothers me, the asshole, the strong arm, the one that ‘talks’ to the person doing the pushing ~ to let them know that it isn’t appreciated that they appear to be forcing things the way they want it and not taking into consideration the rest of us present… Now that is similar, after having to talk with someone, and then, maybe, even having to tell them to consider somewhere else. That I have always hated and always come out worse than the person I’ve had to confront. Now that has left me with similar shakes and a queasiness… And sometimes, the person approached responds with ‘RAGE’…

I am a driving instructor, and observe dangerous drivers (in other vehicles) all day long.
I also teach defensive driving theory classes, and study collision reports (they are not “accidents”).
No, I do not have nerves of steel. No, my job is not stressful.
You can drive safely all your life, or you can get involved in dangerous situations and collisions.
It is like the difference between herding bulls, and fighting them with a small sword. I have done the former, and have no intention of attempting the latter.
I offer the following observations:
*You cannot know the competence or state of mind of other drivers. If they appear dangerous, stay away from them.
*If someone is tailgating you, do whatever you can to safely get out of their way. It is not your job to control them. If you can’t immediately change lanes, signal your intention to do so. In the meantime, slow GRADUALLY to have enough distance to be able to brake without getting rear-ended.
*If you DO get rear-ended, do not assume that you will not be injured, or that you will be able to collect damages from the other driver. If witnesses say that you braked suddenly without cause, you may be held responsible.
*While the average, unthinking driver may be frightened by sudden braking, the out-of-control, psychotic driver may seek revenge. Cut to ceolachan’s opening scenario.
*It doesn’t matter how intimidating you appear when you get out of your car. The other driver may carry a weapon.

I don’t think any of the above necessarily applies to music sessions.
