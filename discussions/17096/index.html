The Gospels according to ~

The vignettes first ~ the contributors will remain anonymous, and besides I’ve cut short and paraphrased some of what was shared…

These are the main seeds for a slight blue mist clouding my mind today ~ and probably also aggravated from having been ill for so long. I’m glad to report that damned ‘lurgy’ seems on the wane, my lungs are much better, though my voice is still a growl, and thank you for your patience and care… Anyway ~ here goes ~

* Dance ~ Not uncommon, friends recently set off for Ireland, in this instance, in part, to catch some set dancing workshops. It is our notion that one has never yet dipped their toes in that wave of passion that swelled up and carried set dancing to new extremes in the 80s. The ‘report back’ was interesting. They commented on how crowded it was at the evening ceilis, and how fast and unforgiving the music and the dancers were. They mentioned how it seemed that there were quite a lot of folks there more interested in showing what they could do than actually dancing with others in a social and communicative way, but that wasn’t helped by the speed or the density of it all.

The workshops were good, they took classes in sean nos stepping and set dancing. It sounded reasonable, not too much all at one go. They had only one disappointment with regards to these workshops, and that were no handouts to take home with them, nothing to aid their memories of what had been taught.

Worse, over the whole experience, were the number of people they bumped in to who had taken to this form, Irish set dancing, as if it were their religion, the obsessed ~ obsessed with wanting more steps, more stepping, more figures, more dances ~ more of everything ~ minus any balance. It seemed that was all many of them could talk about, as if nothing else mattered. What else could possibly be of interest? ~ maybe the beautiful countryside around them? Our friends, in part because of this, in part because they have more interest in their life than just this dance and music, regularly hopped in the car and drove out to both escape this intensity, these zealots, and to enjoy the Irish countryside, and people who were not so dance crazed, obsessed…

* * Music (Sessions) ~ “I seem to have lost all interest in ITM. I don’t go to the sessions in ~ any more. I got so fed up with it. There are two factions ~ the old guard who play the session tunes that I know and the younger lot who turn up and play Lunasa, Dervish and Flook tunes at 100 mph. (The later are the more aggressive in making their demands and achieving their ends.) They don’t play polkas or hornpipes. I can’t be bothered to learn all those (new-wave) tunes. ~ these sessioneers have a very blinkered attitude.”

* * * Personal ~ recently someone suggested I was ‘obsessed’ with all this, someone who doesn’t know me but decided they did after just a few moments of interaction through print. Sometimes, when I’m burying myself in listening, playing and transcription, I wonder myself. :-/ Then I take off the headphones, put away the instruments, turn off the computer and go for a long walk and talk with my best friend, my sweet wife, who I still can’t believe I had the luck to meet and marry. She’s the nicer of the two of us, if having absolutely no patience for fools. I still can’t figure how I got past that acute radar. I also think on friends, past and present, some of who are or were also musicians and dancers. No, despite all the passions and concerns shared with me and absorbed by me ~ the folks that shared their music, dance and concerns with me over time, including many on site here, direct or through lurking, and while those sources and friends have challenged me to think and rethink, and they count in a very important way in my life and thoughts ~ no, I’m not obsessed with it, I hope… :-/ ?

Like those friends and acquaintances mentioned previously, while I respect the passions of others, and can show interest, sometimes too much of it, too much obsessing by another, causes me to go a bit numb and for my eyes and mind to glaze over. I feel a little guilt there, but sometimes the obsessing of others is just too damn much to take in, too out of balance for sense, if any of that remains… (Yes, fully understood and appreciated, I can imagine that happening to some folks reading my rants and rambles…)

All you neophytes, novitiates and priests out there, are we losing something in this passion, are we obsessing too much, do you wear your dress robes everywhere you go, or do you have your civies to change into now and then? Are we too far gone to achieve balance? Can we appreciate simple chat, like about the weather, which the Irish have always placed great value in ~ chat and the weather? Has this really become almost religion and politics for some, the end all and be all, the center of their universe, their escape from reality? Are we in danger of becoming so obsessed that we become zealots and jingoistic, discourteous and inconsiderate, about this thing abbreviated as ITM? ~ obsessing to that extreme? ~ too much ‘I’ in it?

Maybe you’ve experienced that yourself? I know there are opinions and tales to tell and share? Maybe you don’t see it at all and haven’t a problem with it, or it with you? I personally can’t think of anything healthy about zealotry or jingoism, however and wherever applied, as I think it damages the focus of its attention, its passion. Also, being mostly grounded, HA! ~ mostly if not completely, I know things exist in extremes, including this thing, this aspect of our lives and shared passions…

If we were to take a wide view of it all, and our place in it, and be critical, meaning, hopefuly, constructively, where should our greater energies go, what should we invest in and nurture about this thing we care about, what should we try to have some control over, to reduce, minimize or even eliminate, purge (there’s religious talk for yuh ;-) ~ Inquisition!? ~ Reformation!?), those things that we see as having a destructive influence on this aspect of our lives? But maybe even from the ground up, the basics ~ what exactly is this? Do we actually know? Is that part of the problem, that we increasingly lack any real connection to the source of this thing we’re investing time and effort into? Maybe some of us waste too much time trying to hard to distill it into some unquestionable pure drop? I personally, can’t divorce it from the dance, or from the larger context that predated sessions and organizations ~ the social milieau, the visiting houses, the crossroad gatherings, the bridges and platforms and the disorganized gatherings just for the craic ~ which is in scope and history much more than just those tune-after-tune musical scrums some sessions are… In the beginning, as far as ‘Irish Traditional Music’ is concerned, there was no such thing as ‘the session’…

So, what’s your story or stories, your concerns, what do you want out of this ~ what’s the gospel of ITM according to you?

(~ not that we don’t do this regularly, and I could hardly expect everyone to agree… 8-) )

Re: The Gospels according to ~

I love this music - all of it - the plodding hornpipes and the warp-speed reels - the overplayed “common tunes” and the random or newly composed tunes. The tune being struggled through by a newbie and the tunes expressed masterfully by the seasoned player - the laughs, the arguments, the jokes, the drink, the pub, the political conversations, the religious arguments, the debates over who is more “Sligo Style” and yes, even the Kesh Jig and a few polkas now and again. I sit in awe listening to the truly gifted players, and make every effort to reach out and welcome the next generation of new players, young and old, who have discovered what a joy this whole thing called Irish Music can be. That is my gospel. That of the average session player found everywhere around the globe.

Re: The Gospels according to ~

For me, it’s completely selfish. This music has all the excitement of rock and jazz, but without the hearing loss and random-note poseurs. I suppose some would say the same thing about other genres, but there’s something about Irish music that I like better than those other forms. I only recently became confident enough in my understanding of it to play for dancers, and that also has been a great joy.

I live in a place where Irish music is not well known, so I don’t worry about details like whether Lunasa is trad enough, or whether the kids are rotting their brains with the likes of Flogging Molly. I just do what I can to expose more people to what I think is The Good Stuff (hoping that my clumsy efforts include a tiny bit of the magic) so that I will have more people to play with, and more people to play for.

Re: The Gospels according to ~

With Irish traditional music, I finally found what I was looking for in music all my life. I did pretty much everything having to do with music. I worked for the largest record company in the world (CBS, now Sony), and it used to be my job to do things like take people backstage to meet Pink Floyd, Bruce Springsteen, Michael Jackson, or Toto. I owned alternative/goth/industrial nightclubs, and booked national bands like Pigface (which was members of Ministry and KMFDM), Nirvana, Blues Traveller, Screaming Trees, and Blind Melon. And as a club DJ for 18 years, I played everything from frat rock, hip hop, and 90’s Manchester stuff, to electronic, industrial, synthpop, and hardcore techno rave music. I worked on-air in alternative rock radio, and interviewed people like Tears For Fears, Violent Femmes, Red Hot Chili Peppers, and Smashing Pumpkins. But it wasn’t until I discovered Irish music (and got past the twangy, plunky, “folk” phobia I had) that I found what I was looking for - wonderful music that is personable, open, fun, and has very little to do with business, making money, or backstabbing, two faced people (some mustard boarders notwithstanding).

It is something that I am good at, and I like doing things I’m good at. It’s also something that I will never stop getting better at, and I enjoy the learning. On the surface, the music seems so simple, but the better you get at it, the more it opens up in complexity. It’s like delving into the mandelbrot set - the deeper you dig, the more interesting it gets. I get so locked into the exploration of the art that other forms of music bore me to death. It’s not that they’re not as good, or whatever, it’s just that I am so immersed in ITM that I don’t have time or desire to delve into the other styles enough to make them interesting again.

For me, the link between the brain and the ability to play music is extremely interesting. Like how difficult it is for some people to talk while they’re playing, and for others, it’s not a concern at all. The exploration of how you can express yourself musically within the genre is kind of like exploring your own brain. Is it mind over matter, or is it intertwined in a way that we can learn something new about ourselves? For me, it’s the latter.

Oh, and I like the free drinks. ;-)

Even if somebody only has a shaky egg to play along with. It’s not Mozart, it’s folk music.

Re: The Gospels according to ~

well, Fishmonger, I am going to agree and disagree:

I feel your Randy Rhodes reference and empathize with you, because for me, (while I enjoyed Randy and his work until his untimely demise), I equated ITM when I first heard it to techno. You have highly repetitive melodies that are played fast and create a certain “catchiness”. But the thing that I was personally blown away by was the virtuosity of the players. My initial reaction was “wow, these guys play the equivalent of an Eddie Van Halen solo every time through the tune!” Now. comparing the two is rather silly, there are vast differences, but it’s part of the appeal for me, at least initially.

Where I might disagree with you is about Lunasa, Dervish, Flook, etc. I really appreciate them for what they are (I lOVE listening to them), but I do not strive to BE them, because they are NOT inherently traditional in a lot of ways. I can see myself ultimately being in a band that rivals them, but not until I have a rivaling talent in my playing. (which is not likely)

And to your point about inclusion, it should be inclusive up to the point where it ruins everything. And it’s a fuzzy line to draw! The good news is that the area between inclusion and exclusion happens to contain what I love most about this music…
