When ye’d much rather be ashore drinkin’ rum with pretty lassies who’re also drinkin’ rum

What’s your poison? What’s your favourite tipple? What have you recently discovered that took your fancy? What booze would you buy your da or your best buddy for a special treat, or yourself, or that you’d like someone else to buy you? And, while your naming, how about a description too?

Why, well, Will turned us on to something we didn’t even know we had here, a Scottish made gin, and maybe, I’ll finally get to tease him about it. What makes that tough, is following his lead, ‘Hendricks’ is now our favourite gin. Thanks Will. Because of that any tease I might get pleasure out of would be on me too…

My latest discover by accident ~ “Bulleit Frontier Whiskey” ~ a bourbon. I haven’t much experience with American liquor, aside from “Wild Turkey” and “Old Grandad” ~ but I like this so much, both of us do, that I’m considering getting a bottle sent to me da… It was the bottle and colour that first drew my attention, and then I saw humour in the relief ‘frontier whiskey’. It isn’t in any way as harsh as that might suggest, a beautiful rich gold, and the sort of thing you long to share with friends. There’s a sweetness and a smoke to it, lovely stuff.

Aside from that, I love single malts, primarily Islay, but I also am fond of Macallan, a Speyside. In Eire my poison was a boiler maker, just the one per session or pub visit ~ a pint of the dark stuff and one shot of Crested Ten. I’m also fond of Bushmills, and long to someday try their ‘3 Wood’, and also chase up the ‘Connemara’ and ‘Green Spot’ distillations… ;-)

Pleasantly surprised on three occasions that I can recall:
Many years ago on my (only) trip to Ireland, being offered half a teacup full of a clear liquid. Hoping I wasn’t about to go blind, I tentatively sampled the poteen and thought it resembled a good single malt.
I don’t know if it was typical or if I just lucked out.

My sister returning from one of her jaunts to Cuba (we don’t have government interference in our destinations as our southern friends do, nyah nyah nyah) and giving me two bottles of what she called “local rum”. It tasted more like fine cognac than rum. When Sis tasted it, she wanted the bottles back.

Recently, on my first trip to Mexico, I decided to see what the tequila thing was really like. I have never been attracted to the salt/lemon/swallow/grimace ritual, nor been tempted to buy even a small bottle of overpriced cactus juice. I went to a neighbourhood supermarket in Ensenada, where the staff spoke less English than I have Spanish, and that’s saying something. The prices were all marked, no chance of being ripped off, and the peso seems to be conveniently linked to $U.S. @10 to 1. Tequila started at around $4 a bottle, so I looked at the top end, all locked in a glass case. For $22 a litre bottle, on the reverential recommendation of two clerks, I bought two samples of Cazadores Reposado.
Back home, I offered a tasting to a roomful of discerning tipplers (all musos and singers, of course). The cautious better-be-polite-and-not-offend-the-host glances soon turned to requests for seconds. We’re all converts, but I don’t think I can afford the stuff outside of Mexico.

Re: When ye’d much rather be ashore drinkin’ rum with pretty lassies who’re also drinkin’ rum

A drop of water is essential. We did a blind taste test and found that the drop of water dramatically improves the flavor. The best way I can describe it is to compare it to heat in food. If the food’s too hot you can’t taste it as well as you can if it cools a bit. The same is true with whiskey: straight it’s a bit too hot, whereas a drop of water cools it a bit so the flavor can bloom.

Here’s what bourbon has done for me:

A work trip to the US West Coast put me three hours ahead of my internal clock; plus, it was Daylight Savings, springing forward and making it a total of four hours. My mates and I decided to synchronize ourselves to the local time by spending those extra hours in a pub. I indulged my taste for Makers Mark.

Let me digress here for just a moment and mention that I am dreadfully allergic to cats. And the love of my life Herself came with two dear ones. So I take a battery of pills and inhalers and generally try to avoid coming into contact with the otherwise delightful members of our little family. One of our house rules is that the cats are not allowed in the bedroom – I deserve some inner sanctum free from dander, right? And the cats, of course, are absolutely fascinated by what might be on the other side of the bedroom door, and always try to slip in. So, when nature calls in the dead of night, I always make sure the bedroom door is firmly closed behind me when I creep to the bathroom.

The door clicked shut with finality, and I blinked groggily at an unfamiliar hallway. Naked.

Then I became completely awake, and almost sober. I turned back to the door. Yep. Locked. I tried to keep the rising panic down. Okay, think fast. I’ll have to break the door down. It’ll be a lot of noise, and hell to pay, but I should have enough time to slip on a pair of pants, and to hell with the consequences. I threw my shoulder against the door. About a dozen times. Any women reading this who are sometimes nervous about safety when traveling alone, I can whole-heartedly recommend Radisson hotels. The rooms are like fortresses. That door didn’t so much as rattle in its steel frame. I might as well have been hitting a brick wall.

I ran in terror down the hall, my hands clamped over the necessary bits, looking for the linen closet, at least to have a towel. To all of you who have ever pilfered a bath towel: I hate you. It seems that hotels now lock the linen closet. I saw the fire alarm box and briefly considered breaking the glass. The image of the hallway filling with sleepy guests was not attractive. I found the ice machine in its alcove and looking at the ice, remembered the urge that had woken me up. Oh, that would be all I needed, to compound my problems. I snuck back to my door.

Who is it -- the existentialists? – that claim that reality is merely an illusion, a product of our consciousness? They are full of sh*t. I tell you, that night I totally, absolutely convinced myself that I was dreaming, that the door was an illusion, and all I had to do was walk right through it, climb into the bed and wake up. Nope.

A door down the hall opened and a head stuck out. “Call security, ” I whispered loudly. “There’s… a problem.” The head disappeared.

Millennia later, I heard the elevator start up, rising through the quiet building. God I hope that’s security. I imagined some happy party goers, coming back after an exciting evening, dancing in clubs into the morning hours, returning to their rooms, giddy with the adventure, turning the corner, and seeing -- me. I actually felt sorry for them.

At least it wasn’t partygoers about to receive a buzz-kill. It was security. A large African American fella in a dark blue suit came around the corner, chest out, elaborately cool, ready for trouble. He unlocked the door and scuttled inside.

It’s funny, you know how you occasionally have those anxiety dreams where you’re at work or school or somewhere in public and suddenly realize you’re naked or in your underwear? I don’t have those any more.

Re: When ye’d much rather be ashore drinkin’ rum with pretty lassies who’re also drinkin’ rum

Another thing to avoid, unless it’s improved beyond recognition:

English wine.

The landscape of Kent and probably other southern counties is bedizened by pleasant and exotic-looking vineyards, and good luck to those who are having a go. It’s just that when I was down there in the late 80s to 90s I tried the product a few times and it was always an effort to get the stuff down. It’s claimed to have won lots of awards, but the judges must have been crusties who’d been living on home brew for months. A taste of it combined having one’s teeth set on edge with the taste of a syrupy gunk called Swiss Reserve put in it to make up for chronic unripeness. A few years ago I spent six nicker on a bottle of that rarity, English red: all sorts of compliments on the label, uniquely awful contents, not again in a hurry.

If the country does warm up consistently, the wine may improve (it couldn’t get worse, after all), whatever ecological havoc is going on. And don’t laugh, you in the Celtic nations - vineyards have got into Wales, and I dare say there’s the odd one in Ireland by now.

I had a bottle from a glassed-over vineyard in County Durham that was certainly no worse than any I tried in Kent.

They’d started up vineyards in Cymru/Wales before we’d left, but I never got a chance to try them. The one’s we saw were on the coast between Cardigan and Aberystwyth… They were producing whites, but I also heard, and the reason why I didn’t chase them up, that they were doing blends ~ adding to what they were able to produce with the grapes they had…

We fell in love with fruit wines we came across being produced in Oregon, U.S.A. ~ and I remember buying strawberry-rhubarb by the gallon… ;-)

We used to make our own fruit wines, but not the usual homebrew stuff, watered down and highly sugared. We used a really high concentration of fruit, and if the natural sugars were enough we didn’t add anything but the yeast, not even water. We ended up with great apple, peach, plum and other wines that way. We used a press and had fruit trees at the time, but also did self-pick and knew some farms where we could do that. Some of that we bottled up for years and when popping the one a few years back, 10 years old, it was fantastic…

Not so pleasant about the earlier times of that brewing were the exploding 5 gallon caboys… One night we had unexpected firewords, the noise anyway, and a mini tsunami in the kitchen and dining room…
