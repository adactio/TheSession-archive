The Meaning of Life

You’ve been blessed with an extraordinary talent - the ability to perform Irish tunes beautifully on your instrument of choice, together with groups of other, equally or even more talented players.

How do you use that talent? To bring some pleasure and enrichment to the lives of others, or to amuse yourself and a small circle of friends?

If it’s just a hobby or a pastime, do you devote more hours to it than you should?

Re: The Meaning of Life

Ahem. I used to question this too. Particularly when I was at that stage where it took hours and hours to learn a tune and to simply maintain my current level of competence (which wasn’t very competent at the time). I kept thinking, “why aren’t I doing something useful, productive, less selfish?”

Reminds me of Ambrose Bierce’s admonition that we should all have to justify our existence every couple of years, “on pain of liquidation.”

I don’t know if my answers will help you at all, but here’s what I’ve come up with over the years.

1. Human and social behavior being what it is, it’s hard to know what makes a good use of our time on the planet. Making music is at least on the plus side of any equation--if more people wasted time learning tunes, they’d have less time to start wars, commit crimes, overstuff themselves, feed the corporate monster, etc.

2. Follow your knacks. If you have a knack for music (even if you’re still developing the skills to make use of the knack), make music. If your knack is more for neural surgery or book binding or rebuilding auto transmissions, do that. Most of us are multi-knackered (did I say that out loud? :o), and the world’s a better place for it.

3. In very real terms, playing the tunes and doing sessions is a way of participating in--and building and nurturing--a community. Our own local sesh has fostered friendships, raised money for needy causes, provided a place for neighbors to meet and get to know each other ‘in the round’ (in ways beyond “what do you do for a living”). In a small but significant way, sessions improve the places we live and the networks of people we live among.

4. Can you imagine *not* playing?!

5. Rest assured that the more experienced you get, the easier this will come, and the easier it will be to enjoy it and improve with it, even when you can’t spare much time.

Re: The Meaning of Life

Greg, are you wondering whether you can justify spending all that time (and money) playing music as a “hobby”? Or whether those that claim it’s more than just a hobby should be contributing in some larger way?

It’s difficult because Irish music is kind of out of context in places where there isn’t a critical mass of Irish people… If you really want to be a traditional player, you’re facing an uphill battle, both in trying to develop a traditional style and in participating in the larger community.

For the most part, there isn’t a definable community to participate in except on special occasions, and it’s even difficult to find a community of hobbyists - sorry, dedicated learners - to hang out with. There’s a huge range of interest and ability, and we’re scattered all over God’s Half Acre.

It’s difficult to find or build a realistic public session because of commercial pressures and the expectations of the listening public. Lots of people would be pleased and enriched by attending a good session if they’d face away from the tv for half an hour, or tried listening to something other than programmed corporate radio. I suspect that this is not likely to happen in the near future.

Having said all that, if you can find the right place and the right people, you can both amuse yourself and your friends, and contribute to the larger community at the same time. There’s nothing better than playing for a bunch of drunken hockey fans who stumbled across a session and surprised everyone by enjoying the hell out of it.

**Disclaimer - grego and I live in the same town, and I’m thinking about the experience of playing music in western Canada. YMMV. And it’s Monday.
