How do you get your tunes?

Personally, I mostly pick up stuff from albums and tapes/CD’s of sessions that I obsessively play over and over. Occasionally they are picked up from being obsessively played at sessions. I don’t read music (or not good enough nowadays) so that isn’t an option. All that said, I do know a fair few tunes, but am selective about which ones to fashion into something I consider playable. Re: How do you get your tunes?

Mostly from hearing them played at sessions. But you can only do this if someone will play them several weeks on the trot while you get to learn it.

(This raises an interesting point: those people who moan when someone plays the same tune every week at a session - remember that in Traditional Music that is, traditionally, how tunes are learned.)

Occasionally the player is also able to provide the dots, so I can take them to plod through at home in order to get something sorted that I can’t get from session listening. If our mutual musical literacy is not up to achieving that, then I’ll ask them to play it slow, or play certain parts, so that I can get them fixed in my head.

Re: How do you get your tunes?

Obsessive listening to BBC radio shows over the Net… mainly Mike Harding, Celtic Connections, Culan & Folk Club. Also, listening to tracks on the websites of artists mentioned here or at Chiff or elsewhere.

When I hear something I really like and want to play I order the CD.

Weeks go by. I forget all about the tune. Then the album suddenly appears on my desk at work, as if from nowhere, and it’s like christmas! Woooooot!

Tunes that I want to learn go through the following process: record onto disc, edit into three versions: the first is really slowed down, with each part looped a whole bunch of times (I find seemingly endless looping helps switch the mind off so the fingers can get on with the learning).

Next version is unedited, just slowed down to the most comfy level to come to grips with phrasing and ornaments. Final version is the original album track itself.

It was really interesting to find, that all the work only pays off the next day or even later, once neural pathways have had a chance to get used to the grooves made by all the repetitive occupational therapy the day before. In other words, the process teaches me patience in the face of frustration. Er, dunno if that makes any sense.

Probably not the best (and far from the simplest) way of learning, but it keeps me busy and makes me happy.

I can read music but choose not to. Does that make me a gentleman? :-)

Re: How do you get your tunes?

Around a couple of years ago, I was collecting the dots of weird tunes through the net. I was just a bad beginner with bad tastes and trying to learn strange tunes recorded by some popular bands.

Now, I usually learn some nice tunes which often come up in the local session or some local ceili band’s classics. (I’m really lucky to stay here in Leeds because people still play very old-fashioned tunes!) I can sometimes pick up the tunes on the spot, but usually write down some phrases and look for the entire tune on the net. Of course, I don’t stick to the dots found on the database but changed them a little bit listening to what people play in the next session.

Just like other people on this site, I also learn the tunes which appear on favourite recordings. But, now I know the difference between “good” and “bad” tunes, and between fiddle tunes and flute tunes, and so I carefully select some nice straightforward tunes to learn.

It’s sometimes easier to learn tunes by ear, but it’s also true that we can easily see the structure of the tune analysing the sheet music of it. If I can find very similar phrases or certain arpeggios repeated in the tune, I practice them first and then learn the tune from the very beginning.

Re: How do you get your tunes?

There’s only one kind of tune that I learn: tunes that have struck my ear as something I want to play. I learn them in several different ways, all mentioned previously: off a CD played over and over, off a CD slowed down (mainly if I’m being impatient to get the tune) on my computer, caught out of a session a few times, off sheet music (usually only if I’m having trouble with a phrase or two or three), etc. It just depends on the tune.

I’ve found that if I am only learning a tune to be sociable and please someone else who wants to play it with me, and don’t like it in the first place, it’s not really worth the effort, because it takes forever to get it hammered into the head permanently. I’ll end up re-learning it everytime I play it, because it won’t stick. So I tend to only learn tunes that I like.

If it’s for the band, and Dirk is really set on playing it, then I look until I find a setting of it that I really like, so I’ll want to play it.

For instance, and I really really hate to admit this, I’ve never learned the Tarbolton Set. It’s just never caught my ear, and I’ve never heard a setting of it that I love so much that I have to learn any of those three tunes. Hopefully, someday I will, because of course that’s a classic set.

Re: How do you get your tunes?

Hmm how do I get my tunes… well my preferred method is to get them from minidisc recordings of sessions. I find that if you’re sitting waiting for a bus, or sitting on a train or whatever, listening to a recording and allowing yourself to absorb it and hum along to it in your head, by the time you get round to actually sitting down and learning the tune, it doesn’t take you long cuz you know it in your head already, and there might just be a couple of notes you’re not sure of. I’m sure this is the best way of learning session tunes. It’s amazing how many tunes you can learn in a short space of time by this method, without really making much effort.

Another method I use is learning tunes directly off my friends in Sydney. They have to play it continuously, or if they can, bar-by-bar, and I note it down quickly in abc on a scrap of paper, filling in more gaps on each repetition of the tune. A quick check through, then I start to read off the abc and correct any mistakes as I play along. After about 5 or 10 mins, I’m playing the tune as they are. The problem with this method is that because it’s quick, it lures you into a false belief that you’ve learnt the tune, when in fact you’re just going through the motions of playing it, i.e. it doesn’t make you commit it to memory or get to know the tune. If I don’t sit down with it again the next day and keep practising it, it doesn’t store itself in my long-term memory and it just flits away, and the longer I leave it, the less likely it is that I ever get the tune down. The good thing about this method is that it’s quick and you get instant results, so if someone wants to give me a few tunes I’ve never heard before, I can “record” them in abc instead of using an MD player, and work on them later. I’ve learnt a few tunes from Beebs this way.

Another method I use is learning directly off a net transcription. I use this when it’s a tune that doesn’t get played at my session, or one that I haven’t got a recording of. This is the least satisfactory of methods for me, because I find that it takes me ages to memorise the tune. I usually compare a few different transcriptions if they’re available and take bits out of the ones I prefer, and then add my own twist to it. I’ve learnt a few thesession.org members’ original compositions from this database, plus other obscure stuff by that method. I find that this last method is the one that gives me tunes that other people at our session don’t have, and they’re always after new tunes as well.
