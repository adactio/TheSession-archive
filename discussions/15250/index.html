Re: Backing - Help plz

“I dont think its like melody where u just play the notes”

No. That would be melody playing. But it’s a good place to start. Learning chord progressions is one way (and a perfectly valid one) to approach backing. But another approach is to keep playing the tunes and try adding in open strings and double stops at various points in the tune (i.e. playing extra notes at the same time as the notes of the tune). With practice, you will begin to get a feel for what notes sound good in what places. A little of both approaches should give you a well-rounded understanding of backing. As you learn chord shapes and progressions, you will begin to see how these relate to the note-combinations you are playing when you play melody, and learn to switch - or glide - smoothly between them.

For backing, even more than for melody playing, a good ear is paramount. Some formal music theory can be a help (there have been a number of good threads on this), but ultimately, it is essential that you develop a sense of what sounds right when, and how much you can break the ‘rules’ without getting funny looks (which will depend, as much as anything else, on who you are playing with).

The other all-important ingredient in backing (some would say more important than playing the right chords) which, in this thread, has only been referred to once so far, is *rhythm*. I wouldn’t know how to begin to explain it but, my guess is, if you play melody, and if you were drawn to this music in the first place, then you probably already have some implicit understanding of it.

Re: Backing - Help plz

Good point from NCRC about the Open string and double stops. Whole chords are not the only way to go about it although theyr’e a large part of the artillery. There’s bass note accompaniment as well, playing segments of the tune, thirds etc. I’ve found mixing a lot of these techniques the best way to go, and its got less chance of becoming bland for both the player and the listener.
My personal opinion is that if you know the melody to play, if you can’t hear the implied chord of the tune, you can sometimes actually see the chord in the shape of the melody on the fretboard. Thats where having a chord book comes in handy to reference between the melody and the backing.
And always start by holding off a little, that way you’ve got somewhere to go. Like most things.

Nobody is more active in the Tunes section discussions, and any time a post comes up asking what a particular tune is, chances are that he’s got an answer.

Back to the topic at hand:

Listening to Alec Finn, he DOES often just drone a chord with lovely rhythm until he finds some inspiration for something else to play. (Watch him playing Tom Billy’s with DeDannan on YouTube. He doesn’t even put his left hand on the bouzouki until the B part). So that’s not a horrible suggestion. You will find that suggestion in a lot of places (Mr. Coyotebanjo calls it “home”), the root chord of the key is always a safe place to return to. If you find yourself totally guessing at what chord you should play next, you’re in danger of being an annoyance. That’s where knowing the tune comes in handy.

One thing that I find useful as a basis for finding chords is the double stop. I play a fair amount of double stops in tunes, especially on bouzouki. If you spend some time to figure out what chords are being implied by a particular double stop, then you’re on your way to knowing what chords will work in that tune.
