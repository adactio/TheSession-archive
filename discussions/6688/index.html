A new name for our musical genre….

So, it turns out I’m not the only one who dislikes the term ITM. From my playing experience, and chatting to people and reading around the subject (as you do), they older players use the term “the music”….some people capitalise it (when written, obviously, cos how do you pronounce capitals?) to distinguish it from just any other music, ie “The Music”. Other people call it trad, traditional, diddly and diddly diddly…and gawd knows what else.

Until recently I was happy with the term “the music”, or “trad”, but trad can mean trad jazz and the music can mean anything.

So who among us is willing to risk either condemnation to the oblivion of eternal Hellfires or the the prized joy of being perpetually remembered, for coming up with a new working word or phrase for what we play?

Any takers?

This is a collossally difficult one…..

↳ Posted by Dow on May 22nd, 2005.

Re: A new name for our musical genre….

The only terms for musical styles that can be held to a constant referent, it seems to me, are those that refer to particular rhythms. “Latin” music is a broad and flexible category, but a rumba is a rumba, and if you do it wrong, it’s not a rumba any more. Other terms have fuzzier boundaries, and trying to police those boundaries makes you an old fuddy-duddy. Anyone can call their band a jazz/something hybrid, whether or not they know anything about bebop changes, walking bass lines, ride cymbals, show tunes, or what have you - there’s no principled objection, since the term is open to that sort of abuse.
So I’d say the term most likely to mean what you want it to mean would be “jigs and reels”, allowing the inference that the occasional hornpipe or slide will make an appearance.
That aside, people are going to call the music by whatever term they like, and we can generally understand that someone who carries a fiddle and plays “tunes” is likely to mean the same set of music as someone who carries a whistle and says they play “diddly diddly music”. Crusades to reform the language, sadly, don’t tend to have much effect. Good luck, though. If that works out for you, we can have a whack at restoring the past participle to the English (so we don’t hear about “whip cream” any more, wouldn’t that be nice?)
