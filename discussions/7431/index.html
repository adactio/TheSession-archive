↳ Posted by Phantom Button on August 15th, 2005.

Re: Electronic tuners or tuning by ear, which is better?

Well, you’re going to have to learn to train your ear to hear a little better, because the tuner will only take you so far. It’ll take a while, be patient. I always start off with a tuned instrument (generally by tuner, as we play in pubs most often), and then adjust, because I play with pipers and fluters and other fiddlers -- the pipes and flutes especially don’t stay constant, so I’m always checking the tuning every other set or so.

For more info, put “electronic tuner” into the little search box thingy, and read past threads on the subject. :)

Re: Electronic tuners or tuning by ear, which is better?

I use a tuner pretty often, but I’m getting better at tuning by ear as well. Problems occur when relying on a tuner too much, especially if you’re playing with a fixed-tuning instrument that is subject to change. Like a piper, for instance.

My tuner has a mic input as well as a vibration clip, so I can tell where the piper is at for the initial tune-up.

But on the fly in a session, the pipes are likely to change a bit, so it’s good to be able to tell when you’re out of tune and be able to adjust it accordingly by ear.

Re: Electronic tuners or tuning by ear, which is better?

I would advise you start by learning to tune by ear, and then when you have achieved that, go on to use a tuner when you need one (such as at a noisy session, or in other circumstances when you think your ear may be off). ↳ Posted by Guernsey Pete on August 15th, 2005.

Re: Electronic tuners or tuning by ear, which is better?

I love my Intellitouch tuner (the black one with the backlit display) for tuning in sessions, or touching up in performances, and especially in any environment in which there’s a lot of ambient noise.

Having said that, if there’s a fixed-tuning instrument (concertina, accordion, piano) around, and I can tune with it early on, when it’s quiet, I get at least a couple of notes from that and then check with the tuner to see where they are relative to A=440 on the tuner. Then I have a reference for when I -can’t- hear.

Many guitars (in my experience) are either not perfectly in tune all along the fretboard, or their harmonics match a bit better with each other just a few cents out, so I’ve learned where those are with my main instruments.

It is important for me to have an aural reference to tuning, that is, to train myself to hear pitch the best I can, so I go back and forth between the contact tuner and using a fork or a piano when it’s possible to hear, that is, in a quiet place.

Re: Electronic tuners or tuning by ear, which is better?

Electronic tuners are good, and useful to get you in the ‘ballpark’, but you need to be able to hear whether you’re in tune when you’re playing, unless you’re using fretted instruments. If you’re playing the flute you can play an infinity of different notes on one slide setting, by blowing harder/softer, higher/lower etc. so a tuner becomes irrelevant. Also, non contact tuners pick up background noise and noise from strings resonating with the one you’re trying to tune, and can be useless or innaccurate in noisy situations, which is where most people need them most.

↳ Posted by Ottery on August 16th, 2005.

Re: Electronic tuners or tuning by ear, which is better?

I agree with Ottery. The only tuner I ever use is a tuning fork (no batteries, nothing to go wrong, etc) to check the A on my fiddle. Usually, I can tell whether it’s gone sharp or flat anyway. The other strings I tune by ear in fifths up or down from the A.

In full orchestras they take the A from the oboe, which has a penetrating tone, and the oboeist will have previously tuned the instrument to A440 - whether by tuning fork or electronically is up to him/her. A string orchestra will tune from the leader who, again, would take his/her pitch from a tuning fork or electronic tuner.

The only reason we wouldn’t tune to A440 in a session would be if there’s a fixed reed instrument which isn’t quite on A440 (a rare event), so then it would make perfect sense to tune to that instrument.

Electronics are fine for getting one string in tune, but you should really practse tuning strings in pairs and listen to the “beats”.
Those very important “beats” are what you should also be listening to, to tell if you are in tune (or to play out-of-tune for effect).
