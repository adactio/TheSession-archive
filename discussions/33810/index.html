My progress - 3 years in.

Can you believe that it’s been a whole 3 years and some months since I joined this site? Sappily pleading for help because I had no idea where to start? I’m tellin‘ you, those MIDI files just weren’t cuttin’ it. I remember when I played some tunes I learned from MIDI for the family that gave me the fiddle. After I joined this site and started learning from recordings and started going to sessions, I played for them again a few months later. Even in my newbie phase, they could tell the difference. “Those sound a lot different from what you played for us the first time!”. Not to mention the first year, before all of this, when everyday the violin and I coldly glared at each other from across the room.

This place set me off! The people here showed me the underground paths, led me through the darkness, marked for me where the treasures were to be found. Helped me up when I fell into trap doors, or when I wandered off into the unsafe passages. I know I still have a ways to go, but I think i’ve learned how to navigate through this formerly unfamiliar world of music.

Do you think i’ll find the coveted treasure? That distinct pulse, that famous accent, The Legendary Lilt?

Re: My progress - 3 years in.

Well done with the playing and for posting these.

If its OK to make a couple of suggestions to aid your further progression I would suggest trying the following:

Play the same tunes, but:

1. Instead of bowing exclusively with the top half of the bow, try focusing on using the lower half and middle of the bow aswell. Don’t completely disregard the top half, but keep it for special occasions.

2. Try bowing the strings closer to the bridge - right now you’re bowing right above the end of the fingerboard. I think you might find that as you move the focus of the bow toward the frog, it feels a bit more natural, or easier to bow closer to the bridge (I think between half way and 2/3rds of the way from bridge to the edge of the fingerboard works for me).

I’m not saying you should completely alter your playing style - I simply notice that your wrist appears a bit stiff, its seems most evident in the video of the slides - my suggestions are hoped to simply get you to maneuver it a bit more, by using parts of the bow that make that more necessary.

Anyway, these are simply my suggestions - regard them or disregard them as you like. I’m sure given the scope of the community here - some may agree with them, or some may rubbish them - you can decide for yourself whether or not to try them.

↳ Posted by TheBlindBard on April 25th, 2014.

Re: My progress - 3 years in.

You’ve made a lot of progress - well done. The next stage is to concentrate on tightening up your backbeat - so in a reel with 8 notes in the bar it would be the 3rd and 7th note | 1234 5678 | and for a jig with 6 notes in the bar it would be the 3rd and 6th note | 123 456 |. Double-tapping might help with this. Play through all your tunes and notice the notes that come on the backbeat - actually give them conscious awareness instead of letting them just play themselves before you get to the next downbeat. Notice when you’re playing each backbeat note and how much accent you’re giving it and what fingers are where and which note you’re playing. Consider whether you need to bring it in earlier or later. My feeling is that you need to bring it in earlier, especially with your jigs, but also your reels to a certain extent. Try bringing it in slightly ahead of where you have it right now and “chase” it, but WITHOUT speeding up your tempo. Notice how if you accent it a bit sometimes and push it forward in the bar it can give your tunes a lot of lift and energy, but the energy comes not from a turbo but from the torque of a big V8 that needs a split second to get going and then it punches you in the guts. Don’t fall into the trap of accenting it all the time because then it will start to sound too boom-chucky and non-Irish. Just pay it more attention. Work on that for another 3 years until 2017 and then listen to these videos again.

I am not in a position to make criticisms, as an eternal beginner myself. Thanks’ Jerone, for starting this thread and eleciting all this advice, since much of it applies to me as well (I just have to remind myself that the compliments are *not* directed at me;-)).

But I would like to add a suggestion regarding polkas. It seems to be a staple of the Sliabh Luachra style of polka playing to slur groups of 2 quavers (1/8 notes), changing bow on beats 1 and 2 of each bar.

This pattern can be adhered to fairly consistently on straightforward quaver passages:
l:1/8
k:A
(ce)(eA)|(df)(fe)|(ce)(eA)|(cB)(BA)

but may need slight modification when there are semiquavers involved:
l:1/8
k:A
A>(B cA)|(BA) (cA)|A>(B cA)|(BA) F2

l:1/8
k:D
d (A/d/ ed)|(cA) A2|d (A/d/ ef)|g2 (fe)

In addition to slurring, there is a quickening of the bow on the 2nd half of each beat, giving an accent to the note under the bow at that time - even if the note is a crotchet. This helps achieve the ‘pulsating’ rhythm characteristic of the style. If you haven’t already, listen to Padraig O’Keefe, Denis Murphy, Julia Clifford, Seamus Creagh and Matt Cranitch playing polkas and you’ll get it much more easily than from trying to understand my inarticulate gibberings.

There are, of course, other ways to play polkas - for example, they’re played very differently in the North Connacht (Sligo, Leitrim etc.) tradition - and your polkas are good as they are. But I think it is important, if you are playing Sliabh Luachra polkas (which most of the better known Irish polkas are), that you know something of the musical tradition that they come from.

Keep playing (not that you need telling ;-)). I’m running to keep up with you.

Re: My progress - 3 years in.

Hello Jerone, I haven’t been following your progress but obviously you are doing great. I’d just add a caveat to Dr. Dow’s advice re. thinking about the backbeat. In reels anyway, that’s the only clip I have listened to. Tread carefully! I can hear a nice subtle backbeat in The Steeplechase, it sounds natural. Yes it could be more pronounced, but it doesn’t have to be. But if you want more, let it come naturally and don’t go looking at the music or go breaking down the tune and saying, oh here’s note 3 or 7, I need to give it some more wallop with the bow. I hear lots of players who seem to have done just that and I cringe.

Yes, DD does warn you against doing it all the time and says just to pay attention, fair enough, and if that works for you, then good. But if you were my student I’d simply advise listening and paying attention to the kind of fiddle playing you like and let the accents soak in and come out naturally. Better too little than too much. And each tune has to be approached as a unique piece, with different demands and opportunities for accenting. For example, in the second part of Glass of Beer I would like to have heard you giving more wallop on the * downbeat *, not the backbeat - playing a good long hard d3 sometimes instead of a somewhat timid dcd all the time, and throwing the open D string in as well for extra welly.

↳ Posted by Stiamh on April 29th, 2014.

Re: My progress - 3 years in.

“(…)playing a good long hard d3 sometimes instead of a somewhat timid dcd all the time, and throwing the open D string in as well for extra welly.”

Exactly! There are many ways to play a tune, to present the harmonic material. Take control of the tune instead of letting the tune control you. See also the Internal backing-track thread - https://thesession.org/discussions/33834

If you want to change the melody a bit, that can affect the bowing/accentuation, and similarly, if you want to change the bowing for whatever reason, that will sometimes affect which notes you play.
