Re: Who’s Actually Irish on The Session?

I was born in Belfast. Some might consider me Irish, some not. I currently live in England and hold a UK passport. I know a few phrases in Irish (enough to translate a rude comment in a letter an acquantance asked me about). My son once told me that he wished “the blood of the Celts flowed in his veins”. I told him it did, as his both his parents are expatriot Irish. He seemed pleased.
When I was last in America, some twenty years ago, and a callow young man then, it struck me how many Americans told me that they were Irish. It drove me up the wall. You aren’t Irish. You were born in America. You’re American! I’m Irish! Do you want me to draw you a map?
But it means something different in America. In Europe we might use the phrase “of Irish Extraction”. Anybody who can claim some kind of Irish heritage in America will proudly call themselves Irish. You can be Black and/or three-quarters Japanese and still be Irish. Actually, that’s not a bad thing.
But I have to say that there should be some difference between those of Irish Heritage and those who were born in Ireland. Perhaps I could claim that I am “sod” Irish. From the auld sod, yes? My son, alas, born in England, will have to be merely “Irish”.
