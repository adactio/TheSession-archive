Well, a couple of years ago my ex (the subject of my jigs) and I came back from a trip to Australia and visited my grandparents on Vancouver Island for a couple of days. They were both over 80 at the time and moss had invaded the roof of their expansive bungalow. Picking it out from beneath the shingles with a butter knife was on my grandma’s expansive list of things to do (she also keeps the most famous garden in a town full of gardeners, regularly cleans countless yards of linoleum on her hands and knees with a scouring brush, and once climbed into her compost bin with a pitchfork to murder a rat).

She commented, as Derek and I were eating breakfast and rubbing the sleep from our eyes at about 2 in the afternoon, that she was planning tackle the moss that very day (she had already baked cinnamon buns and planted some veggies out back). She mentioned that the neighbours complain when they see her climbing around on the roof, wondering why in the name of all that is holy she doesn’t just hire somebody to do that kind of work. Well, heck, it sure sounded like a fun time to me and I immediately volunteered for the job (seeing as this would free her up to make MORE cinnamon buns!)

Then the story gets very boring and just involves crawling around on a stinky scorching hot roof getting blisters and sun stroke for the next four hours. We made a bit of a dent in the moss situation then called it quits and went for an unexpectedly refreshing dip in the best swimmin‘ hole on earth. (I say unexpectedly refreshing because it was early March and this is Canada - despite the unusual heat of the day the water was still freezing flippin’ cold - ice cubes would not have melted in this water. Also, the best swimmin’ hole on earth is best experienced by jumping from a fifteen foot cliff into a deep gorge of running water, then swimming upstream about fifty feet to the first available exit point. Having just spent two months scuba diving and frolicking in tepid waves off the coast of northern Australia I had forgotten how cold water can be and did not test it before stripping down to my birthday suit and leaping into the gorge.) But I digress…

The moral of the story is, if your 86 year old grandma wants to climb up on the roof and pick moss out from under her shingles because she is too stubborn and capable to hire someone else to do it, by all means, let her! Or you’ll end up with stiff nipples and frozen butt cheeks that won’t thaw for a month!
