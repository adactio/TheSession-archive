Re: Elitism Elitism Elitism I know what it is ……………

I know this post is about to drift into the history of posts but I think should contribute.
When playing music for a long time,there is a very fine line between playing a session you enjoy,and playing one where you just can’t stand it any longer,and as music becomes a much more sensitive and personal thing as time goes on the line can seem to get finer and finer,as you become aware of what you are hearing around you .
I’ve been accused of it in the past and have certainly been on the receiving end in the past as a box player with jazz and experimental tendancies.
1st off,when in Rome….this goes for tempo,swing,timing,chord accuracy/voicings,more than one chordal inst.,versions of tunes,the types of rhythms the rhythm section uses,people who hate added rhythm(gtrs etc),volume,tuning,the pipes won’t tune to the box etc,blend of instrumentation,timidness,eccentricities and personality clashes,musically and non musically, and unhealthy,insecure ego.
These can and have been reasons for people to be ticked off for one reason or another,wether the reasons are good or bad,they are a reality which won’t be going away any time soon.
The school of hard knocks is in play at all times.
Either you are hard headed enough to carry on and to learn from as many of these situations as possible and put it into PRACTISE,or,you don’t give a s***, or subtle enough to not bother anyone,or know when to play or not,or join in or not.
If you join in on a session where you don’t know anyone,you had always be very careful,especially if it’s intimate.
It’s like joining in on a conversation about a speciallized subject in a way.
As an ex member of Miles Davis’ band once told me,“you had better know your s***,otherwise,how are we supposed to converse with each other.”You are joining in on a working unit that somewhat needs to run smoothly,or does somethng seem broken?
Some people don’t mind a big clutter of sounds going on,and it’s more of a communal thing,and they’re happy to be a part of that,and there’s nothing wrong with that either,but it’s not for every situation.
You have to know what you’re getting into,and it can be a rough ride for anyone,even experienced players.
I still get glares from time to time,because of my approach,or tempo,but that’s just my own personality clashes with someone elses view on it,and I know some people hate the way I play and some don’t,and I’ve learned to live with it,and there are so-called experts out there who pride themselves in their superior knowledge,and that music must be played like this or that,and no matter who you are,you’ll run into this at some point.
Now,if there is a really good tune some where every week,it can sometimes be the best kept secret in the world,and there is good reasons for it.
Someone walks in with a bodran case,and you can feel the tension mounting,it’s not a personal thing,it’s “is this the end of our night?”and if you’ve been hired to play you hve to stay til the bitter end as it sometimes can end up.
I’ve also seen some our most “respect players”critisized to their face in sessions by others(I was in session with Ciaran Tourish form Altan in Miltown a few yrs ago and he started a tune,quite up tempo,I joined in and when we were finished I heard an American girl,fiddle player(nothing against Americans,I live there) say to us we were “playing too fast” in an angry voice,,,but he’s from Donegal,and they play alot faster there than many other places.We just looked at each other,smiled,and said nothing.so, you are never away from this so called eliteness,
,so,I’ll leave it there,with a welcome to the hard road of trying to keep everyone happy,or not,and learning to live with yourself no-matter who says what,

P
