Someone acting like its their Concert?

Picture this, you are in a session and everyone is playing and having a good time, then someone comes in and starts a tune and no-one else joins in (as they don’t know the tune) the person continues merrily along and completes their set of three tunes that no-one else knows. They finish to a polite applause, their playing is ok, maybe very slightly better than the average. Then someone else starts a set everyone knows and all goes well and everyone is having fun.

THEN the first person starts another set that no-one else knows and plays 3 tunes, threes times each until the end , then a session set , then that person AGAIN plays their solo tunes, so it goes for two hours.

I’m getting annoyed now thinking ‘do they think this is a concert not a session’? Are they under the impression they are so fabulous they’re treating the rest of us to an amazing experience? (instead of boring us after 30 secs!) So I start drifting off thinking… will I they get another drink , chat to someone, look at my phone or just go home and put the kettle on.

How would you handle that situation?

“How would you handle that situation?” - tricky

I think, loosely, I could divide session attendees into two groups:
A. Those that want to play together, sharing a tune for fun
B. Those that want to show off (and probably have an inflated sense of their own abilities)

The latter group, thankfully, seems to be much smaller. But due to the nature of how they behave, only one is needed to kill the session singlehandedly for those in the first group

I could drone on for ages on this subject, but enough for now….

Re: Someone acting like its their Concert?

I would pull them aside and say that isn’t proper etiquette and solos aren’t allowed unless it is an invited guest…When they start up a set you could even stop them there and ask what are your tunes and say “sorry we don’t know those pick something else”. Likely that person will be fed up. And say “it’s not your turn to pick a set” when appropriate too.

I go to this session where the session leader asks me to play a solo. I’m pretty good and they also are just happy to have a young person join them I think :) I just play one solo set for the night, maybe sneak in a tune everyone knows for the last tune of the set and pick common tunes they would know the rest of the night. I look around the session when I do my solos too to make sure everyone likes it too. If they didn’t, I wouldn’t play solos anymore. The session is already 1/2 singing sets anyway so I don’t want the band to be idle much longer.

I wait until the normal session crowd is gone and it’s just a few players left at the session who are all interested in sharing tunes likely no one else knows just to hear each other. We all take turns sharing tunes at that point.

Re: Someone acting like its their Concert?

Some people, although they may be musically talented, lack ability to read the atmosphere of the room. I have encountered a handful of people like that myself. I have zero respect for them. I don’t care if they are musical genius. There is a time and place to show off their ability. It’s called concert. If they are so good, they should have a concert of their own. And people should pay money to hear them perform. But if a group of people are gathered, and their expectation is that they would play music together, it’s not a concert.

This isn’t just limited with Irish trad. Have you seen a piano in a public space, encouraging people to play? There is one in Oculus World Trade Center station in NYC. I often see a talented musician keep playing for a long time even though there is a line of people who are waiting for their turn. They are convinced that it is their concert. The better you are, the more you should encourage others to play, don’t you think?

Because I’m Japanese, I view any human endeavor through Japanese spirit of “Do” (pronounced Dou). Like, Karate-do, Judo, Ka-Do, Kyu-Do, Aiki-Do.
“Do” means “a way of.” The idea is that through these endeavors, you are supposed to grow as a human being. Mastering the skill is only a byproduct of such personal growth. If you are not growing as a person, what’s the point?

And sadly I have to say, some people with great musical skills, have utterly failed in that aspect.

↳ Posted by Jimi Limpet on October 16th, 2026.

Re: Someone acting like its their Concert?

yes, I have seen this happen.

very common situation with visiting musicians. they are from a different town, they are not sure what tunes we know, we are not sure what tunes they know. sometimes they ask, do you play X (no), do you play Y (no), so we go, “play something, maybe we like it, maybe we can play along”. (yes, often enough).

then there is the question, is it more of a playing session or more of a listening session. obviously, “I just want to play all the time all the tunes!” is not good enough, it has to be a two-way street, some listening must happen somewhere, even if just because not everybody knows every tune. (and you stop playing and listen, you may hear something interesting or pleasing).

then if you can do math, “we only play tunes everybody knows” always degenerates into playing the same 10 tunes every night and this session is dead.

then, you must have the freedom to play your favourite tunes, even if nobody else knows them, otherwise, an oppressive mood can settle in, drives away musicians and punters. (and people may like them and learn them, I did learn the Boxing reels, eventually).

but yea, if a regular participant regularly plays tunes nobody knows or in a way where nobody can join in (Catharsis in Em or Calliope House in Emaj or the Butterfly in 7/8), and everybody else looks bored and starts doom scrolling, yea, there is a problem that needs fixing.

Re: Someone acting like its their Concert?

That’s happened to me visiting sessions while traveling.

I’ll be invited to start a set, and I’ll pick one I think is a common set we play locally, only to find nobody knows the first tune.

If nobody looks like they are going to join by the second time through, I’ll pick another tune.

Again, if nobody looks like they know it, I’ll keep picking another tune until someone else knows it.

Generally when this has happened, I can pick something very common as the first replacement tune and all’s good.

Just seems like common courtesy to me, and I really don’t ever want to play a whole set solo if I can avoid it in what should be a social music environment.

↳ Posted by ross faison on October 16th, 2026.

Re: Someone acting like its their Concert?

I like to introduce new tunes from time to time. Usually, if I suspect that a tune isn’t widely known, I’ll put it first in a set and follow it with some real standards that I’m pretty sure everyone will know. That way people get to hear a new thing (and hopefully they’ll like it and want to learn it) and they also get to play. I’ll also let people know going in - this is one you might not know, it’s called X, I got it from this recording, and I’ll follow it with these other ones. Setting expectations helps. Also, when people know that there’s familiar tunes coming, they listen for them, which means they hear the one I’m introducing instead of just zoning out :)

If someone was playing lots of sets of stuff that other people weren’t joining in on, I’d probably have a quiet chat with them after and suggest that they might want to adopt that policy. If that didn’t work, I might just start going to the bar when they launch into one of their diatribes….

↳ Posted by Gregor Harvey on October 17th, 2026.

Re: Someone acting like its their Concert?

I have 2 thoughts on this.

1). “…polite applause”

I think we need to self-police this a bit better.
I’m sure we’ve all recognised “a performance” or something self-centered happening at a session but, possibly in penance for our angry thoughts, still “politely clapped” afterwards.

This simply re-enforces the behaviour (especially for those who struggle to read the room), and I think we need to get better at this.

If someone self centeredly detracts from a session/other people… why clap?


2). “Musicians are weird”

I’ve said this before, but one of the things that truly unites musicians is we’ve taken the odd decision to spend thousands of hours in a room, *completely by ourselves* to practice music.

I think this genuinely does mean there’s a higher incidence in our population of people who are less comfortable/adept in social situations (medically or otherwise).

Not a bad thing, just a reality.
