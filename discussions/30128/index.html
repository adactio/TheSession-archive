Going Phat? Squeazin’ the Dance out of Dance Music?

“Dance is no longer relevant! This is a session tradition!”

That’s a very close approximation of something stated recently in a thread. Maybe they’re right? After all, two well established forms that we all are familiar with, ‘Classical Musics’ & ‘Jazz Musics’ ~ with dance at their roots, have over time developed fat around their vitals, moving further and further away from community involvement and participation, from dance. What do I mean? I’m not completely sure, which, rather than expose you to too much of my ranting, is a good reason for seeking some discussion here, as there are some folks on site with that fluidity of thought and quirkiness of character that I know can freshen this in discussion, raise it to debate, and those with strong opinions that will likely stir up the coals of this barbie to argument, up and down, crosswise and off topic, while hoping nothing and no one gets burned. (Sadly one is missing from our midst who was good at that, dear Michael, who I tended to call ‘Llig’, with a Welsh pronunciation of the double L…)



While I deleted the bulk of my morning rambling, as I prepare for a new instrument :-) ~ here’s a few fragments I pulled out of the ashes of a longerf series o rants and rambles that bubbled up from remembering and musing over that quote:

* In the primitive both music and dance began together from mimicry of nature, sound and physical movement and cycles, and by our own physical forms in nature, heartbeat and two feet, having been born as rhythm. It only takes feet beating an agreed rhythm on the ground for it to be both dance and music simultaneous and indivisible. As things have developed toward more bums-on-seats performance and exhibition, sessions included, have things in a sense become more obese as they move from that more active community involvement toward something we all tend to keep emphasizing the importance of here ~ listening. Maybe in doing so we’ve lost the wider focus on the greater physical involvement of ‘listening’, feeling the music, the ‘dance’ in it, being at one in it, part of it, getting off our asses and experiencing the tradition more fully, dancing in one way or another?

* With the music on this site mostly being ‘dance’ music, as clear by the many classifications - reel, jig, hornpipe, waltz, etc., is there any evidence we can find to a similar loss and loosening of that connection to those roots, of a fattening of this traditional ‘dance’ music, this site’s primary concern by content and membership?

* New compositions often display a lack of understanding and connection to that vital ‘dance’ root, a clumsiness obvious to anyone with even a slight sense and understanding of dance. It can be something in part or in whole, small or large, one measure’s worth - that doesn’t quite work for dance and dancers, a muddy melodic or rhythmic transition, uncomfirtable points where it is over worked, such as when someone tries too hard to be cute or to exhibit their imagined ingenuity and genius. It’s back to that distraction from the music to the affection - “see what I can do!”. For anyone with any sense of dance it’s pretty obvious when someone muddies a point of rhythm or phrasing, sacrifices the ‘dance’ for artifice, or just out of ignorance. (New compositions were in discussion when the beginning quote was dropped into the discussion.)

* Is it inevitable that things evolve toward the more complex, convoluted, specialized, with changes in environment, application and use or abuse, with increased standardization and a growing bureaucracy of dinosaur organizations seeking to control and codify and direct focus and direction, as toward certification and competition and gradation, for both music and dance? And, in that rise in complexity and specialization, does that also include a growing and equally natural risk of extinction? Is this also the reason for knee jerk reactions against it going weird, OTT and off the deep end, including turning anti-dance and avoiding dance at all cost because of the struggle for control between different interested parties, organizations? Yeah, I know, there are some real jerks in the dance world that instead of respecting the music think the focus should be all about them. I’ve heard some of the strongly expressed reasons why some sessions absolutely do not want any dance or dancers…

* In competition complexity tends to be given the greater focus and influence with higher points and results, and quite often valued more than just good basics, like the lift and verve of damned good foot tappin’ dance music… (Speaking from experience as an adjudicator.) The difference being that complexity and acrobatics have you leaning forward and listening intently, while good dance music even in its simplest forms can inspire things that are not so easily measured and marked, like involuntary foot tapping, body rocking, head nodding, humming, lilting, or the urge to get up and boogie…

* I will alway prefer foot tapping and dancing to just sitting in my seat leaning forward listening intently and only having my mind involved in the experience, though I can appreciate that too. My first preference is that tradition of community and roots, not being fond of the self-centred and cults, cargo or personality… Rock us completely, collectively, body and soul! ~ Music & Dance…

Re: Going Phat? Squeazin’ the Dance out of Dance Music?

Two things come to mind --
1) Competition and tradition are mutually exclusive. You can’t be ‘the best’ at something that everyone does naturally without fundamentally changing it. I imagine you could have breathing competitions, just as you have eating competitions and running competitions and dancing competitions, but in a very short space of time, normal people would be unable to compete successfully.
2) The plethora of recording media is fundamentally altering the evolution of traditional music (and possibly dance, of which I know little). There have always been innovators - those who think rightly or wrongly that they can improve on what they have heard or learned. But before easy recording, the bad stuff quickly fell by the wayside, and the good stuff got played (or quite possibly lost as well, of course, only less frequently). Now, though, anyone can make a recording and hold it up to the world as exemplary without ever fully understanding what they are changing, and there will always be those to copy it without understanding what they are missing.
Whether these things are good or bad is moot, you might believe. I already feel I am fighting a losing battle, but I am not going to surrender. Fortunately there are some splendid young musicians behind me, approaching at speed.

Re: Going Phat? Squeazin’ the Dance out of Dance Music?

I think it’s because dancers have a habit of telling musicians how they should or shouldn’t be playing. They think that the dance has to come before the music, and don’t understand that the music comes first. After all, there’s no sense in dancing unless you have music to dance TO. Some musicians still regularly play for dancers but most don’t, won’t or can’t. But is it the musicians who have deserted the dancers or the other way round? Is it that the musicians just couldn’t play well or fast enough so the dancers abandoned them and used recordings instead, or did the musicians just get p1ssed off with the dancers telling them the reels had to be exactly such-and-such-a-bpm or it wouldn’t do? I suspect the latter. Then once you get dancers who complain at the musicians for not phrasing the music properly and for other details like ornamentation styles, it just gets unworkable and the two groups drift apart. Since the music is an art in itself, why WOULDN’T the musicians go off and do their own thing? After the presence of dancers ceases to be important, the music is free to change to what the musicians want it to be. They are in control of their own art and not dependent on - or answerable to - another group. It’s an ideal situation: the players who enjoy playing for dancers can do so, and those who prefer not to be answerable to the dancers and be generally treated like muck can go off and do their own thing.

For me personally, I don’t care either way. I don’t do ceilis altho some of my friends do. I’ll continue to play music at sessions and if anyone wants to get up spontaneously and dance that’s great. It does happen occasionally as we have a dance floor at one of the sessions here and it’s nice when it does happen, but if they turned round after the end of the set and went “by the way you were playing too slow for a reel” I’d just say “um… why did you get up and dance to it then?”

With regard to the tunes themselves and the way they are changing, I don’t think that’s a problem because the old tunes are still there and people still gravitate to older styles that emphasize the dance. The new tunes that deviate from that are self-consciously different and separate (I’m talking about e.g. Brian Finnegan tunes). They fulfil a role that appeals to a different group of people who find a different kind of energy in it. There are sessions in Ireland where the (generally younger) crowd all learn all of these modern, wacky, syncopated tunes off the latest albums and play them exclusively with friends, whole sessions of the damned things, but when they’re playing with an older crowd or teaching tunes in workshops, they’ll go back to the trad stuff. I think it’s a different genre which you either love or hate. I happen to hate it and would rather cut out my own lymph nodes with a scalpel than go to a session where I had to sit and listen to wall-to-wall modern compositions. Ultimately though, I don’t think this new stuff or the separating of dancers from the session culture will damage the traditional music itself. There will always be people who go right back to the original sources. For every trazzhead there’ll be an anally retentive tradhead who wants to play exactly like Michael Coleman, no matter what country they’re from or how old they are. The tradition is always going to be push-me-pull-you like that and there’s nothing you can do about it. It has always been that way. Do you think everyone in the scene was happy when people in Ireland started playing and dancing to things like polkas and mazurkas? What we see as “culture” is really an emergent phenomenon - the resultant effect of a very complex process of many, many individuals each doing their own thing according to how they perceive the overall, and according to where they’ve played and who has influenced them. Basically, it is as it is, so you just have to do what you think is right for you and not try and hope that other people should have the same tastes, values, perceptions or aspirations. You may as well just sit back and enjoy what’s out there…
