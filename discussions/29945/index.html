Re: Embedding snippets of tunes in a word doc, notes not ABC

If you really want dots, then you could do this:

1) Create the ABC from scratch in tune-o-tron (since you want rid of unwanted headers like title and rhythm), making sure you have the X header, e.g.

X:1
K: Edor
M: 4/4
L: 1/8
E2BE dEBE|

2) Without converting into a pdf, right click on the bar you’ve written and click on “Save picture as”. This will save the bar as a jpeg image.

3) Open up the jpeg in your Microsoft Office picture viewer or whatever you’re using, go to “Edit pictures” and crop it as tight as you can. Save again.

4) In your Word file, go to “Insert” and then “Picture”. You can then move it around and resize as you like.

Someone else may be able to suggest a simpler method.

I still say it seems an awful lot of hassle when we already have ABC which is perfect for this sort of thing.

Re: Embedding snippets of tunes in a word doc, notes not ABC

It can be done without using ABC but it entails even more work initially than the method described by Dr. Dow. Once set up and learned it can be fairly fast. It involves making a load of small bitmaps, noteheads, tails, treble clef, rests etc in Paint.

You can’t create the scores in Word. You create them in Excel and when finished copy them into Word. Use the line drawing facility in Excel to make the stave and bar lines and insert the bitmaps of notes and rests etc onto that.

My folder containing bitmaps and a blank score in Excel is 329 kilobytes in size. If you think this could be sent as an Email attachment you are welcome to it.

I don’t understand why the threads gone off into CSS and html and pdf’s etc. The OP only mentioned pasting pics into a Word doc. The method I use to do that (though usually it’s into Excel) is to take a screen shot of the dots (in the browser or wherever else I find them) using a great tool called snagit which allows you to capture any bit of the screen you want. Then just paste it into the doc. You can then move the picture around as you would any other in a Word doc.

If you don’t use snagit, or some other smart screencap utility, you can always take a fullscreen capture (with ALT-PRTSCR) and the crop it once you’ve pasted it into the doc.

Re: Embedding snippets of tunes in a word doc, notes not ABC

Dr. Dow answered the OP as far as inserting into a word doc.

Here’s one way to use abc. It’s crude.

Text can be added to the abcs in different ways. The simplest being with a postscript command ~
%%begin text
… type away
%%endtext

The first example is one tune with text below

X: 1
T: Hunter’s Purse, The
M: 4/4
K: Ador
ed|:cAAB AGEF|GFAF GFED|cBcd eged|cdef gedg|
%%begintext
I think I learned this off a Kevin Burke recording a few years back. It’s a common sessio tune,

and lends itself to Kevin’s particular style of shuffle bowing, plus lots of rolls and triplets.

Or just play it simply as written and enjoy the tune’s sinuositude ;-)

We do this in a set starting with Charlie Lennon’s, then Hunter’s Purse, and bursting into

D major with the Cameronian.

Will

Posted on September 17th 2001 by Will Harmon
%%endtext

I use abcm2ps, but the above can be pasted into concertina.net’s Contert-a-Matic.

The 2nd example has three tunes, some formatting for alignment, font size, & spacing. Normally I would use a reference number for each tune, & a space between tunes. It’s written to work with the Tune-O-Ton. So, if a copy & paste works you should be able to get a page with all 3 tunes & text from Concertina.net

%%titleleft
%%titlefont NewCenturySchlbk-Bold 12
%%subtitlefont NewCenturySchlbk-Bold 12
%%textfont NewCenturySchlbk-Roman 12
X: 1
T: Drowsy Maggie
M: 4/4
K: Edor
|:E2BE dEBE|E2BE AFDF|E2BE dEBE|BABc dAFD:|
%%begintext
Don’t let the title fool you; there’s nothing drowsy about this tune. It’s usually played at a very fast pace.

Posted on June 1st 2001 by Jeremy
%%endtext
%%vskip 1.5cm
T:The Silver Spear
K: D
A|:FA (3AAA BAFA|dfed BddA|FA (3AAA BAFA|dfed (3BdB AG|
%%begintext
I learned this version directly from Kevin Burke years ago in Portland. When I quizzed him on the bowing,

he played it once using an up-bow to slur three notes and then a down-bow on the accents. Then he changed

where the accents fell but kept the three up, one down. Then he played each note in a separate bow stroke,

and then slurred long phrases (whole bars) on one bow.

Posted on July 1st 2001 by Will Harmon
%%endtext
%%vskip 1.5cm
T:The Maid Behind the Bar
M: 4/4
K: D
|:FAAB AFED|FAAB ABde|fBBA Bcde|fBBA BcdA|
%%begintext
This is a very popular tune that can be heard at most sessions. Try turning the A’s in the very first phrase

(FAAB) into a triplet. You can do this wherever this phrase appears.

The second part can be quite tricky. Reaching those high notes can involve quite a stretch of the fingers.

Don’t try to play this part at session speed until you can play it perfectly at a slower pace.

# Posted on June 2nd 2001 by Jeremy
%%endtext

There additional formatting routines if you want to move things around &/or use columns.

Now as far a multicolumns with abcm2ps, there’s information in Guido Gonzato’s Abc Plus Manual;
Chapter IV
5.5 Multicolumn Output p.50
http://abcplus.sourceforge.net/#ABCGuide

The guide has more information if you’re using abcm2ps.

Back to %%multicol. It’s a bit of formatting to use it for what you need. The only thing I can think of, to have everything on one line, is to forgo the T: info field & opt for the V: voice info field. This example should work if you’re using abcm2ps directly. It won’t work in the convert-a-matic, & I cannot say in which abc apps will work. So, here goes > > >

%%pagewidth 21cm
%%leftmargin 1cm
%%rightmargin 1cm
%%titleleft
%%titlefont NewCenturySchlbk 10
%%subtitlefont NewCenturySchlbk 10
%%multicol start
%%rightmargin 13cm
X:1
V:1 name=“Drowsy Maggie”
M: 4/4
K: Edor
[V:1]E2BE dEBE|

X:2
V:1 name=“Silver Spear”
K: D
[V:1]A|FA (3AAA BAFA|

%%multicol new
%%leftmargin 11cm
%%rightmargin 2cm

X:3
V:1 name=“Maid Behind the Bar”
M: 4/4
K: D
[V:1]FAAB AFED|

X: 1
V:1 name=“Hunter’s Purse”
M: 4/4
K: Ador
[V:1]ed|cAAB AGEF|
