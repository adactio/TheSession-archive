I started playing traditional Irish on a Trinity College bouzouki. I also have a Johnson Octave Mandolin. Both of which are made in Korea, but are decent instruments - good for learning on, but not real high quality.

I am mostly a tenor banjo player these days, but I recently bought a gorgeous Fletcher Brock Cittern/Bouzouki (5 courses). http://home.hccnet.nl/h.speek/bouzouki/brock.html

The Brock is just wonderful, although it takes a bit to get used to the 5th course. I am currently playing it in DGDAE. The low D helping with accompaniment, which I am working more on doing.

I am generally a melody picker, although, I have been working on the style where you play some accompaniment chords along with picking the melody.

I would say that my influences are mostly banjo players, so that doesn’t help much. But I have picked up some nice bits from listening to Zan McLeod when he’s playing his ’zouk. Most influential to my bouzouki playing might be my good friend Sam, who plays wonderfully, both melody and accompaniment.

I have also played a couple of Citterns by Steven Owsley Smith (http://www.celticmusic.com/steve/), and they are absolutely fantastic. Including #001, which Roger Landes had for a long time… Great sounding instruments, but I think Steve has something like a 5 year waiting list (and I don’t have that kind of time OR cash) :-)

Re: ’Zook Roundup

I wish I could tell you who made my current bouzouki. My father-in-law got it for us on a trip to Greece a few years ago, and although he passed along the invoice from the music store, I can’t read it because -- yeah -- it’s in Greek.

I use mine mainly for melody/harmony or some variation thereof, tuned in GDAE. I originally played mandolin when I started out in Irish/Celtic traditional music, but over time I’ve found the bouzouki often provides a good “mid-range” voice among the instruments you’d usually find in a session or performance ensemble.

My influences include some fairly obvious ones, i.e. Lunny, Irvine, Johnny Moynihan as well as the likes of Brian MacNeill and Jamie McMenemy. But because I started out on mando, guys such as Dave Swarbrick and Tim Knight (Steeleye Span) made a big impression on me, too. And does anyone remember Gerald Trimble? He doesn’t play Irish/Celtic trad anymore, but I certainly liked his “Heartland Messenger” from the mid-1980s.
Oddly enough, I think I’ve also been influenced somewhat by listening to Northumbrian piping, even though I don’t play a lot of tunes from that tradition nor have I ever played the instrument. I like to throw in some quick hammer-ons as eighth or even sixteenth notes, which kind of reminds me of what I’ve heard on Northumbrian pipes.

Re: ’Zook Roundup

Hi,

I play a Phil Crump B-II in cedar top over rosewood b/s, 24.5" scale, and tuned GDAD, and I love it. I enjoy life immensely whenever I pick it up.

Your Religious Avatar of Bouzouki plays one of Phil’s instruments too, and had a bit of influence on my choice. Mr. Holmes provides a lot of the bass … er… influence in Dervish, and my Crump, similarly to his, has some pretty rich low end.

Having said that, my Crump sometimes lands in the same low-end territory as guitars in sessions, so I’m learning to use a capo (finally…) to bring the range up a bit.

In looking into the bass and timbral nature of these things I’ve become quite convinced that the depth of the box (not the overall volume inside it) has the greatest effect on the bass response of a zouk. The deeper the box, the more bass-y and guitar-ish it sounds, and the shallower it is the more mandolin-ish it sounds.

Other influences on my playing are Zan McLeod and Eoin O’Neill. Roger Landes recent CD with Randal Bays (“House to House”) is brilliant! I don’t think I play anything like them, but I’ve learned a lot from them and always enjoy hearing them play. There are lots of others, virtually anyone I can hear…

For more specifics of what I do, click on the addr below my signature, there are some sound samples there. A couple are guitar, but the great majority of that recording was done with the Crump. I don’t attempt to play ‘single line’ melodies, but it seems that I sometimes find the melody (or something like it… <GG>) in chordal playing with cross-picking.

Here is the address of the “CBOM” discussions area at the Mandolin Cafe website. There is a lot of good stuff there, as there is in the archive there. There is also a “Builders” section where you can find contacts and more on various luthiers who build zouks.

http://www.mandolincafe.net/cgi-bin/ikonboard.cgi?s=c2de1b5c215dad03c0a8424db62fdede;act=SF;f=16

Fletcher Brock has been mentioned, Chris Baird at Arches instruments is doing well with an octave mandolin he just added to his line. Davy Stuart in New Zealand does great work, Lawrence Nyberg in western Canada does lovely instruments. Sobells are tough to find, Foleys are -splendid-!
I’ve played a couple of really nice Webers (real mandolin-sounding, those, but have some good bass in ’em, too).

Narrow your criteria as much as you can before you hang out much with these guys, it will save time & $$. Oh… and almost all of them are … “optimistic” … (to be kind) about how long it will take to get your new instrument to you. <GGG>

Congrats on your new quest and your new instrument, and have some fun choosing it!

All the best,

Re: ’Zook Roundup

I play a Sobell 10 string mandola tuned GDADA, so it’s like a short-necked zouk with an extra top A string. It’s good for picking tunes and also strumming rhythm. It has a very distinctive sound with excellent balance between the treble and bass. I usually use it for backing in sessions. Despite it being a mandola, it’s large-bodied, so its powerful bass can rival any guitar for backing on its own, but with a capo it can compliment a guitar nicely with countermelody or chords in a 2-person backing effort. Its sustain is excellent considering the length of the neck, and if you’re picking tunes or countermelody, you can make it sound bell-like or twangy or anything in between, depending on where and how you orientate your pick along the length of the string. I’ve played lots of zouks by different makers from Ireland, England, US, Oz, NZ etc over the years, and I’ve picked them up and put them straight back down again. The only cittern that ever came close was a Davy Stuart 10 string I once played that had a lovely bright and clear tone. I’ve had mine over 6 years now, and its tone has mellowed and become more exquisitely beautiful as it has aged. It’s an absolute pleasure to play. People think they’re expensive but they’re not really for what they are. Mine was a shade over 1000 quid.
