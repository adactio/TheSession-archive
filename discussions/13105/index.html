Re: Help with the Bodhran

If both the guitars are strumming ( I’m assuming these are steel-strung guitars ), then I can see a bit of the problem.
Also; how big is your drum ? What is the diameter of the skin ? What’s the depth and the thickness of the rim ? How tight and/or adjustable is the tension of the skin ? How heavy is your tipper ?
Some older bodrhans look like the objects they were made from; a garden sieve with a narrow rim. Some more modern and powerful ones have quite a deep and thick rim, and seem to project more.
Do you have the skills to make one yourself, or find someone else to try ? I’m even curious as to where you got yours from, and I admire your efforts in playing this strange music.
As to the other tempos; don’t play on slow airs, they are solo pieces; as for all the others, listen to the rhythmns and use your head.
Good luck.

Re: Help with the Bodhran

If anyone is going to bring something new to the bodhran, it’ll be a Brazilian (maybe a Cuban)… Good on ya for taking it up!! I would love to hear what you’re doing with it! Have you tried combining it with a pandeiro, tamborim, etc.?

I have a tip unrelated to bodhran playing specifically: check out the Ruben Blades album ‘Mundo’ (if you haven’t heard it already). It’s a brilliant record full of tasteful, uncontrived songs that fuse folk traditions and instruments from all around the world. As he sings on the first track, “En la quena de los Incas oigo Irlanda, y al Iran al escuchar gaitas de Escocia…” or, “In the Incan quena (traditional flute) I hear Ireland, and Iran in the sound of Scottish bagpipes…”

Throughout the album he creates arrangements that combine Highland pipes, Brazilian percussion, Irish whistle, Australian didgeridoo, Cuban tres and more. It is a must-have album for any fan of world music.

I hope it can give you some new ideas to incorporate into your playing as you also explore the traditional side of playing the instrument.
