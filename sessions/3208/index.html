The Musicians played beautiful old Tunes, one particular Tune they played had a personal connection with one of our Musicians, namely Phil Cunningham.
The Famous Fiddle Player Michael Coleman, born in the Townland of Knockgrania near Ballymote was a frequent visitor to the Hunt Household in Cloontia, Phil’s Grandmother, Mary Hunt was a youngster, the Hunt Family loved Irish Music. On one particular visit to the Hunt Household, Michael Coleman on hearing the Swans and Birds Singing, an Irish Tradition Tune came into his head, he didn’t know what name he would call it, at that moment in time, Phil’s Grandmother’s Brothers were out fishing on a little Boat on the Lough that lay in front of their House.
On seeing this, Michael Coleman called his Traditional Irish Tune - The Boys of the Lough.

Another highlight of the night was when a young man from Manchester called Joe Kennedy sang for us. This Joe Kennedy is a Grandson of the late Joe Kennedy who owned and ran Kennedy’s Singing Pub in Doocastle.
Joe sang two songs, the second song has great historical significance with the Famous Singer Jonny McEnvoy and it put Kennedy’s Pub in Doocastle on the Map.

In mid 1970s Johnny McEnvoy began to write his own material. His first original composition of the song, Long Before Your Time, was written in 1975.

Johnny McEnvoy recalls: “The inspiration came to me on a golden August afternoon while sitting on a stone in the sun outside Kennedy’s pub in Doocastle, Co. Mayo.
“As I sat watching the doe-eyed cows grazing on the grass in the field, a feeling of deep melancholy came over me, and a rather sad and tragic story started to formulate itself in my head, the song :- Long Long Before Your Time, because very popular and it reached number one in the Irish Charts”.

The Recitations and Poetry was fantastic.
Peter gave us his own composition about climbing Croagh Patrick.
Garland Sunday, also known as Reek Sunday, is the traditional day for the annual pilgrimage up Croagh Patrick in County Mayo, which falls on the last Sunday of July, hence, Peter’s Poem was perfectly timed for the ascent of Croagh Patrick this Sunday ( 27th of July).

Mick Carty recited :-
“ Mammy’s Day in Tramore “.

“Mammy’s Day in Tramore” (sometimes called “Mammy’s Trip to Tramore”) is a famous, darkly humorous Irish folk recitation and storytelling piece.
The Story of Uncle John Bryan, a man who lived with his elderly, 94-year-old widowed mother in rural Ireland. When his mother passes away suddenly just before her 95th birthday, John is devastated and decides not to part with her right away. Using his basic taxidermy skills he once learned, he preserves her body, dresses her in her Sunday Best, gave her Hair a Perm, she looked like she was very much alive. John’s long term plan was to keep collecting her Weekly Pension Money and take her on a long-promised trip to the seaside. John drove her to the Beach at Tramore in County Waterford.
He sat her on a Deck Chair near the water’s edge with a view of the breaking waves, John then fell asleep, while John slept the tide came in and then receded, his mother was carried out to sea. John eventually woke up, his Mammy was gone, eventually leading to a local News Report stating that an elderly Woman was lost at Sea.
This dark comedy piece was penned by writer and performer Nollaig Seoige.
It gained widespread popularity through humorous spoken-word performances and traditional gatherings.
