This tune is by Brian Pickell, taught to me by Michelle MacGregor, who I believe learned it at VOM Fiddle Camp. The dots don’t do it justice, it’s a beautiful, romantic waltz, and Michelle and Shannon and Matt Heaton played it for my wedding reception for my first dance with my new husband. I think it should be more widely played.

Can’t swear to the guitar chords, but thought I’d throw them in anyway as I found them elsewhere.

Brian Pickell has just released his premiere album of compositions, “Fresh Canadian Fiddle Tunes”. Samhadh v. Seamsóg

Sorrel, Sourlick, the acetic or ‘acute’ (ie sharp, acidic tasting) Rumex acetos(ell)a is a common plant of temperate regions, typically associated with damp Irish meadows and banks for instance.
This ‘sour leek’ (originally meaning a pointed leaf) shares its taste with the woodsorrel or pink-sorrel, both commonly mistaken for Shamrock.
None of these plants are grasses as the American monicker would suggest.

Re: Sourgrass And Granite

This beautiful waltz was written by Brian Pickell for Julie Schryer as noted on the CD liner notes (Fresh Canadian Fiddle Tunes.) Julie, an extraordinary piano accompaniest, is the sister of Pierre Schryer, a very talented Canadian fiddler, one of the Schryer triplets, all equally talented fiddlers and step dancers. The Schreyers are from Sault Ste Marie, Ontario, Canada (The Soo.) Julie and her husband Patrick O’Gorman (Uillean pipes, flute and whistle) founded and run Algoma Trad, which promotes traditional song, dance, stories and tunes through classes, camps and many other activities. They are located on St Joe’s Island not far from The Soo. Check out their website! Brian Pickell, a fine mandoline player and song writer, toured for a number of years with his band made up of Pierre, Julie, Patrick, James Stephenson and latterly Shane Cook. Brian is closely associated with Algoma Trad (although sadly, in failing health these days.) I don’t know much about sourgrass but I’ll take Brian’s word for it that it grows in the meadows surrounding the beautiful granite hills near The Soo. If you know the Dermot Byrne/Pierre Schryer CD, the covers depicts these same lovely hills, comparing them with parts of the Irish landscape, which, in fairness, is quite a bit greener! The tune goes well with Muriel’s Waltz and Waiting for Emilie, both by Pickell.
