Frenzy Polka

An unusual polka with a > 2 octave range, recorded by Cormac Begley on his self-titled album on a standard-tuned C/G treble concertina. According to the liner notes, this tune was composed by his father, Brendan Begley.

↳ Posted by Lennart on October 5th, 2022.

Re: Frenzy

Transposed down to Emin, it doesn’t seem too unusual, although you’re correct that most tunes fall within two octaves, so one that’s slightly more would by default be unusual :)

The part that makes it really unusual is just the placement on a standard concertina, I think, as most people don’t play quite that high… usually.

It’s a great tune, anyway!

Camino Voyage Number Two

Tune by Brendan Begley. I learned it from Marla Fibish. The low E -> high F jump between the A and B parts are largely aspirational on my part, as I play it on mandolin and that’s a bit of a leap, but I like the way that sounds.

Frenzy, X:3

This is one great tune by Breanndán Ó Beaglaoich which he called the Frenzy Polka. It was written for the centenary of the 1916 Easter Rising, featuring in Breanndán’s and Mike Hanrahan’s show, “Dublin Burning”. This being a Brendan Begley tune, however, I can’t help but associate this polka with the frenzy of the Atlantic Ocean which can often be quite wild in his native Corca Dhuibhne.

Here’s the same tune in a more conventional way of polka transcription (written in groups of 2 eighths as opposed to 4). This is from the playing of Breanndán Ó Beaglaoich & Rosie McKenzie with fond memories of Scoil Cheoil an Earraigh, the wonderful spring festival in Ballyferriter, Co. Kerry which marked 20 years just last week.
