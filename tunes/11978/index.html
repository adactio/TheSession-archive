Bridgie Con Mat’s

This polka was transcribed from the playing of Brendan Begley on the Boys of the Lough’s album ‘Twenty’ (2005). Begley learned it from Sliabh Luachra accordionist Johnny O‘Leary. The subject of the title was the sister of legendary fiddler Denis Murphy, who taught the tune to O’Leary.

Bridgie Con Matt’s, X:2

I believe I got this version from Billy Clifford – and even if I made some of it up, I still teach it to my whistle students! :-D That little flourish at the end of Part A suits the whistle and the flute quite nicely. The Bridgie in question was of course Billy’s aunt and Julia Clifford’s sister, Bridgie ‘Con Matt’ Kelleher née Murphy (just how many nicknames and surnames can an Irish person have?? one more!) This polka also got played quite a bit in Newmarket and was recorded by The Monks of the Screw.

↳ Posted by Bregolas on March 27th, 2025.

Harnet’s March, S: Curtin Manuscript (Bridgie Con Matt’s, X:3)

Apparently an earlier version of this tune titled Harnet’s March can be found in the Finnegan / Curtin Manuscript said to be made in the 19th-century Sliabh Luachra. Breandán Breathnach obtained this manuscript from one E. Finnegan, who it turn had it from D. Curtin of Stagmount, Rockchapel, Co Cork.

Here’s a link to the manuscript with a foreword by ITMA:
