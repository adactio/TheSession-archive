The Scottish Lovers

Quirky tune. From the playing of The Battlefield Band on “Beg & Borrow”. Track #4.2.

As the other tune they play in their set, they got it from the “Old Irish Folk Music and Songs” collection.
I think I hear an F natural in the 4th bar of the B part.

Here’s the version available on ITMA if you want to compare them.
https://www.itma.ie/digital-library/score/pw-joyce-old-irish-folk-music-769

And here’s the tune. It starts around the 1:45 mark.
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_A0K_nsEkE

Re: The Scottish Lovers

This is a setting of an old tune, probably English in origin, although it’s been called “St Patrick’s Day” in ireland and Scotland. Somewhere in my memory I’m being told the name might be “Black Joke” or something similar, and there’s a version in Playford, and it’s part of the Cotswold Morris repertoire, but I haven’t looked it up to confirm. It may very well be here under the “St Patrick” title…

Re: The Scottish Lovers

I’ve come across this tune as ‘The Black Joak’: there are various settings in ‘A Handbook of Morris Dances’ compiled by Lionel Bacon (the ‘Morris bible’). I won’t post my setting here as it’s not Irish but, if of interest, it’s at: www.rudemex.co.uk/library/Alphabetical/Black%20Joak,%20The.pdf. There’s also ‘The White Joak’: another Morris tune with the same structure.
