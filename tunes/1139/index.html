Jim Kelly’s Reel

I think Trevor Jennings, when he mentioned above that the tune came from an old (US) collection of fiddle music, is referring to “Irish Traditional Fiddle Music.” Originally published 1977 in New Hampshire, a new edition has just been released. https://www.fiddlecasebooks.com/store/c2/Books.html

Jim Kelly played this tune on tenor banjo at a concert in the early ’70s at Harvard University. At the time, he lived in Newtonville, Mass. His peformance was so electrifiying, and the tune so appealing, we just had to include it in the book. I’ve played it since then under this title, to commemorate the banjo player.

Re: Jim Kelly’s

The only reason it’s called “Jim Kelly’s” is because he played it. See this explanation above :

“Jim Kelly played this tune on tenor banjo at a concert in the early ’70s at Harvard University. At the time, he lived in Newtonville, Mass. His peformance was so electrifiying, and the tune so appealing, we just had to include it in the book. I’ve played it since then under this title, to commemorate the banjo player”.

This reel appears in Breandan Breathnach’s “Ceol Rince na hEireann Vol. 2” - # 200. He collected it from “Shean O’Luchrain of Pomeroy, Co. Thir Eoghain” in 1968. So it was collected - in Ireland - under the title “Dick Sherlock’s” at least 2 years before it became associated with Jim Kelly.

Re: Dick Sherlock’s

Vincent McGrath (from Co. Mayo) says that this reel is another of the compositions of his uncle, John McGrath, who was a Mayo man but lived in New York until his death in 1955.
Vinnie says that John’s other compositions include The Providence, Humors of Lissadel, George White’s Favorite, Maid of Mount Kisco, and the other Ashplant (in Em).

John McGrath was known as “the man for tunes” in the 1940’s in NYC.
My late friend, Ed MacGowan, learned fiddle as a boy (from 8-13 yrs) first from James Morrisson and then from Paddy Killoran (after Morrisson died).
After a year, Killoran told Ed, “you have the technique, now all you need are tunes”, “for those, go to McGrath”.
So Ed studied with McGrath (until Ed laid down the fiddle to study as a Jesuit, picking it up again 30 years later).

Re: Dick Sherlock’s

I don’t know about the others, but “The Maid Of Mount Kisco/Cisco” has certainly been attributed to Paddy Killoran. That’s the first time I’ve ever heard of anyone claiming otherwise. I have also never heard of anyone claiming authorship of “The Humours Of Lisadell” or “George White’s Fancy / Favourite”. In the many, many recordings I have of these tunes over the past 50 years, they have always, without exception, been listed as “Trad”. Had John McGrath composed them, I would have expect to have come across that claim before now.

↳ Posted by Kenny on March 2nd, 2023.

Re: Dick Sherlock’s

Vinnie says that John McGrath made those tunes and gave them to Killoran.
He has a manuscript of George White’s favorite in McGrath’s hand but titled “The Bangor Reel”.
Supposedly, Killoran renamed the tune in honor of a publican who was his friend.

I’m not a big advocate of Vinnie’s claims, but he has visited us here in Ithaca to interview Ed MacGowan about his lessons with John McGrath.
He seems to be a diligent researcher and his claims seem plausible.
Also, none of those tunes are in O’Neill’s and all seem to have first appeared in America, around that time period,
so it is at least possible that they were all composed by McGrath.

Vinnie is producing a book on the subject that I hope will appear soon (Vinnie is getting old, and has been working on the book for many years).
BTW, Vinnie is himself a fine composer of tunes. He made a CD consisting of his own compositions. A couple of his jigs were recorded by Kevin Burke.

Re: Dick Sherlock’s

When there’s this much contention about the name of a tune, especially if it involves who composed or first recorded or first played or taught it, I like to simply pick one of its names that refers to something other than a player. Placenames are often nice, if not overused, as well as terms of endearment like “Favo(u)rite” or “Fancy.” So I personally call this fabulous reel The Tipperary Fancy, one of the many names it has been known by. So far as I know, that is a name unique to this tune and not likely to cause offense to anyone who thinks some individual deserves the credit for it.
