Re: Tucandeira Dance

Not to be confused with Cockanandy (!), the Tucandeira ritual demands of teenage boys who want to become full members of their society to plunge their hands in gloves teeming with Paraponera clavata ants (the bullet ant of Brazil). No ant must be killed because the ants represent living women. By such a public display they gain their adult status. The ritual is accompanied by music and dance and is repeated over several sessions, to obtain both full status and full magic protection (against disease and to gain strength and vitality).
https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=iWKW-gONMBk&pp=ygUKdHVuY2FkZWlyYQ%3D%3D
This famous ritual came to be poetically “celebrated” beyond its indigenous community, through music and dance of a different type, in the wider Brazilian community (for instance by carimbó composer Pinduca in the 1970’s).
https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=ZiCDgMYXqDM&pp=ygUSdHVuY2FkZWlyYSBwaW5kdWNv

Re: Tucandeira Dance

Hi Birlibirdie,

I composed the tune, ‘Tucandeira Dance (Warrior’s Birth)’, as a tribute to the rich indigenous culture of my homeland, Brazil. The melody is crafted to echo the rhythmic foot-tapping of traditional dance rituals. For the best rendition, it sounds better if played at a tempo that mirrors the original dance, with a particular emphasis on the resonating ‘f#s’. Initially, I hadn’t intended to list it here in the session. It was only later that I learned original compositions are best suited for the profile section. Since this realization, I’ve been uploading all my new tunes there. Thanks.
