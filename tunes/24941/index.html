Two comments

The Cup Of Tea (Goodman) = Cabar Feidh / Copperplate reel

Not to be confused with the “session” Cup of Tea and the Lady’s Cup of Tea, this reel is basically James Goodman’s take on the Scottish tune Cabar Feidh. The source for this particular manuscript seems to be unclear, moreover, the original MS contains “Scottish snaps” in several bars which might suggest this particular tune was copied or adapted from another collection.

This reel has been published in Tunes of the Munster Pipers vol. 2, no. 363. Here’s ITMA’s interactive transcription of the manuscript:

https://www.itma.ie/scores/goodman-vol-2-363/

My transcription is based on the version from More Tunes from the Goodman Manuscripts (2021) by Mick O’Brien, Emer Mayock and Aoife Ní Bhriain who played it as a reel (with no snaps):

https://goodmantunestrio.bandcamp.com/track/the-cup-of-tea-the-kerry-lassie
https://thesession.org/recordings/7266

Sleeve notes by Donal Siggins additionally point to “Willie Clancy’s version of The Copperplate and a tune in O’Farrell’s Pocket Companion vol. 4 (c.1811) called Caper Fey” as relatives of this tune.

Caberfeidh

Fiddle setting in D major rather than the original Ador version which is the “parent” of “Rakish Paddy”.
I think I have heard this setting, or something very close to it played in the key of C. Seem to remember it being played on 5 string banjo, unusually.
