“The Balkan/European Folkdance Music Gig Book, Volume 2”

by Miamon Miller
Fuge Imatinea Press, California, 1995
ISBN 0-9626468-6-5

A version of this particular melody, in a different key, can be found here, page 16, which also has another well known tune, “Kapuvári Verbunk”, which can also be found here ~

http://abcnotation.com/tunePage?a=ifdo.ca/~seymour/runabc/balk1/0168

Changes in tempo are not unusual for Hungarian dances, including the czardas…

Much of my experience here, dance-wise, was courtesy of this wonderful character, Andor Czompo ~

http://www.phantomranch.net/folkdanc/teachers/czompo_a.htm

Hungarian Furulya ~ and part of the ‘discussion’ brought forward ~

One of our favourite groups is Teka, but there are some lovely tunes associated with their take on a simple 6-hole woodwind, along the lines of a whistle, called a furulya. Taking in Moldavia too, here are some select ‘whistle’ players, which you’ll also find on U-Tube ~

Kádár Ferenc
Szántó Ferenc
Legedi László Istvan
Hodorog András

I’ve had the gift of such instruments, to own and play, in particular in two keys ~ C & D… I also had the exhausting joy of learning and loving dance and musc from the repertoire of the Vlachi, Vlaşi, Vlachs or Walachians ~

http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlachs

Not forgetting the joy of experiences with regards to the wide world of the Hungarians, inclusive of Transylvania… We love it, and miss it, including having not had a good bottle of Takaj-Aszú in ages, a once favourite tipple, sigh… :-( The Eastern Europeans produce some great wines and brews…
