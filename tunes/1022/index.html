Did you wash your father’s shirt

I came across this reel in an advanced beginner’s class in Bristol a little while ago. One of the ladies there gave me the words which had been taught to her by her Irish grandmother many years before. The words fit the first part. Does anyone know if there are further words for the second part?
The words give me the impression this tune may have been a children’s school playground song. They give a little insight into Irish village life a long time ago.

↳ Posted by fidicen on October 9th, 2002.

There’s an English version of this rhyme (which starts “Oh, can you wash your father’s shirt”), with a different tune, which is fully pentatonic, thus having the advantage of being playable only on the black notes of the piano. As far as I know it doesn’t have a second part.

↳ Posted by CreadurMawnOrganig on October 9th, 2002.

The words don’t seem to have appeared on the site.
Anyway, they are:

Did you wash your father’s shirt?
Did you scrub it clean?
Have you hung it on the washing line
By the village green?

Did you wash your father’s shirt

Matt Cunningham plays this tune as the first of two on track 18 of the CD Volume 12* of his series Dance Music of Ireland. Interestingly, he plays it as a polka - it’s not unknown for bands to turn reels into polkas and vice versa for some set dances - and plays the B part first.
* I am in the course of identifying the 66 tunes on Volume 12 (the only information available is a list of the names of the set dance figures), and will post the results in the Recordings section in due course.
