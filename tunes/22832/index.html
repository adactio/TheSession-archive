Three comments

Irish Lostrum Ponia

The later versions of this tune have been added as ‘Langstrom’s Pony’, which is a 6/8 jig, but this 1727 version is a 3/2 hornpipe, as are other 18th-century versions. The title ‘Lostrum Ponia’ appears meaningless. It’s been suggested the tune is originally Scottish, and that the title might come from Scots Gaelic, but without any suggestion of what Gaelic phrase it might come from. The changing title over the years (see https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Langstrom%27s_Pony for the history) shows how the meaningless title gradually morphed into something with apparent meaning (who was Langstrom?). And other tunes (eg Berwick Billy or Dusty Miller) show how 3/2 hornpipes can easily morph into 9/8 slip jigs (though direct 3/2 > 6/8 seems less natural).
So is ‘Irish Lostrum Ponia’ a unique example of a really old genuinely Irish 3/2 hornpipe? Or are there others? Or did Mr Dermot O’Connor of Limrick either write his own tune for the 1727 joint publication, or 3/2-ise a pre-existing slip jig (not a change that would happen naturally, but easy enough to do, as I’ve found messing around with slip jigs).

Re: Irish Lostrum Ponia

This is one case where The Traditional Tune Archive does need revision. There’s no melodic similarity between this tune and the family of tunes that has become Langstrom’s / Langstern’s Pony, despite the similar name.

Re: Irish Lostrum Ponia

This appears in The First and Second Division Violin, published by D Wright jun‘r, who took over following Playford’s death. There is no date, but it also contains ’The Black Joak as performed in Dublin‘ which dates it to about 1720. It is titled ’A north country ground, called Lostrum Ponia’, and has some differences, one being bar four being an octave lower.

The original can be found here https://ks15.imslp.org/files/imglnks/usimg/b/b0/IMSLP653390-PMLP198850-Digital_Store_g.43_The_First_-_Second_Division_Violin-_containing-_a_Collection_of_Divisions_upon_several_Excellent_Grounds-_for_the_Violin.pdf
