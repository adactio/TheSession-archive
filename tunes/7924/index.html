Re: Cill Cais

Following in Jacqueline Belliu’s footsteps, I will reply two years after her and 18 years after the original post. This tune in three forms can be found ass #6814, 6815, and 6816 in “Sources of Irish Traditional Music.” The editors give source credit for the original transcriptions to George Petrie’s 1855 collection. They also recognize the tune as “Cill Chais” but show the three names Petrie collected for the tunes, none of which expresses anything suggesting a lament. However, other sources refer to the tune as “Lady Iveagh,” the name often used for Lady Kilcash. The tune laments the demise of Kilcash family lands through deforestation by the English. Could it be a famine tune? Maybe. The timing is right. But Petrie and the “Source” editors do not make such a connection.

Petrie’s titles (translated):
-- This Time Twelve Months I Married [could be a lament, I guess]
-- If I Should Meet a Tanner’s Wife
-- A Cock and a Hen That Went Out Together
