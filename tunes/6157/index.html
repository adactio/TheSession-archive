Northern Frisk (three-two)

A second tune that our tune tutor brought back from a fiddle workshop at the Sidmouth Festival 2006, this time a three-two in stark A minor.
Our tutor has a variation on the 4th part as follows:
|:[e2e2][e2d2][e2c2][e2d2] [e4e4]|dcBA G2d2 BcdB|eagb edcB ABcA|E2A4 c2 B2A2:|
Note that there are several places in this tune where a double-stopped open A with the open E can be quite effective, as is an E-drone beneath the A in the last measure of each part.

Re: Northern Frisk

I found some interesting info on this tune at the Traditional Tune Archive.
They have notation & info on this version of ‘Northern Frisk’ as a 3/2 ‘Triple Hornpipe’ (haven’t heard that term before)! Also known as “The Merry Conclusion” and “Mr. Kynaston’s Famous Dance”.
https://tunearch.org/wiki/Merry_Conclusion_(The) English Dancing tune composed by Nathaniel Kynaston, early 1700’s - Shropshire/Wales border area. More publication info here: https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Merry_Conclusion_(The)
There’s also a 6/4 ‘Northern Frisk’, in G major - listed from London, 1713.
https://tunearch.org/wiki/Northern_Frisk_(3)_(A)
AND, it’s written as a REEL in D major from an 1850 source (Cumbria). “in hand B” - what does this mean?
https://tunearch.org/wiki/Northern_Frisk_(4)
Anyway, they’re ALL the same tune. …surprised to see any Major Keys - but I haven’t tried them.
