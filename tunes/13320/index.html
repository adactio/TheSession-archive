The Green Ship

A tune that’s occasionally played at the University of Bristol Folk Club’s session at the Ship Inn, Bristol (UK).

I’ve transcribed it as a reel, but it might perhaps be better to think of it as an undotted hornpipe or a rant.

Re: The Green Ship

The title ‘rant’ should refer to a dance, not a tune. The tunes that are used are reels or hornpipes, however you want to define them. You can also use polkas. If you said to a Scotsman after hearing him play “The Flowers of Edinburgh”, that he had just played a rant ,he would not know what you meant. As regards hornpipes, William Honeyman bought out the “Strathspey, Reel and Hornpipe Tutor” in 1898, where he states that the hornpipe can be played in 3 different ways, giving examples, one of which is identical to that of a reel using the “Nashville Shuffle”.

Re: The Green Ship

The 2nd comment above by Andy Hornby identifies ‘The Green Ship’ as being from John Winder, Dancing Master, in the music of the Winder family, Wyresdale, near Lancaster. Lancaster’s in north west England so it’s definitely English!

The tune also appears on the Traditional Tune Archive website (tune very similar to x:2, same key). The details are as given by Andy Hornby, but the date of 1789 is given for the J. Winder MS. On T T A the tune is described as “hornpipe/clog”. It isn’t clear if the tune was written by Winder or if he merely collected it. I like the idea of it being a clog dance…
