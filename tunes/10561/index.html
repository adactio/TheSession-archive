I’m posting this tune in response to a request made on June 26th 2010 by session member “leftynow”

Please note that it is NOT the same tune as “Gilderoy” AKA “The Beggarman”, neither is it the same tune as “Gilderoy” AKA “The Indian Ate the Woodchuck”.

I’ve transcribed it “dotted”, but it is also played without any swing.

I just stumbled on this tune while picking my way through a 1933 publication called The Robbins Collection of 200 Jigs, Reels and Country Dances for Piano, Violin, Flute, or Mandolin. The tune is called Guilderoy Reel in this publication, but it is definitely this tune and not the Little Beggarman tune. The main difference, other than being labelled a reel, is that it appears to be in A harmonic minor or maybe A melodic minor instead of A natural minor. I leave that to the musicologists.

The technical term for that, Andy, is “a mistake” or “carelessness”. The original in Surenne’s “Songs of Scotland without Words” was in G minor, and in transposing it too quickly, I put a B as the final note when it should have been an A. Sorry.

Re: Gilderoy

Gille Ruadh
The old ballad of Gilderoy was first published, we think, mid-17th century, some twelve years after the titular “hero” of the ballad (real name Patrick Macgregor) was hanged in Edinburgh with five of his gang after committing blackmail, robbery, cattle theft and murder. His nickname came from his red hair; you may be surprised, but the country with the highest percentage of red-haired people is in fact Scotland.

An Earlier Gille Ruadh
In the late 13th century the Irish chief Gilrodh was part of a revolt in South-West Scotland. It was unsuccessful, but the next year he came back with an army, saying he’d conquer or die. In the event he surrendered without resistance and was subsequently pardoned. His Irish followers fared less well, travelling North and being beheaded by the Glaswegians. Gilrodh (or Gilruadh) = the red haired lad.
