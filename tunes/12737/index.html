“Henry Reed’s Schottische”

The ‘once through’ linked to above is just the A & B parts Alan recorded and transcribed from the playing of Henry Reed. That transcription is ABAC X 2 and then concluding on the A part. There were some peculiarities in Alan’s transcription, double bars/measures, beats transcribed as if 2/4 time but squeezed into a 4/4 measure, and all the combinations using the greater than sign ~ > ~ have (3 before them to emphasize what is generally taken for granted, basically a division by 3 rather than 4, for example, instead of merely E>F Alan gives it as (3E>F. It’s not wrong, hardly, but isn’t necessary, generally. Here’s where you can find his transcription:

Fiddle Tunes of the Old Frontier: The Henry Reed Collection
http://memory.loc.gov/ammem/collections/reed/
S for Schottische ~ to chase up Alan Jabbour’s own transcriptions, including for this one played and transcribed ABACABACA ~ unnambed schottische in C:
http://memory.loc.gov/ammem/collections/reed/titleS.html

You can hear it here - realPlayer / MP3 / WAV:
http://memory.loc.gov/cgi-bin/query/D?afcreed:2:./temp/~ammem_F3HM::

I quite like this played as a highland fling with just the A & B parts…
