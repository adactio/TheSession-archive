Flute-friendly version

I play the first part of the tune like this:

K: Edor
B2AF BFAF|~E3F EDFA|B2AF BFAF|DFBA FE~E2|
B2AF BFAF|~E3F EDBA|BEEF DEFA|dBAF FE~E2:|

I think this is almost the same as what Michael McGoldrick does on the low whistle.

In the sleeve notes of Mary MacNamara’s solo album, Jackie Small informs us that the tune was originally composed by Tipperary fiddler Sean Ryan as “The Glen of Aherlow,” but Paddy Canny and P.J. Hayes renamed it after their piano accompanist when they recorded it.

↳ Posted by slainte on July 2nd, 2004.

See The Fiddler’s Companion for the history of this tune and the related English tune known as Brighton Camp.

When did Sean Ryan compose this?

If James Morrison recorded this tune (The Glen of Aherlow) sometime between 1921 and 1936 on The Professor,(1) and Séan Ryan didn’t start composing until the early 50’s, when he was about 30 years old(2), then Séan Ryan could not have written this tune.

Bridie Lafferty

Paddy Canny and PJ Hayes recorded this tune with my Great Aunt ( grandmother’s sister) Bridie Lafferty. It was her favorite reel to play on the piano, hence the name “Lafferty’s Favorite” or Ril Ui Laithbheartaigh.
She had also played with the Castle Ceili Band, Kincora Ceili Band among others.
Her house in Drumcondra, Dublin was a favorite stopping off point and parlor partys with all of the greats in the 50’s and 60’s
were common occurances.
Patrick Murphy

Re: The Glen Of Aherlow (S: Seán Ryan)

Since Seán Ryan wrote several of my favourite tunes ever, I’ve always wondered how he actually played them himself. Surprisingly, in the years since that video of Seán playing The Glen of Aherlow came up on YouTube no one has transcribed his version of this reel, so here it is, an amalgamation of several repeats, as close as possible to the recording: https://youtu.be/q0pXmS1Ljmk?t=5m5s

Interestingly, there’s a notable difference in Seán’s original endings – never mind the slight variations, one phrase in the last bar stands out as it brings the tune towards an E minor feel rather than the thoroughly “modal” one that we’re used to thanks to sessions and various well-known recordings of the reel.

Seán plays:
B,E~E2 B,EGB | dBAF GE E2
as opposed to the commonly played:
B,E~E2 DEFA | dBAF FE~E2

I like both of these versions but this brings up a question of the author’s intent that could spiral into a discussion on whether “the original version” of the tune has a special value. I think if it helps one feel more connected to the musician in question then it surely does but there’s more than that.

It’s definitely not out of the question that Seán Ryan himself played this reel in yet another way on another occasion. But I’ve noticed somewhat of a trend in several of his minor tunes that their transcriptions in “The Hidden Ireland” also share. The “simplified” versions of his tunes that we usually hear in sessions – while it could be said they stood the test of time and got polished by other musicians in the process – lost a bit of character that Seán put into them, namely a few of those “dreamy” twists with wistful, lonesome quality. A good example of that would be The Dash to Portobello that in Seán’s own rendition ends like this: [gfed Bdgd] as opposed to the commonly played [gfed Beed]. That looks very subtle on paper but sounds quite different. Good thing we now have both the tunebooks and the solo recordings of the composer to try various takes on these fine tunes!
