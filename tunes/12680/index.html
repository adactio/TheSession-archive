X: 2 ~ “Katie Mac’s Waltz” ~ C: Kane O’Rourke

Square eyes and tired ~ now that I’ve played through it some I see your structure a little better, and here’s a better transcription than the large correction I’d sent yesterday just adding spaces between note groupings of your own transcription, | FE FG AF | instead of |FEFGAF| ~ this one using repeats and endings… As it is a repeat I’ve added a few of the different ways it took me, including swing (>), which your use of triplets suggested.

Now for a little comment on this tune, your waltz. As a dancer and musician and teacher of both, it’s a bit ‘too much’ for me, oversized, and the short-long-long feels clumsy, and some bits just don’t seem to go together well, especially the middle or B-part. For me it doesn’t have a nice and easy flow to it, and the 3 (c3 cB / c2- c>B) also, for me, adds more drag than interest. I’d be inclinded to either play this as three repeating 8 bar parts/phrases or just two 16 bar parts/phrases, or possibly with one of the parts as a ‘trio’, AABBAACC… That would fall better into a commmon dance tune structure. As it stands, for me, despite some interesting and pleasant bits, it drags, and I lose interest in it as a musician and a dancer… It’s not one I’d come away from humming or lilting. The short-long-long is odd, unusual, meaning the A part with repeats, ( 8:8), is normal, as are waltzes with 16-bar parts that repeat, (16:16), and there are some long 3 part tunes of 32 (16:16) bar parts, but the short one followed by two long ones is quirky, meaning not really comfortable, personally speaking… It is quite normal for there to be three part tunes with 8 bar parts, repeating, and for 2 part tunes/waltzes, with 16 bar parts repeating, but mixing it up is causing me to wrinkle my brow, doesn’t sit well with me, personally, doesn’t get my feet going. As it stands, it would flow better for me if it made that bit more sense dance-wise. I like the C-part, now that was fun to play, but somehow it all just doesn’t fit together - for me… ~ But, I’ll return at a later time and give it another go. ~ ‘c’

All three of these waltzes, it seems, one obvious by title, are newish compositions. I’m not saying that disparagingly, just a point, and shaking convention, odd bar counts, disjointed melodies, a bit of flash, or more, is not uncommon where new age composition is concerned. At least “Josefin’s Waltz” holds together a bit better, in my opinion, however, I’d be tempted not to repeat the B part, in which case it would be, in total, 64 bars (AAB = 16/16 + 32). But, that’s my inclination. As it is, it’s not that interesting a waltz for me to keep hold of it, even if it does, IMO, make a little more sense to me than this one, which I find ‘clunky’… :-D

↳ Posted by ceolachan on April 16th, 2013.

Josefin’s Waltz is Swedish (composed by Roger Tallroth). The Pi Waltz is Danish (composer unknown - traditional - not sure that makes it a recent composition). It’s more that in Scandinavian countries they’re much less tied to rigid rhythmic structures and odd (i.e., strange to us) numbers of bars are far more common.
By the way, Josefin’s has a 16 bar A part and a 24 bar B part.
