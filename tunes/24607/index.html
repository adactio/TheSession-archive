Six comments

Dan Dave Dan’s slide / Art O’Keeffe’s No. 1 (S: Denis Murphy)

This unusual tune with uneven parts, which has recently gained traction in Sliabh Luachra after decades of oblivion, could be associated with several local musicians. I learned it from a recording of Denis Murphy, where it is played in a set of nameless slides – but it soon turned out that there are more archival sources from the area featuring other players of note.

Billy Clifford has christened this slide Dan Dave Dan’s in honour of Dan “Dave Dan” Cronin, a local tinwhistle player who lived near the Quarry Cross and who used to play this tune, as evidenced by a tape from the early 1970s published by the Handed Down Sliabh Luachra Audio Archive.

Denis Murphy can be heard playing this tune on two archival recordings, one of which has also been uploaded to the Handed Down Archive, while the other can be found in the Breandán Breathnach Collection at the Irish Traditional Music Archive (642-ITMA-REEL). Denis is joined by a whistle player who *I think* is his neighbour Art O’Keeffe – who was a fine fife and whistle player back in the day (you can find some solid recordings of his playing and singing from 1949 in the RTÉ Archives online).

I could well be wrong but there are three things pointing to this being Art O’Keeffe on the recordings. First, there’re Breandán Breathnach’s notes listing the performers of this same sequence of tunes on a 1966 reel-to-reel. Then there’s the second tune in the set that the whistle player went into – it’s called Art O’Keeffe’s Jig on the 1949 recording of Denis made by Séamus Ennis (this leads me to think Denis associated these two slides with Art O’Keeffe and played them in this order – but only when playing with him). Finally, Art O’Keeffe played in the local fife & drum band and this tune sounds very much like a marching tune to me!

On the other hand, Dan Cronin recorded a solo tape of himself playing on the tinwhistle roughly in the same time period that is stated on the Handed Down tape of Denis. Dan “Dave Dan” was clearly a big admirer of Denis Murphy’s music, as about a third of his tape features separately made recordings of Denis – but there’s no telling who learned it from whom! “Dan Dave Dan’s” is a great name for a tune though – and refreshingly unique, so I’m happy to follow Billy’s lead on that! :-)

The first transcription follows Denis Murphy’s setting of the tune which you can listen to here:

Re: Dan Dave Dan’s slide

ELCBK, yes, that’s right – the source for both ABCs is the same but the settings are different. I usually post a simplified and a detailed version of the same transcription because some people find the ornamented versions overwhelming. There’s no easy answer as to what amount of ornaments or variation is optimal and any readable notation would approximate to a degree – I just prefer to have several degrees of approximation to choose from!
