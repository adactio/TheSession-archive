Haudanmaan Hääpolska

This is a Finnish tune called Haudanmaan’s Hääpolska, or wedding polska; traditionally, this tune is played at weddings in Finland. Loads of fun, especially for confusing your friends at the Irish sessions :P

Re: Haudanmaan Hääpolska

Nice and catchy tune. It is a mistake to think that all music with a vague count of three in a “bar” is a waltz. Case in point: “Fanny Power” is written in 3/4, but to play it as a waltz is to murder an otherwise beautiful little tune that is best played IMHO in 1.

The first version above of this Finnish tune is indicated as 3/4 but played in 6/8. The second is indicated as 3/4 but is in fact written (and played) in 3/2. If we write 3/4 or 6/8 or 9/8 etc. at the start of a piece of music this figure is intended to tell the reader how many beats of what kind are to be found in each bar or measure. Writing 3/4 at the start and then proceeding to write out the music in 6/4 is not helpful. This happens quite a lot on the Session. Standards, people, standards.

Although I have been a professional musician most of my life (it ain’t over yet!) I am not a stellar sight reader myself, so I am sympathetic to those amongst us who are wary of what is referred in these pages as “dots”. The problem with “dots” is not the system of dots itself. It is rather the erroneous but commonly held belief that music is difficult. Written music is immensely less complicated and easier than ABC. The reason for this might well be that it is graphic. ABC requires an understanding of what each upper or lowercase letter refers to and what the punctuation indicates. But I suspect I’m soon to be hoist on my own petard when someone here points out that ABC really isn’t all that difficult!

Here endeth this morning’s rant.

Re: Haudanmaan Hääpolska

Hi jackmccann2,

I’m not sure how you get that the first setting is played in 6/8 but the second in 3/2. I hear them both three to the bar. Also not sure how you get that the second setting is written in 6/4 when it is plainly in 3/2 (even though the time sig is given as 3/4 - but that’s just because of a lack of knowledge as to how to change it).

It has been pointed out many, many times that the tunes here are grouped by time signature, which is preset. Hence a lot of 3/4 tunes which are not waltzes are posted under WALTZ, usually with a comment that the tune is not a waltz (but often people don’t read the comments).

Also, as has been pointed out many, many times here (on this site), ABC code is used to produce the scores (with those dots). You are not expected to read the ABC - the score is there for you to read. What it does mean is a consistency of presentation, rather than people uploading scores in pdf or jpeg form, produced in different programs (Sibelius, MuseScore, etc) with different house styles and engraving rules.

When compared to other tune collection sites, I think the session.org does a pretty good job.
