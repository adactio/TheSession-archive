Muddy Water

We heard this first at Halsway Manor when we went for a Playing-for-Dance course. Stephen and his wife from the Arish Mel Ceilidh Band were on the course and produced extra music to help the tutors out. We thought this was a nice lively dance tune and have introduced it to our local U3A group. We discovered more about its origins recently on https://tunearch.org/wiki/Muddy_Water_(1)

Re: Muddy Water

Pennsylvania fiddler Sarah Armstrong played this tune around 1944 for Samuel Bayard, who published it in his
collection “Hill Country Tunes”
https://tfmag.s3.amazonaws.com/Coved/Hill+Country+Tunes.pdf

↳ Posted by kiirmit on November 10th, 2025.

Re: Muddy Water -- A Recording

This is such a fantastic tune (so many of Sarah Armstrong’s tunes are wonderful). Her Muddy Water is one of my favorite of the tunes Bayard collected from her (I also love the Snouts and Ears of America, and Sarah’s own tune, Dream Song, which she composed herself).

To Fiddle Aunt’s question above about whether there are any recordings of Muddy Water, I found this version posted in 2025 by Paul Kirk on his YouTube channel:

https://youtu.be/z6TIyt6PfYg?si=yU-y7pFXaq-3y36t

As a southwestern Pennsylvania native myself, I feel a special affinity for some of these tunes. Sarah was of Ulster-Irish descent, and her musical relatives and forebears brought and maintained a rich tradition of tunes, as well as picking up and sharing other tunes that were part of the musical milieu of this unique slice of Appalachia.
