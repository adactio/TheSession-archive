Gan Ainm

This light classical tune simply entitled “Polka” appears in the Reilly Family MSS (c1900s) of Drumreilly, Co Leitrim

↳ Posted by ConorW on December 2nd, 2022.

Re: Gan Ainm

I don’t know if it was notated like this in the manuscript, but it’s not right. In bar 8 of the B part, I guess the first four notes should be quavers (16th notes). Also, the missing 7th bar in the B part should probably be the same as the 3rd bar.

As a matter of interest, does anybody play this sort of thing these days?

↳ Posted by Kenny on December 3rd, 2022.

Re: Gan Ainm

Thanks Nigel, semi quavers added in first part. Manuscript is missing 7th bar so I just put in the 3rd bar as a placeholder until the tune is identified.

Re your other point, is light classical music played nowadays by traditional musicians? The answer is probably no, the vast majority of trad musicians are not playing this genre. Was it played by Irish musicians in the 19th and earlier 20th centuries - yes it was. We know this because this music is found in manuscripts of Irish musicians all over Ireland from that period. The majority of these specific tunes didn’t take hold in the tradition but some did and are still played today. It is only because Irish musicians were interested and experimented in popular worldwide music at that time such as classical music that we have many of the rhythms and tunes in our great tradition today: polka, waltz, schottische (many of these tunes evolved into barndances), varsovienne and mazurka.
