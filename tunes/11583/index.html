The Downshire Camperdown Quickstep

The first tune is not the one I’m interested in, but the one I’m going to edit.
A muh better version is from A Collection of Marches, Quicksteps, Strathspeys and Reels, published by Thomas Calvert, Kelso, 1n 1799(?)
The Downshire in the title is obviously County Down, while Camperdown refers to the recent victory over a Dutch fleet at the Battle of Camperdown in 1797
This later version was published by O’Farrell - Pocket Companion, vol. 1 (c. 1805). It is simply called the Downshire March

Re: March Of The Men Of Devon

Aye, Kenny, that’s the same group of players from Concerto Caledonia, including Lauren MacColl and Shona Mooney.

The earliest printings of this tune are:
Calvert’s A Collection of Marches & Quick Steps Strathspeys & Reels (c. 1799)
O’Farrell’s Collection of National Irish Music for the Union Pipes (1806)
John Paff’s Gentleman’s Amusement No. 2 (1812)

Some of the earliest manuscript settings are:
John Miller’s music manuscript (1798-1801, Strabane, County Tyrone)
John Beach’s music copybook (1801-1825, Gloucester, Mass)
R Hughes’ music manuscript (1823, Shropshire)
Lionel Winship’s music manuscript (c. 1833, Northumberland)

O’Farrell claims that the tune is Irish. He is fairly accurate in stating the provenance of his tunes as either “Irish” or “Scotch”. There are a number of tunes in his collection with no stated provenance – presumably in those cases he didn’t know.
I wonder if the March of the Men of Devon is a later, simplified version of The Downshire. The claim for the tune being Welsh is that “the limestone trade by boat over the Bristol Channel used to bring many Devonians to the south coast of Wales”, according to the TTA.

The Downshire Camperdown Quick Step, X:4

X:4 is the oldest printed version of this tune, found in Thomas Calvert’s “Collection of Marches Quicksteps Strathspeys & Reels”, Edinburgh and Kelso 1799 (?). It is the version played by Concerto Caledonia, see Kenny’s comment above for the video. I think it’s the best version.
The Camperdown part of the title is unnecessary. It was added by a patriotic soul after the defeat of the Dutch fleet in 1797. I have, rather cheekily, renamed this tune.
Thanks to DonaldK for transcribing the tune into ABC and for the information in his post above.
