↳ Posted by AlexKG on January 11th, 2009.

I admit, I wrote this tune

Well I’m sort of hesitant to admit it given the discussion above, but I composed this tune in 1997. It was the first tune I wrote, at age 17 (as Alex mentioned). I play most of the quarter notes as bowed triplets. This tune was first published in a tune book called “Along the River” (http://www.alongtheriver.com/) which is a collection of compositions by contra dance musicians in western Massachusetts and Southern Vermont. It is also published in my tune book “A is for Avocado” (www.ethanhw.com). This tune was never intended to be an Irish session tune; it is more accurately a contemporary contra dance tune. It has been recorded five or six times in addition to the Moving Violations version linked above.
- Ethan Hazzard-Watkins

I learnt this beautiful piece from west coast US banjo player, DB. He tells me it came to him via Ben Hockenberry of Rochester NY. I have since heard this tune at sessions in Toronto , Vancouver, Seattle, and Austin usually followed by The Congress, Brenda Stubbert’s, or Farewell to Cherynobl if in modified D minor.

Reading the comments above of 14 years ago once again evokes a degree of cringe from us newer generation because on the olds because;
1). Ethan wrote a fun and complex piece , wether or not you feel like playing it.
2). It has grown in sessions over time because it’s on now at least 5 recordings since Ethan wrote this.
3). We should encourage new tunes to be written by players of any age. That Ethan wrote this at 17 is fucking phenomenal and makes him legendary in banjo circles wether the gatekeepers of Trad care to pay attention.
