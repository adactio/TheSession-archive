Sitting in the Stern of a Boat

Yeah, okay, this is a slow air, not a strathspey, although I’ve heard this played uptempo with a bit of snap, which I hope can qualify it for inclusion here. Bit of a stretch, I’ll admit, but I love this tune and thought it should be in the database. My friends and I like to play it both ways. It’s a tribute to the melody that it can be heartachingly sweet played slowly, and still sound wonderful played at a jauntier pace.

Composed by the Rev. William McLeod in the late 1700s when he was assigned to a parish in Argyll and had to leave his native Bracadale on the Isle of Skye.

Re: Sitting In The Stern Of A Boat - A complete change of opinion

So I went to the library, looked at the Hunter book and found that I was wrong. I am assuming that Hunter had access to Captain Simon Fraser’s “Airs and Melodies peculiar to the Highlands of Scotland”, 1816 in which the tune first appeared.
So in the second part there is only one D#, in the 4th bar. I was wrong and apologise to trish santer. I see now that Ossian introduced the D# in the first full bar. I’ll adapt X:2 to reflect this and will create another version to reflect what Ossian played and many still do.

Re: Sitting In The Stern Of A Boat

Simon Fraser says of the tune:

“[Mi’m shuidh’ an deireadh bàta] is the composition of the Reverend Mr McLeod, who, the editor thinks, was minister of Bracadale, in the Isle of Skye, before the incumbent, and afterwards removed to Argyllshire, being an adieu to his native country. The editor’s father was extremely fond of this air, as characterising two friends in early life, very partial to him, and whom he highly esteemed, Major McLeod of Balmeanach, and Colonel MacLeod of Talisker. The composer gives a most poetical description of his sailing from Skye, whilst every well-known object, one by one, gradually recedes from his sight, till at last no trace of Skye is visible, except the ‘Bhan Bheinn,’ or white mountain, and, when it vanishes in the misty vapour, he concludes with a benediction on all he left behind him, worthy of a genuine poet.”

Re: Sitting In The Stern Of A Boat

Donald, thanks for showing me scans of the original printed versions;
- Aird’s Selection of Scotch English Irish and Foreign Airs vol. 5, 1797
- Simon Fraser’s Collection, 1816
Both are essentially the same. In nineteen years Fraser has added the D# to the first full bar. Fraser also adds grace notes and a bass line, but little more.
So indeed Ossian were following Fraser. As I first heard this tune played by the recently formed Ossian at Kinross Folk Festival in 1977, this is how I hear it and play it.
