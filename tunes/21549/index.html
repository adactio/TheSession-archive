Old Langolee

Also known as Humours of Ballamagairy or Humours of Ballamaguiry. This is the version in Clinton’s Gems of Ireland. Tunearch.org/Fiddler’s Companion says, “‘Lango Lee’ supposedly derives from an Irish phrase for an engorged penis, although Francis O’Neill maintained ”It has never been our good fortune to learn the meaning or derivation of the word or phrase “Langolee”…“ (Irish Folk Music, 1910). ”

I would really like to know where or what Ballamagairy/Ballamaguiry is, as all Google hits for the word are for this song.

Old Langolee, X:3

Version in Beethoven’s arrangements of Irish songs, WoO 153, no. 8, under the title “Farewell Mirth And Hilarity: Norah of Balamagairy.”

Lyrics by Sir Alexander Boswell (1775 - 1822):

Farewell mirth and hilarity,
Love has my heart in cruel subjection;
Ah me! Norah in charity
Spare a fond soul one throb of affection.
Why, as I pass’d, did I gaze on her casement,
Alas! With one look all my courage she shook!
But while I linger’d in moonstruck amazement,
Not a smile all the while cheers recollection.

Refrain:
Love, love, wins us by treachery,
Yet leaves no choice but humble submission;
What spell can conquer this witchery,
Woman our bane’s the only physician.

Far, far hence tho’ I fly from her,
Where other shores are kiss’d by the ocean,
Blest powers! Draw but one sigh from her,
Let her not live thus dead to emotion.
Yet I must steal one last glance ere I leave her,
Perhaps in her heart she may grieve when we part;
Hope, ah I dread thee, deluding deceiver,
Fair thy cup turn’d up, bitter the potion.

(Refrain)

Ah me! Had we the agency
Of a kindhearted feat little fairy,
Good bye then to the regency,
Norah, the witch of Balamagairy!
Looks she, or speaks she, the lads are all sighing,
She scatters her spells, and then ev’ry heart swells;
Not a young clown but is pining and dying,
Ah! The fools, thus she rules Balamagairy.

(Refrain)

Re: Old Langolee

The play She Stoops To Conquer by Oliver Goldsmith intended to have in it a song based on this tune:

AH me! when shall I marry me?
Lovers are plenty; but fail to relieve me:
He, fond youth, that could carry me,
Offers to love, but means to deceive me.

But I will rally, and combat the ruiner:
Not a look, not a smile shall my passion discover:
She that gives all to the false one pursuing her,
Makes but a penitent, loses a lover.

It was meant to be sung by the character Kate Hardcastle, but it turned out that the actress cast in the role, a Mrs Buckley, couldn’t sing, and the song was left out.

A recent recording of the tune with Beethoven’s arrangement and Goldsmith’s lyrics is on the album “Beethoven’s Irish Songs Revisited”, performed by Shauna Buckingham, Niall Gallagher, David Scott, Alison Thomas, and Duo Chagall.
