Curious & Curiouser ~

I had that soak but followed by a restless night. I did some more searching and have found several hanter dros that are very close to each other and these two, all using basically this first part, the A-part, or some version of it, followed by a different B-part, though there were similarities between a few of these too.

One way or another, and hopefully I can chase up more information on them, I’ll be back with these and some ‘content’. I don’t have the ‘old’ collections with me, but I believe one of these was in a large Breton collection that was made in the 1800s… I’ll see if I can chase up someone with a copy on hand, or maybe one of you have it. There were a few early collections of Breton dance tunes, for example the Mahé manuscript, which was reprinted in the ‘90s but has very few if any hanter dros (treble tunes variously transcribed as 3/2, 3/4, 6/4, 6/8, and even as ’mixed meter ~ 4/4-2/4, etc…)

The results of all this steppin‘ and dawdlin’ around the place has had other results, soon also to follow… The dance like the tunes are beautifully minimalistic and mesmerizing…very ‘community’, and adding to the reasons why I love the ‘socialism’ of them…Fest Nos!!!

Proof of madness & the all night hanter dros ~

A-Part: Alternate ways and keys, this melody occurs in all sorts of ways, sometimes just 4 measures, without the repeat, sometimes the first 2 measures with a repeat (=4), & sometimes the 3rd part for some 4-parters, and even as part of a hanter-dro/an-dro… The Bombarde & Biniou-koz is a bit like penny whistles, there are a choice of keys and some folks prefer them high while others like them low, so recordings and transcriptions can be in a number of different keys for the same melody, as shown here. Any of the variations given in one key can be transposed to another key, as given, in this case just takes on the A-part, the B less varied and to follow:
