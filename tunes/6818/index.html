The Duke of York’s Camp

Another sparkling fiddle jig from the pen of the 18th century Scottish fiddler John Clark. Note that in the last three bars of the B-part the tune goes up into the 4th position on the fiddle.
This tune goes very well with another tune by the same composer, Colonel Graham’s Favourite, which I submitted recently (tune #6810).

The Duke of York’s Camp

If you’re playing this tune in F on the fiddle I suggest a couple of easy alternative fingerings I’ve worked out for the last 4 bars of the B-part:

The first is,
101 212 | 212 2 4 | 311 420 | 121 1 ||

The second, which differs from the first only in the 3rd bar from the end, is,
101 212 | 323 2 4 | 311 420 | 121 1 ||

In the first version at the end of the 4th bar from the end you slide your 2nd finger from the G up to the A at the beginning of the third bar from the end, so that you are now in the 2nd position. Then, in that bar you slide your 2nd finger up to the B-flat in the 3rd position.

In the second version of the third bar from the end you shift up from the 3rd finger on the A to the 2nd finger on the B-flat in the 3rd position.

In both versions of fingering, in the second bar from the end you shift the 1st finger in the 3rd position on the A back down to the F in the 1st position.

There’s not much to choose between these versions, but I have a preference.

Other fingerings are possible, but they tend to be a little more complicated and don’t flow quite as easily as the versions I have suggested.
