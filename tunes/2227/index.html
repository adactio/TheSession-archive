Coachman’s Whip

Composed by Vincent Broderick. Mich Neilsen , of Copenhagen, put me onto this fine flute tune when he sent me a tape years ago of 2 Danish musicians playing Irish music on flute and harp.
Recorded by Desi Wilkinson & Gerry O’Connor. Sometimes played with the 2 parts in reverse order, in fact I think that may have been how it was composed, but this version seems more popular.

The Coachman’s Whip

I’ve always played it as Kenny has it. However, VB’s book (The Turoe Stone) is proof that the tune was originally composed with the parts in reverse order. Another important difference is that bar 3 of each part has C nats instead of B, like this |E2cE dEcE|.

The Coachman’s Whip, X:5

Regarding the discussions about which part comes first and about the B or c in the third bar, I carefully listened to Vincent Broderick’s own recording on https://www.itma.ie/blog/vincent-broderick-tapes/, Tape 1 side B.

The part starting with a low D comes first, and there is no c in the third bar. Actually, he plays his c’s too sharp, somewhere between c and c#, so it’s clearly a B. The same third bar has a low D, not a high d. The last bar of each part has no e or B.

This is different from the version published in Turoe Stone Collection Vol. 1 (preview here: https://www.stretta-music.de/broderick-traditional-irish-flute-solos-nr-166376.html), but seeing that Broderick said in his Composer of the Year address “I don’t read music, and I don’t write music”, the version printed in that book is not necessarily his own.

↳ Posted by Harald Wellmann on August 23rd, 2025.
