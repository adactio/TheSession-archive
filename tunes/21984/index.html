Kilmore K

A tune composed by the Wexford/London/Leitrim accordion player Paddy Hayes and played on his second album,‘The Shores of Lough Melvin’. He plays the two reels in this set at a very steady slow reel speed. (The other tune, The Saltees, I’ve already uploaded to this site). It could be played as a hornpipe either I think.
The title refers to Kilmore Quay in County Wexford, not far from where Paddy was born grew up and learned his wonderful box playing in Piercestown. His brother Tommy R.I.P. was another excellent player and like Paddy had great humour and wit. In fact the clever use of Kilmore ‘K’ for the title is Paddy having the crack with us - since in this neck of the woods Kilmore Quay(Kee) is usually referred to as ‘The Kay’ or ‘Kilmoor Kay’. A walk along the beautiful riverfront in Wexford town is to go for a walk ‘along the kay’ . When the weather in the harbour at Kilmore Quay might be inclement, and the fishing boats tied up for safe shelter, locals might be heard saying something on the lines of ‘Crool baad day in the kay tuhday’….
Anyway I’m sure that Paddy would be smiling and cracking jokes at my attempts to put across the Forth and Bargy accent!!
