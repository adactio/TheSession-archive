The Castleblaney Piper

This is one of those things where the title’s definitely wrong as posted. This is the Castleblaney Piper and it was composed by Ed Reavy. Hare In The Corn is a completely different tune.

The Hare In The Corn tune (aka The Absent-minded Man) is here https://thesession.org/tunes/8444. The similarity in the A-parts cause the two to be confused on recordings (Paul McGrattan’s is one example) and tunebooks and online tune indexes, despite the fact that they have completely different 2nd parts and they’re in different keys.

Clarification: By “this tune” I meant the two settings that are posted above. Looking up a more official transcription of the tune composed by Ed Reavy, it is clear that it is really a different (though similar) jig.

This tune sometimes also appears under the title Cat in the Corner. To add to the confusion, there is also a slide that goes by the title Hare in the Corn, and there appear to also be a number of other tunes that get called by one or the other of these titles. Lots of fun here…

The Castleblaney Piper

A simple jig composed by Ed Reavy. The title of the tune has been attached to a wrong tune for years on this website, but this is the right one.

A bit of research shows that Castleblaney or Castleblayney is a town in Co. Monaghan, where Reavy’s mother used to live.

The version #1 is almost based on Sligo whistle player Carmel Gunning’s version. Hope a few other versions will be transcribed from other sources.

↳ Posted by slainte on September 25th, 2013.

Castle Blayney was the seat of the Irish branch of the Blayney (Blaenau) family from Montgomeryshire, Mid Wales. There are still quite a few Blayneys in this area if Wales.

Re: The Castleblaney Piper and The Hound and the Hare / Hare in the Corn

Pace Dr Dow, but IMHO, the first two settings are NOT The Castleblaney Piper, but versions of the jig from the set piece The Hound and the Hare (https://thesession.org/tunes/16094). The Boys of the Lough play(ed) this (including on Live at Passim https://thesession.org/recordings/911 ). The tune (and set piece), I would guess, predates Ed Reavey’s composition and was played by both Mickey and Johnny Doherty. (Track 8 of https://thesession.org/recordings/5911 probably includes the jig but I do not have the recording to confirm that.) Alan Feldman & Eamonn O’Doherty gave a transcription of Johnny Doherty playing this piece in their book The Northern Fiddlwr (p.95). From memory I believe Cathal McConnell called the jig from the set piece, The Hare in the Corn, which is what Paddy Glackin called it on his first LP. https://thesession.org/recordings/629.
Settings X;3 and X:4 are close to the published version of the Castlebaney Piper.
