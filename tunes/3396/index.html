Correction

By the way, this is indeed a D mixolydian scale, which surprisingly enough sounds major. Notice the C NATURAL in the song… Mixolydians sound almost major but sound funny/cool because of the whole tone between 7 & 8. You could swear the song is D major anyways because of the fact there is only 1 C natural played in the whole tune, which is used to lead up to the second part.

Re: March? Yes, but does this tune fall into other "types* of tune?

It’s obviously a modern composition by Michael McGoldrick who included ‘March’ in the title. But I wouldn’t put too much emphasis on that. Like a lot of McGoldrick stuff it is its own thing, rooted in traditional music but new at the same time. I’m familiar with this tune (and have seen him play it live a couple of times) but if I was given it to listen to for the first time without any other information, I would probably think it was jazz. Even when it breaks in The Noon Lassies I might not instantly have said it was ‘traditional’ music. He did call the album ‘Fused’ after all.

↳ Posted by 🎻 Melody Player 🎻 on July 22nd, 2026.

Re: March? Yes, but does this tune fall into other "types* of tune?

The A part of that tune always reminds me of the Trip To Dingle polka… It could certainly sound more like a march if it was played like one. The same could probably be said for it being played more like a reel or more like a polka… It doesn’t have the right structure to be a hornpipe, but a lot of marches can be played like hornpipes… So to answer your question, this tune could easily fit into boxes other than “march”.
