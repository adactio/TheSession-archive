Bridget Flynn’s

From Martin Mulvihill’s 1978 LP ‘Traditional Irish Fiddling from County Limerick’. He learned this tune “from his mother, who used to play [it] on the fiddle”.

Not the same, but some similar phrases here and there. The first part has echoes from Haughs of Cromdale ( https://thesession.org/tunes/6031 ) and the second part from Pigeon on the Gate ( https://thesession.org/tunes/517 ) or Cabin Hunter ( https://thesession.org/tunes/1183 ).

Irishtune.info lists it as a unique tune:

Bridget Flynn’s, X:2

This is how Paddy O’Brien plays it in his Tune Collection (volume three, reel #78).
He learned it from the Martin Mulvihill record, but it is interesting to see how
different Paddy’s interpretation is from the X:1 setting. The fourth and seventh bars of
the A part have beautiful phrases. Did Paddy’s brain create them, or were they on the
record?
