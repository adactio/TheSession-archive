A Curious Denis Murphy Jig

This is the title given for the tune on Mick O’Brien & Caoimhín Ó Raghallaigh’s “Deadly Buzz”. It says in the liner notes of said CD that Caoimhín spent a summer working at the Traditional Music Archive in Dublin (one of my all-time favorite flute players, Michael Tubridy, got him the job). My guess is that he heard this tune on a recording that bore only the label “Denis Murphy”. Hence the lack of a proper name for the tune. If anybody has an actual name for this one, please put in your 2 cents below…

-Jay

Trip To The Quarry

Simplified transcription for reference, from Tony O‘Connell playing the set “Gold ring / Trip to the quarry / Charlie Mulvihill’s jig” on his 2017 self-published CD “’live and well” (https://thesession.org/recordings/5810). The first part of the tune appears to be in A dorian; the second in A mix.

↳ Posted by gr8oss on January 5th, 2018.

Re: Trip To The Quarry

The Trip to the Quarry is indeed the name given to the jig by Denis, you can hear him commenting on it in one of the archive recordings - which I think are part of the Breandán Breathnach tapes - that you can order to listen to at the ITMA. I highly recommend exploring those for anyone into Denis Murphy’s music, whenever it’s possible to visit with all the lockdowns etc. I don’t think they’re readily available online.
https://www.itma.ie/contact/visit

Re: Trip To The Quarry

It’s a duplicate of this one, with parts reversed: https://thesession.org/tunes/11868

The name apparently comes from The Waivers, Denis Murphy named it so on a recording by Breandán Breathnach, in turn citing someone else in the family.

I only just happened upon this material from ITMA. I’d heard the jig before on the Deadly Buzz album by Mick O’Brien And Caoimhín Ó Raghallaigh.
It’s interesting the way Denis Murphy plays the C’s on the A string - the third note of the scale/mode(s) it’s played in. To my ears it’s sometimes a major third, sometimes minor third albeit on the sharpish side. It sort of hovers between A dorian and A mixo. Proper old-time playing - we don’t hear enough of that sort of thing these days!
---
Anyway, what a fine tune it is.
