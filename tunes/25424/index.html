Túr Mór Jig / Toormore Jig / Port an Túir Mhóir (S: Denis Murphy)

I learned this boisterous jig from an archive recording of Denis Murphy made by Breandán Breathnach in 1967. It can also be found in Ceol Rince na hÉireann 5 (no. 52) but I’m not sure if it’s ever been commercially recorded by anyone. To me it sounds like a tune I’d heard Sliabh Luachra musicians play a few times before even though I couldn’t find this version on The Session – maybe it’s the simplicity of it!

Toormore / An Tuar Mór is the name of a townland near Kilcummin, Co. Kerry. Denis might have picked the title himself, he names the tune on the source tape which you can listen to here:

https://www.itma.ie/blog/denis-murphy-breathnach-materials-jigs/

I’m using Breathnach’s title from the manuscript just to avoid confusion with the much better-known Toormore slide in B minor – although confusions may be unavoidable with several polkas also going by the same name… :-)

The Oak Stick (Túr Mór Jig / Toormore Jig / Port an Túir Mhóir, X: 3)

There are several relatives of this jig appearing in manuscript collections of Irish music, notably James Goodman’s Tunes of the Munster Pipers and Patrick D. Reidy’s manuscript (which he provided to Francis O’Neill).

With two distinct versions of the tune found in Canon Goodman’s collection alone this jig was surely a popular one in Kerry at the turn of the 19th century! That said, both of these versions have a different second part.

The setting that is the closest to Denis Murphy’s – both musically and geographically – is the one Patrick Reidy linked to fiddle player Daniel Kelleher of Clonough, Castleisland, Co. Kerry (Reidy’s own birthplace). The dance master provided a version in G major titled The Oak Stick. Confusingly enough, this is *not* The Oak Stick jig found in the Canon Goodman manuscript – the Goodman relatives are colourfully titled Foxy Mary (vol 1, no. 143) and Better than Worse (vol 1, no. 500).

Here’s Reidy’s / Daniel Kelleher’s version of the jig. You can find the scan of the manuscript online (may require a VPN depending on the country you’re accessing it from). Most of the tunes have conveniently been transcribed by Andrew Kuntz of the Traditional Tune Archive / Fiddler’s Companion but it’s fun looking at the original pages that O’Neill used!

https://sites.nd.edu/irish-hesburgh/2015/07/10/two-manuscripts-in-the-oneill-collection/

NB: I tweaked the endings ever so slightly for the tune to loop nicely, might revisit them later.
