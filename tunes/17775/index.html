Crúiscín Lán

From “Flowers of Irish Melody”, published in Belfast 1848. This is, as far as I can tell, a different melody from the already listed song on the site. The Irish title mean “Full Little Jug”. Here is the version of the lyrics found in the book mentioned above, with the original Irish chorus, beside an anglicised version. The Irish chorus means something like “The bright health of my darling, and the love of my girl, the love of my heart, my little jug full, full, full, oh!, the love of my heart, my little jug full.”

Let the farmer praise his grounds
As the huntsman doth his hounds
And the shepherd his sweet scented lawn
But I, more blest than they
spend each happy night and day
With my smiling little cruiscín lán, lán, lán
Oh! My smiling little cruiscín lán

Sláinte geal mo mhuirnín
(Slantha gal ma voorneen)
Agus gradh mo chailín
(Augus graw ma kolleen)
Gradh mo chroidhe mo chrúiscín lán, lán, lán
(Oh, graw moh khree moh khrooshkeen lawn, lawn, lawn)
Oh! Gradh mo chroidhe mo chrúiscín lán
(Oh! graw moh khree moh khrooshkeen lawn)

Immortal and divine
Great Bacchus, god of wine!
Create me by adoption thy son
In hopes that you’ll comply
That my glass shall ne’er run dry
Nor my smiling little cruiscín lán, lán, lán
My smiling little cruiscín lán

Sláinte geal mo mhuirnín, etc.

And when grim death appears
In a few but happy years
And tells me my glass it is run
I’ll say, begone, you slave,
For great Bacchus gives us leave
To drink another cruiscín lán, lán, lán
To drink another cruiscín lán.

Sláinte geal mo mhuirnín, etc.

Then fill your glasses high
Let’s not part with lips adry
Though the lark now proclaims it is the dawn
And since we can’t remain
May we shortly meet again
To fill another cruiscín lán, lán, lán
To fill another cruiscín lán

Sláinte geal mo mhuirnín, etc.

Cruiskeen Lawn

No. 13 in Clinton’s Gems of Ireland, just because we had 14 through 20 already, and because I like minor-key tunes, even if I may not go with all the articulations that Clinton has.

Fiddler’s Companion notes: “Cruiskeen Lawn” is the Englished form of the Gaelic title Cruiscin Lan/Crúisgín Lán (An) which means ‘The Full Little Jug’.“ Hence also the jig ”Tip the Cruiskeen," I suppose.

↳ Posted by NylonFlute on February 26th, 2022.

Re: Crúiscín Lán

As an aside, and on the off-chance that there exist a few folks who may not already know, ‘The Cruiskeen Lawn’ was the title of a regular column in the Irish Times under the byline ‘Myles na cGopaleen’, a pen name of Brian O‘Nolan (1911-1966; perhaps better known as Flann O’Brien, the name under which his novels were published). Personally, if I were rushing from a burning library with only enough time to grab a single book, it would be one of his - probably ‘The Best of Myles’, a collection of his hilarious, waspish, and erudite Irish Times pieces.
