Four comments

Flitter Daunse

Flitter Daunse is possibly one of the most popular tunes and dances on the Isle of Man. The dance was originally done on Good Friday. Flitters are limpets, which were cooked on the shore on this day, and eaten with barley cakes. After the food had been eaten, the leftovers were thrown into the sea, and the dance was performed to stamp out the ashes of the fire (hence the nursery kids that I’ve taught it to have all called it the stampy dance!).

Now, it is played very slowly to start off, and then gets faster and faster. The first version that I’ve included is played when it is slow, and the second is a version that is played to give it more movement as it speeds up.
