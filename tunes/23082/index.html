Fañchig Bihan

Fañchig Bihan This simple, solid but far from plain an dro was recorded by Barzaz on their 1992 album An Den Kozh Dal. It is the song in the dañs en dro set (Track 3) on that CD, sandwiched between iterations of Jean-Michel’s classic flute tune: Koat-houarn. I recommand the whole album.

Re: Fañchig Bihan

This tune is not a barndance of course but a famous Breton ring dance called an “en dro” or “an dro”. Many Breton ring dances are songs and this one is a fully fledged ballad (although perhaps just a few verses from a longer version).

The Breton band Barzaz recorded it in a set on their 1992 album An Den Kozh Dall; it is sandwiched between utterances of the atmospheric Koat-houarn, an an dro composed by Jean-Michel Veillon.

Fañchig Bihan means “Little Francie” or “Wee Fran”. I’ll attempt a translation so you can get the jest of it.

The tune has a reduced range of notes; it extends down to the 7th and up to the 5th. It is hexatonic (no 6th).

Le P’tit Fanfan
1.
Petit Fanfan mon domestique
allez pour moi à Kerouedi*
2.
Am’nez-moi mon cheval apprêtez-le
et j’irai avec lui dans les bois
3.
Bonjour à vous belle lavandière
vous battez bien lavez bien blanc
4.
Vous battez joliment lavez bien blanc
Laveriez-vous son mouchoir à un homme?
5.
Je ne bats pas joliment je ne lave pas bien blanc
je ne laverai son mouchoir à personne
6.
Car j‘ai un frère à l’armée et si il l’aprenait
il me couperait le cou de mes épaules.

an off-the-cuff English translation rendered from the French:

Little Francie

1.
Little Francie my servant boy
Go fetch for me in Kerouedi*
2.
Bring to me my steed the saddle and the bit
I will ride into the wood
3.
Good morning washerwoman
a-beating the laundry clean
4.
Beating it clean beating it neat
Would you wash a man’s kerchief?
5.
I don’t beat white I don’t beat clean
I will not wash no man’s kerchief
6.
For my brother is a soldier
If he knew about it my head would fall.
