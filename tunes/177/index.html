Sean sa cheo

I’m not sure we’re on the same page, but…

The tune played by John Doherty, and which he called “Lough Isle Castle,” is his version of a great Scottish fiddle tune called “The Reel of Tulloch” (https://thesession.org/tunes/7033). According to Danny O’Donnell* that tune is “closely related to ”Sean sa Cheo“, a Donegal tune believed to have been composed by Neili Boyle.”

However, “Sean sa Cheo” on the Chieftains 8 doesn’t appear to have much relation to “Lough Isle Castle”.

*= sleeve notes for ’John Doherty, Taisce - The Celebrated Recordings (1978)

↳ Posted by Nigel Gatherer on August 27th, 2007.

Seán sa Cheo: Not by Neillidh Boyle, and not by John Mc Grath

Neillidh Boyle never claimed to have composed this tune. In fact he said it was “one of the old, old Irish reels… a pipe tune, an Irish piper tune, … I don’t know who composed it”. It is in essence the same tune as Lough Isle Castle, only the third part differs. (In Lough Isle Castle, as John Doherty played it, the third part is a variation of the first part.) Neillie Boyle learned this tune from his mother, so it’s pretty certain that it was not composed by John Mc Grath.
Neillidh Boyle talked about this tune on the folktrax recording: http://www.scallta.com/ulstersongs/
