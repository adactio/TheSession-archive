Re: The Old Stage

The Old Stage …a.k.a; The Derby ram
It was sung by Bess Cronin in the 1950’s to Jean Ritchie’s microphone.
https://youtu.be/owIaYiVR5Kc?list=PLrCQ6Z-zDSwLHeLMfOBMetuIwMZDO8V1F

When Fred McCormick reviewed Dáibhí Ó Cróinín’s 2000 book ‘The Songs of Elisabeth Cronin’ for Musical Traditions, in the same year, he had that to say about this particular song;
<<Some of the most impressive English language songs Mrs Cronin knew were brought out during the Ritchies visit. I don’t know why, but I have a suspicion that Jean Ritchie achieved an empathy with Elizabeth Cronin, which went a lot deeper than the hospitality enjoyed by other collectors. Perhaps it’s that letter home, the one I mentioned earlier. Perhaps it’s the fact that they were both women, and both superlative singers. Perhaps it’s the fact that Jean Ritchie’s social background, albeit American and Kentucky, more nearly approached Mrs Cronin’s than did the backgrounds of any of the other collectors who recorded her. Whether there is any justification for such a suspicion, the Ritchies rationale for carrying out fieldwork - namely tracing the transatlantic origins of American folksongs - is nowadays regarded as fairly passé. Nevertheless, of all the people who collected from Mrs Cronin, they were the only ones who got her to sing The Derby Ram; that favourite of upstanding Americans from George Washington to The Dallas String Band.>>

I’ve thought it worth reporting this section of the review here.
The whole thing can be consulted at:
http://www.irishmusicreview.com/ecronin.htm

NB:
Notice how, since the high part of the tune carries the verses and the low part the chorus, the high part is of necessity the tune and the low part the turn in Cronin’s version.
Someone else may add the lyrics at some stage. If not I’ll return to it.
