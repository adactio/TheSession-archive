Feadogboy speaks!

We did indeed learn this from O’Neill’s, while searching for tunes having to do with Ballinamore. Ballinamore, Co Leitrim was where fiddler Ed McDermott hailed from, our first introduction to “the pure drop”

As swisspiper notated, the more we played it, the further it got from the “canonical” version.

Recorded in 1985, posted in 2007, commented on in 2011. Charter member, International Procrastinators Society!

Boys of Ballinamore

This is certainly the same jig as the Boys of Ballinamore. Derrane plays this one in D major, and the usual settings (e.g., O’Neill’s) are in D mixolydian.

↳ Posted by Fiddler3 on January 20th, 2016.

Nice tune. O‘Neill gives a C sharp in the key signature. That makes it sound very Lydian, with the C sharp becoming a sharpened fourth in a key of G during the A part. It was sight-reading it from O’Neill’s that brought me to this page. I’d be interested to know if the tune is in any earlier printed sources than O’Neills - to see if that C sharp is a misprint or not.

The Joe Derrane version has a C sharp too of course, but it starts with what is the B part of the O’Neill version, and then has a variant on it for its B part, all comfortably sitting in D major, not G.
