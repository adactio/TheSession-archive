Ten comments

This tune has a great pulse to it: DUM dee dum-dee-dum, DUM dee dum-dee-dum.

If you’re playing this on fiddle, try getting some drones in by playing the open E or A string where possible.

Key signature - shouldn’t this be A mixolydian?

’Cos it starts and ends on an A, and every time I play that G-natural it sounds odd (good odd, that is).

I s’pose it doesn’t matter much, seeing as the key signatures are the same.

By the way, I was reading this music history book and it said that back in the day “folk musicians” were the only ones who didn’t bother putting their tunes in the standard modes. And now nobody else uses them.

Ta ta.

The Dancing Dog, X:3

A version based on Track 5(a) of Jerry Read Smith & Tom Fellenbaum’s 1981 album.
Scottish pipers: play ‘high a’ instead of ‘high b’ in part 2; this version is otherwise wholly suited to the range of your instruments.
If I have arranged the first part significantly, my second part is closer to the original recorded version than either the X:1 or X:2 versions is.
NB: I hear this tune in 6/8, not in 12/8.

NB2: The original ABC for the original goes something like
(a balanced mix taken from the dulcimer + fiddle parts):
R: jig
M: 6/8
|:A2B cBA|eAB cBA|ded cBG|EAG A2z|
A2B cBA|eAB cBA|fga efd|BAE A2z:||
|:{cd}eec cde|Bdg Bdg|{cd}eec cde|Bdg a2a|
