Re: The Colosseum

I was looking for the composer of this tune and found a “hit” at
https://www.free-scores.com
According to this site it was written by George Allan (1864 - 1930)

George Allan (1864-1930) was a composer and arranger of music for brass bands who was born, and resided for most of his life, in New Shildon, County Durham, United Kingdom. As a composer he was a contemporary of William Rimmer and J. Ord Hume.
He has a number of compositions to hia name, some of which have entered the folk music repertoire. Some are listed in a Wikipedia entry and some in the source given above - but confusingly some share titles with James Hill and other compositions.

Re: The Colosseum

Very interesting, Roger, but according to the entry for this tune on the Traditional Tune Archive:
“The tune appears earliest under the title ”Harlequin Gambols“ as a country dance in Samuel, Ann and Peter Thompson’s Compleat Collection of 200 Favourite Country Dances, vol. 5 (London, 1788).”

It’s also in Middleton’s Selection…. of 1870, edited by Peter Milne, as“ Kay’s Hornpipe”.

The settings we have here seem more like “The Pet of the House” which, in the second part has a I-IV-I harmony in the first three bars, as opposed to I-IV-V for The Colosseum.

Re: The Colosseum / The Merry Man

With Wilson’s Companion to the Ballroom. 1816 as his source, O’Neill has the Merry Man Hornpipe in his Waifs and Strays of Gaelic Melody (#357). The first part is identical to the Colosseum but the turn, while broadly similar in form has a high g as its “drone note” (2nd and 4th notes) through bars 1, 2 5 and 6. (Maybe I should post it??). It might make a third part for the hornpipe.
