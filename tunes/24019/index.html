Two comments

An Roilleach

This jig was composed by Joe Carey, accordion player, from Gaoth Sáile (Gweesalia) on the north-west coast of County Mayo. The title means ‘the oystercatcher’, a bird that would commonly be seen on the shores of Gaoth Sáile. It is pronounced something like ‘un ryelyach’. He follows it with another of his own compositions ‘An siocán sneachta’, which Joe says was once commonly known locally by its translation as ‘the frost and snow bird’ (the common English name is ‘fieldfare’). The bird sometimes comes south into Ireland with the arrival of cold weather from the north. Both tunes featured in a recent TG4 television programme in the series ‘Ceolaireacht’ presented by Doireann Ní Ghlacáin. The episode explores traditional music in North Mayo. In the programme Joe Carey plays An roilleach followed by An siocán sneachta after explaining how the names were first suggested by his friend Paddy Fitz. He is accompanied by another local accordion player Liam Mac Congáil. The programme was broadcast on 28 January 2024.

Re: An Roilleach

The oyster catcher is a real dandy of the rocky shore. The Irish population is boosted in winter by the Icelandic flocks. Its call is very evocative.

The 1st phrase of this pleasant jig sounds like a "remininiscence’ of the song
Buachaill ón Éirne (Come by the hills)
https://thesession.org/tunes/13602

As presented here (X: 1), it is almost entirely (save a G# or two) in the key of A pentatonic major (no 4th, almost no 7th).
It transposes easily into G maj. on the flute, uilleann pipe and the tin whistle.
