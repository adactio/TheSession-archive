Strily Vale aka Rorate

It is interesting this tune has just appeared here, as I’ve spent many hours in the past week researching this very tune - and I’ve looked for it on TheSession for many years!

As noted, the tune is “Strily Vale” from James Oswald’s Caledonean Pocket Companion, Book 2, p.5, where it is listed among “scene tunes from the Tragedy of MacBeth … composed by Mr. Oswald.” There it is in A major and given a tempo of “brisk”. I can’t find a meaning for “Strily”, so it is likely a name rather than a description.

The tune has been used as a setting to the poem “On the Nativity of Christ” by Wiliiam Dunbar. Dunbar (b. c1460 d. before 1530) was a poet in the court of King James IV. In the Oxford Book of Carols (1928) under the title “Rorate (Nativity)”, several verses of the poem are set to “a little-known Scottish melody”, elsewhere listed as “traditional”. I haven’t found whether the carol goes further back than the Oxford Book. It is clear, though, that the identification of the carol’s tune with Oswald’s “Strily Vale” is little known. (I first found it on a 2014 post on the Mudcat Cafe under Rorate Coeli desuper.)

The tune has been recorded instrumentally as “Rorate” by various artists, for example, Bonnie Rideout, Maggie Sansone and Al Pettiway (“A Scottish Christmas”), and Sue Richards and Maggie Sansone (“Merrily Greet The Time” thesession.org/recordings/3580). Based on the title, their source was the carol.

As for “Struy Vale”, I suspect it was mistyped, misread or misremembered, ie., the folk process at work.
