Re: Lord Kitchener’s Pipes

Such an unusual tune title. Born in Ireland, Kitchener became a high officer in the British army who advanced his country’s imperialist interests in Sudan and South Africa. He was a top commander at the beginning of World War I, and his image was used in posters for recruitment, much the same as “Uncle Sam wants you” in the US. I very much doubt that the title refers to Kitchener as a bagpiper. Maybe he was known a smoker of briar pipes. I think a mold of his image may have been used in the production of clay pipes. I’d like to know how Martin came up with the title.

Lord Kitchener’s Pipes, X:2

This is closer to what Martin Mulvihill actually played on the recording. He has a different second time through the second part. He also plays a variation on this in the second time through the tune (he only plays it twice):
“variation last time” d^cd EFG|cBc EFG|d^cd EFG|dAB c2 A|
d^cd d^cd|cBc cBc|DED cGE|DED D2 ||
