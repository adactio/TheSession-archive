↳ Posted by Dr. Tøm on May 10th, 2012.

A wall of paper, staves and dots ~

As long as it is tied in any way to the dots one can’t cut loose, it can’t fly. The nature of the beast is that the results tend to be mechanical. Attention that should be on the music and those you’re sharing with is interrupted by a paper wall of staves and dots. The effect can vary between different players, but the dampening affect of sheet music is always there to some degree or another. I would have thought that ‘rehearsals’ are for after one has learned the tune on their own, and that the coming together of people with a common goal is to make a direct connection between the participating musicians, without anything like the dots getting in the way, muffling it, taking attention away from the hoped for main goal, to connect and set the groove.

It’s a subject that the discussions section is riddled with. Too often those who lack understanding attack the ear-centric as being anti notation, anti dots. Usually they’re wrong. I have a great appreciation for notation, music and dance, and its history. But I also know it’s failings well, on all counts… ;-)
