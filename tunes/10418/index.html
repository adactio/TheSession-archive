One comment

This is a traditional Flemish tune, called “de Vleegerd” (free translated, it means “the flail”). It is mostly accompanied by a dance that goes with the same name. I’ll try to explain the dance clearly.

Setup:
All couples stand in a huge square/rectangle, facing inwards, with the lady at the man’s right. The size of the square doesn’t matter, as long as every couple has another couple opposite them at the other side of the square. Obviously, at least 4 couples are needed.
e.g.: f3m3 f2m2 f1m1
m4 f10
f4 m10
m5 f9
f5 m9
m6f6 m7f7 m8f8
m = a man, f = a lady, the numbers are the number of the couple (only assigned to make it easier to explain the dance)
The couples 4, 5, 9 and 10 are “head couples”, 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 8 are “side couples”.

The dance:
Each man takes his partner in polka-hold, pointing inwards

part 1
bar 1-2: head couples go 3 side-steps to the middle, and greet the opposite couple on the 4th beat (so 4 greets 10, and 5 greets 9, and vice versa)
bar 3-4: head couples take 3 steps back to their place
bar5-8: head couples cross the square in 8 steps, changing place with their opposite couple (ladies cross back to back)
bar 9-16: head couples do the same again as in bar 1-8, (now men cross back to back), finishing at their start position
bar 17-32: side couples repeat what head couples did in bar 1-16

part 2 (music repeats)
bar 1-4: same as in part 1
bar 5-8: same as in part 1, but now one couple makes an arch and other goes underneath
bar 9-16: same as in bar 1-8, but now the other couple makes the arch
bar 17-32: side couples repeat

part 3 (music repeats)
bar 1-4: same as in part 1
bar 5-6: couples go back to the middle (4steps), and change partner with opposite couple (so f4 goes to m10, f10 to m4, f5 to m9, f9 to m5)
bar 7-8: the man takes the partner of his opposite back to his place (in 4 steps)
bar 9-16: same as in bar 1-8, so men end up with their original partner
bar 17-32: side couples repeat

the dance can start all over again, as many times as desired

notes:
all the dance has to be done in polka-hold, taking (hopping) sidesteps
couples that are not dancing (i.e. side couples in bar 1-16, head couples in bar 17-32) clap their hands to encourage the dancers.
