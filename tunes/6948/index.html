Guilt?

Hi Dow…

I had to go away and think on this a bit more. As you know, I usually look for the ‘dance’ in the music, it being on the whole ‘dance music’. Some tunes just make you want to dance, but there are also ‘party pieces’, or ‘show pieces’, and I wouldn’t disallow others their pleasure, but I prefer a tune that dances, especially if it falls under any of those numerous categories, like ‘reel’… A good dance tune helps the dancers, nudges them along and helps to identify the changes, is well phrased ~ not just for listening, but that is important too.

So, on that line, that is probably what disappoints me most about this particular tune, but for me, even as a ‘listening’ piece, something for ‘show’, for me ~ it doesn’t do it… And I have come back and played it and played it and played it, out of guilt, and on several different instruments, but it don’t make me jump, not the heart, not a rise in blood pressure, nope, no motivation to get up and step a bit, sorry…

↳ Posted by Kuddel on November 25th, 2007.

Re: The Trip To Fanore

Trip to Fanore is a hornpipe composed by Paddy O‘Donoghue following a visit to Eugene Lamb at the old school house in Fanore. It is featured on Eugene Lamb’s recording of the same name. The score can be found in ’Coel an Chláir‘ - Original Compositions Paddy O’Donoghue’.

Needless to say, none of tunes listed are Paddy O’Donoghue’s composition.

Real composer still not found !

Oups, investigating the possibility that the flutist Paddy O’Donoghue could be the composer of this tune, I listened to his recording (https://thesession.org/recordings/3918), and I found out that the hornpipe that he plays and calls “Trip to Fanore” is not the one transcribed above ! Maybe this one has in fact another name (some people call it Nollaig Casey’s, but I couldn’t find any recording of her playing it). For me, it sounds rather like a fiddle tune than a flute tune, since the melody goes as low as B on the G string.
