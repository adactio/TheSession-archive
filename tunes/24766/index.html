Eight comments

Loch Carron

The original 4-part tune is attributed to D. C. Mather.

At least two different 6-part settings exist, this is the more straightforward one in which each part is four bars repeated.

I’ve heard another 6-part setting where a number of the parts have variations on the repeats, which variations IMHO add nothing to the tune. Indeed the same could be said for the two added parts.

For a more compact (and arguably more musical) 4-part version play parts 1, 2, 3, 6 above.

↳ Posted by Richard D Cook on September 14th, 2024.

Re: Loch Carron

David Charles Mather was born in London in 1870. He won the Gold Medal at the Northern Meeting in 1899 and immigrated to the USA in 1901. He died in Montana in 1943.

The 4-part setting of Loch Carron appears in several old collections. 6-part settings appear in three collections listed in Pekaar. Loch Carron, especially with 6 parts, is very popular with competition pipers and pipe bands to conclude their March, Strathspey, and Reel medleys.
