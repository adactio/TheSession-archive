Re: The Piper’s Cave

I’ve removed James Sutherland as the composer, as the tune predates him by the best part of a century. I am also rather dubious about the alternate names given; they are certainly not widespread.

↳ Posted by Calum on June 1st, 2024.

Re: The Piper’s Cave

I’ve seen it as “The Piper’s Refuge” before, in Strathspey & Reel sets, and I think Bobby MacLeod used that name.

In the Logan Collection (1899) there are two James Sutherland tunes next to each other: “Miss Ainslie Grant Duff’s March” (which is practically identical to “The Piper’s Cave”), and “The Piper’s Cave,” which is a different tune altogether. It’s possible that the titles got mixed up in the book - I don’t know.

↳ Posted by Nigel Gatherer on June 1st, 2024.

Re: The Piper’s Cave

I’ve often wondered which Piper’s Cave the tune referred to and, having now done a little research, it seems to have been “…named after the Pipers’ room in the Barracks in Cairo” (https://pipetunes.ca/composer/james-sutherland/).

Also, the tune is widely attributed to James Sutherland (1865-1946), so I’m curious, Calum, about where this older version of the tune can be found.

↳ Posted by Nigel Gatherer on June 1st, 2024.

Re: The Piper’s Cave

Ah, thanks Nigel! I assumed it had somehow ended up attributed to the rather later Jim Sutherland [of the Radical Road, etc]. I ought to know better considering I published a book with exactly that information in it. I can only blame the sunshine, it’s clearly gone to my head.

Yes, I think that’s pretty clearly a mix-up in the Logan Collection - the later volumes especially weren’t well edited and there are quite a lot of title and attribution mistakes, never mind in the music itself.

As far as I can tell the epithet “piper’s cave” was a nickname for the band practice room wherever it was located. The band room in Cameron Barracks in Inverness is still known as the cave.
