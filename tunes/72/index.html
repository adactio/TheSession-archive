Forty-four comments

This popular reel can be found in O’Neill’s (number 728), and is a popular session tune. I like to play this one together with “The Mountain Road”.

In this setting of the tune I’ve included a tie between the A’s at the end of one bar and the A’s at the beginning of the next, so it becomes one longer note instead of two separate shorter notes. If it’s played this way, the tune has nice, almost American “swing” to it. Try this out, and if you don’t like it, then just ignore the ties.

Apart from that, try rolling the D at the very start of the tune, or the A and F at the start of the second part.

The Merry Blacksmith, X:14

This old Wexford version of this well-known reel was sent to P.W. Joyce and published in Old Irish Folk Music and Songs (1909). It appeared as an Unnamed Reel. It has a number of bars that are different from most settings and unusual endings for each of the parts, notated by Joyce as un-repeated. Joyce published a different version of the tune in another of his publications ( X4 above). Joyce states he got this Wexford version from the musician and composer, Grattan Flood, who was the organist in St. Aidan’s cathedral in Enniscorthy and a regular contributor of material providing him with other airs and tunes heard in Co. Wexford in the late 19th century.

It’ll be hard to prove the tune and the bird are historically connected though, since the Irish name only appeared in print with the likes of O’Neill and Breathnach who both pursued a Gaelicization agenda, while the earliest versions (mid 19th century to 1909) gave all sorts of other names.
Having said that, I am satisfied that the video you posted and the version of the Merry Blacksmith I carry in my head closely match!
