It is probably a variant of variant of “Well May the Keel Row”, a North of England song tune.

The Keel Row, X:2

This setting is from Dan Herlihy’s 2003 publication of manuscripts given to Padraig O’Keeffe’s pupils (page 40 tune 3). It is listed as a reel. It appears to have a name - Kelró which looks like broken Irish (Gaeilge) for Keel Row. Another title in Irish reads something like “An cicoan cú”, but that is little more a guess. Cú is usually a hound.

↳ Posted by brailsford on December 6th, 2021.

Re: Jennie’s Frolics / Johnny When You Die

O’Neill has a setting similar to X:1 (but named Johnny When You Die) in his Waifs and Strays of Gaelic Melody (#284) published in 1922. He got this reel from the Rice-Walsh manuscript, which were tunes taken down from the blind fiddle player from North Kerry, Jeremiah Breen. He notes that it is a variant on “Well May the Keel Row”, a North of England song tune, and that it appears as “Jenny’s Frolics” in Vol.2 of Paul Alday’s A Pocket Volume of Airs, Duets, songs, Marches etc, Dublin c. 1800.
