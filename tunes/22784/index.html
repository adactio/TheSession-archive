Three comments

The Bloody Field Of Culloden

Another stunning composition by fiddler Paul Anderson from his Granite album (2004), which I felt compelled to share.

Below is my original transcription as I submitted it, which I feel best portrays the phrases.

X: 1
T: The Bloody Field Of Culloden
R: reel
M: 4/4
L: 1/8
BA | G3 A G2 F2 | B,3 C D2 ba | g3 a g2 f2 | d6
BA | G3 A B2 c2 | d2 g2 Hf2 dc | d3 G G2 F2 | G6
BA | G3 A G2 F2 | B,3 C D2 ba | g3 a g2 f2 | d6
ba | g3 f d2 ^f2 | g2 a2 Hb2 dc | d3 G G2 F2 G6 ||
D^F | G3 D A2 B2 | GABc d2 ga | g3 f d2 c2 | d6
BA | G2 D2 ^F2 EF | GABc d2 ba | b3 a ^f2 a2 | g6
ba | g3 f d2 f2 | B2 c2 d2 ga | g3 a b2 c‘2 | d’6
ga | b2 g2 a2 b2 | g3 f d2 ba | g3 G G2 F2 | G6 |]

Yes, Donald, you’re quite right - I missed that!

I should also have said that it’s not a reel, but a slow air. Turn the speed down as low as it can go on the playback, and you’re approaching how slow it is. I am in awe of Paul’s skills as a fiddler and as a composer. Another wonderful tune of his on the same album is “Fingal’s Cave” not the one that’s linked to the album on this site, but his own tune. I think I’ll add it to get rid of any confusion…
