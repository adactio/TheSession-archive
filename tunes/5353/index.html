Over The Waterfall, X:5

From Backporch Flatpicking for Mandolin & Guitar:

This is one of our favorite tunes, and is another tune Alan Jabbour learned from Henry Reed. In the notes for the Library of Congress recording, The Hammons Family, under the song Mercian Titterary-Ary-A (whose tune is closely related), Jabbour tells a humorous story of collecting this tune from himself, in effect. He learned it from Henry Reed and played it on the album The Hollow Rock String Band, Kanawha 311. Friends of Jabbour’s played it at an oldtime fiddler’s convention for a man who went home and taught it to his father, an old time fiddler in Pennsylvania, who later played it for Jabbour when he was collecting tunes from the old Pennsylvania fiddler.

The A part, although it is in D, Mixolydian mode, ends on the 4th tone of the scale, G, instead of on D. This gives the feeling of having suddenly changed keys (and modes) in the last bar, from Mixolydian to G Ionian. The A part could be classified as G lonian on this basis. (See the Keys and Modes section.)
You can also hear this tune done on banjo and hammered dulcimer on the New
Golden Ring Five Days Singing, Vol. 1 album, on Folk-Legacy Records.

Re: Over The Waterfall

《 OVER THE WATERFALL. AKA and see “Fellow that Looks Like Me (The),” “Punkin Head.” Old-Time, Breakdown. USA, Virginia. D Major. Standard tuning (fiddle). AB (Silberberg): AABB (most versions). “Over the Waterfall” is sourced to fiddler Henry Reed (1884-1968) of Glen Lyn, Virginia, and was learned from directly from Reed and popularized by folklorist and fiddler Alan Jabbour through his recording with the Hollow Rock String Band in the late 1960’s.》
