Re: Little Red House In Cardiff

I must admit that calling the tune “Little Red House in Cardiff” makes me wince. There is never any hint of “little” (“bach”) in Welsh. The Welsh, “Tŷ Coch Caerdydd”, just means “Red House (of) Cardiff”.

When I first learned it, with other South Walians, we had quite a long speculation on where and what this Red House was. There was nothing about it in Cardiffian lore. Obviously not Castell Coch, or it would be called that. Perhaps an old inn?

So I went off to find out, and eventually discovered the suggestion that the tune was a descendant of “Red House” in Playford. One look at Playford, and that was obviously reasonable. Finding the Playford version as “Hen Dŷ Coch” (“Old Red House”) in Welsh made me feel it even less likely that the resemblance was coincidence. (“Ancient” is more literal than “old” because “hen” is in front of the noun, instead of after it as normal, but it sounds a bit weird and creaky to me in English.)

So I think that “Tŷ Coch Caerdydd” probably just means “Red House, Cardiff Version”. The Ancient and the Cardiff play well as a set.

Very late to the party here…

Looking at the second setting of this tune, it first part is identical to theat of another Welsh tune, Pant Corlan yr Oen https://thesession.org/tunes/6345 .

“I’ve never understood how a version with such major differences could have evolved - maybe someone could explain …”

These things do happen over time, especially with a tune that has versions dating back to the 18th Century or earlier. But in this particular case, I think it may simply be a case of someone accidentally (or deliberately?) mixing up two different tunes and other musicians, in their ignorance, following his/her lead. Since, in session repertoires outside Wales, Welsh traditional music is rarely represented by more than a small handful of token tunes, it is easy to see how this could happen.
