Jack’s Delight

Before anyone asks me, I have no idea of the origin of this tune.

I’ve submitted the abc to generate a score with dots, but it goes equally well if played with no “swing”. Take your choice.

Who was “Jack”, I wonder.?

A person? (“Jack” being a diminutive of “John”).

Or (perhaps more likely) “Jack” would be a reference to a sailor…

A Rant

This was one of the earliest dance tunes that I learnt . I can’t rmember my source but it is in the my book of English tunes that I started putting together when I first started playing for dancing and that is 40 years ago.
I’ve never heard it with swing and play it as a steady rant. It has the characteristics of a rant and certainly sounds and feels better that way. I’ve not searched anywhere else for it yet but am sure it must be North of England in origin.

↳ Posted by hetty on March 5th, 2009.

C18

A little bit of research shows that it was included in a number of collections of dance tunes: ‘Wrights Compleat Collection of Celebrated Country Dances’ c1742: ‘Rutherford’s Compleat Collection of 200of the most Celebrated Country Dances’ c1780-90. Also in Kerr’s 2v1 page 45. It has also been referred to as Scottish and as a hornpipe but note that not all hornpipes are dotted (played with swing).
