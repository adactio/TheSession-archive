Six comments

Mr. Barclay

Contains some f naturals and c sharps.
Composed by Aberdeenshire Fiddler Alexander Walker, born 1819.

↳ Posted by Connor Hickey on September 13th, 2019.

Re: Mr. Barclay

Thank for this, Connor. I have a particular interest in Alexander Walker because he lived not far from where my father was born and grew up (Huntly in Aberdeenshire), and indeed several generations of my forebears. One of his tunes was named “Master John Gatherer, Flutist, Strathdon” which excited me greatly, as there aren’t many tunes with my surname attached (I don’t count “The Cockle Gatherers”). Twenty or thirty years ago someone brought out a facsimile of his collection of tunes, which is upstairs in my attic somewhere. Now, of course, the original book can be accessed, probably through the National Library of Scotland site.

Alexander Walker worked as head gardener for Sir Charles Forbes at Castle Newe, Aberdeenshire. He was a fiddler and composer but, like Marshall before him, he had many other skills and interests, including botany, mechanics, journalism, archaeology and meteorology. Music-wise he was taught by eminent fiddler James Allan of Forfar (1800-1877) and in 1866 published a collection of his own compositions. Walker emigrated to America in 1870 and died at his farm near Williamstown, Massachusetts in 1905. He had seven children.

In his collection, “Mr Barclay, Braes of Enzie” is given in the key of D minor with no C#s, and it also says “May be played Strathspey or Reel”. Presumably your setting comes from the playing of Joseph Cormier, and it’s interesting to hear the C#s - adds a piquancy to the tune.

Mr. Barclay, X:2

This is in fact one of my all-time favorite strathspeys, both because of the minor key and the dramatic second half of the first part. This setting is more or less how Paul Anderson plays it and, I assume, closer to Walker’s own version. Much credit to Anderson for going through Walker’s entire collection on YouTube, and to the late Joseph Cormier for recording this gem on an album.
