Ur-Chnoc Chein Mhic Cainte - slow air, Bflat

Transcribed from Gerry O’Connor’s CD “Journeyman.” It is really in the key of Bflat, but Gm was the only way to get two flats in key signature.

A poem/song by Peadar O Doirnin, 18th century. aka “The Fair Hill of Killin” - Killin Hill is 2 miles N of Dundalk, Co. Louth.

Re: Ur-Chnoc Chein Mhic Cainte

It strikes me as odd that no one has noted that this air shares about 90% of its interval changes and, depending somewhat on the version, its timing, with the air known as An Buachaill Caol Dubh, The Dark and Slender Boy.

Further, the versions I have heard of tunes called by the English translation of Ur-Chnoc Chein Mhic Cainte, i.e., Killin’s Fairy Hill, tend to be even closer to An Buachaill/Dark Slender. (Viz. Mick Maloney’s w Eugene O’Donnell: https://thesession.org/recordings/564)

Ur-Chnoc Chein Mhic Cainte was written by Peadar Ó Doirnín, 1700-69 (at least according to the DIB) who lived in Ulster. An Buachaill Caol Dubh (at least according to Seán Ó Súilleabháin) is by Munster/Dublin poet Seán “Aerach” Ó Seanacháin, who seems to be one half to one generation older.

None of which is especially helpful in determining whether one air is the progenitor of the other or if it was simply floating around on the folk process and every Sean, Dick and Harry was setting his poem to it in the first half of the 18th century.

Oh and for those wondering how “Ur-Chnoc Chein Mhic Cainte” translates as Killin’s Fairy Hill, the significant mythological (thought to be a fairy hill) and folk historical site of Oriel was named after Cian, the father of Lugh who gave his name to Co. Lú (Co. Louth). But anyway, its name was changed at some point (in the 19th century?) from Cian’s hill to Killin’s hill. Nearly cognate and the name-changer figured no one would notice? Not sure.
