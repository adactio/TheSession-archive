Story

this is a long and boring story and i am over this tune and the experience of (trying) to put it up here, but since you are still interested, ill let you know what i know, which is only going to make this more confusing

i dont know anything about the tune, really
a while back, some old hippie in the mountains of california gave me this tune around a campfire, i was a guitar player back then just starting to blow the whistle a bit

it is so simple, that even i could play it back then, so i think it is a childrens song from somewhere

if you are at these crazy hippie parties and super baked and you just happen to have a whistle with you, and a guy says hey i know a tune, he plays it, i dig it, i ask him to teach me
i wrote it down in my homemade notation style that i still use and thats that

i kept playing it here and there, but i am almost positive i am playing it the way he did but maybe it was never “right” to begin with, so

this is the reason why i thought i could get it up here and then we could find out together, this all happening shortly after you helped me figure out that last tune

i was excited to have something to share no matter how insignificant it may have been

so when i learn a tune, i dont really use the abc, sometimes ill use the sheet music, because that makes sense to me, but usually, i just use the sheet music to write it in my own notation, which is a much simpler version of the abc thing

no ornaments, no time signatures, rests or anything, just simply the notes for example aggcdefggg etc., something like that, then i listen to the tune and play along and as soon as i can play at dinosaur speed, i forget the notes
i am currently trying to wean myself off of this method and just strengthen my ear to the point i never write anything down

so i wanted to contribute something, like i said and i had this little tune written down
i read a couple of websites about how the abc worked, and i thought it made sense to me

the only reason i bothered to post it at all is because on concertina.net, i tested it in the tune-o-tron and it sounded pretty close, and i thought after that, people around here might know it from its close-ness, and then post the real thing in the comments or say, “hey thats already here, check it out, its actually called this”

what i obviously underestimated was the whole abc thing and how much i didnt know what i thought i knew

so that is the long story
maybe someday i will learn abc and ill have it proper, maybe someday, serendipitously, ill bump into that guy again at some epic party and ill tell him this whole story and ask him to straighten it out…

i hope this relieves you a bit, but of course we dont know the name or origin of the tune, just that it is more than likely a childrens rhyme, i remember he called it rosie somethings, something with a rose in the title, anyway and i know a girl name rose clayton, so i threw it on there thinking it would be temporary, not realizing the seriousness of this institution called the session

i know that sounds a little harsh, but i mean it in a serious way, like its my fault for not taking this more seriously, but i have learned some good things through this experience and now my drive and desire to become a better player is only strengthened

so i dont know what to tell you except that obviously, i dont know what type of tune it is, a jig, reel, highland, or whatever, so i think that is the major problem
to me, it sounded most like “off to california”, of the tunes that i know, but much slower, so of all of my choices, i chose hornpipe, since that is what “off to cali” is, and then when i put it into concertina, it sounded close, so i stuck with hornpipe

i often find myself playing this tune on the piano and
all of the abcs of this tune that you put up, sound relatively close to what is in my mind and how i am playing it now on the whistle

so it was an insignificant little song, but the participation was tempting…

ceolachan, thanks for your interest in me and for your help with the last tune and for spending your time on this one

see ya round

It was interesting enough to raise a chuckle out of me. Calling me ‘dude’ has me longing for the surf again, though I was never much good at handling a board, but I loved it…

While your ears are valuable, even the best ear players I’ve known still enjoyed a bit of notating now and then, in one form or another. It’s a great way to share memory jogs. It’s amazing how a bar or two will draw out the memory of a whole tune. So don’t give up on that completely.

The dry stuff is that basically music, dance music, is maths, meaning that bars have to add up, on the whole, to what is established by the basic heartbeat of the melody, whether that’s 4/4 or 6/8 or 21/16. Each bar has to end up equalling that. As well the divisions occur within each bar, for example, with reels, 2 Xs 2/4, such as ~ | ABcd edcB | ~ or ~ | A2 B2 c2 d2 | ~ and with 6/8 ~ | ABc def | ~ or ~ | A2 c d2 f | ~ or ~ | A3 d3 | ~ etc… And of course there’s 9/8 ~ | ABc def gab | ~ or ~ | A2 c d2 f g2 b | ~ or ~ | A3 d3 g3 | ~ they all add up to fit the meter, so for 9/8 time there will be a ‘total’ in each bar equivalent to 9 X 1/8, whether you use A or A2 or A3 or A/…

It’s not all that complicated really, just simple fractions and addition… Playing it with life and lift is beyond mere ink and paper, and that’s where your ears are the primary tool of understanding…

Best of luck with all you do ~ ‘c’
