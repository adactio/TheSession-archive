Fourteen comments

Lyrics

The minstrel boy to the war has gone,
In the ranks of death you’ll find him;
His father’s sword he hath girded on,
And his wild harp slung behind him;
“Land of Song!” cried the warrior bard,
"Tho’ all the world betrays thee,
One sword, at least, thy right shall guard,
One faithful harp shall praise thee!

The Minstrel fell! But the foeman’s chain
Could not bring that proud soul under;
The harp he lov’d ne’er spoke again,
For he tore its chords asunder;
And said "No chains shall sully thee,
Thou soul of love and brav’ry!
Thy songs were made for the pure and free
They shall never sound in slavery!

Note: there is a third verse that was tacked on during the American civil war, but since I don’t like it much, I haven’t included it.

Re: The Minstrel Boy

And those Civil War period lyrics… sung by both sides, as it was generic to the condition of war itself. Wonderful tune. Often still sung with the rest, honoring soldiers and others defending hearth and home.

The Minstrel Boy will return we pray
When we hear the news we all will cheer it,
The minstrel boy will return one day,
Torn perhaps in body, not in spirit.
Then may he play on his harp in peace,
In a world such as heaven intended,
For all the bitterness of man must cease,
And ev’ry battle must be ended.

The Minstrel Boy, X:4

Clinton’s no. 54, under the name “The Moreen” (that’s the older name of the tune, while “The Minstrel Boy” was the poem Thomas Moore set to it in the 1810s).

Including the unholy cadenza whoa that Clinton put in there.

Re: The Minstrel Boy

I just found alternative lyrics in an early Irish American newspaper (think 1812-ish). Very anti tithe law:

The minister’s boy for his tithe is gone,
In the ranks of death you’ll find him;
The sword of the spirit he girded on,
And the tax cart jogs behind him
Land of wrong! And the boy in the yard
Tho’ the Peelers were there before me,
One stick at least thy rights shall guard,
And to lay it on them I’d glory.

The peasant fell--and the crimson tide
From his manly breast was pouring
And with his last breath to heaven he cried,
And the shades of death were lowering,
Oh! God of justice an it be
That such deeds are done by knavery?
Accept the spirit about to flee
From parson, tithe and slavery.
