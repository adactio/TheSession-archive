The Magpie Waddle

The second tune in a set (with Anna O’Donnelly), the second track on the wonderful eponymous EP by Tom Campbell Trio. I think it’s actually an Fmix rather than G minor tune: definitely one for the F whistle

Re: The magpie waddle

Rob, why not change the key setting to F then?
This tune is surely not in G, as you said.
Choosing the right key/mode helps people in their search for specific tunes. ;-)


When unsure about a key one can try the double drone method: play the tune against a drone background made of a specified tonic and its 5th above. See if it chimes or jars?
Here you can try F+c to figure , or perhaps. C + G …
Some tune won’t work with two drones, not even one drone. Some may work with a drone on the 7th, or the 4th or the minor 3rd. Even the 2nd. There’s an element of temperament in it too.
It can be in the ear of the beholder, funnily enough.
The (general) key note is not necessarily synonymous with the drone note.
One thing for sure here : this tune is not in G.

The magpie waddle, X:2

The same tune in the key of A mixolydian.*

Playing the bottom E’s one octave above allows this jig to be played on the Scottish pipes chanter (although further adjustments would need to be made in the first part)
