Booth Shot Lincoln

I’m posting this old-time tune because someone brought up in discussion its similarity to the jig, “Hundred Pipers”. The jig seems to have been written by Lady Nairne sometime the forth decade of the 19th century and became popular in Scotland in the mid 1850s. Lincoln was assassinated in 1865 so the tune may well have been the inspiration for “Booth Shot Lincoln.” The version posted here is popular in old-time circles in Kentucky and Tennessee.

Booth Shot Lincoln, X:3

This is from the album “Thunderhead”, by Malcolm Dalglish & Grey Larsen. They don’t play the tune like a polka at all, more like a syncopated hornpipe with a lot of swing, so rather than shoehorning their version into 2/4 I’ve notated it in 12/8. I’ve also transposed the tune from A to D, so that it can be played on flutes and whistles.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sXMlUybe434

Re: Booth Shot Lincoln

The jig that, in my opinion, is the origin of “Booth Shot Lincoln” is “Peter Baillie’s Wife” which can be found in Kerr’s Merry Melodies (c.1875). It’s also in O’Neill’s Dance Music of Ireland (1907) under the title “The Market Town” ( https://thesession.org/tunes/3772 ).
