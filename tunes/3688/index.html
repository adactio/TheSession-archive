Five comments

This is the third tune in a set of three slip jigs starting with Wigsborough Hunt (Hunting the Hare), followed by the Irish Jig. we particularly enjoy the ‘B’ music. I would appreciate any comments from anyone who has had the interest to play these in the order suggested.

↳ Posted by hetty on October 13th, 2004.

I play this in a set starting with “Hunting the Hare” and ending with Jack-in-the-Green but the middle slip jig is “Up and Down again”. The transition from “Hare” to “Up-and-down” is abrupt, but the transition from “Up-and-down” to “Jack” is very smooth.

↳ Posted by Innocent Bystander on February 21st, 2005.

Re: Jack In The Green

This is an old tune, possibly 17th century, but certainly early 18th century. Playford published a setting (1720s) as did Walsh (1730s). In Scotland settings appear in the David Young MS (1740) and Oswald’s Caledonian Pocket Companion (1759). O’Neill included it in his 1903 collection with the parts reversed.
