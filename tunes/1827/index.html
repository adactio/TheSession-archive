The Jig Runrig

Composed by Fergie MacDonald in the mid 1990’s. Still one of the most common jigs in Scottish sessions to this day - easy to learn and the pattern seems to stick well. If the demon players think it’s too easy, try winding up the tempo (and still maintain the precision!). Originally suggested played in Key D, repeat in G, then in A, before returning to the original key D. Also encourage some players (if required) to experiment with simple harmonies in Part B starting with 3rd’s on the cuckoo notes etc - good fun!

Beinn Eadarra

The Gaelic singer Darren Maclean has written words using a simplified version of this melody to create a piece of rhythmic ‘port à beul’ (mouth music).

Chaidh sinn su-as, suas gu Beinn Ea-darr-a, Suas dhan a’ bheinn air son uis-ge ri òl,
Ea-darr-a, Ea-darr-a, suas gu Beinn Ea-darr-a, Suas dhan a’ bheinn air-son uis-ge ri òl.

(We went up, up to Mount Edra, Up to the mountain for water to drink,)
(Edra, Edra, up to Mount Edra, Up to the mountain for water to drink.)

Beinn Eadarra (Beinn Eadra) is in Trotternish, the northern-most peninsula on the Isle of Skye.

Re: The Jig Runrig

“The Jig Runrig” was published - most likely for the first time - in “The Nineties Collection”, edited by Ian Hardie. It’s on page 43, with the following information :
[ Composed by ] - Fergie MacDonald, Acharacle.
[ Playing suggestion ] - “Play this tune 4 times through, first in D, then in key G, key A and finally back to D”.
[ Also ] - Copyright Fergie MacDonald 1995.
Without going through the whole book, a quick check has most of the tunes copyrighted between 1991 and 1995, one or two from the 1980s. The book was published in 1995, and most of the tunes would have been placed under copyright before being included in the book.
When I was a student in Aberdeen in the early 1970s, I shared a room with a guy from Lewis. He and many of his Gaelic-speaking buddies were great Runrig fans, so I heard them many times in their earliest days. It’s therefore possible that Fergie composed the tune as early as the mid 1970s, but only he would know, and sadly he’s not around any more to ask.

Re: The Jig Runrig

I have a copy of “Fergie’s Collection” of 25 tunes, published in 1998 by Rannoch Music, with all tunes copyrighted to them 1998. I bought my copy while on his buttonbox course at Sabhal Mor Ostaig in 2013: he kindly signed it for me.
In the front of the book, he acknowledges the help he had from various other musicians in getting the dots down on paper, as he says he can neither read nor write music.

Fergie wrote wee notes for each tune: here’s what he said about The Jig Runrig:
“Was this tune good enough for association with the Big Time Group? That was my dilemma. Anyhow, during a chance meeting in Inverness, Phil Cunningham convinced me that it was. This discussion took place over a vindaloo at the Riverside Indian restaurant.
Eventually Phil learned the tune himself as I played it to him over the telephone, and went on to record it on his latest album. Fortunately the Runrig boys gave it the “thumbs up” and think it is a cracker of a tune.
Runrig have done so much for our Gaelic culture and language. Their contribution will not be forgotten.”
