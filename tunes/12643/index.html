Self-composed tunes ~ 3 in a row

It would be common courtesy to read the FAQs when you join an online community. You’ll find these under ‘HELP’ below, and note in particular the following, which is there to try to curb the exuberance of the many, many, many composers out there in the world wanting to share their genius, and to keep the focus mainly on the tried and tested, though we all do enjoy checking out the creations of others ~ once in awhile. Three in a row is pushing it a bit.

FAQs: Can I submit my own compositions?

Well… The Session isn’t really intended for that. If you do post one of your own compositions, then you must do your “penance”, so to speak, by balancing each original composition with about five trad tune settings. ~ Jeremy, our webmaster

No, this isn’t an unreasonable request, in a sense it’s like a test, do you value the compositions of others, or the tradition enough to have 5 tunes you can submit for every one of your own you’re just itching to share with the rest of the world.

But, don’t let this seem like I don’t appreciate the efforts and compositions of others, but there is a more considerate way to share:

Discussions: Tunes in the Details ~ member’s compositions
https://thesession.org/discussions/23177

& annually ~

X: 2 ~ having some fun & an attempt at making restitution ~

Fresh fat balls with mealworms and other goodies for the birds, walnut bread hot out of the oven with butter for us. The birds are all natural singers, with their own accents, regional too as proven by research, and the same is true for all of us, our first songs sometimes not so pleasant, but effective in gaining us some attention, and advancement in speech too. Sadly we soon distance ourselves from such explorations and fun, while birds keep singing. Some of us reawaken those early pleasures through music.

But to walnut bread, before giving it a go ourselves we’ve eaten many different takes on this, well thought out and baked by others. All were enjoyable, though some we preferred over others. We’ve since tried a few recipes. Behind all this we have a decent knowledge and past experience with the materials, all kinds of flours, different yeasts, other adjuncts, and walnuts. Now, from experience, we know when nuts are rancid, even mildly. The worst walnuts from outside these islands are from China, they tend to have that rancid taste. The best are from the U.S.A. and France. Of course, nothing can beat fresh local produce, but we haven’t much of that available where walnuts are concerned, which is a shame. Also, I haven’t yet found a source for another loved variety, black walnuts. We also know the trees and the wood, have planted one and shaped the other, beauty all round, and we’ve had walnuts served in many ways, including pickled. So we’re familiar with the ingredients, and we’ve screwed up a few recipes over time. We know enough now that we also do the occassional experiment, try a different take on things, the bread, and using walnuts in other dishes.

So, why am I on this kick, other than the place being full of the smells of baking, and the smell of coffee espressing. (Oops! I’d better attend to that, beans not far from the roasting, dark, and hand ground! I’ll be back.)

Here’s the problem, I’d added the reminder about the FAQs, see above, and then I recieved an email reminder from our beloved webmaster Jeremy saying, again, that he would prefer we reminded people in kind about things like the FAQs and options for those affected by the composition bug ~ by email and not in the tune’s comments. He prefers that we primarily deal with the tune in the comments for a tune. So, I’m back, believe it or not, to try to make restitution, to try to be constructive. And yes, the opening bit on bird song and baking is relevant, at least to my curiously wired brain.

To be fair it ain’t a bad little melody. I’ve had fun playing it. But, let’s now get general about needing some roots to compositions, and the deeper and the more second nature of those roots and that understanding the better. And, yes, I do sense ‘some’ of that here, having given it a little time now and danced to it as well, though it fails a ‘little’ on the latter use. Proof of the fun I’ve had is given above, X: 2… So, here goes that general comment on the process of composition, in this around that tune form called ‘barndance’…

First, and most important, if you’re going to want to produce something called a ‘barndance’, ‘dance’ music, I highly recomment you get out there and barndance, learn a few of the relevant dances, and get lots of experience moving to the tunes, getting physical with it, and its relatives, which include the schottische, the german, the highland fling/schottische, and the hornpipe. That’s the most direct way to have it sink in and become second nature. The other is, of course, to learn as much as you can about the music, this form of dance music, and that means listening and picking up barndances to add to your repertoire. Session playing doesn’t really do it, too often failing to be ‘dance music’ while at times also a little too open to being sloppy. Instead get out there and play this ‘dance muisc’ for dancers, and with people with that experience ( not that there can’t be problems there too :-D ).

There are some barndance compositions by well established and well known Irish musicians that, in my opinion, also don’t cut the mustard, miss the mark, are clunky, even silly. And no, I’m not going to name names, but some close associates already know some of those specifics. Some folks feel that just throwing in a bit of swing and a couple of bumps is all that is required for them to call it a ‘barndance’, whether or not it’s any good to dance a barndance to. And then there’s this website where all kinds of things fall under what can be a bit of a catch all banner, including marches. That said, the dances that fit marches well are quite similar to the whole family of barndances. On a wider note, for some folks ‘barndance’ is a kind of catch all term anyway, and for others with that bit of anarchy about them, and I do understand, a ‘barndance’ is ANYTHING danced in a barn. :-D ( I do also believe that bicycling can be good for establishing rhythm too, especially around Puget Sound and the San Juans, understandably inspirational… ;-) )

For me, for starters, realizing this is just the dots, a skeleton, this was a little odd to dance to, but maybe that was me trying to play it and dance to it at the same time, in my stocking feet. :-D That’s also why the first thing I mentioned to consider, if halfway down this ramble, was dance, and the suggestion to dance ~ to learn and dance barndances, and the rest of that family of dances & music. It’s great craic, just so long as you don’t take it too seriously. Back to the bread, it is all about knowing the ingredients and the process, and giving it the required time. As to the birds, they just sing. For them it’s taken for granted, it’s just part of their life, whether it’s a raw and raucaus crow or a melodic blackbird with lots of various twiddles. For us, it takes effort, time, experience, and an openess to learn and a willingness to try and to not worry too much about making a fool of ourselves or screwing up. Those are good education, not just in what not to do or do, but also in attitude, cultivating that openess and willingness that makes for patience with ourselves and others, with understanding on all sides, and which makes for great craic, good fun, enjoyment…

Enough of my rambling, best of luck in all you do ~ ‘c’ (P.S. There are also a few ‘minor’ problems in your transcription.)
