From the flute playing of Galway musician Tom Morrison on an old 78 RPM recording, which can be heard here: http://www.archive.org/details/TomMorrisonMyOldClayPipetheCowWithOneHorn Morrison varies the notes in the 2nd bar, but I’m not sure precisely how. His other variations are much more obvious.

My Old Clay Pipe, X:2

To either clarify or add to the confusion of these comments, Harry Bradley does indeed refer to this tune as The Humours of Whiskey, and attributes the tune to the playing of Tom Morrison, acknowledging that My Old Clay Pipe is the title that Morrison recorded it under. Not to be confused with the tune that he is playing in the video linked above, which he refers to as the modern version of the tune. I am adding The Humours Of Whiskey as an alternative title for this tune, bringing the current count of slipjigs posted here under that title up to six, god help us all. Anyhow, my setting is the version that he teaches, and it’s a pretty cracking slipjig at that.

Re: My Old Clay Pipe

The title on Morrison’s 78 was My Old Clay Pipe/The Cow With One Horn. This latter was a version of the Green Fields of Woodford, which figures as Morrison was from Galway. https://archive.org/details/TomMorrisonMyOldClayPipetheCowWithOneHorn

Indeed it is akin to the Humours of Whiskey - and an earlier title is A Kiss in the Shelter: https://thesession.org/discussions/25692 Piper Jimmy O’Brien-Moran sourced this from notations of mid 19th century piper Paddy Conneely.
