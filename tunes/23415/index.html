Three comments

Fis Terra

Oh Waow!
A beautiful haunting air, inspired by the Spanish ‘Land’s End’ in Galicia: “Fisterra”.
Composed by Brian Boothby and -really beautifully- arranged by Rodrigo Romani for harp, hurdy-gurdy, silver flute and gaita (yes, a funny mix …but sensibly mixed!)
It is incredibly evocative for me as it always bring back the same numinous memories of standing in the cool cloud zone on top of some unnamed mountain ridge somewhere between Fisterra and the valley of the Duro, being at once lost and found, of this world and aloof.
It’s like that moment has found its music! (or vice versa)

The tune, a very slow ‘waltz’ in 6/4, may sound rather ‘sparse’ to some readers as it appears on the page. It would make sense to add the h-arpeggios lines as a bonus. I’ll try and see, at some point, if it’s doable for me.

NB: On the recording (https://youtu.be/Dra_LpvqLE4), each instrument enters in turn and provides variations. The final fundamental (/tonic) is played only at the very end.

Fis Terra, X:3

Here ’t is again, in B to suit “diatonic” flute and low whistle players.
The fiddlers will figure a key that’s mellow enough.
I think it is in the key of C on the record.
The mode is ‘harmonic minor’, by the way (presence of the leading note): J.S. Bach’s favourite mode (:
Also popular in Classical Indian music (keeravāṇi, or 21st Melakarta scale) and various European vernaculars (Greece, Ukraine, Sweden, Limousin, etc)
