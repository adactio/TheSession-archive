No 13

Both “Paistin Fionn” and “The Fair Haired Child” are the names given to this tune -albeit a more elaborate form thereof- in Edward Bunting’s 1796 edition of his ancient airs of Ireland; No13, p7.

The Fairhaired Child, X:2

This moving slow air version was arranged and recorded by Aidan Coffey and appears on his album, The Corner House Set (https://thesession.org/recordings/5528) as An Fáistín Fionn. It is played on an Eb box in the key of Bb, but I have transposed it to the key of D major to make it suitable for more instruments. Chet

↳ Posted by cac on March 22nd, 2023.

Re: The Fairhaired Child

I forgot to mention that the eighth notes (quavers) are often played as semiquavers (sixteenth notes) by Coffey, with the value of the longer notes adjusted accordingly. Examples are measures 14 of both parts and 2, 3, and 4 of the B-part.

The Fairhaired Child, X:4

Transcribed from Jimmy Power’s playing on his ‘Irish Fiddle Player’ recording, also on youtube https://youtu.be/S7C2uLYfI9A?si=wQJ4m2U6zxCra_MT

I love Jimmy’s traditional intonation on the fiddle, especially that strong first F note, which he pitches somewhere between F natural and F#. He follows the waltz with the same tune in jig time, which he calls Statia Donnelly’s https://thesession.org/tunes/1471 The jig is written with the F#, so I’ve followed that approach with this setting too.
