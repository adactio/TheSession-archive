Stick Across the Hob / Lyon’s Favourite / “O’Keeffe’s Morrison’s”, X:1

And now for something completely different! Here’s a three-part version of the Stick Across the Hob aka Morrison’s from Denis Murphy & Pádraig O’Keeffe.

Sliabh Luachra musicians will be marking Denis Murphy’s 50th anniversary this month so I’m transcribing some recordings and editing ABCs to highlight the tunes of the Kerry fiddle legend – including some wonderful creative versions of popular session tunes.

This particular version comes from the 1961 recording made at the Lyons’ Bar in Scartaglin. The recording has been passed around on tapes for years and can now be found on The Rushy Mountain (in full) and on Handed Down Sliabh Luachra Audio Archive (partially). A SoundCloud link below leads to the track in question.

It’s curious to note that perhaps the most popular (or overplayed) Irish jig in the world comes from Co. Kerry since James Morrison got it from his accordion playing pal Tom Carmody of Listowel, who in turn called it after his father, Maurice Carmody. In North Kerry, the tune had been known as the Stick Across the Hob.

As Paul de Grae notes, “the three-part version is sometimes called “O’Keeffe’s Morrison’s”, but Pádraig didn’t have to go to Morrison for it; the tune never left Kerry.” You should definitely check out Paul’s latest work for the World Fiddle Day Scartaglin fiddle recital, in which he transcribed the versions of Denis Murphy tunes handpicked and recorded by fiddle extraordinaire Aidan Connolly.
https://www.facebook.com/groups/283125488560326/

A particular care has been given to making these versions as “basic” and readable as possible – which is the opposite of what I’m striving for with my transcriptions, meant for an in-depth study – so the two complement each other perfectly :-)

But the most important part is this: listen to Denis Murphy & Pádraig O’Keeffe play this jig and other tunes, it’s super cool!

https://rushymountain.com/2019/11/16/padraig-okeeffe-and-denis-murphy-lyons-bar-1961/
https://soundcloud.com/pj-teahan-884614828/padraig-o-keeffe-and-denis-murphy-4-lyons-bar-scartaglin-1961

Stick Across the Hob [3-part] / Lyon’s Favourite / “O’Keeffe’s Morrison’s” (Update!)

OK this should’ve been Breaking News back in the day as it expands the history of the illustrious “Morrison’s Jig” but an update will do! 😃

The three-part version of the Stick Across the Hob (which I posted here: https://thesession.org/tunes/25000#setting50045 ) had long been thought to have come from Pádraig O’Keeffe (as in, he added more parts as he liked to do with other tunes – think of Johnny Cope!)

However, new information has come to light with the release of the Paddy McElvaney tape of Pádraig, in which he doesn’t just play his fiddle but also – surprisingly! – talks.

Speaking on the tape, O’Keeffe attributed this version of the jig to none other than Tom Billy Murphy (1879–1944), the blind fiddle master from Ballydesmond, Co. Cork, who at least in the folk tales had been painted as a bitter rival of O’Keeffe’s.

This expands the list of the old Kerry-Cork musicians playing versions of this jig in the pre-radio / pre-Morrison era, along with Maurice Carmody of Dromlought / Listowel, Co. Kerry (born in 1862), his son, Tom Carmody, his neighbour, Paddy Stack from Ballyconry, and *quite possibly* his teacher, blind fiddle player Jeremiah Breen of Ballyconry, all previously mentioned in Diarmuid Ó Catháin’s article, “The Old Musicians of North Kerry”. If we’re creative enough (there’s no proof of that thus far), we might even think of Breen’s pal, the legendary North Kerry piper Tom McCarthy, as having had played this tune for the dancers some time in the 1800s – McCarthy was born in 1799. Now that’s old!

You can scroll down and listen to the entire half-an-hour tape of Pádraig playing and talking about his tunes here:

https://www.itma.ie/blog/taoscadh-on-tobar-andrea-palandri/

Hopefully ITMA will release more Sliabh Luachra-related gems from the Tom Davis Collection – who knows what groundbreaking revelations await us there! ;-)

The Stick Across the Hob / Lyon’s Favourite / Tom Billy’s / S: Denis Murphy, 1967

Here is a simplified transcription from Denis Murphy’s playing of this tune which Breandán Breathnach recorded in November 1967.

This three-part jig has usually been referred to as a version of Morrison’s – or The Stick Across the Hob, as the (in)famous two-part tune was actually called in North Kerry at the time of James Morrison’s recording with Tom Carmody (see the brief story of that recording here: https://thesession.org/tunes/25000#comment306216).

I wasn’t any different and have previously submitted this tune under the two-part Morrison’s. However, during my recent research into the archive recordings from Sliabh Luachra I was surprised to learn that neither Denis Murphy nor Pádraig O’Keeffe associated this tune with Morrison’s / Maurice Carmody’s Favourite / The Stick Across the Hob etc. etc., at least they never mentioned any of those names. Instead, Murphy wondered whether it was a version of Whelan’s jig and O’Keeffe cited Tom Billy as the source for this tune without providing a title. For all we know, instead of nicknaming it “O’Keeffe’s Morrison’s”, fiddle players from the area might as well have called it “Tom Billy’s Whelan’s”.

In the end, I sat down to compare the two “classic” jigs with this one – and while tune clearly starts the same way as Morrison’s / The Stick Across the Hob, it then veers off in an unexpected direction, ending like Whelan’s in part A, starting close to Morrison’s again in part B but then building up non-stop until it reaches the third part that’s unlike both Morrison’s or Whelan’s (and instead reminds me of An Seanchaí Muimhneach or The Old Jig as Denis Murphy used to call it). In any case, that’s a pretty unique version distinct from the usual two-part tune so maybe it should have its separate page and ID (another argument for this is that it’s been published in Johnny O’Leary of Sliabh Luachra, no. 198).

In keeping with the tradition of calling twin / namesake Sliabh Luachra tunes by alternative titles (Hare in the Corn vs. Cat in the Corner; Green Cottage vs. Glen Cottage vs. Glin Cottage), maybe it would be fine to title this one The Stick Across the Hob, just to keep the likely link between the two. Incidentally, that’s also the name Paul de Grae has proposed for this tune citing the original Kerry title of Morrison’s!

The Stick Across The Hob, X:3 / Lyons’ Favourite / Tom Billy’s / S: Johnny O’Leary

Here’s the version of this three-part jig that appears in Johnny O’Leary of Sliabh Luachra: Dance Music from the Cork-Kerry Border under the title “Lyons’ Favourite” (no. 198). A note suggests that Johnny learned it from Pádraig O’Keeffe “in Lyons’ of Castleisland” – but that likely refers to Lyons’ Bar in Scartaglin which O’Keeffe frequented.
