Re: The Risin‘ o’ the Moon (Lyrics)

words by John Keegan Casey (1846-1870), c.1865.

The ‘Fenian Poet’ refered to events of the late 18th century. Several variants were printed and sung.

1.
And come tell me Sean O’Farrell tell me why you hurry so
Husha mbuachaill hush and listen and his cheeks were all a-glow
I bear orders from the captain get you ready quick and soon
[For the pikes must be together by the rising of the moon]…
.&. (chorus*)
By the rising of the moon, by the rising of the moon:
[For the pikes must be together by the rising of the moon]
2 .
And come tell me Sean O‘Farrell where the gath’rin’ is to be
At the old spot by the river quite well known to you and me
One more word for signal token, whistle out the marchin’ tune
[With your pike upon your shoulder by the rising of the moon]…
3.
Out from many a mud wall cabin eyes were watching through the night
Many a manly heart was beating for the blessed warning light
Murmurs rang along the valleys to the banshees lonely croon
[And a thousand pikes were flashing by the rising of the moon]…
4 .
All along that singing river that black mass of men was seen
High above their shining weapons flew their own beloved green
Death to every foe and traitor! Whistle out the marching tune
[And hurrah, me boys, for freedom, ’tis the rising of the moon]…
.
(*N.B.: the chorus incorporates each [bracketed] last line)

Re: The Wearing Of The Green

A rare, pre-1917, recording of this march has been restored and posted on youtube last April.
It is the 2nd tune in the set (from 1’35");
https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=AQhVkTYRLUg&pp=ygUec3RhcmNrIGJyaWFuIGJvcnUgc2V0IG9mIHBpcGVz

It is noteworthy for it was recorded (played) by Josephe Starck, brother of Henry who developped the so-called Brian Boru Irish bagpipe" at the turn of the century.
This type of instrument seemed to have been very instable and never competed with other types of pipes produced at this period.
More on the National Museum of Scotland website;
https://nms.scran.ac.uk/database/record.php?usi=000-000-579-602-C
