Miss Catherine Stewart Of Pittyvaich’s

Jig (not really a slide, but 12/8) by William Marshall from his 1845 Collection of Scottish Melodies, volume 2. The version in the book has double and triple stops, which I’ll add as a second setting. I heard it on Susan Worland Bentley’s Breakdown album.

↳ Posted by benhockenberry on April 8th, 2015.

Miss Catherine Stewart Of Pittyvaich’s, X:2

This is the version in Marshall’s Scottish Melodies, with one small change-- I left out double stops in bars 3 and 7 of the B part where there is a low F beyond standard fiddle tuning (like this: [E_B,G,]D[CF,]). I’m not sure if the fiddle used for this tune at the time may have been detuned to FCGD throughout, or if this was a mistake in the transcription. Does anyone know if fiddles were tuned down a step in northeast Scotland in the early 19th century?

The source for my ABC was adapted slightly from http://tunearch.org/wiki/Miss_Catherine_Stewart%E2%80%94Pettyvaich to match the book.

↳ Posted by benhockenberry on April 8th, 2015.

I think those “double stops” are for the piano only. Two reasons - one, the whole collection is scored with a piano bass line (which on first inspection looks to me unplayable on cello/bass) and secondly, if you look at the two triple stops preceding the low F, they are not playable on a fiddle however it is tuned as you need to play two notes on the same string. Furthermore, the bass line matches the low F.

Scordatura and the like were not unknown but I don’t really see them as Marshall’s style!
