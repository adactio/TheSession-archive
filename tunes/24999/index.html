Two comments

Jack Sweeney’s Jig

This tune is published in Johnny O’Leary of Sliabh Luachra: Dance Music from the Cork-Kerry Border (no. 183) where it’s been transcribed in two versions played by Johnny in 1993 and 1998.

Johnny O’Leary got this jig from Jack Sweeney, a 10-key melodeon player from Tuar Mór / Toormore (one of the many Irish townlands of this name – O’Leary specified it was the one near Kilcummin, Co. Kerry). The second part / turn of this jig places it in The Lark in the Morning family but part A seems to be pretty unique – unless I’ve missed a tune or two! Please let me know in the comments if you find another match.

After a bit of tidying up and linking, I think this is the last tune that was missing from the JOLSL collection on The Session as far as the old edition of the book is concerned. Someone who has the new edition at hand might check nos. 349-377 just in case there are ambiguously named tunes linking to the wrong page. With all the tune name variations existing in Sliabh Luachra, I find that this book serves as an important common reference point and sometimes it’s really handy to be able to look it up on The Session (by clicking on “…appears in N other tune collection(s)” under the tune titles and recordings).

Re: Jack Sweeney’s

There’s some interesting context related to this jig that one would discover by connecting the dots. Johnny O’Leary is cited in the book as saying he played with Jack Sweeney in Tuar Mór “at the age of twelve years”. Since Johnny was born in 1923, this brings us to around 1935.

Checking out Jack Sweeney’s biography on the Handed Down Sliabh Luachra Archive Bios page, I found that he played with Johnny O’Leary, Denis Murphy and others in the Tuar Mór Dance Hall – said to have been the only place in Kerry to hold set-dancing all-nighters between 1926 and 1934! Some time after that this hall was “raided and shut down by the clergy and Gardaí”. Could this jig be the tune Sweeney and the young O’Leary were playing when the cops and the priests burst in?? Sounds pretty metal!

Jokes aside, that’s a fairly dark side of the history of the independent Irish state that is not mentioned too often… In Sliabh Luachra, the dances continued in private houses, sometimes even on a regular commercial basis – whether it was allowed due to the remote nature of these places, I could not say, but elsewhere the Public Dance Halls Act, 1935 and the subsequent crackdown pretty much wiped out traditional dances in some areas and dealt a blow to musicians playing at these events. The Act formally remains in force to this day with some amendments! A happy jig but a grim situation?
