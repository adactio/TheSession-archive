Mary Devlin’s

This tune is by Sam Bartlett. In addition to being one of the few tunes out there in B minor, it’s also a really good uilleann piping tune, although it probably wasn’t intended to be. I learned it at a trad music weekend in Vermont.

…oh, and if you want to know more about this Sam Bartlett fellow, feel free to visit his website at http://www.sambartlett.com/, but prepare to be surprised.

↳ Posted by Cormac G. on November 27th, 2014.

Mary Devlin’s, X:2

Cormac is right: Sam Bartlett is delightfully surprising.

Mary Devlin’s is possibly the most fun one could have while practicing B rolls on a flute. This is the Evil Diane setting, though dots can’t capture the funky groove. For some extra funk, check out this performance in Bloomington -- terrific dancers and a sung/scatted melody line with a Balkan interlude in the middle. And Mr. Bartlett on the banjo?

Mary Devlin’s, X:3

Cracking tune from Sam Bartlett, and the clip posted by B-bird with the brilliant lilting by Moira Smiley inspired me to learn it on fiddle. In doing so, I at first closely followed Moira’s line. Then, as I kept playing the tune, I fleshed it out into the version shown in X: 3. Works a treat to play it with the pauses and syncopation a la Moira, and then into X:3.

My ear hears this not in Bm but in Bdor (with any Gs sharped). No Gs in my setting, but I still hear Bdor. That said, I might flatten a G passing note to natural as a bluesy way to get back to one of those Fs, particularly in the A part.

I wonder, is this Mary Devlin the author/musician with the Bay Area’s Sherwood Consort (ren faire style music)?
