Bheadh Buachaillín Deas Ag Síle

This is a great song… which I believe translates to “Sheila Would Have a Good Husband”. Solas & a few other people have recorded it… the tune I’ve submitted here is the melody from the Solas version. I classified this as a “jig”, though I suppose it’d be possible to interpret as a waltz or mazurka, in 3/4, as it’s not played incredibly fast. I think of it more as a "treble jig’. It’s certainly not rhythmically akin to a waltz, but its not played as fast as a standard double jig, though it can be played faster, like 100 BPM. I think Solas plays it more like 80-90 BPM. Lots of ornaments & variations are possible with this melody that I haven’t notated here.

It’s interesting because the instrumental arrangement by Solas, which is very Mediterranean or “Latin” almost, seems to be related to another tune of theirs called “Who’s in the What Now?”, from their “Edge of Silence” (2002). You can hear a bit of it at the end of the track, after the final line of the song says: “Is ni doichí go bh’fanam sa tir séo ach ag reacaireacht síos amach”. Then there’s a short, “Spanish”-inspired instrumental til the track ends. The day I realized they were related, or literally the same, I was dumbfounded. Or stupefied. lol. Idk.

Re: Bheadh buachaillín deas ag Síle // Tres morillas m’enamoran

you’re right: although many Irish songs in 6/8 are quite waltzy. This one isn’t.

And there’s more to a jig than its abstract 6/8 signature: this song’s form is ABBA whereas most Irish dance tunes -including jigs- are AA‘BB’.

ABA is a very old European form (often ABBA in Ireland).⁷
[ABA] can be heard in the famous C.15th Spanish song “Tres morillas m’enamoran en Jaen” for example.
The A part in this song (= Three Moorish girls have my heart enthralled) is almost note for note the A part in Bheadh buachaillín deas ag Síle. Could be a fluke but i wouldn’t rule out an ancient (Spanish/Medieval/liturgical even?…) influence.
