Lydian Tune

I’ve come to the conclusion, from posts here and elsewhere on the internet, that apart from a few Sweedish tunes, and a couple by contemporary English composers, there are practically no actual Lydian tunes out there at all. I imagine that many have been lost. I’ve explored the territory a little and written a few tunes in this mode. Here’s one of them:

I hope some of you enjoy it.

X:1
T:Portuguese Rain
M:4/4
L:1/8
K:GLyd
“G” G3Gd2c2| “Bm” B3B1 ABAB| “G” G2GGd2c2| “Bm” B6 B2|
“G” G3Gd2c2| “Bm” B3B ABAB| “G” G2GG dedc| “Bm” B6 B2|

%%begintext fill
There’s not many tunes written in the Lydian mode, so harmonising them presents an interesting creative challenge. The basic idea here is that the chords from a D major scale are used, but the “home” note is G. Also, the strong A-D cadence is lost, so we have to find another cadence which will provide a similar tension/release effect. In the Lydian mode this can be achieved with Bm-G, A-G or Em-G.
One really interesting aspect of the Lydian mode in G is that Em is still the relative minor, except it comes out as Dorian. So if the B part of a tune moves to the relative minor as this one does, then it can be treated as an E Dorian tune in its own right. There’s also the possibility of using Bm as the basis for the B part.

Here’s my analysis, from a musically theoretical standpoint.

If any of you feel it is not my place to comment on this because of my lack of experience with this music, I understand. I am speaking from what i’ve learned about styles of music overall.

For what it’s worth(maybe not much) here’s my analysis on why your tune doesn’t sound Irish.

“that apart from a few Sweedish tunes, and a couple by contemporary English composers, there are practically no actual Lydian tunes out there at all.”

You wrote it in a non-Irish mode. It’s not Irish to end a tune on the 7th scale degree. There are 10’s of thousands of tunes, if none of them are written in a certain mode, it’s safe to say that that certain mode doesn’t fit into the style.

“having played trad for many years, as well as music from other traditions (particularly Indian), I’ve developed a curiosity about how ITM might be fused with other influences. I have found that the dance form of ITM acts as a wonderful container for some other scales and modes which I guess may have once been more prevalent in music from Britain and Ireland.”

The next problem is, you didn’t use a “dance form of ITM”. You listed the tune under “reel” in 4/4, but your tune does not have the rhythmic contour of any of the 4/4 forms of Irish dance music. The way this is rhythmically set up, it could not be a reel, polka, or hornpipe, because of where the emphasis on beats has been placed.

Because of these two crucial factors, your tune sounds Indian instead of Irish.
