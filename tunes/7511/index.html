O’Mealy’s Blackberry Blossom (reel)

I hope Jeremy won’t delete this post. This is a very distinct version of the well-known reel, and my transcription is based on Harry Bradley’s version on his latest solo album. “O‘Mealy” refers to pipes maker and piper Richard Lewis O’Mealy (1873-1947), and Harry found the version in O‘Mealy’s manuscript stored in NPU’s archive. He also write Connemara box and melodeon player Johnny O’Halloran plays a similar setting.

Vesey’s Harriers

This was the 2nd tune on the first track of John Vesey’s Shanachie LP The First Month of Summer; on that disc it was called the Merry Harriers, which of course is the title of another reel. For the CD reissue Two Sligo Masters the title was changed to the Blackberry Blossom, which is yet another tune distinct from this one - it does bear some resemblance to Vesey’s reel, ala the Star of the Munster and the Stone in the Field, the latter of which sounds like the former played with the start on its 2nd bar.

I’ve come up with this new title for Vesey’s tune in the same spirit as the jig Gillan’s Apples, which O‘Neill collected from a musician who called the tune Apples in Winter; as O’Neill had already collected another jig with that name he rechristened Gillan’s tune in his honor.

Cathal McConnell’s Blackberry Blossom

I’ve been ruminating over this version of the tune today. Firstly, despite Harry having learnt this from the manuscript in NPU, I always found this version to be literally the “ordinary” version offset by one bar (as billwolfe mentioned a few years ago). Not only that but he ends the tune on a G in much the way one might end the ordinary version. So, as such, when hearing this track, I always felt like the two tunes in the set (first being The Green Gowned Lass) were “bridged together” by the |ea a2 a2 ge| opening bar, after which I just hear the ordinary Blackberry Blossom with a bit of a different melodic approach to the B-part.

Then, in recent weeks, while working on a project involving Cathal McConnell tapes submitted to the ITMA, I came across this tune which a friend had told me was this alternative version of The Blackberry Blossom as recorded by Harry Bradley. I think he’s right. It is an alternative version of the The Blackberry Blossom but a number of things make it distinct from Harry’s (and, I suppose, by extension, O’Mealy’s) version.

1. It is in the key of E dorian and a backer would focus the tonality around Em. As noted above, Harry’s recording resolved in G major which, if consistent with this version, should have ended in Am.
2. The melody line makes way more sense in a stand-alone fashion, to the point where I found it
unrecognisable as The Blackberry Blossom and only on a focused look did I recognise it. O’Mealy’s version, based on transcriptions above, seems a bit nonsensical in parts and, in my opinion, doesn’t “fit together” as neatly as the version below.
3. There is a crotchet sequence in the B-part which is reminiscent of many Fermanagh tunes which initially led me to believe it was from that region. Maybe it is?
3. This version is crooked, including an extra bar in the B-part. Cathal played a few passes of this tune on the tape and the crooked bar was always included.

Here it is. Honestly, I’m almost inclined to say it deserves a separate post. I’ve titled it “Cathal McConnell’s Blackberry Blossom” to distinguish it from other versions but this is only a working title. It was mystery to me until today.

X: 1
T: Cathal McConnell’s Blackberry Blossom
R: Reel
M: 4/4
L: 1/8
K: Edor
|:Beef e2 dB|A2 FE FD D2|D2 DF A2 BF|AFDE FE E2:|

|Beef edBA|FABc d3 A|defe d3 f|edBc d2|defe d2 BA|
|B2 e2 e2 dB|A2 FE FD D2|D2 DF A2| BF AFDE FE E2|

I’d be interested to hear some of your thoughts on this one.
