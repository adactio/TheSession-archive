Five comments

I got this tune from a scratchy tape of some solo pipering. That doesn’t mean I haven’t changed it over a few years. A version appears in Ceol Rince na hÉireann 1. Breathnach notes the The Angry Peeler (O’Neill’s) and The Drunken Gauger (Goodman) are settings. And so they are.

The name should be written/spelled Carraig an tSoip, not Carraig An TSoip. I’m not sure of what it means (‘an tsoip’ is a but of a mystery). Is it a place-name? Is it a stone in a mattress? Help appreciated.

↳ Posted by PCL on June 2nd, 2005.

Apologies for my (very) poor Irish, but “Soip” seems to be a stem of the verb “to nestle”. My guess is it’s the idiomatic Irish for “a cold shoulder”. But I’d love to hear the right word from someone who really knows.

↳ Posted by Innocent Bystander on June 3rd, 2005.

Well, sop is a small bundle of straw, a straw-man, a class of a mattress, a type of a bed. You get the drift. Breathnach gives no translation. A native speaker friend thought it odd but interesting. Let’s say it’s an idiom, this rock of straw. Help still sought.
