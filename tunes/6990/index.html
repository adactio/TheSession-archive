Re: Wedding Day

I’m not an authority on things modal but, a simple way to get Dorianism is to take a scale in a major key and play it as an 8-note sequence - but from the second note of the scale. Thus if you consider the notes in a C-major scale and start playing them up and down from D, you end up with D Dorian, which has two significant changes from a D major. The third is a semitone flat (F, not F#) and the 7th note is also a semitone flat (C, not C#). It sounds a bit D-minorish, but the B is natural, not flat (as it would be in a true D minor), and it’s probably this that gives the Dorian mode its flavour.

Thus G Dorian has the notes of the F major scale, but starting the sequence at G, and so on.

If I’m mistaken in this, I have no doubt that someone on the site will put me right.
