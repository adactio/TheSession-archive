The Ballad Of Ballynure

Dear Members of The Session.org,

I have searched the site for the above titled tune and there is no record of it.

The piece is in E flat major, but that is not an option in the Key signature box, so I put it in C and added the accidentals where appropriate.

My father is learning tin whistle in “D” and the only version I can get hold of this tune is in E flat major.
I have copied the link below
https://www.8notes.com/scores/3378.asp

If there is someone who could please take a look at this tune and transpose it into “D” we would be very grateful. If however someone knows it by a different name or tune, that would also be useful.

My father first heard this tune on “BBC folk on two” in July 1984 and he has since found out that it was performed by the McPherson family- Spelling may not be correct.

I have posted the lyrics below as well

Thank you very much and I hope you all enjoy the tune

Fiona
A Ballynure Ballad

As I was goin’ to Ballynure,
The day I well remember,
For to view the lads and lasses
On the fifth day of November,
With a ma-ring-doo-a-day,
With a ma-ring-a-doo-a-daddy oh.

As I was goin’ along the road
When homeward I was walking,
I heard a wee lad behind a ditch-a
To his wee lass talking,
With a ma-ring-----etc.

Said the wee lad to the wee lass
"It’s will ye let me kiss ye,
For it’s I have the cordial eye
That far exceeds the whiskey,"
With a ma-ring-----etc.

This cordial that ye talk about
There’s very few o’ them gets it,
For there’s nothin’ now but crooked combs
And muslin gowns can catch it.
With a ma-ring-----etc.

Re: The Ballad Of Ballynure

Sorry…

There are a few problems with your abc, Fiona.

- Firstly, the tune as transcribed in your source sheet music is not in Eb major - more like C minor, but all the As are natural, so that makes it C dorian (which *is* an option for tune submissions). The key signature has two flats (Bb and Eb) which makes the natural accidentals on the A unnecessary. (Incidentally, you omitted the flat accidental on the E in bar 5).

- Secondly, the first bar has five beats, so you need to put in a change of time signature.

- Thirdly, you have not notated the triplets properly (i.e. marked with a “3”, as in the source sheet music), which throws the timing out.

- Fourthly, you have omitted the ties in bar 2, which are also important for the rhythm.

The most useful key/mode for playing this tune on a D whistle would be E dorian, which has the same key signature as D major and keeps the tune within the range of the whistle. I have submitted a corrected version, in both the original key and transposed to E dorian. Note also that I have used a much more concise way of notating dotted rhythm.

I recommend this site http://www.lesession.co.uk/abc/abc_notation.htm for teaching you all the ins-and-outs of abc notation. Also, this site http://www.mandolintab.net/abcconverter.php lets you transpose an abc with a click of your mouse.
