Bonnie Prince Charlie

This is the melody to the Jacobite song “Cam’ Ye By Atholl”, and I think is the weakest track on what is otherwise a great recording. The “Boys Of The Lough” did a far better job of it on one of their earlier LPs - I think “Lochaber No More”, and I think Altan recorded it too.
Incidentally, Johnny Cunningham was once asked on a USA tour with “Silly Wizard” in the 1980s if he knew that old Jacobite air “Come Eat My Apple” - true !

Re: Bonnie Prince Charlie, X3

I forgot to say that I wrote it as a march in 4/4 … but:
- there’s no “Marches” option on The Session,
- the tune is still set in the “Polkas” (probably because of that lack of options)
- we can’t change the heads of the abc files (it had been done that way probably to avoid most of the abc mistakes which prevent the playing and score display of the tunes; but it brings some limits … );
So that’s the reason why I hadn’t add the bar lines that would write a correct 2/4 rythm: so if you want to copy the abc file, no need to take them off, just change the rythm in the head. ;-)

Bonnie Prince Charlie, X:4

I heard this in the wild at the Kent in Baltimore, Maryland last year from ol’ Donny B, who is a fine purveyor of Scottish tunes. This is a transcription of the audio I recorded. This tune in particular seems to be having a little bit of a renaissance. It was featured on the latest Copley Street album, but the setting on that album is quite different. There actually seems to be lots of naming confusion with this tune, and variation in the settings, for whatever reason.

The amazing Jenna Moynihan recently posted a mostly similar version of this tune to her Instagram, which was the impetus for me to push this transcription over the finish line.
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DWFOyZqD1Mm/?igsh=MWl2NGk5cXoyaWZ1eg==

Thanks to Jack, my frequent partner in crime…errrrr I mean partner in trad, for the chords! Nowadays, I play this in a set starting with l’Air Mignonne, followed by Multnomah March (which isn’t here and I’ll post at some point), and then Bonnie PC. All at sort of a leisurely pace. It’s good fun!
