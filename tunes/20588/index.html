Dust

Here’s my go at Dust off of Brian Finnegan’s new album Hunger of the Skin. It’s an awesome tune. I always struggle with 6/4 versus 3/2, but I’m sure you all will correct me if I’m wrong. I also tried to transpose Brian’s ornaments into the sheet music, just so the feel isn’t lost. Eighth notes are swung. Brian plays it on a Db whistle; if I were a betting man, I’d say it’s a Goldie. Enjoy!

Re: Dust

The meter is fixed. There is a way to override the default but are you sure you want it in 6/4? 6/4 means two groups of 3/4 in the same way that 6/8 is two groups of 3/8. It doesn’t sound that way to me - sounds very much 3/2 (as in three beats per bar, not two).
The tempo on the audio for 3/2s here is unfortunately not very useful from a learning perspective but we just have to accept it for now.

Re: Dust

If you investigate, you will hear that on the audio on this site the tempos for reels, waltzes and polkas are all the same in terms of crotchets/quarter notes per minute. For some reason for 3/2s that same tempo is used, but for minims/half notes. Hence the audio for 3/2s is twice the tempo it should be for comparison with reels.

There’s always been something HD, clear cut, about Brian Finnegan’s tunes.

Paradoxically, they can be very difficult to make sense of on paper (or without the backing chordal/rhythmic section) as often remarked by contributors on this site.

It is clear from the recorded track that the intention is 3/2, as noted above: 3 groups of notes per bar not 2 groups.

Having said that, reading the tune in 6/4 makes good musical sense. And now you have a 6/4 waltz. Two tunes for the price of one. (-:
