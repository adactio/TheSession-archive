Three comments

The Snowy-Breasted Pearl Waltz

O’Neill’s Music of Ireland no. 512. Easy to confuse with the “Pearl of the White Breast” aka “The Snowy-Breasted Pearl,” which is a 4/4 tune just above it on the page as no. 511!

↳ Posted by NylonFlute on February 14th, 2022.

The Snowy-Breasted Pearl, X:2

Version in Bunting’s Ancient Music of Ireland, transposed from E-flat to D.

↳ Posted by NylonFlute on February 14th, 2022.

The Snowy-Breasted Pearl, X:3

Version in Beethoven’s arrangement of Irish songs, WoO 153, no. 1, as “When Eve’s last rays in Twilight die.”

Lyrics by David Thomson (flourished c1812) (the brother of Beethoven’s editor George Thomson, as George couldn’t find anyone else to write lyrics for these; he had hoped for Thomas Moore, but Moore had his own Irish songs project going, and Robert Burns died before the project could get started):

When eve’s last rays in twilight die
And stars are seen along the sky,
On Liffy’s banks I stray;
And there with fond I regret I gaze,
Where oft I’ve pass’d the fleeting days
With her that’s far away.

When she would sing some lovely strain,
How sweet the echoes gave again
In fainter notes the lay;
Tho’ mute the echoes of the grove,
In fancy still I hear my love.
Though now she’s far away.

Her from the stream reflected clear,
And still it seem’d, when she was near,
To move with fond delay;
But though its wave no trace retains,
Her image in my heart remains,
Tho’ now she’s far away.

↳ Posted by NylonFlute on February 16th, 2022.
