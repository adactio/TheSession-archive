The Low Tide At Merryman’s

"Though no one remembers it, Salem, Massachusetts County, England sprang up in the Northwestern wilderness near the England / Scotland border in the middle of May, 1802. For reasons unquestioned, the werewolves first inhabiting the area were civilized by the musical talents of a lone fiddler, Cailean MacWaller, who stumbled out of a pub in Edinburgh one average night and somehow ended up in England the next morning. Not long after, he would meet a musically-uninclined survivor from Fife, one Mr. Taylor Merryman, an old and displaced sailor long affected by lycanthropy from his unfortunate time in the wilderness. Nonetheless, within five minutes, Salem had its first building and live-music venue, the now-historic ‘Merryman’s Pub’ on North Boland Ln.

From its humble-albeit-ridiculous beginnings in 1802 to the present day, Merryman’s Pub has stayed busy, if antiquated and no longer the beating heart of Salem it was in times past. Still, folks will say that there has always been something special about the silence in the final hours at Merryman’s, the fabled “low tide”, where the grace few who stick around can feel the bright spirit of a life before, one that keeps the empty seats warm even after everyone else has gone home.

Wish You Were Here,

-Jon"

(In F Mixolydian)
