Nine comments

My all-time favorite jig. Goes absolutely perfectly on pennywhistle (higher keys only, I find…) and doesn’t sound too great on a fiddle…. but be your own judge. Play it 3 times - slowly, faster, blazing fast. Great fun, gets people’s feet a-stomping. good for sessions when there’s too many bodhraniis…

↳ Posted by Mark de Jong on June 20th, 2002.

I have this jig in my pennywhistle instruction book. It was one of the first tunes I learned to play on whistle.
I played this one for a friend once, and he danced. I told him that it’s called “Fool’s Jig” and he said, “Aw, I danced the jig, and I’m a fool!”

↳ Posted by The Whistler on June 21st, 2002.

Yep… i learned it from Cathal McConnel’s (I think that’s his name…) “Irish Tinwhistle Tutor” and it stuck with me for 4 years… it was the third tune I learned.

↳ Posted by Mark de Jong on June 21st, 2002.

This tune appears in O’Neills, written in 4/4, as a hornpipe (or reel, I forget), under the name of Roxburgh Castle. It is also popular in Northumberland and the Scottish borders, where the above title comes from.

The theme in the 2nd part is common to a few tunes, namely:
Roxburgh Castle as noted above and this other hornpipe: Walshe’s https://thesession.org/tunes/2332
Both the ‘reels’ (they sound like hornpipes)
Flatley’s https://thesession.org/tunes/3605
and New Years Alone https://thesession.org/tunes/9445 have similar sounding 1st parts.
