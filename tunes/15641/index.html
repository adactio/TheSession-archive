The Campbells Are Coming

This is a march of the Campbell clan which is to a jig rhythm. This version is one that I heard many years ago, but haven’t found this setting elsewhere. It is a strong tune, and simple to play. There are several versions of words to this tune which were published in the 1700s.

Re: The Campbells Are Coming

Barry McDonald collected this tune around 1984 from a man named Jim Lowe in Backwater, New South Wales, Australia. Jim was about 88 at the time, a mouth organ player, who called this tune The Camels Are Coming (Barry hypothesized the name was either a shifting of sounds from the original name, or an intentional parody).

Barry recorded it with his group Marooan on their album “Where the Sunlights on the Dewdrops Shine,” which showcases tunes and songs from Australia’s New England region.

He reported that Jim had a ditty he sung to the first part of the tune that went as follows:

“The camels are coming, they are they are. The humpty-back buggers, they are they are.”

The original recording of Jim playing this tune on the mouth organ is in the Barry McDonald Collection of field recordings housed in the National Library of Australia.

↳ Posted by tamsyn on November 22nd, 2023.
