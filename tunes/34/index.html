The Frieze Breeches, X:17

This is a 9 note pipe setting of this old chestnut that I learnt a few years back as the Friar’s Britches. And for your refined enlightenment, I’ll add the words to this song as it is known in Occitan (a once widely spoken language from Gascony to Lombardy and Limoge to Val d’Aran) under the name: “Sarremilhòque”

§§.
Sarremilhòque mi toca ma maïre
1.
Mi vòu tocar lo pè, ma maïre
E deisha li tocar ma hilha
2.
Mi vòu tocar la cama, ma maïre
E deisha li tocar ma hilha
3.
Mi vòu tocar lo jolh, ma maïre
4.
Mi vòu tocar la cuesha, ma maïre
5.
Mi vòu tocar lo « », ma maïre
E deisha li tocar ma hilha.

… a rye, humorous and/or warning song, a dialogue of sort, between mother and daughter:
“If it be so let it be so”!

Choru§:
Sarremilhòque is touching me, mother
Let him do it daughter
1.
He wants to touch me foot, mother
Why don’t you let him daughter
2.
He wants to touch me leg, mother
Why don’t you let him daughter
3.
He wants to touch me knee, mother
Why don’t you let him daughter
4.
He wants to touch me thigh, mother
Why don’t you let him daughter
5.
He wants to touch me “_”, mother
Why don’t you let him daughter?

…the words are a bit more explicit than yon puppy dog tickling toes (that’s the Gallic twist for you!) ..;and to be taken perhaps less lightly…

The melody is totally different but there is that tongue-twisting, lilting element to it as well.
Having said that it is an interesting variation on the theme and a reminder that themes and tropes transcended Geographic boundaries in Europe/in the World in all ages;
