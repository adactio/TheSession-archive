The Scottish Branle is a very popular traditional dance in certain parts of France, though originally it has been danced just by the aristocracy…
you can find it in the renaissance dance collection by Thoinot Arbeau, published in France in 1589. He called it “Orchésographie”.

information about the orchesography:
http://users.erols.com/eacain/dance/Arbeau1.html

French variation

In France, the A part is repeated. Also note the “AG” rather than “A2” in the second part.
You can play this with irregular strathspey-style quavers, with the first one shorter.
Anyway, I love this tune!

df ed | cB AG | GA BG | AB c2 |

Re: The Branle

I had a look at the setting I shared 14 years ago and as I had a few quibbles with it I decided to replace it with a new version. The main changes in this revised version are:
* Repeats - the B part should repeat too.
* Time signature - it seems better to me to write it in 4/4 (except for the last bar) with 3 bars per line - this follows the structure of the tune. (On this site we can’t edit the ABC headers, so maybe that’s why I wrote it in 2/4, 14 years ago. I think I probably didn’t think of adding an instruction “[M:…” in the body of the ABC to change the time signature.)
* The key - I think I’ve always heard it played in D (dorian) in the Paris, Quai de Seine “bals” (dance meet-ups).

A few other remarks:
* This quirky tune is one of my favourites. One thing I particularly like is the odd phrase length in the B part; hopefully this setting makes that more easy to see.
* Dancers have special steps for this tune, that go beautifully with it. Worth seeing, but I don’t have a video to share. At the end of the B part there’s a skip and a hop thing happening. I wonder how that could be notated too.
* The name/spelling used in France for this tune is: Bransle d’Écosse (with a silent S in the first word).
* In a comment above, “replicant” shared a link for a recording (Tempus Saltandi by Wolgemut). As that link is broken, here’s another link:
https://music.youtube.com/watch?v=RkiS93nkwbs
