Five comments

La Patte De Mouton

As part of the Wexford tune project, I’ve been trying to match tune titles named in the folklorist Patrick Kennedy’s books with possible or probable notation. One tune that caused me problems was finding a reel he named as ‘The Leg of mutton’. I failed to find anything until it occurred to me to try the name in French, and so I’m pondering if this Quebecois tune La Patte de mouton may be the answer. It’s not inconceivable that tunes may have traveled from the south east of Ireland to Quebec…there were several ships a year leaving the port of Wexford for Quebec City in the 19th century and there are quite a few French speaking Murphy’s in Quebec City. It would be good if someone might be able to say if this Quebecois tune is an old traditional melody?

Re: La Patte De Mouton

It’s based on a recording by Robin LeBlanc and Nicolas Basque. On the recording it’s played in D. I chose to transpose it to G as in the reference to ‘The Leg of Mutton’ reel in Patrick Kennedy’s writings, he was naming tunes played by pipers, as I recall. It’s a bit of a long-shot that the Acadienne tune is in fact the same tune that Kennedy referred to. I put it up here in the hope that someone might shed more light on the origins of ‘La Patte du Mouton’ or have a ‘Leg of Mutton’ tune perhaps in a manuscript or whatever.
You can listen to the recording here:
