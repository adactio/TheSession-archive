“The Wattentaler Mazurka / Masolka” ~ for Mr. & Mrs. Ptarmigan

“Wattentaler Masolka” ~ “VAH-ten-tahl-er Mah-ZOHL-kah”

This mazurka is from the Tyrol / Austria but is played in many other places too, including Slovenia and other places touched by the influence of ye ol’ Austro-Hungarian Empire, including North America, both sides of the border, used for couple dancing, including for just waltzing to…

I’ve been sitting on this one, but as you can see, it fits, being 32 measures in the transcription given. However, for the actual Austrain dance it is played as 16 measures in length, without repeats, but I’ll add other transcriptions next. I’ve known it to be played in G, C and F Major, depending on the limitations of the instrument being used, or the whim of the musician(s) playing it.

“The Wattentaler Mazurka / Masolka”

This tune, for the dance of the same name, is not usually repeated but is played as 16 measures in total, however, there are those that choose to play it in several keys, for example G to start, then maybe into C for a spell, and they you could drop back into G to end… This is a popular mazurka and is also played for couple dancing, including waltzing, in North America, both sides of the border. Here it is, again in G, simplified and without the parts repeating as in the given transcription.

I used to dance this one, and I quite like it. I’ll try to rough out a transcription of the dance and come back with it for those that might be interested. It’s a fun dance. I’ve known a number of these sorts of tie-yourself-and--your-partner-up-in-knots type dances, like one called the Zillertaller Laendler. The moves have travelled and I have seen Irish folks doing a few of them up Ulster way. They’d probably been representing the ol’ sod somewhere over on the European mainland and had picked up foreign influences? I know, I know, I should have reported them to the Gaelic League… ;-)
