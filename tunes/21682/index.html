Three comments

Scudding Through The Whins

I learned this fun reel off of John Rea’s album Traditional Music on the Hammer Dulcimer. He plays it rather quick and really takes off after he’s done playing the proceeding highlands. According to the LP liner notes he learned it from his father, whom had learned it from a Glenarm fiddler named Stewart Martin. I haven’t really been able to find much information about the tune, if anyone has anything to add I’d love to hear it.

↳ Posted by Cloudling on March 13th, 2022.

Scudding Through The Whins, X:2

This is very nearly the same as the previous setting, and also comes from John Rea’s playing. The minor differences really come down to his variations. This seems to be a fairly rare tune, and I haven’t encountered it anywhere else. Supposedly it also appears in Carlin’s Master Collection of Dance Music for the Violin, though I can’t say what his source was.

Just a quick note, it sounds like the dulcimer in the original recording was tuned a half-step flat, so the tune sounds in F# major.

As for the title, “scudding” means moving quickly and “whin” is an archaic name for furze or gorse. “Scooting through the Gorse” probably wouldn’t have the same appeal.

↳ Posted by j_clark on October 1st, 2025.
