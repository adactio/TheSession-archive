Crucaharan Cross

This was one of the first Irish tunes I learnt. It’s a duple time march, so it should be transcribed in 2/4 or 2/2 really, but I find that marches are too easy to confuse with polkas when they are written in 2/4, so I’ve made do with 4/4.

See? The Irish have dag too like the English, they just won’t admit it.

Re: The Crucaharan Cross

Crucaharan is the name of a mountain which shelters the town of Dingle in Co. Kerry on its northern side. At the base of the mountain, Cairn Ard runs down to meet Goat Street, and the local name of this junction is Crucaharan Cross. Charlie Flannery, who lived close by in Ashmount Terrace, taught this tune to the Dingle fife and drum bands. His grandson, Charlie Piggott, recorded it with De Danann along with another wren polka they misnamed The Banks of the Quay.
