Re: The Rose-Bud Of Allenvale

There are more recordings of that tune, if you accept string quartet.
The Scottish based Maxwell String Quartet on Linn Records (CKD 682) presents a quartet version, and also in a YouTube clip (Maxwell Quartet NY Debut: The Rosebud of Allenvale/ …).
The preceding Linn CD take, the final Allegretto of String quartet op. 71.2, by Joseph Haydn, and the rosebud tune, are very similar in their initial melody line (takes 9 and 10).
Skinner published his tune in the 1920s (Allenvale is the Aberdeen cemetery, where he is buried), Haydn published his quartet in 1973. Haydn had stayed in England 1991/92 (and again 1794/95).
Was there, is there a basic tune, which has influenced both, Haydn and Skinner?

You can hear Skinner himself playing here (with sympathetic piano accompaniment, something of sore point for JSS at times!):
https://www.abdn.ac.uk/scottskinner/music/cd119a.mp3

The manuscript arranged by McKenzie Murdoch and published versions:
https://www.abdn.ac.uk/scottskinner/display.php?ID=JSS0323
https://www.abdn.ac.uk/scottskinner/display.php?ID=JSS0710

I’m no expert but is the bass clef line in both written for cello (as the slightly different handwritten version suggests)?

“Dedicated by Skinner to his cousin Jessie, who was married to James Macdonald, the gardener at Allenvale Cemetery, Aberdeen, where Skinner is now buried. In a prophetic note to James Macdonald, Skinner wrote: ‘The Rosebud’ is in full bloom & will be played when you & I are asleep’.”

The print copy in Aberdeen uni collection shows “Allanvale” with the “a” crossed out and an “e” handwritten in.
In MacKenzie Murdoch’s handwritten manuscript of his arrangement, and Glasgow publisher Bayley & Ferguson, it seems it has assumed the more common Scottish spelling of “Allan” and perhaps some irritated Aberdeen local had written in the correct spelling of this cemetery on Allenvale road, opposite Duthie Park by the banks of the Dee

This Cape Breton tune is similar to but distinctive from the waltz by the same name. Not properly a jig, but rather a slow 6/8 march.

↳ Posted by Paddy Squeezebox on December 28th, 2024.

Re: The Rose-Bud Of Allenvale

IIRC, I learned The Rosebud of Allenvale waltz from two fiddle-mentors of mine, Kim Vincent and David ‘Papper’ Papazian, who resided ‘down East’ in Canada’s Prince Edward Island and Cape Breton Island, Nova Scotia respectively ..
They in turn may well have learned it from fiddlers around there, or perhaps from the playing of Buddy MacMaster (RIP)
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_MacMaster

Buddy MacMaster’s playing The Rosebud followed by a traditional Cape Breton medley of marches, schottisches and reels, can be heard on this Smithsonian Folkways recording, and attributed to J. Scott Skinner, whose compositions were a bridge between classical and trad-fiddle /-bagpipe music --
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TILYnvlqTr0
