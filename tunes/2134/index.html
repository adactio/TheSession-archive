An “Unofficial” Title

You may hear this tune referred to as “The Trip to Bangor” by 40-something musicians from the north of Ireland because of it’s melodic similarity to the song of the same name. Learned from Gary Hastings and Ciaran Curran, I haven’t heard or played this tune for years.

↳ Posted by Arthur Gondolor on June 7th, 2014.

When repeating the ‘A’ part, play the first lead in note (an A). When you are going from the ‘A’ part to the ‘B’ part, play the F# instead.

So first time round, the last bar is: ABc d2A

Second time round (on your way to the ‘B’ part) play: ABc d2f

Re: Captain White’s - repeats?

The settings shown here, and those I’ve heard in sessions, vary in respect of the way the parts are played.
X:1 gives it as a three-part tune, each part repeated.
X:2 has the first two parts repeated, but not the third.
X:3 has no repeats at all.
X:4 & X:5 repeat the first part but not the second and third.
I heard it played recently with the first part repeated, and the second and third run together as one and then repeated.

It’s probably not that important in the cosmic scheme of things, and I see it’s been raised before (see above) but does anyone know if there is a convention about the order of events in this tune? Or does it just get played in whatever way fits the length of the dance it’s being used for? Or what you feel like on the day?
