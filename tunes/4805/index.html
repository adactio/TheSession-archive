Ten comments

This is the second of a set of two slides under the same name on Denis Murphy & Julia Clifford’s “Star Above the Garter” recording. The transcription is from the playing of Johnny O’Leary.
The first slide in the set was called Padraic O‘Keefe’s by Johnny O’Leary, and I think Tommy Peoples plays it as a jig called “The Bank of Turf” on his Iron Man album. I don’t think it is posted yet, and if not, I will put it up also.

Bank of Turf

The Bank of Turf as a jig is posted here on the session as tune 1819. The slide form of the tune, as played in Slieve Luachra before the Scartaglen slide, can be found in Terry Moylan’s book on Johnny O‘Leary as tune 38 under the name of Padraig O’Keefe’s slide (1).
Hi Matt- yes, the Scartaglen reel is another Slieve Luachra classic, and is always played after a tune which is called Banks of the Ilen in O’Neill’s.

The Scartaglen, X:2

This setting is based on Track 5 of the 1998 c.d. by Ruth Dornfeld, Joel Bernstein and Keith Murphy. They call it a jig. I love the way that the harmonica bends notes and chugs away at the melody til the fiddle bursts in and restores some decorum. Nice change into ‘Out on the Ocean’ too. Couldn’t recall how to re-“naturalise” notes (in ABC) after accidental f#’s in the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd bars but hope that recognising it’s the key of C major will save me from the ABC polizie. Some excellent playing on this c.d., though very little Irish stuff.

The Scartaglen, X:3

The Scartaglen Jig from transcribed from the Star Above the Garter album. This is transcribed from the second playthrough, the first few bars of the first playthrough are a bit different…

I would wonder why is listed as a slide - accents seem to be on the 3rd triplet of every four rather than the 4th like in many slides. Also a big clue for me being the name containing the word ‘Jig’ - but perhaps it has morphed to more of a slide in recent times?

Re: The Scartaglen

A nice touch is to play the ‘c’ as a natural, last note of the 2nd last bar.

This comes from Johnny Duffy, born in Dublin, who grew up and learnt the music in Australia. Now lives in the peaceful town of Stanthorpe, Queensland.
Extraordinary musician. But quiet about it. Maybe others have had the same idea, but I learnt it from Johnny and I suspect he came up with it.
