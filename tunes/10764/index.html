Polka De Gustave Ythier

Quaint little French tune coming from the playing of Gustave Ythier.

↳ Posted by irishfiddleMA on October 28th, 2010.

One can hear this “polka piquée” performed by Jean-Marc Delaunay and a pupil at: http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wFext3wVp80 (in the key of G)

‘Quaint’ perhaps it is to Irish ears… It is fascinating to see how the Bohemian polka evolved into so many different sounding tunes wherever it found a niche for itself; from Mexico to Portugal, Kerry, Limousin, Russia…
A great number of fiddle and pipe tunes from the Limousin / Auvergne area are mixolydian/ plagal when, say, typical Scottish pipe tunes are only mixolydian when in the authentic mode and major in the plagal mode.

↳ Posted by birlibirdie on October 28th, 2010.

↳ Posted by birlibirdie on October 28th, 2010.

PS: that other “Polka à Gustave Ythier” shows an altogether quainter side to French music: an ‘odd’ number of bars and a melody repeated on the offbeat! This is also a feature of some Breton or Basque dance tunes as far as I’m aware.

Alfred Mouret’s Polka…

…could be an alternative title to this polka, he is the late fiddler from the Artense region who played it under the name ‘Pas Piqué’.
I got these details from the sleevenotes of François Lazarevitch’s album ‘danses des bergers’ http://www.lesmusiciensdesaintjulien.fr/cadre-engl.htm This polka is the very last tune on this album.
FL toured with “The 4 Pipers project” that involved Mick O’Brien amongst participants a couple of years ago.
