Thirteen comments

F# minor

Very old lovely tune. Not to be played too fast, in fact I first learned this as more of a slower listening piece. There are some opportunities for grace notes, but definately don’t use any icky rolls. Keep it simple and sensitive with this one. i also would like to stress the balance of A major and F# minor in this tune, very nice. And in case you’re wondering I’m pretty sure this one’s Scottish, though it has elements of some standard Irish jigs and even hints at a lot of the more modern comps I’ve heard being played. Learned from an ould Scotsman who had a taste for the prettier tunes.

Re: Cairngorum Mountain

I found this lovely old slow air under the name Cairngorum Mountain in “Neil Gow’s 4th Collection of Strathspeys, Reels &c” published in 1817 by his son. Neil Gow was Scottish, but that doesn’t mean the tune was originally Scottish. It does feel Scottish to me with the dotted rhythms and the key of A. There are two notations included: “Very Slow” and “A very Old Galic Air.”

↳ Posted by Raymond Gargan on April 26th, 2025.

Re: Cairngorum Mountain

This tune was published in Patrick McDonald’s collection of 1784 under the title “An Irish Air.” I’ve also come across it as “The Highlander’s Lament,” “The Irish Girl,” “The Hibernian Jig” and “A Clan March.” Contrary to the Gows’ “very slow,” the expression in Patrick McDonald is “Brisk.”
