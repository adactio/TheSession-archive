Eleven comments

This tune has an unusual structure. The short third part is actually more like the end of the second part.

The second part has a lot of high, long notes. These can be rolled or turned into triplets. For instance, you could start the second part with BAB GAG instead of just B and G.

Re: Johnny Boyle’s

A “jig to mazurka workshop”;

These two jigs are good material for a 6/8 to 3/4 transformation:

Here’s one of the possible 3/4 take on the first one;
R: mazurka
M: 3/4
|: DE GB AG|B2 dB AG|c2 ed BG|cB cA GE|
DE GB AG|BB dG AB|de f2 dB|cA G3 B:|
|:b2 g3g|dg bb ag|a2 f3f|df aa fa|
b2 bg gd|dg b2 ag|a3 aff|df af gd:|
|:e2 e2 dB|ed Bg dB|ed cB AG|cB cA GE|
DE GB AG|Bd BB AB|de f2 dB|cAG3 G:|]

(Play dotted quavers in each pair; “>”)
