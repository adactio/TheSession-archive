Originally a Shaker song in Cmajor by Elder Joseph Brackett, later adapted to Sydney Carter’s “Lord of the Dance” song. Now used in Michael Flatley’s The Lord of the Dance, from where it’s usually known.

Here is the original Cmajor form:

X:1
T:Simple Gifts
M:4/4
R:Reel
C:Elder Joseph Brackett
K:C
|c2cd ecef|g2gg e2dc|d2d2 d2d2|dedB G2G2|
|cBcd e2dd|e2f2 g3g|d2de d2cc|d2cB c4|
|g4 e3d|efed c3d|e2ef g2e2|d2de d3G|
|c4 c3d|e2ef g2gg|d2d2 e2ed|c2c2 c4||

Re: Simple Gifts’ complex history

Giovanni Gabrieli (1557 - 1613) was a historically important composer from Venice and a contemporary of Shakespeare and William Byrd. His “Canzon seconda a quattro”(1608) uses the motif which Brackett ‘came up’ with 240 years later. (viz. 2 to 3 bars match).

It was Aaron Copland (1900-1990) who was responsible for giving a new popularity to Brackett’s dance tune in the 20th century.

Carter and Flateley simply reused a material already ‘proofed’ for popularity/a larger audience.

Here is what Gabrieli work sounds like;
