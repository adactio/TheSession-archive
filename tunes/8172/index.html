Transcription Query

The version given has strayed from the collected version substantially. Bert played a simple one key harmonica with a solid vamping rhythm. So many of the accidentals given here are embellishments/ variations that are more reminiscent of 19th C sheet music. I am interested to know of its evolution from its source.

↳ Posted by Dave Johnson on February 27th, 2008.

Lancers gan ainm

I would have likely picked it up my way with it in the Maritimes, possibly also New Enlgand, both have strong quadrille/Lancers traditions (as does Australia), and most likely from dance fiddlers, an instrument not suffering the limitations of a diatonic harmonica… As is true with much of this music, since the accidentals given in the first transcription don’t fall on the primary notes of the beat, it doesn’t matter one iota whether everyone in a session or playing for dance uses accidentals or not. However, I agree with you about the tune in general, accidentals or not, it sounds like something out of the 1800s and I suspect it was likely a tune Bert picked up rather than composed himself… It’s another of those chicken or egg questions… ;-) That is also why I gave the added transcription, for comparison…

I did try an initial search of early literature to see if I could find a transcription for it from those times, and a ‘name’, but there was a hell of a lot of publishing going on then, and with regards to music for the sets of quadrilles (for which “The Lancers” is a member)… I used to have a load of it digitized but lost that recently with a major hard drive failure… I don’t have easy access to those libraries anymore, but am slowly rebuilding that valued and missed resource…

19th C Music ~

You’ll find that the majority of the melodies found in that lovely collection, as well as in many other places, have that identity, accidentals or not, of being “reminiscent of 19th C ~ music”… After all, it is the time of the waltz and polka, the mazurka and the varsouvienne and a hell of a lot of the dance and music that much of what we do now is firmly rooted to… And, bending to accidentals is not something a diatonic harmonica is completely unable to do…but fudging such things was common practice with playesr of the harmonica and single row melodeon… As one jolly old fella put it ~ “you play around the notes you don’t have…”

Origins of Bert Jamieson’s Lancers Set Tune

I transcribed this tune back in 1990 from an original recording of harmonica player Bert Jamieson. Bert’s tunes had been recorded by John Meredith and Rob Willis. The original tapes are in the National Library of Australia. The tune was part of a collection of Bert’s tunes I put together and published through Wongawilli Colonial Dance Club. The Wongawilli Band recorded The Tunes of Bert Jamieson in 1991 with Rob and Olya Willis. The band played to the original music of Bert and was structured for dancing.
