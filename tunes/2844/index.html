The Rose Of Drishane (hornpipe)

Source: “The Smoky Chimney” by Eoghan O’Sullivan, Gerry Harrington, Paul De Grae
Transcription: gmp

↳ Posted by gian marco on April 17th, 2004.

The Rose Of Drishane, X:2

This is a really nice Sliabh Luachra hornpipe. It has a bit of a Northumbrian feel, as many of the local hornpipes tend to, but I don’t know of any relations it has. Drishane is near Millstreet in the southern part of the region and is host to a beautiful castle. Interestingly it made it into O’Neill’s (#1631), a testament to its history as it was likely given by Edward Cronin, who would’ve left the area in the 1880’s.

Johnny O’Leary and Denis Murphy turn it over for Seamus Ennis:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYEz71R8J8A

Jack O’Sullivan turns it over as one of two Tom Billy hornpipes:

Re: The Rose Of Drishane

It’s actually more likely to be a Welsh tune originally. There are two versions of it in Goodman. One under the title ‘A Welsh Hornpipe’ (in Goodman’s 1st manuscript dated 1861) and another as ‘The Swansea Hornpipe’ (in his 3rd manuscript dated 1866). These predate both O‘Neill’s collection (1903) and The Francis Reynolds Collection (1885), in which it is titled ’The Dublin Hornpipe’. Interesting how tunes travel and adapt.

↳ Posted by FerrysidePiper on April 6th, 2023.

Re: The Rose Of Drishane / Egans’s

O’Neill has two settings: one in his Music of Ireland 1850 (#1631 as Rose of Drishane). His source was likely Michael Hartnett of West Cork. The other in his WSGM ( #352) as Egan’s Hornpipe, taken from the Rice-Walsh, Mss, and so from Jerry Breen the North Kerry fiddler.

↳ Posted by brailsford on January 20th, 2024.

The Rose Of Drishane, X:3

This version in G major was published in a 2022 issue of the Comhaltas Magazine “Treoir” and attributed to the 1948 recording by Denis Murphy and Johnny O’Leary, but they played it in D, by the sound of it (above). So why did Comhaltas transcribe it in G? I have to say I myself like it better in the lower register, as it sounds more mellow and pleasing, but I can appreciate that it is not so good for the flute, whistle and pipes players as it goes down to low B several times. Grand for a box player, though.
