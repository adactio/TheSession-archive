Hand Me Down My Petticoat

Hand me down my petticoat

Nb 65 p.73. from The Singing Island
a book of songs published by Peggy Seeger & Ewan MacColl in 1960
(£1.50 from Belwin-Mills Music Ltd. of Denmark Street, London)

1.
Hand me down my petticoat and
hand me down my shawl
Hand me down my petticoat
for I am off to the Linen Hall
-Chorus:
With you he was a quare one
“folty took away wi dat”
He was a quare one, I’ll tell you
2.
If you go to the Curragh camp
just ask for number nine
You’ll get three squaddies standing there
And the best looking one is mine
3.
Oh if you join the army
all under a false name
To do me out of me money
it’s his ould one’s all to blame
4.
If you put him to the wars
and out to fight the Boers
Will you try and hold the Dublins back
see the bogmen go before
5
My love he’s over the ocean
and my darlin’s over the sea
My love he was a darlin chap
left me in the family way.

~

The notes p.112 tell us the song was collected “from the singing of Brenden (sic) Behan of Dublin, 1957”.
My friend, who is a true Dub and learning the banjo, sung this song to me today.
She got it off the book but (unwittingly?) reinterpreted the music; I wrote it down more or less the way she sung it.
Whether you read the original or listen to the arrangement, the melody (a polka) sounds rather more American to me than Irish.
But here goes.

↳ Posted by birlibirdie on May 8th, 2023.

Hand Me Down My Petticoat, X:2

This setting (X: 2) is a transposition in D maj. of the notes as they appear in the book (F maj.)

So, yes, my Friend’s version (X: 1) is a rather creative take on the original.

Last night the tune sounded rather American old timey to me. This morning my perception has changed. The world of perception is rather shifty I guess!

This tune sounded pentatonic to me at first but it includes all 7 degrees of the ionian mode. Degrees 2 and 6 are rather scarce though: a feature which makes you wander if the tune was in the pentatonic minor (‘blues scale’) in a previous incarnation?
