Many Irish tunes are quite simple, which is very different from easy. You can play almost any tune in a wide range of levels. I could happily listen to Kevin Burke, James Kelly, Liz Carroll, Paddy Keenan, any of the greats, play the Kesh.

The Kesh is a good tune to give to beginners because it is catchy (and so they can learn it fairly easily by ear), it swings easily (so they can start learning feel), and, if you have learned well and know what you’re about, you can make it a less simple tune as your skill level rises.

Kesh - a bog passgeway

For anyone interested in the origins of names, the name Kesh is found here & there in Ireland. It refers to a passageway across wet ground, bog etc. A sort of bridge of wicker matting used in older times to facilitate crossing without getting your feet too wet.

Kesh Jig

X:7 from a 1919 recording of piper Patrick Fitzpatrick: https://archive.org/details/PatrickFitzpatrickComedyMaid I once saw Fitz’s set of Taylor pipes - “One of Taylor’s masterpieces” as Chief O’Neill described it. The bass and double bass regulators were made entirely from sheet metal, and the toneholes could be slid up and down to perfect tuning/tone. Just amazing work. On the label of the 78 this title here was “Comedy Maid” - in all likelihood some record company flunky couldn’t decipher Patrick’s brogue pronunciation of “Comely.”

Re: The Kesh

to answer Nicholas’s question from 18 years ago (“Who, if anyone, played /recorded this tune before the Bothies made it a hit round 1975? Its earlier history remains unknown to me”) this was one of the very first pieces of Irish Traditional Music ever recorded, by Edward Herborn (tenor banjo) and James Wheeler (button accordion) in 1917. On the recording it is called The Rambler’s Jig, though what they are playing is definitely what we would call The Kesh.
