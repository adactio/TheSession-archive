Five comments

Paddy Hayes’s

A fun little jig I learned from Karen Tweed. In her words:

"The jig was learned by button accordionist (from Newtownforbes, Co Longford) Jim McLoughlin who taught it to me. He attributes it to button accordionist Paddy Hayes, who I knew when he lived in London - marvelous accordionist and mighty character. Hilarious, loving and so encouraging to me as a young player. He moved back to Ireland some years ago but I am not sure where - possibly Leitrim. Paddy was part of that great London-Irish accordion scene and probably knew Finbarr Dwyer and definitely was one of the group that included players such as Raymond Rowland and John Bowe. For me, Paddy Hayes‘ music shines out like a beacon yet he was never really celebrated in the same way as Raymond, John or others. For me, Paddy Hayes’ music has a magical lyrical quality all of its own. As you can see, I’m a big fan.

I believe the tune came from a cassette recording of Paddy Hayes and I have never met anyone else who knows this tune so Jim and I wonder if it is a composition of Paddy’s."

↳ Posted by Ian Varley on December 4th, 2019.

Re: Paddy Hayes’s

This is indeed a composition of Paddy’s although he composed it in the key of A major, with some variation from the notation here. It’s called Claudie’s Davy Brown (referring to a tractor, I reckon?) and it’s on his second album, The Shores of Lough Melvin. I’m busily at work with the Wexford tune project and since Paddy R.I.P. was a proud Wexfordman, exiled for much of his life in London and later in Leitrim, his music is naturally being included in the project. I had in fact only today notated the two tunes that were in the set of jigs on the recording where this is the second one. ( The first is a tricky tune called The Gate Lodge). As soon as I’ve more completely deciphered (as fully as my limited ability allows) Paddy’s version, I’ll upload the setting from the album. I only realised now that this listing was here. Paddy Hayes composed around 20 tunes of which only a few seem to be known or notated as far as I know.
And yes, he did play the concertina upside down!

↳ Posted by John E Roche on June 23rd, 2022.

Paddy Hayes’s, X:2

Here, as far as I can notate it, is Paddy’s version of the tune from his second album, where he calls it Claudie’s Davy Brown. Now I’ve reached the conclusion that he ‘recomposed’ this jig at some point, because a jig from his ‘Return to Kilty’ first album, which he calls The Cornmore, has many similarities (and it’s in G like X1 above) but this ‘A’ version has a totally reworked second part. I wonder did he dislike the second part of ‘Cornmore’ (similarities to other jigs perhaps?).
I think he may have decided to move the pitch of part 1 up a tone and compose a new turn for the jig, giving it a new name. Anyway, I’ve included the Wexford ‘signature triplets’ that he uses throughout his accordion playing in my notation. I feel then that using the playback feature hopefully gives a bit more of the feel of the playing.

↳ Posted by John E Roche on June 25th, 2022.

Re: Paddy Hayes’s Jig

Claudie’s Davy Brown - yes that title does refer to a make of farm tractor manufactured by David Brown & Company.
The Claudie in question was Claude Francis, a farmer, on one of the Saltee Islands off Kilmore Quay, Co. Wexford. There was a small tractor in use on the island where potatoes and other vegetable crops were sown. Tommy Hayes, a brother of Paddy Hayes, worked at one time for Claude Francis on the island. Like his brother, Paddy, Tommy was also an excellent 2-row button accordionist - playing a Paolo Soprani.
