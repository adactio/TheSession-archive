The D version sits beautifully on the Uilleann Pipe’s drone, as does the A version on the Scottish pipe’s.

Hi Zane,
one year on, and perhaps you have found what you were looking for.
There are numerous tutor books on the dagdad / “Celtic” guitar technique , and videos.
The simpler the better (less is more) ; remember than this tune (We’re a case…) was originally composed for the pipes, which sole harmonic backing is a drone. And it works.

Also, in Irish and Scots trad. circles, songs are songs (they are sung) and tunes are tunes (instrumentals); it’s both lazy and vague to call all pieces of music “song” (this usage can be traced to the mp3 platforms who’ve been using this term for decades now, the philistines! But here you go).

The video you referecenced contains several tunes: only the last one, by the great Allan MacDonald, is a dance / session tune.
This site is more concerned with melodies than chords, but if you check some of the discussions you’ll find tips and links to propell you forward on your Celtic journey.
