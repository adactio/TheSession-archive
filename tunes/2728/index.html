Benoît, note that it is customary -and more legible- to write the quavers in groups of 4, as in:

X: 1
T: Ginley’s
M: 4/4
L: 1/8
R: reel
K: E dorian
|:GEBE E2BE|G3B AFDF|GEBE GABd|(3efg fg edcd|
BEEF G2BE|G3B AFDF|G2BG FGAF|1 EFGA BEEF :|2 EFGA Beef||
|: gbef gbeg| fddc dfaf| gggz gfeg| fedB eeez|
geBA ^GABd|cdeg fdd2| (3gab eg fece|1 dBAF eeef :|2 dBAF EEEF ||

Note also the Key: E dorian, not D major. The #’s are the same, but it is helfpul to write the right mode in as it helps people in their tune search.

Starry Lane to Monaghan Commentary

Barnagrove is near the Monaghan border and on a starry night, as the story goes, one could look down this lane and see clear through to the neighbouring county. Reavy’s mother came from Monaghan and Ed had a special fondness for Monaghan people.

Source:- ‘The Collected Compositions of Ed Reavy’ by Joseph M. Reavy

This is by far one of my favourite Reavy compositions, lovely tune all round.
