Being helpful, my advice would be to keep learning tunes, keep writing tunes. Keep a record of all the tunes you ‘write’. Don’t post them here to clog up the database and, potentially, to embarass you for years to come. If you need advice/help on a tune, try posting a discussion, or, perhaps better, ask a helpful member here - like, say, ceolachan. (Anyone except me :-) )

One day, the tunes will just work. And then, maybe someone else will post them. Until then, put in the ground work.

↳ Posted by ethical blend on January 20th, 2010.

Hi Dom,

The rule of thumb is that if you’re submitting your own tune, you should submit five other tunes you didn’t write beforehand. It’s not a “forum rule” like “don’t call people douchebags on the message board” is a forum rule; rather it’s grounded, as ceol said, in respecting the traditions which came before you and will be here long after you. It also has purely practical value -- not clogging up the tunes database -- but more importantly the element of self-indulgence of writing a tune and posting it here is anathemic to the traditions we know and love.

Granted, not everyone shares my slightly extreme views. I refuse to even write tunes. What have I to contribute to the tradition? F*ck all. Would any tune I write be as good or grow wings of its own and enter into the tradition like thousands and thousands of fantastic tunes? I doubt it.

I have friends who write tunes and some of those tunes are brilliant and have made the rounds of the Scottish session scene and even crossed the Atlantic. Some may even be posted here, but by other people. As ethical blend says, put in the groundwork. :)
