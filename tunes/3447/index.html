A Yow Cam Tae Wir Duir, Yarmin, Yarmin.

For many years I played with a Shet fiddler,Ian McAlpine, a carpenter (who at one time had an apprentice called Aly Bain)
He told me this story of the terrible blizzard one time in Shetland and how the shepherd heard this piteous bleating coming from outside his door. A strange thing, a ewe had come to the door and was bleating in a particular way.
On with the big coat and boots , the collie already waiting at the door and out into swirling snow, following the ewe leading ahead .
Then the small sound of higher feeble bleating. A lamb fallen off the edge and in danger of being buried by the snow. He climbed down and up it comes back up to anxiously watching mama.
Back to the cottage then , following the Shepherds Lantern, the white tip of the collie’s tail.
Back to Dulce Domum, very glad of the roaring fire and a wee dram.
Bairns all crowding around the da, wanting to know the story.
The fiddle handed down.
And handed down with it, the wee song and tune.
