Turlutte Arcadienne

“In Quebec, the turlutte is a form of traditional popular song, which is associated with specific melody ornaments. It is more accurately characterized as a song or vocal style in the French chanson tradition.”
(From Wikipedia)

↳ Posted by Siúlóir on November 10th, 2018.

Re: Turlutte Arcadienne

“Turluter” is a way to sing without words, something like a bird, like melodic instruments. The singer “La Bolduc” was very good at that. The instrumental solo part of her songs was often made by herself singing a turlute. I don’t think this way of doing is confined to Québec or Canada as some very nice “Turlutte” can be heard when Irish musicians play the chorus of “The Real Old Mountain Dew”.

↳ Posted by Laurent Hubert on November 12th, 2018.

Re: Turlutte Acadienne

I changed the title from Arcadienne to Acadienne….99.99% sure that’s what’s intended here. Acadien/Acadienne means from Acadia, which refers to parts of the Eastern Canadian Maritime provinces, plus part of Québec (Gaspé) and part of Maine, from back when the French controlled that whole area. Arcadia is a region of Greece, and used as a place name in several other countries, but this tune, and the turlutte in general, is French-Canadian for sure, so I’m going with Acadia.
