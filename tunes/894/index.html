Mr. Burke is a wonderful storyteller and a very funny man.

Real Kevin Burke Tunes

For starters, he’s written 2 other jigs in Bm! One, The Split Rock, is on the Open House album Hoof and Mouth. It’s a real Kevin Burke slow and sensual one. The other is called Across the Black River. I’m not sure if he’s recorded this one or not - it’s a bit livlier by nature.

I first heard him play Up in the Air at a concert in 1981 or 82 - he hadn’t got a name for it yet and was joking about having a suggestion box on the way out. Wonder how he came up with the name in the end. Anybody know?

The 3 albums by Open House feature quite a few of his compositions.

Re: Up In The Air

I love this moody tune. It holds a special place for me (both in the repertoire and in my memories: it seems to sum up the mood of the Portland album somehow)
If it holds such a special place for me it is because it aludes to other tunes -well known; bright or energetic- but recasts them in a more atmospheric (dusky rather than gloomy) light.
I would compare it with a familar Irish mountain silhouette which, now, half disappears under a cloudy shroud. (Its “attractivity” disappears but its gravity increases, sort of a thing)
Namely;
The first part begins with a pattern found (in A)
in The Lilting Banshee
https://thesession.org/tunes/60#setting60
The second part is a recollection (in B aeolian) of The Pipe on the Hob’s third part (in A minor hexatonic)
https://thesession.org/tunes/81
To me it’s like the basic, shared Irish repertoire is a (mental) landscape to Burke & Ó Dhomnaill and they repaint it, in tunes like this, in the colours of their mood, with their own emotional brush (Portland, Promenade… The finest stuff!)

Up In The Air, X:4

A setting in A aeolian.
Note that this jig is almost entirely hexatonic, with no 6th in it except for the semi-climactic F natural at the end (the aeolian 6th); this F can be reached (fingered) by sliding in from the e (an almost required move), and back, and thus easily doable on the whistle.
