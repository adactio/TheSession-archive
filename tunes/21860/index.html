Y.892

I found this tune in Beòlach’s newest album, All Hands, and took quite a liking to it. I inquired about the tune’s rather strange name to the band’s pianist, Mac Morin, and it turns out that it actually has quite an interesting story behind it. Apparently, this tune was composed by a Cape Breton fiddler by the name of John MacDougall, who passed away in 2008 at the age of 83. John composed upwards of 38 000 tunes; but he did not take credit for any of them, instead believing himself to be a “conduit” for the unwritten tunes of fiddle players long passed. Most of the tunes did not have names, and so they were instead catalogued alphanumerically, as was the case with this tune. John was very secretive about his music, and was known to have gone so far as to lock it away in a safe so that others could not access it. He was happy to share his music with others, according to Mac, but he would not let anyone else learn it, for fear that the melodies would be corrupted or altered. John’s third cousin Ian MacDougall inherited John’s music after his passing, and Ian has begun to share some of it to the world. According to firsthand accounts, all of MacDougall’s tunes have an extremely unique quality to them, and incredible distinctness despite the immense volume of them.

Here is Beòlach’s recording of the tune (1:32): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d83JJss6qls

Re: Y.892

I think the claims of “uniqueness” were probably rather heavily exaggerated by those who knew him personally. I kind of agree that this tune doesn’t really stand out as anything special (though Beòlach’s interpretation of it was quite fun). As for the distinctness, Mac told me that there were certainly many tunes that were near carbon copies of each other—I don’t think anyone would be able to write over 38 000 tunes without at least some overlap. When he was telling me about the distinctness, I think he was more referring to the body of works as a whole; there was quite a large variation in the melodies he composed, and you could flip to any random page in his manuscripts and find something fresh.

@Kenny there are some-200 manuscript books that came into Ian MacDougall’s possession after John’s passing. Some of the books have been circulating around among musicians on Cape Breton Island—Mac showed me one that he had kept after the 2017 Celtic Colours concert, which featured some of John’s music. I’m not sure what Ian’s plans are in terms of publishing John’s tunes, he seems intent on sharing them with the world. I imagine the archival of such a number of tunes will take some time.

Re: Y.892

Listening to Ian MacDougall play through a spate of John M’s tunes, they’re reminiscent of many standard Cape Breton/Scottish tunes. Which is no surprise, since they follow the usual forms, format, and chord progressions. They’re also mostly simple repeated phrases rather than sinuous Fahey-esque meanders or Reavy-like forays into fresh territory.

Like Kenny, I’m more interested in quality over quantity, but sometimes you have to churn through a lot of ore to find a few gems.

So while the tunes aren’t “extremely unique,” they are a contribution to the Cape Breton tradition, and the sheer scope of them is remarkable. On the program in the clip, they said he birthed about two tunes a day, every day, for the last 40 years of his life. That works out to just under 30,000 tunes, so perhaps some days saw the water break on three, four, or more tunes. Seems like Colin Farrell’s been slacking off. ;-)

I’m glad they’re seeing the light of day. There might be some real keepers in the lot. Time will tell.
