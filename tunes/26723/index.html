Three comments

The Miller’s Daughter

This “Miller’s Daughter” is much older than that in O’Neill’s (https://thesession.org/tunes/20216),
having appeared, as a two part tune, in several 18th century publications (Thompson’s Vol3[1773], Aird Vol2[1785], etc).

This four part setting has been transcribed from Kevin MacLeod’s playing on Highland Strands:
https://youtu.be/CPaPHalh00s?list=RDCPaPHalh00s&t=91
and is probably adapted from John MacColl’s 1939 bagpipe setting (which, of course, has no G# in it). It seems likely that John MacColl added the third and fourth parts, as all pipe settings prior to this, as far as I can tell, are just (the first) two parts. In several of the pipe collections it is called “The Miller’s Fair Daughter” (from the Gaelic “Nighan Bhan A Mhuiller”).

Re: The Miller’s Daughter

It sure is very like The Scotsville.
There’s a two part bagpipe setting (as Miller’s Fair Daughter) in William Gunn’s “Caledonian Repository” of 1848:
https://ceolsean.net/content/Gunn/Book03/Book03%201.pdf
as well as various late 18th century publications (as mentioned above).

It’s curious (and possibly uncoincidental) that The Scotsville appears at roughly the same time as the four part setting of The Miller’s Daughter. Very intriguing.

It’s also worth pointing out that four part pipe tunes in A tend to have a certain shape, and the simplicity of both tunes means that similarities with others are possible.
