Re: The Hawk Of Ballyshannon

Emer Mayock very convincingly turned this Planxty/stately piece into a breakneck pace polka, launching headlong into semiquaver runs as she did.
It works either way (the Mulholland Belfast edition of 1810 bears the mention “dolce” at the head of the stave reminding us of its prepolkian days).

There is no agreement as who originated the tune, by 1950’s scholarly standards at least, which was exclusively based on written source material. Most of the harpers gathered by Bunting in 1792 seemed to be in agreement Rory Dall O’Cahan composed it, could a scholar take their word for it?
Whatever the truth of those assertions, Turlough O‘Carolan added words to the melody to celebrate the wedding of Charles O’Donnell (of Co Mayo) to Katherine O‘More (from then on O’More no more!). Charles was one of the brothers of Mrs MacDermott Roe, another lady musically celebrated by O’Carolan. Hence the two main strands of titles.
