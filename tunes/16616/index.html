Flowers Of The Forest

This is a slow lament rather than a reel, and commemorates the Battle of Flodden in 1513. This old tune is
kept in an Edinburgh library (lute tablature, Skene manuscript). Title refers to Scots from Ettrick Forest who
fell in the battle. Jane Elliot wrote a lyric published c 1755 :

I’ve heard them liltin‘ at the ewe milkin’,
Lassies a liltin‘ before dawn o’ day
Now there’s a moanin‘ on ilka green loanin’
The flo‘ers o’ the Forest are a’ wede away.

So tune is also known as “The Liltin”. On The Session “Flowers of the Forest” is also posted in waltz time and
that tune certainly resembles this. I’ve only seen versions of it in 4/4 time. The above is courtesy of J.
Murray Neil (his 1991 book about the Scots Fiddle). He also gives a modern version of the tune.

I’ve only ever heard this as a slow 3. However, rather than being notated in 3/4, like many other Scottish laments (e.g., Hector The Hero) it’s notated in 6/8. That’s the tradition, it seems.

Re: Flowers Of The Forest

I think the Skene manuscript setting is the earliest one (1620), and there it’s in 4/4, but marked “slow”. I have seen it in another old manuscript, also in 4/4, but as a reel, but I can’t quite remember where that was. Oh, hang on - it was the Gillespie manuscript of 1768 (via Scottish Fiddle Music in the 18th Century by David Johnson, 1984).

Re: Flowers Of The Forest

If the tune is that old (1620) it no doubt evolved over the centuries, in 3/4, 6/8, 2/4, or 4/4 and I rather like
that. Another version appears in J Murray Neil’s book (he calls it Modern Air (to distinguish it from the old
air) and says it is played 4/4 in march tempo at the Selkirk Common Riding, along with the old air. Similar
but more complex.
