An Bratach Bana

Transcript John Chambers. Scots ,Scots Irish, recorded as Bratach Ban by Horselips.

↳ Posted by Proinnsias MacSleimhne on January 25th, 2020.

Re: An Bratach Bana

Originally a song in Scots Gaelic, it was composed by singer Calum Kennedy and guitarist George Elliot and recorded in the mid 1950s. Sometimes Kennedy’s credit on songs was merely to ensure it got recorded, in a quid pro quo agreement (“Lovely Stornoway” is a good example of this). I don’t know whether this is the case here, however. The melody quickly became popular with Scottish dance bands for The Dashing White Sergeant etc.

An Bratach Bana, X:2

The version and key I’ve most often heard played for Scottish Country Dancing.

Sometimes one hears the first two lines of the 2nd part repeated, then played a 3rd time with the other ending phrase, thus the 1st part is played twice but the 2nd part is played thrice. I almost thought I’d write it out that way, but it’s a bit odd and confusing.

Re: Bratach Bàna

Perhaps it would have been more accurate to say that Kennedy and Elliot CLAIMED authorship, as we know that in the murky waters of the recording industry, occasionally copyright claims were a way of ensuring a greater financial return. I don’t know [1].

Calum Kennedy recorded the song in 1956 on the Beltona label, accompanied on guitar by George Elliot. The attribution given was Elliot/Kennedy; other recordings at that time gave “trad arr. Elliot/Kennedy” for other songs. He rerecorded it with orchestra in 1959 with the same Elliot/Kennedy, and subsequent reissues all had the same.

The Lorax Archive recording from 1951 suggests an earlier version, so perhaps Kennedy and Elliot simply rearranged it, possibly with different words; again, I don’t know [1].

Kennedy certainly had much to do with recording industry stalwarts of the 50s, especially Bob Halfin, a Tin Pan Alley veteran who wrote or had a hand in countless songs of all types (he wrote that ‘classic’ song “You’re a Pink Toothbrush, I’m a Blue Toothbrush”). Somehow he got involved in composing Scottish songs, often in collaboration with performing artists. “Terrible rubbish,” he said, “but the Jocks seem to like them.” For Calum Kennedy, he wrote “Rollin’ in the Heather,” “The Heart of Scotland,” “Fair Strathlene,” and “Lovely Stornoway.” Elsewhere he contributed such gems as “When McKay Tossed the Caber,” “The Lovely Lass of Inverness,” “Silver Darlings” and “The Voice of Scotland” to our country’s cultural miscellany drawer.

If we are to believe record labels (and we shouldn’t), Calum Kennedy wrote Bratach Bana, but I think midwayfair has shown us that it is not as straightforward as it seems.

[1] = In years past I would have remembered where I got my original information, but now as I descend into the Vale of Years, my memory isn’t as sharp…
