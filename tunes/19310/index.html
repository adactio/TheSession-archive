Gan Ainm

A great but nameless barndance from Gerry Harrington and Billy Clifford’s recording “Now She’s Purring”, track 7, first tune, before Dónal Ó Pumpa. Apparently they got it from a Denis Murphy recording, but I have been able to find it; if anyone knows where it is, I’d love to hear it. Both parts of this tune remind me strongly of John Egan’s Reel (https://thesession.org/tunes/909).

Denis Murphy’s / Bill the Weaver’s Single Reel (S: Denis Murphy)

The intonation of the older fiddle players is a curious thing: while trying to learn or note down a tune, you can either open the door to weird and wonderful pitch-bending possibilities that could help expand your imagination and arsenal of tricks – or you can be guided by your training, habits or limitations of the instrument and “restrict” the tune to a certain mode that you believe is “appropriate” or “sounds better” to your ear. In the end, either approach works – this is your interpretation of the tune – but which is more faithful to the original?

While learning and transcribing tunes from Denis Murphy I think I had to do a bit of both. Denis’s c naturals tend to be sharper on average – but there’re ‘c’s that are “a bit sharp” and those that are “sharp sharp”. On top of that, he does a whole spectrum of slides, some subtle, others taking up a whole beat, others still bending the pitch in both directions – like a quick one-finger mordent! This is just to ram the message home that any ABCs of Denis’s playing is inevitably an approximation.

Gerry Harrington interpreted this tune as an A dorian tune with a few accidentals in the second part – which is entirely valid – but to me it sounds like Denis was clearly playing c sharps all the way through Part B, making it an A dorian / A mixolydian tune. A brief double stop with an open ‘e’ at the end has convinced me further that it’s the case – so here’s my Ador/Amix interpretation! Since that’s quite a rare tune and I’m transcribing from the source, I’ve taken the liberty to add as much detail as I reasonably can, including the pitch-bending flourishes. I’ll follow it up with a simplified version.

This is yet another tune that comes from the Breandán Breathnach Collection at Irish Traditional Music Archive, which remains largely unexplored (those visiting Dublin – it’s your chance to go there and discover something beautiful!)

If my research into the ITMA annotations is correct, Breathnach marked it as “an old single reel that was played by the performer’s father”, i.e. Bill “The Weaver” Murphy. Bill “The Weaver” (usually pronounced “The Waiver” in the area) headed the Lisheen Fife and Drum Band and was the source for many a tune, both well-known and obscure. In addition, the tune was categorized in the archives as a “single reel / polka” but Denis clearly plays it as a reel. That said, Gerry Harrington and Billy Clifford identified it as “a set tune / barndance” on their 2018 recording based on how it sounded to them. Another mystery tune of the Waivers!
