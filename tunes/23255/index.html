Pipe Major George Allan

I believe this Quick Hornpipe in 4 parts was written by Pipe Major D. MacLeod.
Here, I have transcribed it down as a reel, without any of the dotted notes normally associated with a hornpipe and the snaps associated with some Scottish hornpipes, this one included.
I have added some high G#.
Parts 3 and 4 are in the form of variations from the main tune and turn.
Even without the snaps, it has a jolty rhythmical contour and jagged melodic line which make it funky and fun to play.

Hey,

I agree about the snaps ‘making’ the last three parts.
And I agree about not trying to make tea while poring over the screen. One pouring may spill into the other!

Some of the snaps in this tune are particularly interesting because instead of bringing in a rhythmical irregularity as they often do, they actually bring a regularity about:
for example, in the 3rd part, “c-e” is uttered 4 times:
e2A>c e>Ac<e|A>ce>A c<ea>f|…
and c is always short and e always long (in a “short-long” pair only).
If we had:
e2A>c e>Ac>e|A>ce>A c>ea>f|…
“c-e” would be perceived as “short-long”, “long-short”, “short-long”, “long-short”:
an interesting Gestalt phenomenon (among other)!
