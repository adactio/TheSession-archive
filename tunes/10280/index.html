Re: Give Us Some Treacle And Bread

(Clearly commenting years later but ……) I think this tune is actually a jig. I had it originally in that form from a handwritten manuscript book from concertina player Mark Davies. I think it may have been a copy of a MS at Cecil Sharp House but not sure. I think most of the tunes in it were what Sharp had taken down from the Stow-on-the-Wold fiddler John Mason. He located Mason after meeting the “whistling sewermen” - see Paul Burgess’s fascinating article in the EFDSS journal. The tune is written in 6/8 and and is 48 if you repeat each section. As I have found that callers usually ask for more 48 bar jigs than they do 2/4 tunes I continued to play it that way after hearing the Old Swan version. A lot of tunes get called Irish and many of them are but this one certainly has the characteristics of an “English” jig i.e. it is mainly (in 6/8) crotchet, quaver rather than lots of groups of 3 quavers.

↳ Posted by Ed Caines on October 10th, 2021.

Give Us Some Treacle And Bread, X:2

Notated from the Old Swan Band’s 1981 album Gamesters Pickpockets And Harlots. Their setting isn’t a jig, and The tune appears to have been collected by Cecil Sharp in 1908 from George Wright of Wenley, who learned it from “church musicians.” (Info from Old Swan Band sleeve note; there is no mention of it being a Wren tune.)
