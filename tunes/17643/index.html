Two comments

Henry Reed’s Breakdown

Wonderful tune by Henry Reed. Usually for string instruments, but works great on the whistle too. Apparently Alan Jabbour named the tune, because Henry didn’t have one for it.

Some notes on playing it:
1) Many of the notes can be slid up to. Such as:
(a) c-to-c# in the 1st & 5th bars (both parts),
(b) c-to-c# in the 3rd bar of the B Part,
(c) G/G#-to-A in the 2nd & 6th bars of the A Part, and the 6th bar of the B Part,
(d) d/d#-to-e in the 4th bar of the A Part,
(e) G/G3 to A from the 7th bar to the 8th bar (both parts).

2) It’s nice to give a staccato effect on some of the double notes, such as the two A’s in the 8th bars (in which case, sliding into it might not work as well).

3) Some of the c’s can be sharp or natural. Have ordered them here in such a way that works. Some combinations/orders don’t sound quite as good to the ear … at Q: 1/4 = 210 bpm anyways.

4) This tune can be notated as Amajor, with all of the G’s sharp. Having only a few G’s sharp doesn’t work quite so well - they all seem to need to be natural or sharp, not a mixture.

Henry Reed’s Breakdown, X:2

A few variations, and a C Part (mostly like the A Part, but an transposed an octave down).

There’s a few recordings of this tune. Personally enjoy Notorious on their album “Elkins”, No Name String Band on their album “Anytime”, and Behind the Times on their album “From the Ground Up”.
