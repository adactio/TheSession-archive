↳ Posted by Sligofluter on July 8th, 2012.

K: A minor ~ Range ~ G, - c’ ~ which is quite a stretch…

There isn’t one whistle that would work. What you’d need to do is either pull up some of the lower notes or down some of the higher ones. You could get c‘ on a C whistle, but then you’d have to raise lower notes ~ G, - A, - B, ~ by an alternate interval or an octave. If you used a G whistle then you’d have problems with the higher notes of a - b - c’…

Which ever way you went you’d need to make compromises…

Jerusalem Ridge, X:3

Looking at the available transcriptions (above) and comparing them to the Kenny Baker source recording (https://youtu.be/JWySDSagHGU), I really wasn’t satisfied (they’re unusable IMO). The Tunearch one (https://tunearch.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Ridge) which appears as the second entry above, whilst somewhat better and more complete than the first, is still rather poor. The repeat structure is not correctly represented (bad notation) and the timings of various bits really aren’t right and there are some wrong notes. I have attempted to make an accurate (as far as I can manage it), direct transcription from Baker’s playing (but without his double stopping), making good use of the YouTube slow-down function.

N.B. The alternative bar D4 I have given is actually preferred by Baker – he only plays the first-given version in his very first iteration of the B section, but plays the alternative in the repeat and both times in the second time through the tune in the source video.

See also Baker with the composer, Monroe: https://youtu.be/D0AA8lAwH1s - in which, when the mandolin takes the lead, Monroe’s playing seems to me scruffy and rather indeterminate at best - if he’s playing his original conception of the tune, Baker’s version is clearly dominant (and is very close to being exactly the same as his solo rendition in the video I based my transcription upon).

You can download my transcription (dots and ABCs) as a Word document here: https://app.box.com/s/zj5md423n3kp4k3wj53m1k06ckprsb0c. In it I have also provided two different octave-folded adaptations to suit instruments which don’t go below C (or D), one mostly as low as can be, the second going higher. I’ve also included a transposition up a tone to produce a setting playable on C whistle (B minor fingering on D whistle) to sound actual A minor to play along with others. Help yourselves!

Re: Jerusalem Ridge

Early Monroe was top class. He really was an incredible manner player in the context of what else was out there at the time. Right through the 1960s. His dexterity weined with age, as did many players over the years. But there was a special simplicity about some of his playing even in later periods and, personally myself, I find a lot of interesting subtlety on that Baker recording. In lots of ways, he is of course “outclassed” by Kenny Baker, who really makes the tune his own - figuratively and literally - but Monroe’s playing isn’t out of place and has it’s own groove and magic in another way.
