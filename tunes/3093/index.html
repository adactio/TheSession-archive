Follow me down to Carlow

This is a tune I play with Maggie Pickens (submitted by me on May 20th 2004) and which I liken more to a Schottishe than a Hornpipe. I have submitted this one without the dots (hope that will please Jack Gilder). There is another tune by the same name (submitted 17th May 2002) but that one is a reel.

What I implied (if it needs to be clarified at all) is that “The Drunken Sailor”, for all its English/British cultural associations didn’t come from nowhere: this fling (part one) shares the same musical DNA.
Many Irish ditties still run on one leg (being made out of a ‘tune’ but no ‘turn’), so does The Drunken Sailor.

The melody used for the turn of this fling reminds me of a Cork/Kerry polka which, written in 2/4 sounds like;
|: ef/e/ df | ed Bc | d>e dB | AF FD |
ef/e/ df | e>d Bc | dB AF | E2 E2 :|

Here’s another member of the family,
De Ni Mi,
to which Runrig gave an instrumental 2nd part (c.2’00")
