The Pleasures Of Hope, X:4

This version is from the Power manuscript from Kilmore in County Wexford, where the tune is notated in the earliest part of the manuscript (probably mid 19th century) as ‘Mark Handrick’s Hornpipe’. Handrick is the local pronunciation of ‘Hendrick’ which is a not uncommon surname in south east Wexford. The Pleasures of Hope is regularly played as part of the local repetoire, I learned it from the late Tom Ferris who was All-Ireland senior accordion champion in 1966.

Re: The Pleasures Of Hope

In Ryan’s MC (#881) it is called Burn’s Irish- Hornpipe.
Meanwhile O‘Neill in his Waifs and Strays of Gaelic Melody has a Miss Carroll’s Hornpipe (#347), which looks like a fairly close (if incompatible) relative. O’Neill cites O’Farrel’s National Irish Music 1797 as his source.
