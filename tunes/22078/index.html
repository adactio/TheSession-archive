The Donegal

Not to be confused with the old Canadian fiddle tune (same name !). Not even, of course, with the Irish Donegal Reel ! This Donegal Jig is a tune composed by accordionist Martin Tourish, while “reminiscing the excitement of New Year’s Eve in Co Donegal”. Recorded with the band Altan with whom he plays, on their Widening Gyre album (2015). Nice switch from minor to major.

The Donegal jig X:2

This is the Donegal Jig,
all gathered up in the one octave.

NB 1. Played this way it falls all in the whistle/flute/uilleann pipe range and can also be performed on the Scottish pipes.

NB 2.
Note that the first part of the melody has no use for C’s and has only one F (f#), the tune can therefore be written all in the same key/mixolydian mode, even if some will perceive part B to be in A dorian.
The second part, with its c# and f# and g natural is unambiguously mixolydian.

abc;
X:2
K: A mixolydian
|:e2A ged|B2A ABA|B2A BGA|GAB dBd|
e2A ged|B2A AGA|B2A ABG|1 ABA Agf:|2 ABA Aed||
|:c2A AB/c/d|eag e2f|gGB B>gA|GAB dBd|
c2A AB/c/d|eag e2f|gGB BAG|1 ABA Aed:|2 ABA Aga||

NB 3. The small changes i made do not affect the character of the tune, I feel, and allow both X:1 and X:2 to be played simulteanously.

↳ Posted by birlibirdie on September 27th, 2026.

Re: The Donegal

NB 4. It is possible to give the first part a marked mixolydian mood by simply raising some of the 2nds (B) to a 3rd (c#) position;

|:e2A ged|B2A ABA|B2A BGA|GAB dBd|
=>
|:e2A ged|c2A ABA|c2A BGA|GAB ded|
