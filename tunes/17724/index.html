Re: Looking At A Rainbow Through A Dirty Window

Having asked Calum himself, he very kindly sent me pdfs of his own transcription in both A (the original tonality) and in G. (I wasn’t aware he had transcriptions available on his website - and whilst DaveF’s link above works to download a pdf file, I can’t find currently any notations visibly accessible on www.calum-stewart.com.) Calum has explicitly given me permission to host the pdfs of his own transcriptions on Box and make them available. He of course retains all intellectual property rights. Anyone can access them here (unless Calum asks for them to be taken down at some time in the future): in A -https://app.box.com/s/rmrepodhh13nbhy5joit2ij26lrjo7gi & in G https://app.box.com/s/coegb0mgthr2mkr5kqbmeawvuhk02xmq

Calum also commented that:

“The tune is really just a 2 part tune, AB. The other parts are part of the arrangement and could be said to be variations I suppose. I always play the full arrangement with my band, but if someone was to teach it or play it out of context, I wouldn’t be shocked to hear only AB. Hope that helps! I can send dots in A and G (to be taken with a pinch of salt, as I phrase the tune fairly freely, and in the moment). It also works in Bb major for 6 keyed flutes wanting to play the 2nd part in the lower octave. I like both. I tend to play it in G now, though originally wrote it in A and for a long time played it live in Bb (when I moved to a 6 keyed flute). G is lovely too, and I enjoy going up the octave for the 2nd part.”

Re: Looking At A Rainbow Through A Dirty Window

Has anyone ever considered playing it in the key of D? I find that when I’d do, I don’t have to play the B part as written here, but down an octave - for me, on whistle, I like that it keeps the B part “high notes” not too high. I guess it depends on a mood- you may want the B part to be more dramatic, or you may want the B part more pensive. Just ideas.
