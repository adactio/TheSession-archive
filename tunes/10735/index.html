Re: Wackidoo

You’re right, simonet, it’s an extra bar. Some Norwegian & Swedish tunes do have extra phrases or extra bars (i.e., a 10-bar section instead of standard 8, or 5 bars instead of 4), plus when the tunes are newer or composed by a modern musician, like Annbjørg Lien, sometimes they can have funkier arrangements like this. Annbjørg has written a number of cool tunes on her many albums that demonstrate this. Tunes in 5/4 or 5/8 with a few bars of 2/4 and 7/8, or in 7/8 with bars of 9/8 and 6/8. Tunes in 2/4 with a few bars of 3/4. It all depends. And some are trad dance tunes!

Furthermore, there are tunes in Norway (both trad & modern) with odd amounts of bars because they have sections with mixed or changing meters. There’s a jig called “Villvinteren” (‘the wild winter’) which is played AABB-AABB-CCDD, but the C-part changes from 6/8 to 3/4, 5/4, 4/4, 6/8. So it ends up with an odd number of bars.

↳ Posted by Erik-Fiddler on December 8th, 2025.

Re: Wackidoo

The transcription in D minor is a lot easier on the left hand, but also sounds more authentic with the low G-string tuned up to A, if you dare. Just be careful whenever adjusting strings higher. Tuning that G to A instantly makes Norwegian tunes resonate more (when played in D/A) on a standard violin..

However, Annbjørg Lien, on her album Baba Yaga and her live album “Aliens Alive” (plus w/the String Sisters) recorded ‘Wackidoo’ in its original key, E min. This is possible to play on a standard fiddle with GDAE tuning, of course, but it’s a bigger stretch on the high notes; tuning it down or playing in D minor, raising the G string to A, gives more open double-stops and a more “Nordic” sound. Many of these Norwegian tunes are played on Hardanger fiddles that are slightly smaller & sharper. Annbjørg has almost always recorded on a fiddle pitched to B-E-B-F#, and occasionally C-F-C-G for really high tunes.
