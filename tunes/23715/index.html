Mrs Wemyss Of Cuttle Hill

A tune from the Skye Collection, composed by Nathaniel Gow. The book calls it a reel, but it sounds more polka-ish in my opinion. Other sources have it listed as a strathspey.

↳ Posted by BenL on November 19th, 2023.

Re: Mrs Wemyss Of Cuttle Hill

This tune is part of a large family of tunes stemming from, I believe, the 18th century strathspey “Alistair MacAlister” - in spite of Nathaniel Gow’s claims to have composed it. In other 18th century collections it can be found as “Lochgary’s” and “Miss E Bell’s Favorite Strathspey.”

It’s also known quite widely in Ireland, therefore it’s worth checking out titles such as “The Canamine Highland,” “Con Cassidy’s Highland,” “Little Katie Kearney,” “St Patrick was a Gentleman,” “The Glen Cottage” and “Denis Doody’s Polka.”

↳ Posted by Nigel Gatherer on November 20th, 2023.

Re: Mrs Wemyss Of Cuttle Hill

There is a trend among (Scottish country) dance devisors to use popular singalong tunes, “Mairi’s Wedding” and “The Irish Rover” - just two examples - which, to my ears, are polkas rather than reels. These tunes encourage a flat-footed, sometimes heel-led traveling step, which tends to create a scrape on the second beat of each step. I call this the Wilson, Keppel and Betty effect. Just showing my age here. Most responses to this will be “who?”. Google or Bing will find them. Dancing standard was much higher when I started dancing, 70+ years ago.

Oh, perhaps you knew Nathaniel Gow then Bob? Although, as your profile says you are now 70, you started at a VERY young age. Anyway, it’s great to have all your contributions.

There are a lot of tunes in Scotland which match your description. They’re not quite polkas, but they have some of the same qualities which I love in polkas: there is a beautiful simplicity about them, they’re easy to learn, and they’re fun! I usually call them “2/4 reels” to suit my own desire for categorisation, but that’s certainly not a standard term.
