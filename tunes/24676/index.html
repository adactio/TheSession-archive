The Twelfth Lock

A power reel from John Lee first heard on The Wooden Flute Obsession (vol.3, CD1, track 12.b).

I love the tritone drop from the ionian 7th to the major third in first bar; a bold opening!
(Some of the 7ths in John Lee’s are sharp mixolydian ones or flat ionian ones, as is the norm in ITM, btw; if the first 7th is too sharp to your taste, you can adjust it at your pleasure)

I transcribed what I heard in D, although John Lee uses a sharper keyed flute. c.2006

Ah!
I see you’re right Kenny, and it confirms my suspicion this reel sounded like a classic.

It already exists in several versions on this site (duplicates!), at;
https://thesession.org/tunes/571 (The stoney steps)
and;
https://thesession.org/tunes/6086 (Peter Flanagan’s Stoney steps)

I associate the first with Matt Molloy (and, incidentally, with the kind of tunes, as jeff_lindqvist remarked, that you don’t actually know cause you’ve never learned them, even though they sound “annoyingly” familiar!).
I don’t play The stoney steps and would have never made the connection, so thanks.

The Peter Flanagan version is associated with Fermanagh and shows significant departures from the #571 variations; especially in part 2.

John Lee’s Twelfth Lock variant shows what we could call “syntaxic” variations, the type which could arise from naturally varying the tune. Although, in this case (John Lee’s Twelfth Lock), these variations are his normal (he even varies them).
https://ceolnacoille.ie/5739-2/

So yes, it is the same tune, I have no real preference now, but will probably choose to learn John Lee’s version for its attractive powerful, angular style (almost like a march).

These versions offer an interesting illustration of how personal use of “syntactic” variations + personal style can help form distinctive, almost new tunes when these variations “gel” or solidify.
In comparison, Peter Flanagan’s version appears more like a flight of fancy or a new direction which could be explained by a gap in the memory (misremembering or having to fill in a lost part with ad hoc material).
