Thistle Of Scotland

The Thistle o’ Scotland“ (Scottish Gaelic: An Cluaran o h-Alba) was originally called ”The Badge of Scotland“ (Bràiste h-Alba) but it is more commonly known as ”The Thistle o’ Scotland".

Malcolm MacFarlane (1853-1931) translated this song from the Scottish Gaelic of Evan MacColl (1808-1898).[1] It was first published in Macfarlane’s book, Songs of the Highlands, Inverness: Logan & Company, 1902, pp. 44–45.[2] The accompaniment was by Frederick Wilson Whitehead (1863-1926). It is considered by some to be a possible National Anthem for Scotland.

Re: The Thistle O’ Scotland

The pipe tune is commonly known as “Badge of Scotland”, and appears in the 19th century David Glen collection, as well as in the Glenallan and Seaforth (if I recall correctly) pipe tune collections. Key of D on the pipes. It’s interesting that I first heard it as the fade-out tune after the song “Battle of New Orleans” on a Nitty Gritty Dirt Band album from the early 70s. I don’t know what David Glen volume it’s in, but the final Glen volume is from 1900, so I assume publication of the tune pre-dates the Macfarlane collection.
