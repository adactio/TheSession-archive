A jig played by Paddy O’Brien: Temple Hill

Around a week ago I got interested in a jig called Temple Hill - not yet entered in thesession. I thought it might be nice to start a discussion about it here. I have a few questions…

# The jig

I think it’s a lovely tune. Deceptively simple. I first heard it on an ITMA playlist:
https://www.itma.ie/playlists/mark-bickford-tune-selection/?track=10
- Mark Bickford’s picks from the Paddy O’Brien Tune Collection

Mark’s score is is on the excellent, new ITMA score website, Pórt, and you can hear Paddy O’Brien playing it too:
https://www.itma.ie/scores/pob1268_itma0290338_016/
The interactive features where the score follows the recording are very handy.

ITMA / Paddy O’Brien have it as a single jig, but despite looking at several discussions here and there, I still haven’t grasped what’s the difference (between a jig, and a single jig).

I hear Temple Hill as part jig, part slip jig – for me the B part alternates twice between one bar of 9/8 and one of 12/8. It’s something you can really hear in the tune. (In my opinion.) I’ve tweaked Bickford’s score along those lines and you can see it here:
https://goplayerjuggler.github.io/tuneTable/?l=default&ttId=561

Written in 6/8, the B part of Temple Hill takes up seven bars, whereas normal jigs are made up of chunks of eight bars of 6/8. Another indication that something unusual is happening here.

I’d be interested in any other similar jigs out there. To start with, are there any similar jigs where one of the parts is in seven bars (of 6/8) rather than the usual eight?

# The set of tunes for the Battle of Cnoc na nDos
There’s some interesting info alongside the ITMA score:
>>>
Temple Hill’ is the final tune of a long descriptive set of tunes similar to ‘The Fox Chase’, but in this case it sets the mood of the Battle of Cnoc na nDos (also known as Knockinoss), fought in 1647, that ended with the death of Alastair ‘Colkitto’ MacDonald, a Scottish general who fought with the Irish side
>>>
I presume these notes are Paddy O’Brien’s.
Just wondering, what are the other tunes in this descriptive set? I have found some leads (a march called Allisdrum’s march; and a lament by the same name) but I haven’t yet looked into it properly.
