Re: Duvil’s Hornpipe / Highland Fling - etc…

As you’ve noted, in the ‘Comments’, the Riley version is given there, in ABC notation, including a direct transposition into the key of G. They are both given there for the history and for contrast, the key of A Major being another common option within that range of notes. The one I’d entered first had no intention of being an exact of the Riley collection but represents my love affair with the dance and the dance music, as a dancer and as a musician, with classic treatment options from that tune form, having danced, played, collected dances and tunes specifically of this form, admitting a preference for swing. When playing through the Riley collection I found ‘Duvil’s Hornpipe’, which for me is clearly in the form of a 16 bar highland fling/highland/fling (highland schottische). It isn’t unusual for this dance form to show up under hornpipes or as a reel, more commonly a single reel, not surprising as the dances themselves became less popular. This tune form at its height included full sets of quadrilles danced to the form - as too waltzes, polkas, mazurkas, etc… A good melody can always be adapted to other ends, other tune forms… - - - Mea culpa!
