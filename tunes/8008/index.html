Re: Buttermilk Mary

《Dick Brennan of Kilavil told this story to Dan [Healy] some years ago. It is a jig composed by Michael Coleman. He was returning from a (coisir) party early one morning and he was in dire need of a drink. He met a girl driving her cattle home for milking. He asked for a drink of cold water. She said ‘I have better than that for you’. She went into her house and brought out a large jug of buttermilk. He drank it all in one gulp and said he would compose his next tune for her.》
rapported by Maire McDonnell Garvey in the liner notes of The Wyndy Turn by Sligo flautist Dan Healey.

As indicated in a comment above this tune appeared in Ryan’s Mammoth with that title, published 1883 before Coleman was born. I looked in my Cole’s and it’s there in A major titled Butter-Milk Mary’s.

Re: Buttermilk Mary

Thank you for that correction edl.
The story I quoted isn’t a particularly convincing narrative. Both the argument and the style are rather trivial but are made to sound special. Who tried to make what point exactly? For the reteller, maybe something akin to a hagiographic note (as in providing yet another proof of a man’s -or a people’s- genius)? Or the need to ascribe an identity or a reason to things that do not need one? In modern hermeneutics, “reason is treason” and there are ways of doing oral history without straying into myth writing. Paradoxically, I was tempted to attribute this tune to Coleman (1891-1945) without doing a bit of cross-referencing first, but hoping I would be contradicted. So thanks for providing the reminders/refs.
NB: ‘Michael Coleman’ has now been removed from the author’s rubric. ;)
