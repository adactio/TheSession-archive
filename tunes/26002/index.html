Day Dreams

Piper Leo Rowsome recorded this for the Columbia label on August 10, 1926. He followed it with the well known song air The Low Backed Car, as a set of waltzes. In the liner notes of the Topic LP reissue of Leo’s 78s Classics of Irish Piping Seán Reid says that “I have failed to trace the origin of Day Dreams. Perhaps it is just a product of Tin Pan Alley but, whether it is or not, it is a catchy little melody that goes well on the pipes.” He also states that these tunes are in the key of D, “which the drones favor,” but this tune actually begins in G, modulates to D, then to A - and I think the melody perhaps originally was in A major in this section, thus you would play a2g2f2, which poses no problem on the fiddle, but Leo plays G natural here, as his chanter didn’t have a G# key. So A mixolydian.

Or this was how the tune truly goes, for its provenance is known: in the Vol. 9 No. 2 issue of An Píobaire Terry Moylan provides “Two Waltzes From Willie Rowsome” which were written out for a student of Leo’s, Hugh Quinn; the other is “Old German Waltz” but Day Dreams is also described as a “Beautiful German Waltz.” Day Dreams especially has all the hallmarks of these old Continental waltzes/schottisches/polkas/etc, modulating through related keys. As Terry says something like this is a hint of the repertoire pipers had in olden days, with tunes of this sort occasionally being thrown in, along with the typical Irish dance music. You hear much more of this from players of other instruments, like John Doherty.
