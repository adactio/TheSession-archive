The Sword Knott

An 18th Century country dance tune, composed by Ignatius Sancho (1729-1780).

Sancho was born on a slave ship outward bound for Cartagena (now part of Columbia). He was orphaned at an early age, and christened Ignatius by the bishop of Cartagena. His master subsequently brought him to England, where he came to be employed by three maiden ladies at Greenwich. Subsequently, he attracted the attention of Lord Montague, and was employed by the Montague family as their servant and protégé. When lady Montague died, he was given a pension, which enabled him to set up a grocery business in London.

He was acquainted with Felice de Giardini, David Garrick and Laurence Sterne and had his portrait painted by the artist Gainsborough:

http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/IgnatiusSancho.jpg/220px-IgnatiusSancho.jpg

While with the Montague family he was provided with a classical education. He was an accomplished musician, and published many songs and dance tunes - including this one.

Here are his instructions for the dance:

First Cu. foot it and cast off two Cu. foot it and cast up again. Cross over two Cu. Lead up to the top and cast off. Hands round four at bottom Right and left at top.
