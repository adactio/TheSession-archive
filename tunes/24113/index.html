Five comments

Bog Braon (Suantraí) / The Old Man Rocking The Cradle (Air)

Bog Braon is a well-known Munster lullaby that has particularly strong ties with the Múscraí Gaeltacht (West Cork). The tune exists in several song versions (all slightly free-metered), as an air in 3/4, and as a separate complex slow air in Co. Kerry. Bear with me as I try to transcribe and contribute them one by one!

NB: None of the tunes are actually played as a full-on mazurka or waltz, it’s just the limitations of the system.

First comes the song: my sources for this version are Yuri Andreychuk & Breanndán O Beaglaoich who sang it in Moscow in 2010. I’m pretty sure Iarla Ó Lionáird used a similar if not the same tune but I can’t find the video that used to be on YouTube.

Now, the general consensus is that the lyrics are about rocking a baby to sleep and that the “old man” in question is an allegory for an infant while the “warm drop” is an allegory for milk (and definitely not whiskey). This sort of makes sense but the lyrics start getting more and more suspicious with each verse. At first, it’s just giving the warm drop to the old man and washing his feet, then comes the offer of eggs and butter, then it’s fresh meat and soup. Whatever it takes to keep the baby happy and sleepy, I suppose! (No, I don’t think Munster(wo)men actually fed all that to their babies, don’t quote me on that:-D)

Here’s the typical text of the song with spelling variations in [*]:

Bog Braon don [t]Seanduine

Bog braon, bog braon, bog braon don [t]seanduine,
bog braon, ‘[i]s blais féin, ‘[i]s é a thabhairt don [t]seanduine.

Curfá:
Cuir a chodladh, cuir a chodladh, cuir a chodladh an seanduine,
cuir a chodladh, ‘[i]s nigh a chosa, ‘[i]s bog braon don [t]seanduine.

Ubh c[h]irce, ubh c[h]irce, ubh c[h]irce don [t]seanduine,
ubh c[h]irce, ‘[i]s blúirín ime, ‘[i]s é a thabhairt don [t]seanduine.

Curfá

Feoil úr, feoil úr, feoil úr don [t]seanduine,
feoil úr, ‘[i]s braon súip, ‘[i]s é a thabhairt don [t]seanduine.

Curfá

Bog braon, bog braon, bog braon don [t]seanduine,
bog braon, ‘[i]s blais féin, ‘[i]s é a thabhairt don [t]seanduine.

Bog Braon, X:3 (Peadar Ó Riada – Suantraí / The Old Man Rocking The Cradle)

This distinct version of the tune was recorded by Peadar Ó Riada on Amidst These Hills. The rhythm is slightly dotted but not as heavily as the midi or the notation would suggest (the usual limitations).

I learned this tune from Timmy ‘The Brit’ McCarthy who in turn learned it from Peadar. Timmy called it The Old Man Rocking The Cradle which is the name of the related air Pádraig O’Keeffe used to play.

↳ Posted by Bregolas on March 3rd, 2024.

Bog Braon, X:4 (Timmy ‘The Brit’ McCarthy – The Old Man Rocking The Cradle)

Here’s Timmy ‘The Brit’ McCarthy’s variations on the same tune. He played it livelier than Peadar (who was his source for the tune) and with slightly more syncopated rhythm.

Timmy had an interesting tradition regarding this tune, using it as a stand-in for “national anthem” at the end of his workshops in Brittany (where his son Tony lives). Timmy & Tony McCarthy recorded this tune on their beautiful rare album From Cork to Confort Berhet.

↳ Posted by Bregolas on March 3rd, 2024.

Bog Braon, X:5 = An Seanduine ag Luasgadh an Chliabháin / The Old Man Rocking the Cradle

This is a distinctive setting of the same air found in Canon James Goodman’s manuscripts and published as no. 295 in Tunes of the Munster Pipers vol. 1. The tune’s title in the collection is An Seanduine ag Luasgadh an Chliabháin or The Old Man Rocking the Cradle.

Mick O’Brien, Emer Mayock and Aoife Ní Bhriain recorded this version on Tunes from the Goodman Manuscripts (2012) as a slow air – the transcription follows their take on it.

https://thesession.org/recordings/4340

Notably, a similar but fancier version of this air – with extra parts and meter change – was played by Pádraig O’Keeffe, who called it The Old Man Rocking the Cradle. Some day I’ll get to transcribing the whole thing!
