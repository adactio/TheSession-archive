Top The Candle, X:5 - hop jig, as played by Frank & Pat Reilly

I feel it’s best to share the full ABC of this setting here. Details about the source (musicians, link, …) are in the headers. My comments are in the “N:” headers.

X: 1
T: Top The Candle
N: 1. A hop jig played in the good old way by two County
N: Leitrim fiddlers.
N: 2. Quavers are played “straight”, and not “swung”; writing
N: this in 9/8 would be wrong here, IMO.
N: You could perhaps write that as “𝅘𝅥𝅮𝅘𝅥𝅮 ≠ 𝅘𝅥𝅘𝅥𝅮”.
N: 3. In the recording this was played a few moments before
N: Do It Fair.
N: 4. In the recording it’s played in C; I suspect the fiddles
N: were tuned one whole tone down from the standard tuning. Here
N: it’s transposed up one tone so as to be more accessible to
N: fiddlers nowadays.
N: 5. I give some rough indications about the ornaments/
N: embellishments with the “footnotes” on the last line. But
N: there are lots of subtleties of the rhythm, or phrasing, that
N: I’ve left out.

Re: Top The Candle

I firmly disagree that writing it in 9/8 would be wrong. It’s a typical hop jig, which are better written in 9/8. I hear the feel in this recording (and in Brian McNamara’s) as having a triplet feel under each pair. Brian got them from this recording.

↳ Posted by Nico on November 27th, 2024.

Re: Top The Candle

Well, Nico, thanks for your comments. I haven’t found Brian McNamara’s recording. But about this specific recording, we’ll have to agree to disagree. The basic pulse isn’t around triplets. Not what I’m hearing, anyway.

Disagreement isn’t necessarily a bad thing! It’s interesting if there are multiple ways of hearing, or understanding, a tune.

↳ Posted by GoPlayer on November 27th, 2024.

Re: Top The Candle

For sure, it is interesting how people hear things differently! And agreed that there’s plenty of room for disagreement.

For what it’s worth, in private communication with Brian (who got the tunes from these very recordings made by his father), he confirmed that these, and hop jigs in general, are in 9/8, but with distinct rhythmic differences to slip jigs. His liner notes are available here:
https://www.cranfordpub.com/recordings/mcnamarajewel.htm
And it’s worth noting that the fiddlers in the recording learned from their father / uncle who learned from Peter Kennedy, whose son in turn is the source for O’Neill’s transcription of Top the Candle.
