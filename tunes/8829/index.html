Sault’s (hornpipe)

Source: Fortune Favours The Merry by Peter Horan And Gerry Harrington
Transcription: gian marco pietrasanta

↳ Posted by gian marco on August 19th, 2008.

Re: Sault’s

I first heard this tune under the title “The Irish Club” played more or less as above, on a the 1960s album Irish Dance Party” by The Ciaran Kelly Ceili Band from Athlone. (Riverside Records NY).
Just recently it has appreared again under the original title, Sault’s Own, on a CD of old recordings by the accordion icon Paddy O’Brien (Nenagh). Paddy’s version is slightly different to the above, but I have heard others playing it his way. They have a strange syncopation in the B part that I can’t quite figure.

↳ Posted by seanquinn on March 14th, 2023.

Sault’s, X:3

This is the version in O‘Neill’s 1903 book, #1660. Much of it concurs broadly with the version I first learned from Ciaran Kelly’s Ceili Band LP record “Irish Dance Party” (circa 1960) but not the 4 short notes in Bar/Measure #3 which seem totally anomalous ! I hope to post a proper transcription of Kelly’s version and also one that has recent;y come to light from Paddy O’Brien (Nenagh). I have loved this tune for years, mainly for it’s range of notes and lovely turns, so please excuse the extra attention it’s getting here.

↳ Posted by seanquinn on April 17th, 2023.

Sault’s, X:4

This is the tune as I first heard it played by the Ciaran Kelly ceili band on a vinyl LP “Irish Dance Party” (Riverside Label) purchased in Dublin in 1962. I prefer the way he plays the second part, but it’s not the way others play it - see my transcription of Paddy O‘Brien’s version, which is closer to the original as per O’Neill’s.

↳ Posted by seanquinn on May 16th, 2023.

Sault’s, X:5

This version is as played by the late Paddy O‘Brien (Nenagh). It is among a number of resored old recordings of her father recently published on CD by Eileen O’Brien entitled “Paddy O’Brien 1922-1991”. Paddy uses the original title and his version is perhaps more faithful to the original. It’s a lovely hornpipe, but still not widely played, I think.

↳ Posted by seanquinn on May 16th, 2023.

Re: Sault’s

Paddy O’Brien seems to play it in F Major, so that’s how I transcribed it. There’s always a worry with the pitch of old tape recordings, but Eileen O’Brien confirms that it was indeed F.

↳ Posted by seanquinn on May 20th, 2023.

Re: Sault’s

Paddy Killoran recorded the O’Neill’s setting of this: https://archive.org/details/PaddyKilloranMaguiresFiddleODonnellsHornpipe Note how he plucks the G D G part pizzacato. Very violin.

On the B/C box you can play a high F roll stronger than a high G roll, so it makes sense to take a tune in G and play it in F instead, which I’d guess is why Paddy chose to play this in that key.
