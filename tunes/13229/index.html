Mike Sullivan’s

This is a very nice tune, hornpipe or fling, and quite an old one as well. Johnny O‘Leary and Denis Murphy played this for Seamus Ennis (X:2) and my great grandfather played a similar version of it himself. The first hornpipe in both sets is the Rose of Drishane (https://thesession.org/tunes/2844), a reference to the castle near Millstreet, which my great-great-grandmother used to play. She claimed she gave it to Tom Billy but that’s hardly fact checked. Johnny O’Leary plays this in a more stacato style than usual and of course it’s lovely stuff.

Re: Mike Sullivan’s

Wtnott is right: on my copy of the LP this hornpipe has the name Tom Billy, along with countless other tunes. The connection to Tom Billy is mentioned above. In JOL’s book he states that he got the first tune (https://thesession.org/tunes/2013) from Mike Sullivan. By implication Mike was the source of the above tune (2nd in the set, track 6 side1) because they call it Mike Sullivan’s in the book. Apparently Mike Sullivan, from Kilcummin, was a great set dancer who did not play but had “every tune under the rising sun in his head”.
