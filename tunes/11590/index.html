An original

… by yours truly. Let me know what you think of it. The second time through I like to double stop on the open string, a bit cajun’y, it turns it into a real foot-stomper. Ornaments 2nd eighth note of the first bar of the A section and the first eighth note of the 2nd bar of the A section. That should give you a feel for it, there are others but too awkward to write them all out there.

Runs well into “Cajun Fiddlin’ on the Bayou” if you do open strings the last time through.

Sure. IMO “Traditional” came first, “derivative” is a mere imitator. The first examples of a genre are put on a pedestal because they were innovative at the time. The bar is set higher for subsequent composers.

Re: Flagrant violation of “own compositions” guideline

Well there’s a difference too between being derivative, and simply playing common intervals that follow a standard chord progression. The former hinges on knowing the music that came before. The latter is just assembling notes in unsurprising ways.

But don’t stop stringing notes together. You’re off to a decent start, and it helps to get the banal stuff out of your system so you can move on to more interesting ideas. Melody is king--think less about the chord progression and more about simply letting interesting phrases and melody lines unfurl.
