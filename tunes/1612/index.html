Mo Ghile Mear

Everything you need to know will be found everywhere else online but there’s some extra info on how the song got to the one it is today. Seán Ó Riada got lots of different verses from different people around the country and made a collection. When he died, his son Peadar knew that his father loved the tune and because he respected his father, he brought the verses together to be sung by the choir at his father’s funeral. He recorded it with the choir and some other performers which he called Claisceadal Chúil Aodha on a record called Ceol & Cibeal Chúil Aodha. This was the first time the tune was recorded and everyone else from the Chieftains to Mary Black used this version.

Re: Mo Ghile Mear

There are different versions of the song, but the original poem (“Bímse buan ar buairt gach ló”) was composed by Seán ‘Clárach’ Mac Domhnaill c. 1746. [https://www.dib.ie/biography/mac-domhnaill-sean-clarach-a5030] The original was published by Patrick Dinneen in “Amhráin Sheághain Chláraigh Mhic Dhomhnaill” in 1902. [https://archive.org/details/amrinsheaghi00macduoft/page/xxxii/mode/2up]

It was originally set to ‘a Scottish tune’ but it’s unclear what that was. Many Jacobite songs used The White Cockade (An Cnota(dh) Bán), so that’s a possibility.

The modern tune was collected by Seán Ó Riada from Domhnaill Ó Buachalla of Cúil Aodha in the 1960s and later (c. 1972) used by Domhnall Ó Liatháin to create the modern song, using original verses plus the extra verse from Ó Buachalla plus a verse from another (Jacobite) poem by Mac Domhnaill (“Seal do Bhíos im’ Mhaighdin Shéimh” c. 1715).

The verses have a fairly strong pattern of assonance (~rhyme), e.g. the first two verses stick to the pattern í - ú - ú - ó (b*í*mse — b*u*an — b*u*airt — l*ó*) all the way through.

An article by Iarla Ó Lionáird has some info about the development of the modern song. [https://journalofmusic.com/focus/o-riada-s-vision]

Re: Mo Ghile Mear / The White Cockade

A few notes, to add to the above, from Ó Riada’s Farewell (https://thesession.org/recordings/1821): an alternative name for the song was The old White Cockade. As stated above words were written by Seán Clárach Mac Dónaill for Prince Charles Edward Stuart. Ó Riada recorded this before his funeral, so maybe this was its first recording. He requested Ceoltóirí Chualann play it at his funeral. The song used the air An Cnota Bán, which translates as The White Cockade.

Re: Mo Ghile Mear & “sounds like”

Mo Ghile Mear and Mairi’s Wedding aren’t the same tunes but they do have 4 bars in common - the first four, crucially- as does the Spanish Lady (by virtue, in the last case, that it’s almost entirely the same tune).

Since we tend to form our tune recognition on the first couple of beats or bars of a tune -and hook the rest of the melody on it- paying attention to these partial or passing similarities is valuable. And drawing attention to them is often helpful.
