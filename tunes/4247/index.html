Ten comments

Flaherty’s Hornpipe

This is a bit tricky on the fiddle with the crossing between the 2nd & 4th strings in the begining bit. The open A can be used instead of the low A as a variation. The Scotch snap in the B part is optional too, I’ve heard it done with the snap & liked it - so I left it in. This is not the most common of tunes but it is very nice.

↳ Posted by b.maloney on February 20th, 2005.

This one was a favorite of Paddy Reynolds, who recorded it with Andy McGann (as “Flaherty’s”) and on his LP with Charlie Mulvihill, where he called it after Lad O‘Beirne. It’ was printed in Breathnach’s Ceol Rince no. 4.

↳ Posted by Don Meade on March 22nd, 2005.

Flaherty’s, X:5

This version is from the CD “Atlantic Wave” by Paddy Reynolds, Charlie Mulvihill, and James Keane, which is a reissue of the earlier recording “Sweet and Traditional Music of Ireland” together with some new material. Track 3 consists of this hornpipe, called Lad O’Beirne on this recording, followed by Quilty. Most if not all of the tunes on this CD are in flat keys. I’ve transposed this tune up a half step to D in this transcription for ease in playing on the fiddle and most other instruments.

↳ Posted by erichm on July 18th, 2023.
