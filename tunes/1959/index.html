Re: The Belfast

Mea culpa. I should have consulted Volume 3 of Kerr’s Merry Melodies, already cited above as a source, never mind compared the Belfast with the Bluebell Polka. Both these and other polkas of that vintage have A and B parts in different keys, with or without a third section labelled “trio”, implying classical structure as well as the Polka’s Germanic roots as a dance form.
The question remains about origins of the “Belfast” name, as well as how the three parts of the tunes would originally have been played and danced. The trio sections of the Belfast and the Belfast polkas are of a different musical character from the A and B parts, as is the case with the trio section of a classical minuet, hence “da capo”- go back to the top and play the other parts again. Jimmy Shand made the Bluebell Polka famous playing the three sections, which are in G, D and C major, respectively, in the order AABBACCA, or some extension of this form rather than a 48 bar sequence as such.
