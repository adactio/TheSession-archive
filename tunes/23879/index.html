Go Dig My Grave

Not sure who wrote this ancient classic suicide ballad but Raie Peat does a great version and she learned it from the singing of Jean Ritchie, who recorded it in 1963 with Doc Watson at folk city. Haunting proto goth vibes. Delicious!

Re: Go Dig My Grave

From the band’s own site - https://lankumdublin.com/go-dig-my-grave-1:

‘Go Dig My Grave’ is a member of a family of songs (‘Died for Love’, ‘The Butcher Boy’, ‘TheBrisk Young Farmer’ etc.) which seem to be largely made up of what are known as ‘floating verses’. Many of these verses were originally composed as stanzas of various different ballads, such as Robert Johnson’s ‘A Forlorn Lover’s Complaint’, which dates back to c.1611. What separates the various ballads in this family is the order in which the verses appear. One element that most of them share is the description of the maid’s epitaph, like this one from a broadside dating to c.1790.

Re: Go Dig My Grave

Hi, I tend to agree with sixholes6 on this. There is some merit in this ‘theme’, in that it tells a story. Unfortunately, it is one that is oft repeated. A sonorous and tuneful voice always helps, but I found the version in the video had neither of these qualities. Many years ago, Tommy Makem recorded ‘Butcher Boy’, and it is a pleasure to listen to.
Why various artistes record it ad nauseum, when they have nothing to add any different, is inexplicable.
As for the timing, it tends to be sung in ‘free’ time, so to specify a single signature is difficult. Much work I have studied annotates the script with the various signatures embedded.
