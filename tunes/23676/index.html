Re: Drimen Duff

The Bunting settings above are nearly identical to those found in “12 Scotch Airs” (1745) and James Oswald’s “Caledonian Pocket Companion, Vol.8” (1747). Otherwise known as “Drumion Dubh” (“The Black Cow”) or “An Druim-fhionn Dubh.”

In 1787 the tune was used for the old Border ballad “Hughie Graham” in Johnson’s ‘Scots Musical Museum.’

When Burns was heading to Greenock to emigrate to Jamaica in 1786 (he didn’t go in the end), he composed a song, “The Gloomy Night is Gathering Fast.” In the 1840s the editors of Wood’s ‘Songs of Scotland,’ disliking its original air, set the song to “Drimon Duff.”

In O‘Neill’s ’Waifs and Strays’ he noted, “In poetical literature those titles are allegorical. ”Drimmin Dhu“ was a political password among the Irish Jacobites, and all ”Drimmin“ songs breathe a spirit of fealty to the Jacobite cause.”

↳ Posted by Nigel Gatherer on November 9th, 2023.
