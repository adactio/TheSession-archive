Seven comments

Heman Dubh

This tune is not a strathspey but a fulling song (a.k.a. ‘waulking song’ or ‘òran luaidh’)
from the Hebrides.

Note its unusual, perhaps archaic, structure in 4x3 bars.
Despite of -or because of- its rudimentary melody and under-developped tune/turn structure, this ditty has a lot of drive and has inspired many musicians to record it as an instrumental.
In its song form, the structure is responsorial (call+response).

I set it here in D to be suit the Highland pipe chanter.
It is arguably in E pentatonic (no 3rd no 6th) or D pentatonic major (no 4th, no 7th).
This sort of tonal ambiguity gives many traditional tunes their charm.

More details + Lyrics; see below.

The instrumental version is rather different from X:1 and comes in more or less developed settings.
The music has been in print since 1784 (see:
https://tunearch.org/wiki/Annotation:Heman_Dubh)
Alan Stivell’s harp version (written out on two staves) can be printed from the musescore webpage:
https://musescore.com/user/30764673/scores/8836023
