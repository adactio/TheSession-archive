Seven comments

The Shetland Bus

Learned from the first La Lugh album. It’s named in honour of the clandestine Norwegian resistance operation in WW II, providing sea transport between Norway and Shetland mostly on fishing boats.

I’ve been playing it for many years but still don’t know who composed it. Anyone?

↳ Posted by Jürgen on September 10th, 2009.

The Shetland Bus

I think it was composed by Dr. Tom Anderson.

↳ Posted by snorre on September 11th, 2009.

The Shetland Bus

According to the La Lugh album sleeve notes it was written by the great Shetland accordianist and tunesmith Ronnie Cooper, who also wrote ‘Da Tushkar’, ‘Miss Susan Cooper’ etc.

↳ Posted by straysider on September 11th, 2009.

The Shetland Bus

Just a note to confirm what straysider said: this is a composition of Ronnie Cooper which he published in his book “Shetland Music by Ronnie Cooper Vol V” (1974).

↳ Posted by Nigel Gatherer on June 11th, 2013.

The Shetland Bus Service(s)

There is a website specifically about the secret support operation between the Shetland Islands and the Norwegian resistance, which refers to a waltz written to commemorate it. However, it’s nothing like Ronnie Cooper’s, nor - in my opinion - as appealing a tune. There is a link to it: http://shetlandbus.com/audio/mtbwaltz8m.wav . The Cooper tune has been recorded by Sussex band, The Rude Mechanicals: there is a short clip of it at http://www.rudemex.co.uk/audio.html .

↳ Posted by Bazza on November 20th, 2015.

Re: The Shetland Bus

It’s a lovely tune and I think needs to be played quite slowly. It’s interesting that all the versions I have seen, and above, are written as not dotted but it always seems to be played mostly dotted. I learned it dotted except bars 13-15 of the A music which were played straight and make a nice contrast.
