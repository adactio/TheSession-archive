Re: Pasa Corredoiras 439

Brincadeiro is the first tune in that Milladoiro set. This tune is know as ‘Pasacorredoiras de Pontevedra’. A pasacorredoiras is a tune type and it’s fairly common in Spain, and Portugal, for tunes to only be know by their place of origin, so this is a pasacorredoiro from Pontevedra. I’ll add my setting later.

I’m no expert on Galician music but I have been playing the gaita for nearly 25 years and I’ve never heard of Brincadeiro as a tune type.

Pasacorredoiras De Pontevedra, X:3

Here’s my setting of Pasacorredoiras de Pontevedra. I don’t remember where I got it from but it was definitely one of the first Galician tunes I learned when I purchased my first gaita nearly 25 years ago. Pasacorredoiras are ‘going about’ tunes, played by bands when moving through the streets at gatherings and festivals. I play it at around 100bpm

The custom in Galicia is to notate this type of tune in 2/4 with 16th notes so I’ve changed the tune type to polka. It doesn’t seem possible to change default note size to 1/16 and I know you’re not supposed to add headers to the ABCs so I’ve halved the note length so it displays correctly. Below is how I would normally write the ABCs with L:16

X:1
T:Pasacorredoiras De Pontevedra
M:2/4
L:1/16
Q:1/4=100
K:D
|:AG|F2FD FDFG|A2AF AFAF|G2GE GEGB|AGFE D2AG|
F2FD FDFG|A2AF AFAF|G2GE GECE|D4 D2:|
|:A2|B2>G2 B2>G2|A2>F2 DEFG|A2Bc dcBc|A4 FDFA|

Re: Pasacorredoiras De Pontevedra

@birlibirdie Hm, it’s maybe not as clear cut as I thought. This tune, Pasacorredoiras de Pontevedra, is the name I was given when I learnt it and all the results I can find online seem to confirm it. So I think we’re good for this one.

As for Brincadeiro. They recorded it at least three times, once with just the two tunes and twice with something else in front. The video you posted above, from the O Berro Seco studio album, has the short intro tune. My gut feeling was that it was just a bit of noodling, not a named tune, but I may be wrong (see below). They also recorded it on two live albums, Milladoiro en Ortigueira (Live), with what sounds like an Irish tune first and on As Fadas de Estraño Nome with only the two tunes, nothing in front. They helpfully just called the track Brincadeiro on each recording. Unfortunately I no longer have the CDs or I would have a look at the sleeve notes.

So there’s a comment on a 16 year old YouTube video of the track which says this “This piece is a suite composed of: Pasacorredoiras de Ponteareas (one of them, there are several), Golpe de Mondariz and Pasacorredoiras de Pontevedra”. Right enough, is does sound like a Pasacorredoiras played slowly although I can’t find any other recordings of this tune.

As for the second tune, as you say it is known as Golpe de Carballiño, but to add to the confusion it seems to also be known as Golpe de Mondariz. Searching for both names gives notation and videos of the same tune. :)
https://folkotecagalega.gal/pezas/golpes/golpe-do-carballino
https://asociacionamigosdelagaitagallegaextremadura.wordpress.com/2012/10/10/partitura-o-golpe-de-mondariz/

To add to the confusion, on the folkotecagalega site, the dots for something called Brincadoiro include both the golpe and the pasacorredoiras as one tune. My guess is someone just notated the two tune set from the live Mirradoiro album.
https://folkotecagalega.gal/pezas/polca/brincadeiro/brincadeiro-partitura

So, my opinion now is that yes, Brincadeiro is a set name. As someone commented above, in Portuguese (a sister language to Galician) brincadeiras means games or fun.

The intro could well be based on a pasacorredoiras from Ponteareas.
The second tune is a golpe know both as Golpe de Carballiño and Golpe de Mondariz
The third tune is Pasacorredoiras de Pontevedra.

Oh and I haven’t found any other tunes called Pasacorredoiras de Pontevedra
