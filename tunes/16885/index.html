Re: Hava Nagila

Most versions I have heard just stick on the same note for the first 6 notes of the B tune (in this case, that would be a G, rather than going GB GBGB): often in the bars in the second part where you have the rests, the motif is repeated, almost as an echo, by another instrument, or singers. And finally, I think your last bar has halved the timing, so should be crotchet, crotchet, 4 quavers, with final note as a semibreve in the next bar.

And you might hear it in a Scottish session as Matt McGinn’s “Have a banana” song!

Re: Hava Nagila

“Most versions I have heard just stick on the same note for the first 6 notes of the B tune (in this case, that would be a G, rather than going GB GBGB)”

This type of figure (the ‘shout chorus’) is a very common feature of klezmer* tunes and it is simply a matter of variation whether it is played all on one note or around an arpeggio - it may be different on each iteration of the tune.

“Have you ever heard it played in a traditional Irish session ?”

That depends how ‘traditional’ and how ‘Irish’. I have heard it played now and again at sessions that were otherwise Irish, although probably with tongue somewhat in cheek.

*It is a moot point whether this is a true klezmer tune. The song has its origins in the early Zionist movement, composed in 1915 - therefore not from the repertoire of the klezmorim (Jewish wedding musicians) of Eastern Europe. But the melody is said to be an adaptation of an old nigun (a wordless devotional chant).
