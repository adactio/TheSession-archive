Three comments

The Stockport

Happy hornpipe, as played by Manchester flautist and whistler Mike Walsh. I learnt it from him some time ago.
I transcribed the tune from his album, Quarehawk, just to make sure I got it right.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lyKCwaNovKE

↳ Posted by DaveF on March 7th, 2021.

The Stockport, X:2

As DaveF says, this is a happy hornpipe from flautist and whistler Mike Walsh’s excellent album, Quarehawk.
On the album Mike precedes The Stockport Hornpipe with The Boys of Blue Hill and at a recent live gig the fleet-footed Dr Dalrymple added clog dancing too.
Mike’s album notes tell us that he “wanted to find something from my home-town to play in that style [English folk music] with Bryony [Griffith, fiddle] and Will [Hampson, melodeon]. I found this online in the 19th Century Kohler’s Repository of Dance Music collection housed in the National Library of Scotland. I can’t be certain that it’s named after Stockport in the north west of England, but as there is no Stockport in Scotland, I’d like to think it is.”
This setting is as near verbatim as possible to the Kohler Repository version with the low ‘b’ being transposed to a ‘d’ when playing the flute/whistle.

↳ Posted by FlugelStu on April 30th, 2023.

Re: The Stockport

This one is in The Charlton Memorial Tune Book (Ed. A Hall & W J Stafford, Northumbrian Pipers‘ Society 1956, p 58) listed as ’The Quay Side’, and is here at: https://thesession.org/tunes/19040.

I like to play it with another one from the Charlton book, Catterthun (ibid. p 35).
