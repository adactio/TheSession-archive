I’m A Man Youse Don’t Meet Everyday

A Scottish Borders song made popular by the Pogues

previously recorded by Jeannie Robertson (amang ithers)

the song is set to a waltz (or mazurka) meter, a simple but satisfying tune which can be danced

it can be played in the pentatonic mode on the Scottish pipes (no 4th, no 7th -i.e.; no g# in A maj.)

Line 2 - should be “Canny gaun man”. “Gaun” = “going”, as in “easy going”. Nothing to do with firearms.

Verse 4 - he didn’t shoot his own dog, he went out shooting with it. A gun dog.

If Shane McGowan sang those lyrics, he got it wrong.
It was well-known and popular in Scotland many years before The “Pogues” got hold of it.

Re: I’m A Man You Don’t Meet Every Day

Cait O‘Riordan (not Shane) sang the Pogues version (and the Pogues also played it with Sinead O’Connor later). She sings the second line “correctly” (gaun), but she shoots the dog in the last verse.

I prefer it the way Jeannie does it. I always just hear the song as some dude in a pub talking themselves up, and I think The Pogues and Tannahill Weavers’ versions don’t line up in my head.

While it was well known in Scotland it seems silly to argue that the Pogues didn’t take it to a much wider audience. People who don’t listen to traditional music know the Pogues’ music, but I know people who play a thousand Scottish tunes who don’t know who Jeannie Robertson is.

Roud’s index has sources from all over England, Scotland, America, Australia, etc., but funny enough no Irish sources. The liner notes to Jeannie’s album claimed the song was Irish (https://mainlynorfolk.info/folk/songs/imamanyoudontmeeteveryday.html), so it’s completely possible there were different lyrics being sung in Ireland that for some reason aren’t attested, or it might just be that Cait O’Riordan was being edgy when she sang that she shot the dog. Or she made up something that made sense to her from hearing the song, because it’s not like you could just go look the words up on the internet back then …

While we’re on the subject of “wrong lyrics,” Jeannie sings “river Kilearl,” which is obviously not a real thing … and Archie Fisher says “River Kildare,” which is also not a real thing … but the Pogues’ version says County Kildare, so it seems like the score card should be even.

Here’s a broadside copy of the lyrics from the 1800s, which retains County Kildare:
http://ballads.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/static/images/sheets/05000/02627.gif
