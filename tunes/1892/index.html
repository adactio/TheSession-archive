↳ Posted by Weejie on April 24th, 2014.

Time Signature

Time signature and tempo are different parameters. A 6/8 marking does not always indicate a fast beat, though because jigs are so common, it may seem that way. And the slow 6/8 is not interchangeable with 3/4: the first and fourth eighth notes are stressed, but not equally. So while a waltz would be **1** 2 3 etc., a slow 6/8 is **1** 2 3 *4* 5 6. A dry term for this is “compound duple meter,” meaning that the two beats (hence duple, each lasting a total of 3 8ths or a dotted quarter) are each divided into three (this compound: 123 and 456). This is a central parameter in notated music, and since Skinner, e.g., chose to notate Hector in 6/8, one suspects he meant something by it. ( The manuscript mentioned above is beautiful. And an old musical form that uses slow 6/8 is the Siciliana; there is a beautiful one by Fauré, often played on flute.) You can try playing Hector or the Fiddle’s Lament for Niel Gow :-) thinking of each possibility and see if it makes you play differently. It’s actually an intriguing example of how notation is more than a record, and an example I use when I teach people who play by ear how to read notation (after I ask them if they really want to learn notation, since they do fine without it). Something else to think about: when you play a jig in a fastish tempo, say you are beating two beats, each divided into three: do you play/hear **1** 2 3 **4** 5 6? Or **1** 2 3 *4* 5 6?

I didn’t attempt to notate all the strathspey-like dotted eighth / sixteenth notes, because (a) that makes it harder to read, and (b) it’s not always consistent anyway from one repetition to the next. But suffice it to say, as with most tunes, don’t play it exactly as written, listen and play it with feel. :)

Re: Niel Gow’s Lament For His Second Wife

I think here the difference between 6/8 and 3/4 are musically equivalent. Notice how all of the 3/4 versions also double the note length and double the amount of bars. Either way, the same notes are being accented, be it the 1 and 4 of the 6/8 version, or the 1 and 1 of the next bar in the 3/4 version. Musically you end up counting the same beats and accenting the same beats, and to argue over it further is to argue whether the base note length is a crotchet or a quaver, which is just a silly argument to have about any piece of music. In conclusion, its both in 3/4 and 6/8 depending entirely on what you find easier to read or understand.
