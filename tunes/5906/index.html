Two comments

“The Atlanta Schottische”

I suspect this is the Scottish influence in Kentucky. They got around those Scots and had a huge influence on music all over North America, along with the Irish, and not just in the lovely maritimes of Nova Scotia, and the isles of Cape Breton, Prince Edward and Newfoundland… This has that 16 bars about it that is shared by what has been variously called the “Highland Schottische” and the “Highland Fling”, including a second ending for the B-part. Then there’s the differences…

This can also be played in the keys of G and D Major.

A recording by the fiddler Darley Fulks is available on that very enjoyable collection from Rounder “Traditional Fiddle Music of Kentucky”, the second CD of the pair, track 2. Darley Fulks seems to confuse the ‘endings’ in a way that extends notes at the end of 4-bar phrases, making the 4th measures, both first time through and second time to end (8th) for the A-part and 1st time for the B-part, over long for the dancing, but then he was no teenager when the recording was made. There’s a nice bit of banter before he plays this number and I’ll transcribe it for you and add it here with other comment later. For now, here is a transcription of his playing from that recordings:

Darley Fulks, 1895 - 1990

There are great notes to accompany the two Rounder CDs…

In the background of the fiddler Darley’s recollection of a the dance he did in Michigan that started him off on his quest for schottisches (recorded by John Harrod July 2nd, 1978 Campton, Kentucky) can be heard a rooster, geese, and various other farmyard sounds… And he liberally seasoned this with laughter…:

“They had a square dance in a big barn. I never will forget it. And this fella got up and said, ”Anybody in this place want to square dance?“ And he was a caller you see. Well, I’d been playing the fiddle and they asked me if I could play a schottische, and I says no. I said no. Well Johnny just cooked my goose. There I had the crowd a going for me and I couldn’t play it. And that learnt me a trick brother, I went to learn schottisches and John don’t ever get caught again like that. Isn’t that a shame, by God, yeah. I had to lose all of my prestige you know, that I’d gained, that I’d built up. I was embarrassed the worst day you saw in your life. I never had anything to embarrass me so. So if you’re gonna play you better, (chuckles) you better learn more than just square dance music…”
