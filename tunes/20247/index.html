Re: The Same Old Tale That The Crow Told Me

Hard to say, and I suppose the history of the phrase might give a clue (i.e., did Grateful Dead create it, or, does it pre-date them?).

After a quick Google search, it seems the song you refer to is from the 1960’s.

Dorman Ralph’s self-titled album (which contains this tune) was released in 1999, but, was put together by Peter Narváez after Dorman’s passing in 1999. According to the album liner notes, the actual recordings took place in 1986.

With all that said, Dorman apparently learned the harmonica when he was 8 years old, and the accordion in his early teen years (so, the 1930’s for both instruments). Stating the obvious - it almost certainly must then be the case that he wrote & named this tune sometime after learning to play, and, before the date of recording. This gives a range of the 1930’s to 1986, and so, it could have been written before or after Grateful Dead wrote their song.

Perhaps someone who knew Dorman would be able to shed more light on why he had chosen this name for this tune. Unfortunately, the liner notes don’t provide all that much information about the tunes he wrote.

For this one in particular, it says:
“A rollicking original tune again performed concurrently on the accordion and the harmonica.”

Re: The Same Old Tale That The Crow Told Me

Further research turns up an answer to my own question . . . It turns out that ‘ It’s The Same old Tale . . .’ is the title of a whimsical song popularised by John Gale “Johnny” Horton (1925 –1960). a country music singer/musician/songwriter. The song therefore existed before the Grateful Dead’s ‘Uncle John’s Band’, which came out on ‘Workingman’s Dead’ (1970). Garcia would no doubt have known the Johnny Horton song, having been an old-time banjo player in his day. (He played banjo in the remarkable ensemble that recorded ‘Old and in the Way’ - along with Pete Rowan, Vassar Clements, etc.)

All this has nowt to do with ITM, of course. It just struck chord with me because, in a former life, I spent 25 years in a local pub/function group which took the Grateful Dead’s song title for its name. A song of which I have fond memories, and which will feature in my reminiscence therapy sessions as I progress further into my dotage.
