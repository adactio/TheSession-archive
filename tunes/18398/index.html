Re: Excelsior

It’s in Ryan’s Mammoth Collection (1883). Sean McGuire recorded a dramatic setting in the early 1970s with “The Mason’s Apron” (on The Best of Sean McGuire Outlet OLP 1006, 1971). James Scott Skinner composed another hornpipe of the same name.

Re: Excelsior

Annapolis button accordion player Peter Brice taught this tune at Tradfest last weekend. I got a little bit of his talk about it:

“It was published for the first time as far as I can tell in the 1840s by a man named George Saunders who was a violinist and … I don’t know if he was a music publisher but he published an important collection of music in Providence called ‘The self-instructed violin tutor’ or something like that I might not have the words exactly right*, but in that book is this hornpipe called the Baltimore Hornpipe. It was re-published in Ryan’s Mammoth Collection at the end of the 19th century as the Excelsior and Sean Maguire recorded the Excelsior Hornpipe from [that collection]. But since it was published first as the Baltimore Hornpipe and we are in Baltimore, we should call it the Baltimore hornpipe.”

*The book is called “New and scientific self-instructing school for the violin”: https://explore.digitalsd.org/digital/collection/mahoney/id/1458/

Peter plays it (and taught it to us) in G because it’s “better in that key on the accordion.” I really like that you get the low G string on it on stringed instruments as well, and it’s super good on the guitar without a capo in G, all of the arpeggios work nicely. He does one or two things slightly differently with some of the arpeggios in the B part (he played a diminished chord sometimes) but it’s essentially the same as here.

Peter’s a thorough researcher, and, well, I’m from Baltimore, so I’m inclined to be sympathetic toward his calling it the Baltimore Hornpipe ;)
