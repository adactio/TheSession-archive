Art’s Jig

From the playing of Dave Panting. Listed as traditional.

“Art” likely refers to Newfoundland accordion player “Art Stoyles”.

Dave phrases the tune a bit differently than notated here, he plays starts on bar 5 of the A Part, and continues from there. So, the first four bars of the A Part here are effectively the last four bars of his B Part, and the first four bars of the B Part here are the last four bars of his B Part. I prefer it as shown here, as the A Part is clearly all the same, and likewise for the B Part.

The tune seems to be a version of The Creel Of Turf (https://thesession.org/tunes/537 ), particularly in the first four bars of the B Part. It also has a (more distant) resemblance to Gallagher’s Frolics (https://thesession.org/tunes/160 ), particularly in the A Part.

Re: Art’s

I don’t have a different source, though I’ll keep my eye out for other versions of the tune. As you say, both do work, though, the way I’ve notated it just feels more natural and intuitive (for me, anyways), going by how most other Irish jigs are structured.

If I were playing along with the original, and wasn’t paying close attention, I’d probably start going into the 5th-8th bars of X1’s A Part, which would clash with what’s actually being played (i.e., the first four bars of the B Part, as notated in X1).
