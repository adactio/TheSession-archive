Der Schotiche

There are a considerable number of Schotiches notated in the Power manuscript from Kilmore in Co. Wexford and they must have been a popular dance in the area at the time of the manuscript’s creation - the middle of the 19th century. I’m uploading it as a polka - although it could be equally played as a barndance today. Maybe someone may recognize this particular one from the ‘Military and Old Time’ repertoire and have a name for it…the polka and schotiches in the collection would mainly appear to be what could be described as ‘Light Classical’ in style I think.

Re: Der Schotiche

The first two parts of this tune are in Kerr’s First Collection of Merry Melodies (p46) as ‘Duet for two violins’, where it’s referred to as a German Schottische, and given as an accompanying part to a setting of ‘Home Sweet Home’. It also crops up, with a rather different third part, as ‘The Original Schottische Polka’, in A Sussex Tune Book (Ed. Anne Loughran and Vic Gammon). It’s a popular session tune in Sussex.

Some years ago, I knocked together an amalgam of the two versions which, if of interest to anyone, is at: http://www.rudemex.co.uk/library/Alphabetical/Original%20Schottische,%20The%20(a).pdf

Re: Der Schotiche

The first part is virtually the same although they diverge a good bit after that, the same as ‘The Military Schottische’ that I had located.
I’ve been thinking quite a lot about this crossover of material between manuscripts in different places around the country and the fact that these old manuscripts seem to have this ‘mixture’ of what is considered traditional music today and light classical pieces, ‘old time’ waltzes quicksteps etc. There are a few possible conclusions:
1) the collections were compiled by people who considered themselves as ‘musicians’ not ‘folk or traditional musicians’ and played the full range of dance repertoire - they were obviously musically literate and may have even been ‘trained’ amateur violinists - just with a much greater range of ‘Irish’ tunes in their repertoire than contemporary violinists in Ireland would have. In the Kilmore manuscript, there are some bow markings, indications of ‘part playing’ with initials of the two players marked (‘first part mf, second part mp’) particularly in quadrille pages.
or
2) the compilers were more closely related to what we consider a traditional performer is today, but literate, and would play whatever type of ‘dance’ music was required at a gathering - making no distinction between ‘Traditional’ music and the rest of their programme. So if required to play at a crossroads dance or loft dance etc they’d have a ready supply of reels, jigs, hornpipes, set dances etc…or if it was a military dance or ballroom dance in a local gentry house they’d have their Irish repertoire along with strathspeys, galopes, schottisches, waltzes etc.
or
3) that all ‘country’ dancing in the mid 19th century had a far greater range of dances like the list above but with the Gaelic league etc and the ‘creation’ of ceili dances, the ‘foreign’ dances and music were gradually eliminated.
When I started playing in the mid 1970’s with Ross and other established ‘traditional’ players, when we played for ‘Conradh’ ceilis it was straight ceili with the odd ‘Irish’ waltz….but on occassions we might play for what were called ‘Ceili and Old Time’ dances which had a much more diverse range of dancing and hence tunes.
