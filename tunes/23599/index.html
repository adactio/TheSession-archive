Eleven Oaks, X:2

On more careful listening (and some research on tin fiddles) it sounds like the lower root is an open string, suggesting that the tune was fingered in A, although sounding in F because the fiddle was tuned down two whole tones.
In A the ornamentation falls naturally under the fingers and the plucked accompaniment is much easier, being mainly open strings.

Oh, and tempo is somewhere between second and third slowest setting.
