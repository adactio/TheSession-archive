Forty-one comments

You could play this a fast reel in amongst a set but you’d be missing out on the tune’s real beauty.

This is a tune to play slowly. It has a very haunting, almost medieval sound to it.

Some well chosen triplets or rolls could enhance it nicely. And watch out for that F natural.

↳ Posted by Jeremy on June 1st, 2001.

Brad Maloney kindly pointed me to an rte radio program, Ceili House (http://www.radio1.ie/weekend/ceilihouse/) this week, featuring the Burren Band, of Boston, led by Tommy McCarthy Jr. on fiddle. They galloped through a set of reels, and I really enjoyed their setting of Castle Kelly, as follies:

S: Burren Band, rte Ceili House
M: 4/4
L: 1/8
K: Am
|:A3 G ABcA|GE{G}E^F G2 EG|A2 cA GAcd|1 egdB cAAG:|
|2 egdB cAAg||
|age^c dfed|cAGE G2 eg|age^c d2 =cd|eaag a3 a|
|age^c dfed|cAGE G2 EG A2 cA GAcd|1egdB cAAa:|2 egdB cAAG||

The lads actually played the A part four times and the B part twice.

Shifting between the c sharp and nat gives the tune more bite, as does the F sharp in bar 2 of the A part. For variety, I play that same bar as |G~E3 G2 EG|

↳ Posted by Will Harmon on May 29th, 2003.

UPipes, I hope you’re not actually playing it with F sharps in the second part. In 50+ years, I’ve never heard F sharps there. Castle Kelly is sometimes written in Ador instead of Am, but the Ador versions usually either omit the F from the second part or mark it with a natural sign.

↳ Posted by ACW on January 25th, 2025.

The Aughrane Canal

This tune is named for the canal built to drain the bog above Aughrane Castle, Co. Galway (aka Castle Kelly), and to create a pond in front of the Manor House. The Grand Bridge crossing the canal is all the stone that remains.
