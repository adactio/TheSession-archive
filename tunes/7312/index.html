Ev Chistr ’Ta, Laou, The Chieftains

The Chieftains do this in F minor on their Celtic Wedding album. They do not do those short phrase repeats as so many others do. I like their way flows and have played it as a tune in Em for years but lately I’ve been playing it in Dm.

Those three measures of 2/4 could be two measures of 3/4 but the harmonic motion there is moving every two beats.

Here are the bones of it as a tune. As always, the dots are at best a “napkin sketch” and listening to fine musicians has no substitute.

Cheers,
J

Re: Ev Chistr ’Ta, Laou

According to Wikipedia:

“Son ar chistr” (lit. ‘Song of cider’, also called “Ev chistr ’ta Laou!”, lit. ‘Drink cider, Laou!’) is a Breton song written in 1929 by two Morbihan teenagers, Jean Bernard and Jean-Marie Prima. It was made famous through a 1970 recording by Breton singer and harpist Alan Stivell, and later, in 1976, by the Dutch band Bots under the name “Zeven dagen lang”.
