The Ramblin’ Rover, X:3

Andy M. Stewart, front man for Silly Wizzard and sadly no longer with us. This tune was from one of his solo albums, “By the Hush”, and was recorded in C#. The words are good:
(Starts with Chorus)
Oh there’s sober men & plenty
And drunkards barely twenty
There are men of over ninety
That have never yet kissed a girl.
But give me a rambling rover
Fae Orkney down to Dover
We will roam the country over
And together we’ll face the world.

There’s many that feign enjoyment
From merciless employment
Their ambition was this deployment
From the minute they left the school
And they save and scrape and ponder,
While the rest go out and squander
See the world and rove and wander -
And they’re happier as a rule.

CHORUS

I’ve roamed through all the nations
Ta’en delight in all creation
And I’ve tried a wee sensation
Where the company did prove kind.
When parting was no pleasure
I’ve drunk another measure
To the good friends that we treasure
For they always are in our mind.

CHORUS

If you’re bent with arthritis
Your bowels have got colitis
You’ve galloping ballicitus
And you’re thinking it’s time you died.
If you’ve been a man of action
While you’re lying there in traction
You may gain some satisfaction
Thinking “Jesus, at least I’ve tried”

CHORUS x 2

Re: The Ramblin’ Rover

“bollock-itis” was the ailment in the last verse, ie - “-itis of the bollocks”, a comical ailment invented by Andy M. for the song.
Possibly Andy’s greatest hit, recorded by “The Dubliners”, among others. Coincidentally, we were talking about him and his music a week past Sunday on the way home from the [ very successful ] Edinburgh “FluteFling”. We [ thanks Peter and John ] gave a lift to Tayport to guest tutor Kirsten Alstaff, who had mentioned how much “Silly Wizard” had been an inspiration to her when she became interested in folk and traditional music when quite young.

Re: The Ramblin’ Rover

Hi John, hope all is well with you. I’ll tell you a story about Andy M. and his name. “Silly Wizard” were playing at a folk festival in the Netherlands in the 1970s, and were in the company of an English folk singer, no longer with us, who had found it hilarious that Andy shared the name with the “tartan” Andy Stewart. There was a studied silence from Andy, and realising this had not gone down well, yer man tried to back-track, apologising, and saying that he supposed Andy was fed up with people laughing at his name all the time, and did it happen often ?
Andy replied, “No really, but there’s always some boring pedestrian bastard who’ll try to make a joke of it !”
You didn’t mess with Andy M. :)
