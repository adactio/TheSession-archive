Eleven comments

Molloy’s Favourite

Molloy being Matt Molloy, recorded with Kevin Burke on the album ‘Up Close’. Played with The Bloom of Youth and The Cabin Hunter, the set persuaded me to abandon all my efforts with the pipes and take up the flute instead.

↳ Posted by Conway on September 2nd, 2003.

Molloy’s

I think this one pre-dates Matt as it was included in David Lyth’s Bowing Styles (Book 1) where - it normally follows Maude Millar. This comes from the Sligo tradition of Coleman, Morrison and Killoran who recorded mainly in the 1920s and 30s.

↳ Posted by Bannerman on September 3rd, 2003.

In Baltimore sessions, a fairly common set starts with Lady Anne Montgomery, goes in Maude Millar’s, then ends with Molloy’s Favourite. Guess Bannerman’s explanation gives a bit of insight into the grouping of the last two tunes together.

↳ Posted by Jason G on September 3rd, 2003.

Molloy’s Favourite

According to the book ‘Second Wind-300 More Tunes from Mike Rafferty’, this tune was composed by Paddy Killoran:

“Mike suggests it may have been named either after Tony Molloy the great flute player from Loughrea, or named after Matt Molloy’s father.”

Molloy’s Favourite Misnomer

For you Mick Maloney fans out there, this is incorrectly given as the title for John Brennan’s, a not so very different (but different enough) Sligo reel. I don’t doubt that Mick learned the tune from Matt Molloy, as he states in the liner notes. And it was good and all of him to name it for him on his 70s album “We Have Met Together” but it is manifestly not Molloy’s Favorite and I hope this saves anyone else from spending their entire Sunday morning learning the tune and playing it into Folkfriend to get the right name…

Speaking of Matt Malloy, on his 70s albums, he played all his D tunes on a flute that was playing just shy of Eb to keep people from learning them too easily. This may be all part of the 70s-era conspiracy to keep the bar high…
