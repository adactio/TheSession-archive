Two comments

Larach Do Thacaidean

Taken from Anna Lorne Gillies on the Grand Ole Opry, some years ago. I appreciate it’s not really a march, and the B part is probably sung more as quavers (depending on the singer), but it’s a good tune nonetheless. The vocables in the chorus make it more like a waulking song (it’s anecdotal so this bolsters it) but I’ve never come across a waulking song that wasn’t in compound time. I’ve included links to the YouTube video and to the lyrics.

And I know it’s not an Irish tune…but please don’t get annoyed because it was the first time I (mostly independently) wrote something using ABC *cue pat on the back for me*.

Larach Do Thacaidean, X:2

I first heard this tune played by the Glasgow-based Riverside Ceilidh Band. The accordionist was John Carmichael, and I asked him once where he got the tunes. If I remember correctly, he told me they were from the late Clackmannanshire band leader Andrew Rankine (1930-1985), with whom John played. Both played this tune under the title “Moulin Dhu” (the black mill or the black snuff-mill, but not the same tune as the other with the same name) in a set with other schottische-type tunes.
