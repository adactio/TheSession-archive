Also played as a reel

This is also played as a reel, which was composed by J. Scott Skinner. The reel’s title is “Angus Campbell”. Skinner often wrote tunes in more than one form, i.e. as a strathspey and a reel. I wonder if he did it with this pair?

Strathspeys and Reels

Yeah, Strathspeys are commonly followed by reels, especially for dancing. Traditonally in Highland dance you do the Strathspey and then either the Highland Reel, 1/2 Tulloch Reel, Reel of Tulloch or Hullachan Reel (which, the Tulloch and Hullachan I’m told are the same dance)

Re: The Laird o’Drumblair

I’ve made a close (but not quite identical for practical reasons) transcription of the melody line of “The Laird o’Drumblair” from an original Scott Skinner manuscript held by the University of Aberdeen (available online) which has a harmony line/bass part for piano. The melody line is a little different in detail from any of the above settings, especially regarding scotch snaps. Also there are no section repeats. I’ve done the same for the reel setting “Angus Campbell” as Skinner himself arranged it. I’m not going to post an ABC here - you can access (view, download, print) my Word document containing both tunes in staff and ABC notation together with facsimile images of the manuscript originals (which have notes by the composer of especial relevance to fiddle players) here: https://app.box.com/s/dvixl4o2ez5mmuwtadupzpgxqazajurj

↳ Posted by Jemtheflute on September 30th, 2022.

Re: The Laird Of Drumblair

Further note - “Angus Campbell” is not an alternative title for the strathspey version. Scott Skinner himself made the 2/4 time reel version which he entitled “Angus Campbell”. It clearly has a linked but distinct identity and it isn’t the closest possible shift note by note between the two forms. IMO the reel really deserves to be listed as a tune in its own right, although clearly Skinner intended that it should (or at least could) be played directly after the strathspey. Refer to my transcription and source document linked in the previous comment to see what I mean.
