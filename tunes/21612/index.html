Three comments

Oonagh

I admit that after adding “Kathleen O’More” (Clinton’s Gems of Ireland no. 32) I noticed that we had no. 30 and nos. 32-37, and the gap was bothering me, so I looked up what no. 31 was, and it is a lovely minor-key jig I want to learn.

Clinton calls the tune “Onagh,” but Thomas Moore’s setting of “While gazing on the moon’s light” calls the air “Oonagh” and so does the Haverty collection, and Oonagh is a name more familiar to me (I know people with that name) so I’m going to assume Clinton made a typo. I’ll put up Moore’s version in a moment.

↳ Posted by NylonFlute on February 18th, 2022.

Oonagh, X:2

Version in M. W. Balfe’s edition of Thomas Moore’s “Irish Melodies” with the lyrics “While Gazing On The Moon’s Light.”

A recording on harpsichord (yes, I know, super Irish trad, but I like it, and the harpsichordist does remark that no one else seems to be recording it, so he will): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xFejUe8wWYM

Lyrics by Thomas Moore

While gazing on the moon’s light,
A moment from her smile I turn’d,
To look at orbs, that, more bright,
In lone and distant glory burned.
But too far
Each proud star,
For me to feel its warming flame;
Much more dear,
That mild sphere,
Which near our planet smiling came;
Thus, Mary, be but thou my own;
While brighter eyes unheeded play,
I’ll love those moonlight looks alone,
That bless my home and guide my way.

The day had sunk in dim showers,
But midnight now, with lustre meek,
Illumined all the pale flowers,
Like hope upon a mourner’s cheek.
I said (while
The moon’s smile
Play’d o’er a stream, in dimpling bliss),
The moon looks
On many brooks,
The brook can see no moon but this;
And thus, I thought, our fortunes run,
For many a lover looks to thee,
While oh! I feel there is but one,
One Mary in the world for me.

↳ Posted by NylonFlute on February 18th, 2022.

Oonagh, X:3

Because I like the octave leaps in Clinton’s version, I’m transposing it down to E minor so it is playable on D whistle or keyless flute (if you can half-hole a D-sharp!), and removing the eighth-rests because they sound awful on playback and interpreters should decide for themselves where to breathe.
