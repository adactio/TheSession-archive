↳ Posted by slainte on June 5th, 2007.

King of Meenasillagh - I don’t think so

On The Brass Fiddle, James Byrne plays the Old Wheel of Fortune (this tune) and Ri Mhin na Salach (The King of Meenasillagh). They are not the same tune at all and on this basis the alternative title for this tune and https://thesession.org/tunes/3268 are quite wrong.

The turn of King of Meenasillagh doesn’t go into A. If you want to hear it, go to http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wUv3SL3Cio4

The name according to the LP liner notes refers to a local stepdance who frequently asked for the tune to dance to. (Not this tune!)

THIS is the King of Meenasillagh

Seeing as there is some confusion as to what tune is really the King of Meenasillagh, here is a rough-and-ready ABC of it. I originally learned it from “The Brass Fiddle”. Of course, the flute can’t recreate all the great droning effects. That video of James Byrne from 1988 is marvellous stuff.

The Old Wheel Of Fortune, X:4

The fourth setting here is the “strathspey” or “highland”-inspired version heard on Altan’s ‘Blackwater’ album, from the “Strathspey/Con McGinley’s/The Newfoundland” set. Took me far too long to even recognize it as a slower version of this reel, and when I did, well… stupid me. Ha… although it contains Scottish snaps and various highland elements, I wouldn’t really classify it as a “strathspey”, by any means, nor even a Donegal “highland”, really. It’s got more of a steady, “march” feel, clearly built off of this reel. Maybe a similar feeling to tunes like “Calum’s Road”.
