Mulholland’s Fancy (barndance)

This is a fun tune from a good album called Three Way Street https://thesession.org/recordings/467 by Seamus Egan, Mick Moloney and Eugene O’ Donnell. The fourth and fifth parts are “Farewell to Whisky”.

↳ Posted by Nigel Gatherer on August 21st, 2008.

Edit

I’ve relabeled it as a polka as it is a set of polkas (according to Mick Moloney). A more thorough edit would be to divide it in 2/4 bars and, I think, also to separate the two tunes, since it’s a set. Farewell to whiskey already exists on the site (https://thesession.org/tunes/1529)

↳ Posted by sixholes on May 15th, 2013.

Re: Mulholland’s Fancy

Yes indeed, Sixholes, you’ve spotted it as two distinct 2/4 tunes. I’d say it is probably composed out of parts of three 2/4 tunes! The first three parts are concocted from the 2nd figure of Roche’s “Orange and Green” quadrille (Volume 2, page 42), and Roche himself may have been using two polkas, the first eight bar part being the half of one polka in D, the second sixteen bar part made up of the two parts of a different polka in G! A little more probing may identify them.

↳ Posted by Seán Ó Coinn on November 8th, 2023.

P. S.

As I went to file away the first part of this medley, just as I suspected, Roche was using one part of a two part polka. The complete polka is to be found, untitled unfortunately, in Breandán Breathnach’s Ceol Rince na hÉireann, Cuid 5, Uimhir 93. Bill Black has given to it the spurious title, “Kerry Border”, using which it can be found on Tunepal, appropriately asterisked.

↳ Posted by Seán Ó Coinn on November 8th, 2023.

P. P. S.

Roche uses the first part of “Neil Gow’s Farewell to Music” in the 4th figure of the same set of quadrilles, “Orange and Green”, further down the same page, and this may help to explain the inspiration behind combining it with the 2nd figure tunes to form “Mulholland’s Fancy”.
