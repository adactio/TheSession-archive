Six comments

Monea Castle

This is a lovely slow strathspey written by Fermanagh box-player Jim McGrath. He says “This plantation castle was built in 1618 by Rev. Malcolm Hamilton on a site some six miles from Enniskillen. Although it was burnt in the 18th century, it remains in a reasonable state of preservation. This composition tries to reflect its sad and somber history.”
Thanks to https://www.cranfordpub.com/ for the abcs.

Re: Monea Castle

I found Jim McGrath’s solo CD of his own recordings on eBay, and bought it immediately . As I said above, this tune appealed to me on first hearing, but because the recording is a bit obscure, as many “own label” recordings are, I thought maybe there were only myself and “coppers and brass” in Scotland who might know it.
Not so, as it turned out, and I should have known better. Cathal McConnell was a guest at the recent “Alex Green Whistle Festival” this weekend past. He arrived at the Lemon Tree Arts Centre, secured a pint of Guinness, and had the whistle out within 10 minutes. Two Aberdeen whistle players, Sara Reith and Helen Lynch asked Cathal to play “that strathspey”, which no-one had a name for. I recognised it immediately and the 4 of us played it together. Interestingly, all 4 of us had moved the tune up to “D”, instead of Jim’s original “A”, which had too many low notes for whistles. Downside of that is that it forces a “3rd octave” “d‘’” in the second part, which is fairly brutal with 4 whistles. Nevertheless, I would like to think that Jim McGrath would be happy to have this tune in the process of becoming famous in Scotland, - now the rest of you, go on and learn it !
Many thanks, Jim.

Re: Monea Castle

I met jim some 40 years ago when I moved to Fermanagh and played with him many times during the 1980s. We are still in touch although somewhat intermittently. He was much influenced in his early years by the accordeon playing of Jimmy Shand hence when he began composing himself it was no surprise that he included some strathspeys in his collection. His CD Melodeous Accord earned him Composer of the year (which year I cant recall) at the Gradam Ceoil Irish music awards. A kind generous and funny man and a mighty musician.
