Rainbow Schottische

RAINBOW SCHOTTISCHE. AKA and see “Andy Kerrin’s Hornpipe/Set Dance,” “Duck’s Eyeball [2],” “Right Foot, Left Foot,” “Stephen Baldwin’s Schottische,” “Tom Bhetty’s Hornpipe,” “Winter(‘s) Night Schottische.” American, Schottische. USA, southwestern Pa. D Major (‘A’, ‘B’ and ‘C’ parts) & G Major (‘D’ part) {Ford}: G Major (‘A’ part) & D Major?? (‘B’ part) {Bayard}. Standard tuning. AA’B (Bayard): AABCDD (Ford). The “Rainbow Schottische” title dates at least to the 1852 when it was published in New York by Firth, Pond & Co. with composer credits to Henry Kleber. (Another “Rainbow Schottische” was composed in 1878 by N.A. Bouse, but is a different melody). It remained a popular title for more than 60 years, republished in numerous versions for the pianoforte, in brass band arrangements, and for fife and drum corps. In the 1890’s “Rainbow Schottische” was published in Australia by John Snow and company

http://www.ibiblio.org/fiddlers/RAA_RAJ.htm

A lengthy biography of Kleber follows, then a bit more info:

“See also Henry Reed’s “Kiss Waltz [2],” which uses the ‘A’ strain of “Rainbow Schottische.” Paul Mitchell informs that piedmont North Carolina fiddler Lauchlin Shaw played the tune under the title “Duck’s Eyeball [2].” Virgil Craven called the tune “Rainbow Schottische.” Paul also connects the melody with an Irish tune played by south Ulster native Gerry O’Connor, who called it “Tommy Bheaty’s Hornpipe,” a nod to Tommy McCarthy. There were two Tommy McCarthys, relates Paul, Tommy “Puck” McCarthy, and Tommy, son of Betty (and hence “Tom Bhetty”). Allan’s Ballroom Companion, 19??; pg. 23. Bayard (Dance to the Fiddle), 1981; No. 409B, pg. 388. Ford (Traditional Music in America), 1940; pg. 156.”

The Tommy Bhetty in question is actually Tommy ‘Bhetty’ Maguire of Fermanagh, he of the waltz. I learned this from Ira Ford’s book Traditional Music of America, which I obtained for a surprisingly low price years ago. This is my adoption of what was in Ford, whatever that was…! It was in D, I remember that at least. A fun tune on the pipes or flute too - you get a good workout manipulating your keys here.

I have a recording of fiddler John Joe McElroy playing this tune. I think he was from Co. Down. I also have a recording of Indiana fiddler John Summers playing it, with a note that he learned it in Decatur Illinois in 1920.

Here’s a video of some well coiffed dancers strutting their stuff to a dainty ballroom rendition: http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wZvAhYDtNtc&feature=youtube_gdata_player
