Wouldn’t call it polka, nor dance tune in any modern sense…. =)

Actually it is a medieval song of which two voices have been recorded in the Spanish LLibre Vermeille de Montserrat. Text goes:

Stella splendens in monte ut solis radium
Miraculis serrato exaudi populum
Concurrunt universi gaudentes populi
Divites et egeni, grandes et parvuli

Hanter Dro

Benoît, this, again, is a Hanter Dro: best written in 3/4 (see the comments I made about: Er Pasker
https://thesession.org/tunes/10102 )
The original medieval tune has been modified to suit the needs of this dance. The 1st down beat “B/B:A” is repeated as a down beat again, after only 3 groups of 3 beats. That is it: 3x3. Don’t you join this pedestrian dance in your big-arse 4x4!

Re: Stella Splendens

Adenda;
In the original version, there are 50 beats, if what is written corresponds to what the tune would have actually sounded like at the time. But you’ll notice they do not fall neatly into 2/4, 3/4 nor 5/4 patterns.
We do not know how these religious songs were danced but they were danced (in the form of ring-dances such as they are still danced in Brittany or Greece today); there was no contradiction between religious worship and dancing in that particular time & place.
The original manuscript came together at the very end of the 14th century in Catalunya.
The Llibre Vermell de Montserrat was compiled specifically to harmonise the needs of the dancing pilgrims and the praying pilgrims (those were porous categories), so there would be no clash at the holy shrine of Montserrat. So much we know from the book itself:
“ Quia interdum peregrini quando vigilant in ecclesia Beate Marie de Monte Serrato volunt cantare et trepudiare, et etiam in platea de die, et ibi non debeant nisi honestas ac devotas cantilenas cantare, idcirco superius et inferius alique sunt scripte. Et de hoc uti debent honeste et parce, ne perturbent perseverantes in orationibus et devotis contemplationibus. ”
= “ Since the pilgrims wish to sing and dance while they keep their watch at night in the church of the Blessed Mary of Montserrat, and in the light of day as well; and since no songs should be sung in the church unless they are chaste and pious, this is why we have put forward these songs in written. And they should be decently performed, taking care that no one who keeps watch in prayer and contemplation be disturbed. ”

https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llibre_Vermell_de_Montserrat
