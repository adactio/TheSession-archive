The Humours Of Cullen

A Jig or Quadrille, published in the William Christie Collection of 1820.
William Christie (1778-1849) was a dancing master, fiddler postmaster and composer from Cuminestown, in Aberdeenshire.
However, although published in Scotland, this tune must surely be Irish. I know of a Cullen in Co. Cork & another in Co. Tipperary, but no doubt there are others.
I wonder if anyone here has more information on the origin of this tune & if it has other names? If so, it may already be in this library under a different name.
Cheers,
Dick

Hi Dick - good photo of yourself and Davy on the homepage. I’m sure it would have been a good session.
A wee bit more information about yer man :
“He published a collection of reels etc. at Edinburgh, containing 71 pieces, 68 of which were his own composition or arrangement, the other 3 being by John Christie, which were very good, and [he] was the first to popularize waltzes in the North of Scotland. His best strathspeys are ”McGillan’s“ and ”Mormond“. As a violinist he was much admired”.
Source : “Musical Scotland Past And Present” by David Baptie, published originally in 1894.
As Matt has pointed out above, Cullen [ famous for “Cullen Skink” ] is not far from Cuminestown, so it is quite possibly an original composition. Having said that, it would be the first time I’ve ever come across any Scottish tune with “The Humours Of…. ” in the title, that’s something I’ve only ever associated with Irish traditional music. It does sound more Irish to me than Scottish - but maybe that’s just the way you play it ? :)
Assuming you have access to the whole book, does it have any obvious Irish tunes in it ?
A’ the best, Kenny

Re: The Humours Of Cullen

It’s not claimed by Christie, and he attaches his name to all tunes he wrote. There aren’t many Irish tunes in the collection*, and those that are there are labelled as such; “Humours of Cullen” isn’t. I have written before about a very few Scots tunes with the name “Humours” in the title - https://thesession.org/discussions/39754#comment801450 . Anyway, I see no reason why this should not be a local tune.

* = actually only one that I can see: “Dungarven”.

Re: The Humours Of Cullen

Thanks Nigel, it certainly helps when you actually have the book itself, when tracking down the details. Interesting to know that there two other ‘Humours’ tunes in Scottish collections.

As Kenny said above, the Irish flavour to this tune may well come from my interpretation of it, but having said that, noted Scottish Fiddle player Duncan Wood also felt this tune had more of an Irish than Scottish lilt to it.

Of course, with so many Irish summer migrant workers travelling around Scotland each year, for so many years, I suppose it’s inevitable that many tunes were shared.
