Re: The Devil’s Polska

It can often help to think of a tune like this as being in 9/8 or not to look at notation at all but just use the ears.
The written complexity is to try to make sense of the uneven beats in that, as written in the first setting, the second beat of each bar is longer, like the tempo slows down. As written in the second setting, all the beats are the same length (beats being crotchets, three per bar).

Actually, Edgar, I suspect that if you heard it played by a tutor a couple of times, it would start to make a lot more sense that any written representation. It seems to me, at least, that many polskas (like this one) are extremely difficult to notate in a way that really conveys the pulse and rhythm. A bit like trying to capture a good jazz solo in print. It only goes to illustrate a point often made on this site, that MSS of trad music can, in general, only be a guide to how it goes, and hearing good players is the way to get familiar with the style and groove.

Re: The Devil’s Polska

If you watch a Swedish musician playing a polska (many examples on YouTube) they are likely to be tapping a foot on beats one and three of each bar. I did this while playing the Audio of X:1 (slowed down a bit) and the pulse of the tune falls into place. It has been described to me as ‘the rhythm of an egg rolling downhill’.

I have two left feet and no inclination to take to the floor, but no doubt a good way to get the hang of some of these polskas - which constitute a very varied genre, of course - is to dance them, or at least watch them being danced. I claim no expertise in these things, but there is an old muzos‘ adage about playing for dancers - ’look at their feet’.

And yes, it’s unfortunate that they have to be listed here under ‘Waltzes’, but the site was set up to focus on Irish session tunes and it would be bit much to expect Jeremy to expand the categories to embrace absolutely everything that anybody thinks of bringing along.
