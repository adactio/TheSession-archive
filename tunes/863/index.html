Eight comments

The Boys of Coomanore

I came across this tune in Dervish and Claire Mann’s albums and found the sheet music at JC’s ABC tune finder.
Really great jig. The first half of the tune sounds quite similar to that of “the Rolling Wave.”
Enjoy!

↳ Posted by slainte on July 27th, 2002.

Boys of Coomanore

Great, rolling jig! I like Dervish’s setting, which is a little different--lots of tweaked notes that lead the melody from one bar to the next so you never want it to stop. Something along the lines of:

M: 6/8
K: D
|:FGE FDD|cde dAG|~F3 FDD|FGE FAG|
|FGE FDD|cde dAG|FGE FAG|1 EDC DAG:|
|2 EDC D2 B|
|:ABc dcd|fed ecA|ABc ~d3|fag fed|
|ABc deg|fed edc|AGF EAG|1 FDD D2 B:|
|2 FDD D3|

↳ Posted by Will Harmon on July 27th, 2002.

The Boys of Coomanore

I’ve been thinking this jig sounds like a composite of these two tunes:

The Rolling Waves: https://thesession.org/tunes/88 (1st part)
The Hag with the Money: https://thesession.org/tunes/351 (2nd part)

You will also find some resemblance between this tune and Allistrum’s March: https://thesession.org/tunes/2136

On a Dervish CD the Jig is called The Boys Of Coomanore, which actually is a place in Ireland.

Re: The Boys Of Coomanore

A really beautiful setting of this jig on pipes and fiddle (both played by himself) opens the fine CD by Maurice Bradley of Ballinoscreen, County Derry, just released. He names it as a Kerry jig, and follows it with Farewell to Ballyhulla and The Kilkenny Jig.
