Five comments

Mrs Savage’s Whim

A robust three-two hornpipe that I particularly enjoy playing. It is from the Playford Dancing Master of 1713, so is presumably much older. Note that there are no repeats; repeats over-egg the cake with this tune.

There are a few little technical problems - fingering, intonation, bowing and timing - for the fiddler to be aware of. For instance, the quaver pairs should not be hurried but kept to the beat (it is a dance tune); likewise the running quavers in bar 12. Make sure the first beat of each bar is always clearly identified by its accent to bring out the typical syncopation of the three-two, otherwise the tune will start sounding like a waltz! Regarding intonation, watch out for the octaves in bars 7, 11 and 14, and the A-sharp in bars 12 and 16 - I like to shift the first finger back quickly on the A-string to play that A-sharp (well, that’s one way - there are others).

The ties between bars 5 and 6, and between 15 and 16 are my own suggestions for further syncopation, which of course may safely be ignored.

Re: Mrs Savage’s Whim

Regarding the matter of repeats for this tune: when this has been played in the English session that I go to, the A part and B part have both been repeated. And that’s how I play it.

For the record, the instructions in the original Playford are for the A part to be repeated but the B part not.
