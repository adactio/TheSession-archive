A misunderstanding of the title…

Does the title come off their CD as well, or is it your attempt at a translation, Joe? You’re obviously not from the northeast! It’s not “had” as in “to have”. It’s “haad” or “ha’d” or “haud” (or however you want to spell it) meaning “hold”, or more probably in a looser sense of “keep her there” or “keep her with you”, rather than physically detaining her! Your title doesn’t make sense as it stands. It’s not “I” did anything. It’s a request for someone else to do it.

I’m not sure but I think “win at her” means something like “get to her” or “find my way to her”.

I think your key sig should be Amix. It’s a Border pipes tune.

Re: Haud The Lass Till I Come At Her

An old tune with many settings and name variations. I think the oldest is from David Young’s 1741 ‘MacFarlane Manuscript’ where he has it as Had The Lass Till I Win At Her. The Traditional Tune Archive suggests the name really means ‘hold the horse while I mount’ but, if so, the euphemism is a bit uncomfortable to my modern ears. Fits perfectly on the Lowland / Border pipes and you would have to imagine it would have been part of the repertoire of Lowland pipers back in the day. I play it with more of a 2/4 feel than as a standard reel, similar to a polka and it does appear in some of the old collection in 2/4, Aird for example.
