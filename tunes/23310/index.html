Five comments

Once I Had A Lover

This beautiful air in 3/4 is the melody for the well-known folk song ‘Once I had a sweet heart’.
The feel is that of a slow mazurka or waltz, or a retreat march.
The song was recorded by Nuala Kennedy recently and, on hearing it, I was moved to transcribe it.
(It appears as ‘Sally sits weeping’ on her new release: https://thesession.org/recordings/7854).

The song is beautiful of course but the tune does a good ‘stand alone’ job as an instrumental.
It sounds particularly well in the key of D on the uilleann pipe, hence this transposition (Nuala sings it in F).
The modal tug between the c# and c natural and that yearning slide to the e#/f natural play a major role in giving expression to this lament.

Re: Once I Had A Lover

I used the first verse of the song sung by Nuala Kennedy in 2023 to guide my transcription.
The slurs/ties in the score reflect the melismas in the song.

This song has been covered by many artists of the folk song and folk era and you’ll find more info and other versions on the net, if not at your local sing or session.

The subject matter (the heart broken wanderer) probably makes it a Romantic or Late Romantic era production (for the words).
The theme of the dream was common in those days and one of the verses seems an echo of the song ‘She moved through the fair’.

Re: Once I Had A Sweetheart

The song can be found in the Cecil Sharp/Maud Karpeles collection ‘The Crystal Spring’. Another verse goes: “I’ll set sails of silver and sail towards the sun/ I’ll set sails of silver and sail towards the sun/ And my true love shall weep/ My true love shall weep/ My true love shall weep for me after I’ve gone”. My favourite recording is from the classic Pentangle album ‘Basket of Light’ featuring Jon Renbourn on sitar.
