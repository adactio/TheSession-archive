Second part

I learned this tune from Mickey Dunne last week, and one thing that’s missing is that he never played an f sharp on that long f of the second bar of the B part. He would slide up from e and linger somewhere on f-nat, I really have no idea how to get even close to write this down. Plus, he would play c sharps when doing the triplets.

The Stone Wall

Joe mentioned that he was went over to a friend’s house one day but there was no answer at the door. To pass an hour away, he took out the whistle and composed this tune. He was up against a stone wall and, so, gave the tune the title “The Stone Wall”. Maybe this could be changed?

Regarding that F, I heard him play it as a very exagerrated slide to F# which was then tied across the bar creating a sort of syncopated feel to it. Unusual tune anyway.

I never met Joe but he seemed like a character from the video footage I watched at the ITMA.
