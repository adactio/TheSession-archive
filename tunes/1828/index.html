Niel Gow’s Wife

Scottish fiddler Bruce MacGregor recorded a similar tune as Invergary Well: the first part of the tune is almost indentical to that of Niel Gow’s Wife. According to the liner notes, he pick it up from Simon Fraser’s second collection. Does anybody play the tune?

↳ Posted by slainte on July 19th, 2003.

Niel Gow’s Wife / Invergary Well

Until recently slainte - no. As you mentioned, Bruce took the tune from Simon Fraser’s second collection of 49 tunes which was never published. (Fraser’s son Angus planned publication but died before the project was completed) The original mss are housed in Edinburgh University Library.

However, Bruce’s CD “101 Reasons to do Nothing” is very highly regarded, certainly in Scotland. Also when not playing solo or with Blazin’ Fiddles, Bruce is active in many fields not least workshops where he’s often asked about the tunes. That said, most of the tunes played subsequently in sessions and elsewhere tend to be those by his mentor Donald Riddell.

There’s a fairly major project currently underway with Simon Fraser’s collections and tunes - perhaps things will change.

Neil Gow’s Wife, X:4

I sat down to transcribe this, as none of the three settings above matched what I was hearing from the Danny O’Donnell recording on Milestone at the Garden, and then quickly realized that my finished ABC was almost exactly the same as the one listed in ceolachan’s comment above. The only differences being some minor rhythms and F sharp/naturals.

Not sure if the inside booklet from that album gives more info, but the track that I learned this from is only listed as “Thistle and Shamrock - schottisches ” on the back of the CD case. This recording is listed as “The Watchmaker” on irishtune and is listed as a highland. That makes sense to me, given it’s the bouncier Donegal version, despite being composed and played as a Strathspey originally in Scotland. This is one of the first highlands I learned and still one of my favorites to this day.

Neil Gow’s Wife, X:5

Another setting of Danny O’Donnell, this time from his album Bean Ag Baint Duilisc. Its very similar to the versions listed by myself and ceolachan, but on this recording he plays it in G dorian, alters some of the rhythms (particularly by getting rid of most, but not all, of the triplets) and hits that high bflat at the high point of the B section.

The CD booklet lists this tune as “Neil Gow’s Wife” and states: “The second tune was naturalized in Donegal, and perhaps beyond, as a highland and would have been more widely known throughout the county, usually in the key of A minor”. So whether or not you think of this tune as a “highland” or a “strathspey”, seemingly the fella (Mick Brown of Teelin) who wrote the album notes 24 years after this was recorded thought of this version as a highland and I’m content with that haha
