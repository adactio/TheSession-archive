Fifteen comments

Finbarr Dwyer

I learnt this tune from the Finbarr Dwyer recording “Accordion music from the Soul”. It certainly sound like it could indeed be one of his own compositions. It’s quite a difficult tune (even for box players) but has that unmistakeable Dwyer stamp on it.

I think it’s a fantastic tune and, if it his one of Finbarr’s, then I think it’s one of his best. I’ve never heard it played elsewhere so I’m going to try and give it a bit more publicity next time I’m around at sessions.

Bear in mind, this is a very difficult tune to transcribe and I found it particularly difficult to find out what exact notes FInbarr was playing in bar 5 and 13 (ie bar 5 of part 2). That semiquaver G before the A roll in bar 5 is not a mistake. Listen to his playing and you’ll something along those lines. Now, I think he’s throwing some trick there which is very hard to notate or transcribe. He may be playing an A, and E together giving a chord effect. I’m not exactly sure, but this version is very close anyway.

By the way, my adequate standard of Irish tells me that Clais an Adhmid mean “The Wooden Sword” or “The Oak Sword”.

Clais An Adhmid, X:2

Setting for mouth whistling fitting my own register. I hope it will be helpful for other people. For this particular tune, as it keeps very low, I didn’t need to do any change of octave in the middle, I just have to whistle the whole tune one octave higher.

This setting is a bit different to setting X1 (nice job on the ABC!) and it is very much inspired by the playing of box player Pat Barton.

This is the video of him playing, to watch it you must click on the link (not on the little screen or the play button):

https://youtu.be/3GdfZWGCxVI

Clais An Adhmid, X:3

Setting in D Mixolydian.

The original setting in A Mixolydian would be very difficult to play for tin whistle players, flutes and pipes because of the G#s. This would be a much more suitable key to play in a session.

A setting in this key but with changes of octave for tin whistle will follow.

Finbar Dwyer’s

I learnt this tune in Glasgow in the 90s from Donegal musicians Dinny Boyle and Danny Duffy (both now sadly gone) when we played together in The Four Provinces Ceili Band. They thought it was a composition of Finbar Dwyer and played it after The Kylebrack Rambler (in D) but I haven’t been able to find it under Finbar Dwyer’s compositions, or anywhere else.
If it is one this site already, I’ll be glad to add this version to it.
