Re: The White Cockade

The White Cockade has also been recorded by the fife and drum corps of the US Marine Band. It was the tune played by the Minute Men when they marched to face the British at Concord at the beginning of the American Revolution. Some say they chose it because it was a Jacobite tune and so likely to irk the redcoats.

Re: The White Cockade

The White Cockade is a Jacobite tune from Scotland and Lillibulero an old Irish tune from the Orange side. Paddy Moloney paired them together somewhat mischievously and the Chieftains played the set in Belfast, raising some eyebrows at the time as Lillibulero is still very much a blood and thunder Orange marching tune known as The Protestant Boys.
The chorus seems to be from the Gaelic , meaning ‘ The lily was evident, we carried the day’. The triumphant Orange lily being the emblem of Dutch William’s house as opposed to the White Cockade worn by the Catholic James II side.
Of course it was a kind of a mutually respectful gesture by Paddy Moloney to reference history in this way. At the end of the performance, he remarked, “They won” and smilingly and smoothly moved on to the next set. But it might still be one of the tunes you’d be careful with in NI if you just played it by itself. Not quite as bad as The Sash which could get you lamped. But it’s definitely safer paired with the White Cockade.
Nevertheless it’s still a “Party” song . And that doesn’t always mean balloons and squeakers.
