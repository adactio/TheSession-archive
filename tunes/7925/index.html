It’s know collectively with another similar Polka (that I will sumbit shortly) as the “C Polkas”. I’ve only ever heard it referred to by that name.

Re: C polka a.k.a. Polka d’Auvergne (Bouscatel’s)

X: 1
T: Bourrée d’Auvergne
R: polka
M: 2/4
L: 1/8
K: C ionian
|:G,A/B/ CC|B/C/D/B/ C2|D/E/F/D/ EC|1 B,/C/D/C/ B,/A,/G,:|2 DB, C2||
|:DE/F/ GE/G/|FE D>E|F/E/D EC|1 B,/C/D/C/ B,/A,/G, :|2 B,/C/D/B,/ C2:||

This is a transcription of a track recorded by the famous Auvergnat/Parisian piper (cabretaïre) Antoine Bouscatel circa 1930.
This polka must have been popular in the Massif Central for a number of decades before it was recorded. It is still popular among the French ‘Folk/Trad’ musicians today. Polkas, as it is commonly known in the research/music community, took Europe (and the rest of the colonial world) by storm in the 19th century. Some of the polkas travelled and integrated local repertoires virtually unchanged, some were printed, played and eventually forgotten, more were composed in various vernacular styles and are still with us today. This one is recognisably “Central French” and its contour is not dissimilar to the “Mazurka d’Auvergne” also posted on this site (https://thesession.org/tunes/8758).
If these precisions can help situate the above dots and discussions…
